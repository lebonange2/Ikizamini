Problem 1 (Q1):
Given the parametric equations \(x(t) = 3 \cos(t)\) and \(y(t) = 3 \sin(t)\), identify the graph.
A) A circle centered at the origin with radius 3.
B) An ellipse centered at the origin with semi-major axis 3.
C) A parabola opening to the right.
D) A line through the origin.
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The parametric equations \(x(t) = 3 \cos(t)\) and \(y(t) = 3 \sin(t)\) represent a circle because they follow the form \(x^2 + y^2 = r^2\), where \(r\) is the radius 3.

Explanation:
The coordinates (x, y) are derived from trigonometric functions \(\cos(t)\) and \(\sin(t)\), making a circle.

Common mistake note:
Mistaking the shape as an ellipse; however, the parametric form \(\cos^2(t) + \sin^2(t) = 1\) confirms a circle.

Problem 2 (Q2):
What is the shape of the curve given by \(x(t) = t\) and \(y(t) = t^2\)?
A) A circle
B) A line
C) A parabola
D) An ellipse
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The parametric form \(x(t) = t\) and \(y(t) = t^2\) indicates a parabola \(y = x^2\).

Explanation:
As \(t\) varies, \(x\) is \(t\) and \(y\) is \(t^2\). Graphing \(y\) in terms of \(x\) gives \(y = x^2\).

Common mistake note:
Confusing the curve with a circle due to squared terms without recognizing the parametric independence of \(t\).
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 3 (Q3):
Which of the following parametric equations represents a line?
A) \(x(t) = 3t, y(t) = 2t + 1\)
B) \(x(t) = t^2, y(t) = t^3\)
C) \(x(t) = \cos(t), y(t) = \sin(t)\)
D) \(x(t) = e^t, y(t) = \ln(t)\)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Both \(x(t) = 3t\) and \(y(t) = 2t + 1\) are linear in \(t\), describing a parametric line.

Explanation:
Parametric equations \(x=a_1t+b_1\), \(y=a_2t+b_2\) graph a line. Here, \(a_1 = 3\) and \(a_2 = 2\).

Common mistake note:
Misidentifying \(x(t)=t^2, y(t)=t^3\) as linear; however, the powers of \(t\) should be 1 for both.

Problem 4 (Q4):
Describe the curve formed by the parametric equations \(x(t) = 2 + \sin(t)\) and \(y(t) = 3 + \cos(t)\).
A) A circle of radius 1 centered at (2, 3)
B) An ellipse centered at (2, 3)
C) A line parallel to the y-axis
D) A hyperbola centered at (2, 3)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The equations describe a circle of radius 1 due to \((x-2)^2 + (y-3)^2 = 1\), centered at (2, 3).

Explanation:
Shifting the circle by (2, 3) from the origin, its radius remains 1 due to \(\sin(t)\) and \(\cos(t)\) functions.

Common mistake note:
Assuming it forms an ellipse; however, \(\cos^2(t) + \sin^2(t) = 1\) confirms a circle.

Problem 5 (Q5):
Find the Cartesian equation of the curve given by \(x(t) = 4\cos(t)\) and \(y(t) = 4\sin(t)\).
A) x^2 + y^2 = 16
B) x^2 + y^2 = 4
C) x^2 - y^2 = 16
D) y = x^2 + 4
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Replacing \(\cos(t)\) and \(\sin(t)\) in \(x^2+y^2=r^2\), we get \(16(\cos^2(t)+\sin^2(t))=16\).

Explanation:
The identity \(\cos^2(t) + \sin^2(t) = 1\) plugs into \(x^2 + y^2 = 4^2\), simplifying to \(x^2 + y^2 = 16\).

Common mistake note:
Miscomputing radius as 4; forgetting to square 4 in \(x^2+y^2=r^2\) which results in 16.

Problem 6 (Q6):
What curve is traced by \(x(t) = 6t\) and \(y(t) = 3t - 1\) when \(t\) ranges over all real numbers?
A) A parabola
B) An ellipse
C) A hyperbola
D) A line
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
Both components have \(t\) in degree 1; thus, the lines are linear equations in \(t\).

Explanation:
Linear equations in \(t\) produce a line in the \(xy\)-plane with slope derived from coefficients of \(t\).

Common mistake note:
Mistaking linear growth as parabolic; confusion arises when ignoring linearity of both terms.

Problem 7 (Q7):
Convert the parametric equations \(x(t) = 1 + 2t\) and \(y(t) = 3 \text{e}^t\) to Cartesian form.
A) x = 1 + 2 \ln(y/3)
B) y = 3e^{(x-1)/2}
C) x = y^2 + 1
D) y = \log(x - 2)
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
From \(x(t) = 1 + 2t\), solve for \(t\): \(t = (x-1)/2\). Substitute into \(y(t) = 3e^t\): \(y = 3e^{(x-1)/2}\).

Explanation:
Solve \(x(t)\) for \(t\) then substitute into \(y(t)\), leading to \(y = 3e^{(x-1)/2}\).

Common mistake note:
Confusing the process with simple algebra; logarithmic transformation is necessary for exponential \(y(t)\).
