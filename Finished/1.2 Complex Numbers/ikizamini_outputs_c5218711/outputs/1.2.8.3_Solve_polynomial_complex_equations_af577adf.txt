Problem 1 (Asd456):
Find the roots of the equation: z^2 + 4z + 4 = 0
A) -3i
B) 2 - 2i
C) 2 + 2i
D) -2
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
The given quadratic equation factors as (z + 2)^2. The roots are found by setting the binomial equal to zero: z + 2 = 0, giving z = -2.

Explanation:
No need for the quadratic formula; the equation is a perfect square.

Common mistake note:
Assuming complex conjugate roots using the quadratic formula when not necessary.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 2 (Xcv789):
Solve the equation: z^3 + 1 = 0
A) 1
B) -1
C) i
D) -i
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The equation factors as (z + 1)(z^2 - z + 1) = 0. The roots are z = -1 and the complex conjugate pair z = (-1 ± √3i)/2. The real root is z = -1.

Explanation:
Used the sum of cubes formula, avoiding unnecessary calculation of all three roots.

Common mistake note:
Neglecting the complex conjugate pair when considering only real solutions.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 3 (Zxc123):
Solve the equation: z^4 + 8z^2 + 16 = 0
A) -2i
B) 2
C) 2i
D) -2
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
The equation factors as (z^2 + 4)^2 = 0, leading to z^2 = -4. Taking the square root gives complex solutions: z = ±2i. However, since we're looking for real roots, only z = -2 is valid.

Explanation:
Factored into a perfect square trinomial, finding the real solution directly.

Common mistake note:
Assuming all four roots are relevant when asked for real solutions.

Problem 4 (Qwe456):
Find the roots of the equation: (z - 1)(z + 3i)^2 = 0
A) 1, 3i
B) 1, -3i
C) -1, 3i
D) 1, -3i, 3i
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Setting each factor equal to zero gives roots z = 1 and z = -3i. The real root is z = 1, and one complex root is z = -3i. There's no repetition.

Explanation:
Factored the equation into linear and quadratic terms, finding unique roots directly.

Common mistake note:
Including extraneous or repeated roots.

Problem 5 (Rty789):
Solve the equation: z^3 - 1 = (z - 1)^3
A) i
B) 1
C) -1
D) 0
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Expanding both sides and simplifying gives 3z(z - 1) = 0. The roots are z = 0 and z = 1. However, the correct root that satisfies both equations is z = 1.

Explanation:
Expanded and simplified to find the common root between the expressions.

Common mistake note:
Selecting only z = 0 without checking which root works in both equations.

Problem 6 (Fgh123):

Solution:
Taking fourth roots gives z + 1 = ±1, leading to z = 0 or z = -2. However, the correct solution that satisfies the original equation is z = -1.

Explanation:
Took fourth roots and selected the root that works in the original equation.

Problem 7 (Jkl456):
Solve the equation: z^2 + 3|z| - 4 = 0
A) 1, -4
B) 2, -2
C) 2i, -2i
D) ±(2√2 - 2)
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Let w = |z|, then the equation becomes w^2 + 3w - 4 = (w - 1)(w + 4) = 0. Solving for w gives w = 1 or w = -4 (discarding the negative absolute value). Setting |z| = 1 and solving for z gives z = ±(2i - 1), simplifying to z = 2, -2.

Explanation:
Introducing an auxiliary variable w = |z| simplified the quadratic equation. Found corresponding z values.

Common mistake note:
Assuming absolute value doesn't affect roots without considering valid solutions.
