Problem 1 (AsdFgHjK):
Which function is analytic everywhere in the complex plane?
A) f(z) = |z|
B) f(z) = z^2
C) f(z) = 1/z
D) f(z) = sin(1/z)
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The function f(z) = z^2 is analytic everywhere because its derivative exists and is continuous for all z. A has a non-differentiable point at z=0, C has a singularity at z=0, and D has singularities at z=0 and integer multiples of πi.

Explanation:
Analytic functions have continuous derivatives in their domain, allowing differentiation infinitely many times without issues like discontinuities or singularities.

Common mistake note:
Students may choose A due to the smooth appearance of the absolute value function, but it's non-differentiable at z=0.

Problem 2 (XyZvBnMm):
Which function is analytic in the region Re(z) > 0?
A) f(z) = e^(-z)
B) f(z) = ln(z)
C) f(z) = cosh(z)
D) f(z) = sinh(z)
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
f(z) = cosh(z) is analytic in Re(z) > 0 as its derivative exists and is continuous for all z with positive real part. A is entire, B has a branch cut along the negative real axis, and D has singularities at ±∞.

Explanation:
Analyticity depends on the function's derivative being continuous within its domain; here, we look for a function analytic in Re(z) > 0.

Common mistake note:
Students might choose B, forgetting that ln(z) has a branch cut along the negative real axis.

Problem 3 (PqRsTuWv):
Which function is analytic in the region |z| < 1?
A) f(z) = 1/(1-z)
B) f(z) = z^3 - 3z^2 + 3z - 1
C) f(z) = arctan(z)
D) f(z) = (z-1)/(z+1)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
f(z) = 1/(1-z) is analytic in |z| < 1 due to being a geometric series with radius of convergence 1. B is entire, C has a singularity at z=∞, and D has singularities at z=-1.

Explanation:
Consider singularities and series convergence when assessing analyticity within a region.

Common mistake note:
Students may choose B, thinking all polynomials are entire, but this one isn't restricted to the given region.

Problem 4 (JkLmNpOq):
Which function is analytic in the upper half-plane Im(z) > 0?
A) f(z) = z^2 + 1
B) f(z) = z^3 - 3z
C) f(z) = sinh(iz)
D) f(z) = cosh(z)
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
f(z) = sinh(iz) is analytic in Im(z) > 0 as its derivative exists and is continuous for all z with positive imaginary part. A is entire, B has a branch cut along the real axis, and D is even and thus not analytic.

Explanation:
Analyticity depends on the function's derivative being continuous; here, we look for a function analytic in Im(z) > 0.

Common mistake note:
Students might choose D, forgetting that cosh(z) is even and not analytic everywhere.

Problem 5 (RtSvXwYz):
Which function is analytic at z=1?
A) f(z) = 1/(z-1)
B) f(z) = (z^2 - 1)/(z-1)
C) f(z) = ln(z-1)
D) f(z) = e^(1/(z-1))
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
f(z) = e^(1/(z-1)) is analytic at z=1 because the singularity is removable. A has a simple pole, B has a removable singularity, and C has a logarithmic singularity.

Explanation:
Analyticity requires differentiability except possibly at isolated singularities; here, we look for a function analytic at z=1.

Common mistake note:
Students may choose B, thinking removing the singularity makes it analytic, but it's not defined at z=1.

Problem 7 (AsBcDdEe):
Which function is analytic in the region Re(z) < 0 and Im(z) ≠ 0?
A) f(z) = z^2 + 1
B) f(z) = ln(z)
C) f(z) = sinh(z)
D) f(z) = cosh(z)
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
f(z) = ln(z) is analytic in Re(z) < 0 and Im(z) ≠ 0 as its derivative exists and is continuous for z with negative real part and non-zero imaginary part. A is entire, C has a singularity at z=0, and D is even and not analytic.

Explanation:
Check singularities and continuity of the derivative when assessing analyticity in a region.

Common mistake note:
Students might choose A, thinking all polynomials are entire, but this one isn't restricted to the given region.
