Problem 1 (:Q1):
Find the first three non-zero terms of the Taylor series expansion of the function f(z) = e^z around z=0.
A) 1 + z + (z^2)/2!
B) 1 + iz + (iz^2)/2!
C) 1 - z - (z^2)/2!
D) 1 + 2z + (2z^2)/2!
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The Taylor series expansion of e^z around z=0 is given by the sum from n=0 to infinity of (z^n)/(n!). The first three non-zero terms are 1, z, and (z^2)/2!

Explanation:
This question tests the ability to apply the Taylor series for a complex function. The correct answer uses the known Taylor series expansion of e^z.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to use i in the expansion without considering that the base function is e^z, which does not involve i.

Problem 2 (:Q2):
Find the first two non-zero terms of the Taylor series expansion of the function f(z) = sin(π/4 + z) around z=0.
A) (√2)/2 - (z)
B) (√2)/2 + (z)
C) -(√2)/2 - (z)
D) -(√2)/2 + (z)
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The Taylor series expansion of sin(π/4 + z) around z=0 is given by the sum from n=0 to infinity of (-1)^n  * (z)^(2n+1)/(2n+1)! * cos(π/4). The first two non-zero terms are (z) and -(z)/3.

Explanation:
This question tests the ability to apply the Taylor series for a trigonometric function. The correct answer uses the known Taylor series expansion of sin(a + z).

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget the alternating sign (-1)^n or to use the wrong power in the numerator (2n instead of 2n+1).

Problem 3 (:Q3):
Find the first three non-zero terms of the Taylor series expansion of the function f(z) = ln(1 + z) around z=0.
A) z - (z^2)/2 + (z^3)/3
B) z + (z^2)/2 - (z^3)/3
C) z - (z^2)/2 - (z^3)/3
D) z + (z^2)/2 + (z^3)/3
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The Taylor series expansion of ln(1 + z) around z=0 is given by the sum from n=1 to infinity of (-1)^(n-1)  * (z^n)/n. The first three non-zero terms are z, -(z^2)/2, and (z^3)/3.

Explanation:
This question tests the ability to apply the Taylor series for a logarithmic function. The correct answer uses the known Taylor series expansion of ln(1 + z).

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget the alternating sign (-1)^(n-1) or to start the sum from n=0 instead of n=1.

Problem 4 (:Q4):
Find the first two non-zero terms of the Taylor series expansion of the function f(z) = cosh(z - π/2) around z=π/2.
A) (e^π/2)/2 + (z-π/2)e^(π/2)
B) (e^π/2)/2 - (z-π/2)e^(π/2)
C) (e^-π/2)/2 + (z-π/2)e^-π/2
D) (e^-π/2)/2 - (z-π/2)e^-π/2
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The Taylor series expansion of cosh(z - π/2) around z=π/2 is given by the sum from n=0 to infinity of (z-π/2)^{2n}/(2n!)  * e^(-π/2). The first two non-zero terms are (e^-π/2)/2 and (z-π/2)e^-π/2.

Explanation:
This question tests the ability to apply the Taylor series for a hyperbolic function. The correct answer uses the known Taylor series expansion of cosh(z - a).

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to use the wrong base function (e.g., sin or cos instead of cosh) or to forget the exponential term e^(-π/2).

Problem 5 (:Q5):
Find the first three non-zero terms of the Taylor series expansion of the function f(z) = 1/(1-z) around z=0.
A) 1 + z + z^2
B) 1 - z - z^2
C) 1 + z - z^2
D) 1 - z + z^2
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The Taylor series expansion of 1/(1-z) around z=0 is given by the sum from n=0 to infinity of z^n. The first three non-zero terms are 1, z, and z^2.

Explanation:
This question tests the ability to apply the Taylor series for a rational function. The correct answer uses the known geometric series expansion.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget that the denominator is (1-z) or to use the wrong power in the numerator (n instead of n-1).

Problem 6 (:Q6):
Find the first two non-zero terms of the Taylor series expansion of the function f(z) = z^3 around z=2.
A) (z-2)^3 + 3(z-2)^2
B) (z-2)^3 - 3(z-2)^2
C) 6(z-2) + 6(z-2)^2
D) 6(z-2) - 6(z-2)^2
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The Taylor series expansion of z^3 around z=2 is given by the sum from n=0 to infinity of (z-2)^n  * 3^(n-1). The first two non-zero terms are 6(z-2) and 6(z-2)^2.

Explanation:
This question tests the ability to apply the Taylor series for a polynomial function. The correct answer uses the known Taylor series expansion of z^3.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget the power of 3^(n-1) or to use the wrong base (e.g., z instead of z-2).

Problem 7 (:Q7):
Find the first three non-zero terms of the Taylor series expansion of the function f(z) = sinh(z - π) around z=π.
A) (z-π)e^π - (z-π)^2e^(2π)/2!
B) (z-π)e^-π + (z-π)^2e^(-2π)/2!
C) (z-π)e^π + (z-π)^2e^(2π)/2!
D) (z-π)e^-π - (z-π)^2e^(-2π)/2!
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The Taylor series expansion of sinh(z - π) around z=π is given by the sum from n=0 to infinity of (-1)^(n+1)  * (z-π)^{2n+1}/(2n+1)!  * e^π. The first three non-zero terms are (z-π)e^π, -(z-π)^2e^(2π)/2!, and (z-π)^3e^(3π)/3!.

Explanation:
This question tests the ability to apply the Taylor series for a hyperbolic function. The correct answer uses the known Taylor series expansion of sinh(z - a).

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget the alternating sign (-1)^(n+1) or to use the wrong power in the denominator (2n instead of 2n+1).
