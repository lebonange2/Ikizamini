Problem 1 (One):
Given a rectangle with vertices at (0, 0), (3, 0), (3, 4), and (0, 4), what are the coordinates of the vertices after reflecting it across the x-axis?
A) (0, -4), (3, -4), (3, 0), (0, 0)
B) (-3, 4), (-3, 0), (0, 0), (0, -4)
C) (0, 4), (3, 4), (3, 0), (0, 0)
D) (-3, -4), (-3, 0), (0, 0), (0, 4)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Reflecting a figure across the x-axis only changes the sign of the y-coordinates. The new coordinates are (0, -4), (3, -4), (3, 0), and (0, 0).

Explanation:
Reflections preserve shape and size but flip the orientation.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to forget that only the y-coordinates change sign.

Problem 2 (Two):
After translating a triangle with vertices at (-2, -1), (1, -1), and (1, 2) by 5 units right and 3 units up, what are the new coordinates?
A) (3, 2), (6, 2), (6, 5)
B) (-7, -4), (0, -4), (0, -1)
C) (-7, 5), (0, 5), (0, 8)
D) (-2, -4), (1, -4), (1, 5)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Translation moves each point by the specified amounts. The new coordinates are (3, 2), (6, 2), and (6, 5).

Explanation:
Translations preserve shape and size but move the figure along a straight line.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to add or subtract the translation amounts incorrectly.

Problem 3 (Three):
Given a parallelogram with vertices at (1, 2), (4, 2), (5, 3), and (2, 3), what are the coordinates of the vertices after rotating it 90 degrees clockwise around the origin?
A) (2, -1), (2, -4), (3, -5), (3, -2)
B) (-1, 2), (-4, 2), (-5, 3), (-2, 3)
C) (1, -2), (4, -2), (5, -3), (2, -3)
D) (-2, 1), (-2, 4), (-3, 5), (-3, 2)
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
A 90-degree clockwise rotation around the origin changes x and y coordinates: (x', y') = (-y, x). The new coordinates are (1, -2), (4, -2), (5, -3), and (2, -3).

Explanation:
Rotations preserve shape but change the orientation.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to apply a counterclockwise rotation instead of clockwise.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 4 (Four):
After dilating a pentagon with vertices at (1, 1), (3, 1), (4, 2), (3, 3), and (1, 3) by a scale factor of 2, what are the new coordinates?
A) (2, 2), (6, 2), (8, 4), (6, 6), (2, 6)
B) (-1, -1), (-3, -1), (-4, -2), (-3, -3), (-1, -3)
C) (0.5, 0.5), (1.5, 0.5), (2, 1), (1.5, 1.5), (0.5, 1.5)
D) (-2, -2), (-6, -2), (-8, -4), (-6, -6), (-2, -6)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Dilation multiplies each coordinate by the scale factor. The new coordinates are (2, 2), (6, 2), (8, 4), (6, 6), and (2, 6).

Explanation:
Dilations preserve shape but change the size.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to forget to multiply both x and y-coordinates.

Problem 5 (Five):
Given a regular hexagon with vertices at (1, 0), (2, √3), (2, -√3), (1, 0), (-1, 0), and (-2, √3), what are the coordinates of the vertices after reflecting it across the line y = x?
A) (0, 1), (√3, 2), (-√3, 2), (0, 1), (-1, 0), (√3, -2)
B) (-1, 0), (-2, √3), (-2, -√3), (-1, 0), (1, 0), (2, √3)
C) (0, 1), (√3, 2), (-√3, 2), (0, 1), (-1, 0), (-2, √3)
D) (-1, 0), (-2, -√3), (-2, √3), (-1, 0), (1, 0), (2, -√3)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Reflection across the line y = x swaps x and y-coordinates. The new coordinates are (0, 1), (√3, 2), (-√3, 2), (0, 1), (-1, 0), (√3, -2).

Explanation:
Reflections preserve shape and size but flip the orientation.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to reflect across a different line or forget to swap x and y-coordinates.

Problem 6 (Six):
After rotating an equilateral triangle with vertices at (0, 0), (3, 0), and (1.5, √3) by 180 degrees around the origin, what are the new coordinates?
A) (0, 0), (-3, 0), (-1.5, -√3)
B) (-3, 0), (0, 0), (-1.5, √3)
C) (0, 0), (3, 0), (1.5, -√3)
D) (-3, 0), (0, 0), (1.5, √3)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
A 180-degree rotation around the origin negates both x and y-coordinates. The new coordinates are (0, 0), (-3, 0), and (-1.5, -√3).

Explanation:
Rotations preserve shape but change the orientation.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to apply a rotation by an incorrect angle or forget to negate both x and y-coordinates.

Problem 7 (Seven):
Given a circle with center at (2, 1) and radius 3, what are the coordinates of the new center after translating it 4 units right and 5 units up?
A) (6, 6)
B) (-2, -4)
C) (6, -4)
D) (2, 1)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Translation moves the center of the circle by the specified amounts. The new coordinates are (6, 6).

Explanation:
Translations preserve shape but move the figure along a straight line.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to apply the translation to the wrong point or forget that only the center moves.
