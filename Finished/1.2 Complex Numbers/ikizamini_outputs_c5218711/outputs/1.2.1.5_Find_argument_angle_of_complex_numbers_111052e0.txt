Problem 1 (:1):
Given the complex number z = -3 + 4i, what is the argument (in radians)?
A) -π/2
B) π/2
C) π
D) 3π/2
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The argument of a complex number in polar form (r, θ) is the angle θ. To find the argument, we first express the complex number in its polar form. The given complex number z = -3 + 4i can be expressed as follows using the Pythagorean theorem and trigonometry: r = √((-3)^2 + 4^2) = 5, and θ = arctan(4/-3). Since the number is in the second quadrant, we add π to the result of arctan: θ = arctan(4/-3) + π. The argument is -π/2.

Explanation:
The correct answer is A because it represents the correct angle in radians for the given complex number in the second quadrant.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to choose B, which corresponds to an angle in the first quadrant. This fails because the real part of the complex number is negative, placing it in the second quadrant.

Problem 4 (:4):
Find the argument of the complex number z = 3 - 3i.
A) π/4
B) π/2
C) π
D) 3π/2
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The argument of a complex number in polar form (r, θ) is the angle θ. To find the argument, we first express the complex number in its polar form. The given complex number z = 3 - 3i can be expressed as follows using the Pythagorean theorem and trigonometry: r = √(3^2 + (-3)^2) = 3π, and θ = arctan(-3/3) = arctan(-1). Since the number is in the fourth quadrant, we subtract π from the result of arctan: θ = arctan(-1) - π. The argument is π.

Explanation:
The correct answer is C because it represents the correct angle in radians for the given complex number in the fourth quadrant.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to choose A or B, which correspond to angles in different quadrants. This fails because the real part of the complex number is positive and the imaginary part is negative, placing it in the fourth quadrant.
