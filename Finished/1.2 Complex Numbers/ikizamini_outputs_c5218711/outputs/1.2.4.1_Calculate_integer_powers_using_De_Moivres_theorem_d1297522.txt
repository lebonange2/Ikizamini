Problem 1 (:1):
Find the fourth power of the complex number (3 + 4i) using De Moivre's theorem.
A) (9 - 16i)
B) (81 + 256i)
C) (81 - 256i)
D) (27 + 64i)
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
De Moivre's theorem states that (r cis θ)^n = r^n cis (nθ), where 'cis' is the shorthand for cosine + i*sin. First, we find the modulus and argument of the complex number: r = √(3^2 + 4^2) = 5, θ = tan^-1(4/3). Then we apply De Moivre's theorem.

Explanation:
De Moivre's theorem states that (r cis θ)^n = r^n cis (nθ), where 'cis' is the shorthand for cosine + i*sin. First, we find the modulus and argument of the complex number: r = √(3^2 + 4^2) = 5, θ = tan^-1(4/3). Then we apply De Moivre's theorem.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to forget to square the modulus and multiply the angle by the power. Answer A neglects to square the modulus, while answer B multiplies the modulus incorrectly.
