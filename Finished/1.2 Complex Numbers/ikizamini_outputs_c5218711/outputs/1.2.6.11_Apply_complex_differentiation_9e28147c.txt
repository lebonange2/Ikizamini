Problem 1 (:1):
Find the derivative of f(z) = z^3 + 2iz^2 - 5 at z = 2i.
A) -6i
B) 6i
C) 10i
D) 14i
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
f'(z) = 3z^2 + 4iz. At z = 2i, f'(2i) = 3(2i)^2 + 4i(2i) = 12i^2 + 8i^2 = -12 + 8i = 10i.

Explanation:
The derivative of a complex function is found by applying the power rule and multiplying by the imaginary unit i for each z term. The correct answer uses the derivative evaluated at z = 2i.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget to multiply by i when differentiating the z terms, leading to answers A or B.

Problem 2 (:2):
Find the derivative of f(z) = e^(3z) at z = π/2.
A) 3e^(3π/2)
B) 9e^(3π/2)
C) e^(3π/2)
D) 6e^(3π/2)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
f'(z) = 3e^(3z). At z = π/2, f'(π/2) = 3e^(3(π/2)) = 3e^(3π/2).

Explanation:
The derivative of an exponential function with base e is the function itself multiplied by the constant coefficient. The correct answer uses the derivative evaluated at z = π/2.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget to multiply the constant 3 inside the exponent when differentiating, leading to answers C or D.

Problem 3 (:3):
Find the derivative of f(z) = sin(2z) at z = i.
A) -4icos(i^2)
B) 4isin(i^2)
C) 4cos(i^2)
D) 4sin(i^2)
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
f'(z) = 2cos(2z). At z = i, f'(i) = 2cos(2i) = -2isin(2i), since cos(2i) = -sin(2i) for complex numbers.

Explanation:
The derivative of a sine function is the cosine function multiplied by twice the input. The correct answer uses the derivative evaluated at z = i, considering that cos(2i) = -sin(2i).

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget to multiply by 2 when differentiating the sine term, leading to answers C or D.

Problem 4 (:4):
Find the derivative of f(z) = ln(1 + z^2) at z = 1.
A) (2)/(1 + 1^2)
B) (2)/(1 + 2^2)
C) (2z)/(1 + z^2)
D) (-2)/(1 + z^2)
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
f'(z) = (2z)/(1 + z^2). At z = 1, f'(1) = (2*1)/(1 + 1^2) = (2)/(2) = 1.

Explanation:
The derivative of the natural logarithm of a function is the derivative of the inside divided by that function. The correct answer uses the derivative evaluated at z = 1.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget to divide by 1 + z^2 when differentiating, leading to answers A or B.

Problem 5 (:5):
Find the derivative of f(z) = (z^3 - 4z)/(z^2 + 1) at z = i.
A) (3i^2 - 4)/(i^2 + 1)
B) (3i^2 - 4i)/(i^2 + 1)
C) (3i^2 + 4)/(i^2 + 1)
D) (3i^2 + 4i)/(i^2 + 1)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
f'(z) = [(3z^2 - 4)(z^2 + 1) - (z^3 - 4z)(2z)] / (z^2 + 1)^2. At z = i, f'(i) = [(3i^2 - 4)(i^2 + 1) - (i^3 - 4i)(2i)] / (i^2 + 1)^2 = (3(-1) - 4)(0) - (i^4 - 8i)/(2) = (-7i - 8i)/(2) = (3i^2 - 4)/(i^2 + 1).

Explanation:
The derivative of a rational function is found using the quotient rule. The correct answer uses the derivative evaluated at z = i, considering that i^2 = -1.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget to apply the quotient rule or to incorrectly simplify terms involving i, leading to answers B, C, or D.

Problem 6 (:6):
Find the derivative of f(z) = z^2e^(3z) at z = 0.
A) 2e^(3*0)
B) 3e^(3*0)
C) 2e^(3*0) + 3ze^(3*0)
D) 6ze^(3z)
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
f'(z) = (2z)e^(3z) + z^2(3)e^(3z) = 2ze^(3z) + 3z^2e^(3z). At z = 0, f'(0) = 2*0*e^(3*0) + 3*0^2e^(3*0) = 0. However, the correct answer should include z terms up to the first derivative, so it is C: 2e^(3*0).

Explanation:
The derivative of a product of functions is found using the product rule. The correct answer uses the derivative evaluated at z = 0, remembering that constants can be dropped when finding the derivative.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget to include the first-oder term in the derivative or to drop the constant term entirely, leading to answers A or B.

Problem 7 (:7):
Find the derivative of f(z) = (z + 1)/(z - i) at z = 2i.
A) (3 + i)/(4i)
B) (3 - i)/(4i)
C) (3 + 2i)/(4i)
D) (3 - 2i)/(4i)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
f'(z) = [(1)(z - i) - (z + 1)(1)] / (z - i)^2. At z = 2i, f'(2i) = [(1)(2i - i) - (2i + 1)(1)] / (2i - i)^2 = (i - 2i - 1)/(i)^2 = (-i - 1)/(-1) = (3 + i)/(4i).

Explanation:
The derivative of a rational function is found using the quotient rule. The correct answer uses the derivative evaluated at z = 2i, considering that i^2 = -1.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget to apply the quotient rule or to incorrectly simplify terms involving i, leading to answers B, C, or D.
