Problem 1 (:1):
Find the inverse Laplace transform of F(s) = (s + 5)/(s^2 + 4s + 5).
A) e^{-2t}cos(t)
B) e^{-2t}sin(t)
C) e^{2t}cos(t)
D) e^{2t}sin(t)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The given Laplace transform F(s) = (s + 5)/(s^2 + 4s + 5) can be simplified by completing the square in the denominator: s^2 + 4s + 5 = (s + 2)^2 + 1. This suggests that the original function is an exponential multiplied by a cosine or sine function with frequency 1. The correct inverse Laplace transform is thus e^{-2t}cos(t).

Explanation:
The denominator factors into (s + 2)^2 + 1, which indicates an oscillatory solution with an exponential decay. Choice A matches this form.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing B or D, as they represent sine functions instead of cosine functions. The numerator s + 5 does not match the form for a sine transform.

Problem 2 (:2):
Find the inverse Laplace transform of F(s) = (s - 3)/(s^2 + 9).
A) cos(3t)
B) sin(3t)
C) e^{3t}cos(3t)
D) e^{3t}sin(3t)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The given Laplace transform F(s) = (s - 3)/(s^2 + 9) suggests an exponential function with a frequency of 3. However, since there is no exponential term in the numerator, it's just a cosine function without decay. The correct inverse Laplace transform is cos(3t).

Explanation:
The denominator s^2 + 9 indicates oscillation with frequency 3, and the absence of an exponential term in the numerator means there's no decay. Choice A matches this form.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing C or D, as they include exponential terms that are not present in the original transform. The Laplace transform does not have an exponential growth or decay.

Problem 7 (:7):
Find the inverse Laplace transform of F(s) = (s^2 + 1)/(s^3 + 6s^2 + 9s).
A) t^2 - 3t + 2
B) t^2 + 3t + 2
C) e^{-3t}(t^2 - 3t)
D) e^{-3t}(t^2 + 3t)
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
The given Laplace transform F(s) = (s^2 + 1)/(s^3 + 6s^2 + 9s) can be simplified by factoring the denominator: s^3 + 6s^2 + 9s = s(3s + 9). This suggests an exponential decay with a polynomial function of degree 2. The correct inverse Laplace transform is e^{-3t}(t^2 + 3t).

Explanation:
The denominator factors into s(3s + 9), indicating exponential decay with a base of e^{-3t} and a quadratic polynomial in the time domain. Choice D matches this form.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing A or B, as they represent polynomials without the exponential decay term. The Laplace transform has an exponential component.
