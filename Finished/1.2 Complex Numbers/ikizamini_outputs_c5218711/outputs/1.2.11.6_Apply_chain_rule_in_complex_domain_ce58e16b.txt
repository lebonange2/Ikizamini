Problem 1 (One):
Find the derivative of f(z) = (z^2 + 3i)^5 with respect to z.
A) (10z(‌​z^2 + 3i)^4)
B) (5z^2 + 3i)^4
C) 5(‌​z^2 + 3i)^4
D) 20z(‌​z^2 + 3i)^3
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The chain rule states that if you have a composite function f(g(z)), the derivative is f'(g(z)) 	imes g'(z). In this case, f(u) = u^5 and g(z) = z^2 + 3i. So, f'(u) = 5u^4 and g'(z) = 2z. Applying the chain rule gives us (5(‌​z^2 + 3i)^4) 	imes 2z = 10z(‌​z^2 + 3i)^4.

Explanation:
The correct answer is obtained by applying the chain rule correctly. The mistake in choice A is multiplying by an extra factor of z, while choices B and D use incorrect exponents or derivatives of f(u).

Common mistake note:
Common mistakes include not accounting for the derivative of the inner function (g'(z)) or incorrectly applying the power rule to the outer function.
