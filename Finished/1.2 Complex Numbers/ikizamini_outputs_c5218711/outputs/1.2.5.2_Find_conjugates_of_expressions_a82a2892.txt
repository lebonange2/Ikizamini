Problem 1 (:1):
What is the conjugate of the complex number 3 + 4i?
A) 3 - 4i
B) -3 + 4i
C) -3 - 4i
D) 4 + 3i
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The conjugate of a complex number a + bi is found by changing the sign of the imaginary part. So, for the number 3 + 4i, the conjugate is 3 - 4i.

Explanation:
Conjugates are used in operations involving complex numbers, such as rationalizing denominators.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to switch both parts of the complex number (e.g., -3 + 4i), but that's not the conjugate; it's just reversing the sign of the real part and keeping the imaginary part unchanged.

Problem 2 (:2):
Find the conjugate of (-2 + 5i)(6 - 7i).
A) -12 + 29i
B) 12 - 29i
C) 47 - 8i
D) 47 + 8i
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
First, multiply the binomials: (-2 + 5i)(6 - 7i) = -12 + 30i + 35i^2. Since i^2 = -1, we have -12 + 30i - 35(-1) = -12 + 30i + 35 = 23 + 30i. Now find the conjugate of this result: 23 - 30i.

Explanation:
When finding the conjugate of a product, you need to conjugate each term separately and then multiply them again.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to simply conjugate one factor and not both (e.g., (-2 + 5i)(6 + 7i)), but that's not correct; you must conjugate both factors.

Problem 3 (:3):

C) rac{-1 + 3i}{4 - 2i}

Correct Answer: B

Solution:
To find the conjugate of a fraction, you need to multiply both the numerator and the denominator by the conjugate of the denominator. Here, the conjugate of 4 - 2i is 4 + 2i. When rationalizing a denominator with complex numbers, you multiply both the numerator and the denominator by the conjugate of the denominator.

Common mistake note:

Problem 5 (:5):
What is the conjugate of the complex number rac{3i}{1 - i}?
A) rac{-3i}{1 + i}

C) rac{3i}{-1 + i}
D) rac{3i}{1 + i}
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
To find the conjugate of rac{3i}{1 - i}, multiply both the numerator and denominator by the conjugate of the denominator: rac{3i}{1 - i} * rac{1 + i}{1 + i} = rac{3i(1 + i)}{(1 - i)(1 + i)}. Explanation:
When rationalizing a denominator with complex numbers, you multiply both the numerator and the denominator by the conjugate of the denominator, even if it's in the numerator.
