Problem 1 (Asd123):
Which complex number is greater than 3 + 4i?
A) 2 + 5i
B) 4 + 3i
C) 3 - 4i
D) 5 + 2i
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
To compare complex numbers, first compare the real parts. Both B and D have a real part of 4, which is greater than 3 in 3 + 4i. If the real parts are equal, compare the imaginary parts. Since 3 > -4, 4 + 3i is greater than 3 + 4i. The correct answer is B.

Explanation:
When comparing complex numbers, consider both their real and imaginary parts. If the real parts are identical, compare the imaginary parts. A larger imaginary part indicates a greater complex number.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to only examine the imaginary part without first checking the real part.

Problem 2 (Xcv123):
Which complex number has an absolute value smaller than 2 - 3i?
A) 1 + 4i
B) 3 + 2i
C) -1 - 4i
D) 0 - 5i
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The absolute value of a complex number is its distance from the origin. Compare their magnitudes by squaring both sides and summing real and imaginary parts squared: A: 17, B: 13 (equal to 2 - 3i), C: 17, D: 25. Only B has the same absolute value as 2 - 3i, so it's not smaller. The correct answer is C.

Explanation:
To compare complex number magnitudes, square both sides and sum the squares of real and imaginary parts. The choice with a lower squared sum has a smaller absolute value.

Common mistake note:
A common error is to directly compare real and imaginary parts without considering their squares or overall magnitude.

Problem 3 (Zxc123):
Which complex number is closest to 0?
A) 1 + 0i
B) -1 + 0i
C) 0.5 + 0.5i
D) 0 - 1i
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The distances from the origin for A, B, and C are all 1, while D is √2. Since 1 < √2, option A is closer to 0 than the others. The correct answer is A.

Explanation:
To determine which complex number is closest to 0, consider their distances from the origin in the complex plane. The smaller the distance, the closer the number to 0.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is focusing on real or imaginary parts without considering overall distance from the origin.

Problem 4 (Sdf123):
Which complex number has a conjugate greater than 5 + 6i?
A) 4 + 7i
B) 6 - 5i
C) 5 - 6i
D) 7 + 4i
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The conjugate of a complex number keeps the real part unchanged and flips the imaginary sign. Compare the original numbers directly since they have the same real parts: only B has a greater real part than 5 + 6i (its real part is 6). The correct answer is B.

Explanation:
The conjugate of a complex number does not change its real part but flips the imaginary sign. To compare conjugates, focus on the real parts since they remain identical.

Common mistake note:
A common error is to compare original numbers without considering their conjugates or forgetting that real parts stay the same in the conjugate.

Problem 5 (Qwe123):
Which complex number has a greater real part than 4 + 2i and a smaller imaginary part?
A) 5 - 1i
B) 3 + 3i
C) 6 + 4i
D) 2 - 4i
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Find a complex number with a real part > 4 and an imaginary part < 2. Comparing: A has a real part of 5 (greater than 4) and an imaginary part of -1 (smaller than 2). B's real part is less, C's imaginary part is larger, D's imaginary part is smaller in absolute value. The correct answer is A.

Explanation:
When comparing complex numbers based on specific criteria, consider both real and imaginary parts independently. Here, we want a number with a greater real part than 4 and a smaller imaginary part than 2.

Common mistake note:
A common error is overlooking one of the conditions (greater real part or smaller imaginary part) when selecting an answer.

Problem 6 (Rty123):
Which complex number has the same argument as 1 + i?
A) 2 + 2i
B) -1 - i
C) √2 + √2i
D) 1 - i
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The argument is the angle with the positive real axis. The polar form of 1 + i is (r, θ) = (√2, π/4). Find another number with the same r and θ: A has an argument of π/2, B: -3π/4, C: π/4 (same as 1 + i), D: -π/4. The correct answer is C.

Explanation:
The argument represents a complex number's angle in the plane. Compare arguments by converting to polar form or recognizing geometric properties.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is confusing the modulus (r) with the argument (θ). The question asks for the same argument, not necessarily the same modulus.

Problem 7 (Ghi123):
Which complex number has a greater magnitude than 3 + 4i and a smaller real part?
A) 2 + 6i
B) 5 - 3i
C) 4 + 2i
D) 1 + 7i
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
Find a number with magnitude > √(3^2 + 4^2) = 5 and real part < 3. Comparing: A has magnitude > 5 but real part < 3, B's real part is > 3, C's magnitude is < 5, D has both greater magnitude (√(1^2 + 7^2) > 5) and smaller real part (1 < 3). The correct answer is D.

Explanation:
When comparing complex numbers based on specific criteria, consider both magnitudes and real parts independently. Here, we want a number with greater magnitude than 5 and a smaller real part than 3.

Common mistake note:
A common error is overlooking one of the conditions (greater magnitude or smaller real part) when selecting an answer.
