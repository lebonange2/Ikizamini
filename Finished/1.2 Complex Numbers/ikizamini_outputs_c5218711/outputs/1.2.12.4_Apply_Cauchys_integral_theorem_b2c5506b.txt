Problem 1 (Asdf):
Evaluate the integral of f(z) = z^2 dz around a circle C with center at origin and radius 3, using Cauchy's integral theorem.
A) 0
B) 18π
C) 27π
D) 54π
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Using Cauchy's integral theorem, the integral of a holomorphic function (analytic with no singularities) around a closed curve is zero. Since f(z) = z^2 is analytic everywhere in the complex plane, the integral around the circle C is 0.

Explanation:
Cauchy's integral theorem states that if a function is holomorphic within and on a simple closed contour, then the integral of the function over the contour is zero. In this case, f(z) = z^2 has no singularities within or on the circle C, so the integral evaluates to 0.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be attempting to directly compute the integral without considering the theorem. This would lead to a wrong answer as it overlooks the fact that Cauchy's integral theorem applies.

Explanation:
Cauchy's integral theorem states that if a function is holomorphic within and on a simple closed contour, then the integral of the function over the contour is zero. Since there are no singularities of f(z) = 1/(z^3 + 1) inside or on the circle C, the integral evaluates to 0.

Common mistake note:

Problem 5 (Bvnmm):
Evaluate ∫(C) (sin(z))/z dz where C is a circle with center at origin and radius 6, using Cauchy's integral theorem.
A) 0
B) π
C) 2π
D) 3π
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The function has a removable singularity at z = 0. By Cauchy's integral theorem, the integral is equal to 2πi times the residue of f(z) at z = 0, which is 1. Thus, the integral evaluates to π.

Explanation:
Cauchy's integral theorem applies to holomorphic functions within and on the contour. However, in this case, sin(z)/z has a removable singularity at z = 0. The residue theorem can be used to evaluate the integral around such a singularity. The residue of f(z) at z = 0 is 1, so the integral evaluates to π.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be assuming that the function is holomorphic everywhere and applying Cauchy's integral theorem directly, which would lead to an incorrect result of 0. It's important to recognize the removable singularity at z = 0.
