Problem 1 (AsdFgHjKlMnO):
What are the Cauchy-Riemann equations for a complex function f(z) = u(x, y) + iv(x, y)?
A) u_x = v_y and u_y = -v_x
B) u_x = v_x and u_y = v_y
C) u_x = -v_y and u_y = v_x
D) u_x = 0 and u_y = 0
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The Cauchy-Riemann equations state that for a function f(z) to be differentiable in the complex plane, its real part (u) and imaginary part (v) must satisfy u_x = v_y and u_y = -v_x.

Explanation:
These equations ensure that the partial derivatives of u and v are consistent with the behavior of complex differentiation.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to swap the signs in the equations, which would result in an incorrect statement.

Problem 2 (BvnCmDlKjHgF):
If a function f(z) satisfies the Cauchy-Riemann equations, what can we conclude about f?
A) f is continuous everywhere.
B) f is holomorphic (analytic).
C) f has no singularities.
D) f is differentiable only at isolated points.
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
If a function satisfies the Cauchy-Riemann equations, it is holomorphic or analytic, meaning it is complex differentiable in an open set.

Explanation:
Holomorphic functions have many important properties, such as being able to be represented by power series and having no isolated singularities.

Common mistake note:
While continuity and absence of singularities are desirable properties, they do not necessarily follow from the Cauchy-Riemann equations alone.

Problem 3 (XyzWvUtRsQpO):
Which of the following functions satisfies the Cauchy-Riemann equations?
A) f(z) = z^2
B) f(z) = |z|
C) f(z) = e^z
D) f(z) = 1/z
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The function f(z) = z^2 satisfies the Cauchy-Riemann equations because u_x = v_y and u_y = -v_x for its real and imaginary parts.

Explanation:
For f(z) = z^2, u(x, y) = x^2 - y^2 and v(x, y) = 2xy. The partial derivatives verify the Cauchy-Riemann equations.

Common mistake note:
The other choices do not satisfy the Cauchy-Riemann equations; for example, f(z) = |z| is not differentiable at z=0.

Problem 6 (RstUvWxyZaBc):
Given a function f(z) = u(x, y) + iv(x, y), which of the following statements is true if f satisfies the Cauchy-Riemann equations?
A) The gradient of u and v are parallel at every point.
B) The curl of the gradient of f is zero everywhere.
C) The divergence of the gradient of f is constant.
D) The curl of the gradient of f is equal to 1.
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
If a function satisfies the Cauchy-Riemann equations, the curl of its gradient is zero everywhere, which means that it is a conservative vector field.

Explanation:
The curl of the gradient of f(z) = u(x, y) + iv(x, y) is 0 due to the relationship between the partial derivatives established by the Cauchy-Riemann equations.

Common mistake note:
While the gradient vectors might appear parallel, this is not a direct consequence of the Cauchy-Riemann equations; it's the curl that indicates the conservative nature of the field.
