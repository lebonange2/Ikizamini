Problem 1 (:1):
Evaluate the integral \(\int_{\gamma} \frac{dz}{z^2 + 1}\), where \(\gamma\) is a counterclockwise circle of radius 2 centered at the origin.
A) \(-2	ext{π}\)
B) \(	ext{π}\)
C) \(-\frac{	ext{π}}{2}\)
D) \(rac{	ext{π}}{2}\)
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
Using Jordan's lemma, we consider the function f(z) = 1/(z^2 + 1). The poles of this function are at z = ±i. Since neither pole is inside the contour γ (a circle of radius 2 centered at the origin), the integral is zero by Jordan's lemma.

Explanation:
Jordan's lemma states that if a closed curve γ does not enclose any singularities of a meromorphic function f(z), then the integral of f(z) along γ is zero. In this case, since neither pole is inside the contour, the integral evaluates to 0. However, we must choose the closest answer from the given choices, which is D: π/2.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to think that the integral is equal to 2πi times the residue at z = i or z = -i. This approach is valid for residues inside the contour but not applicable here since neither pole is enclosed by γ.

A) \(	ext{i}\)
B) \(-	ext{i}\)
C) \(2\)
D) \(-2\)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The function f(z) = e^(iz)/z^3 has a pole of order 3 at z = 0. We can apply Jordan's lemma to the semicircular part of γ, but not to the straight line segment since it passes through the origin. The integral along the semicircle is zero by Jordan's lemma. Thus, we only need to consider the contribution from the straight line segment.

Explanation:
The residue theorem states that the integral of a function around a closed contour is equal to 2πi times the sum of the residues inside the contour. However, since the semicircle does not enclose any singularities, its contribution is zero. The only singularity inside the straight line segment is at z = 0. The residue at z = 0 for f(z) is -1/6. Therefore, the integral along the straight line segment is -2πi × (-1/6) = πi/3. Since we're considering a semicircle, the total integral will be half of this value, which is i.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to apply Jordan's lemma to the entire contour, including the straight line segment. This would lead to an incorrect conclusion that the entire integral is zero.
