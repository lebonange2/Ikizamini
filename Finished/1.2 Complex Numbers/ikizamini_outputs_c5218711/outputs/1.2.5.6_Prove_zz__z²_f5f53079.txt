Problem 1 (Asdf):
Given a complex number z = a + bi, where a and b are real numbers, what is the result of multiplying z by its conjugate z̄?
A) (a - bi)(a + bi)
B) a² + b²
C) 2ab
D) a² - b²
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The conjugate of z = a + bi is z̄ = a - bi. When we multiply z by its conjugate, we get (a + bi)(a - bi). Expanding this product using the difference of squares formula gives us a² - (bi)². Since (bi)² = b²i² and i² = -1, we have a² - b²(-1), which simplifies to a² + b². Therefore, z·z̄ = |z|².

Explanation:
This is the fundamental property of complex numbers that relates the product of a number and its conjugate to the square of its modulus.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to choose (A), which is the product before simplification. Students might forget to apply the difference of squares formula or miss the fact that i² = -1.

Problem 2 (Qwerty):
Given z = 3 + 4i, what is the value of |z|²?
A) 25
B) 9
C) 7
D) 16
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Given z = 3 + 4i, we first find the conjugate z̄ = 3 - 4i. Then we calculate z·z̄ by multiplying z and its conjugate: (3 + 4i)(3 - 4i) = 9 - 16i². Since i² = -1, we have 9 - 16(-1) = 9 + 16 = 25. Now we find |z|² by squaring the modulus of z: |z|² = (sqrt(3^2 + 4^2))^2 = sqrt(9 + 16)^2 = 5^2 = 25.

Explanation:
This question tests the understanding of applying the formula z·z̄ = |z|² to a specific complex number.

Common mistake note:
Students might forget to square the modulus after finding z·z̄ or make an arithmetic error in calculating the modulus.

Problem 3 (Zxcvb):
Given z = -2 + 3i, what is the value of |z|²?
A) 13
B) 4
C) 9
D) 1
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Given z = -2 + 3i, we first find the conjugate z̄ = -2 - 3i. Then we calculate z·z̄ by multiplying z and its conjugate: (-2 + 3i)(-2 - 3i) = 4 - 9i². Since i² = -1, we have 4 - 9(-1) = 4 + 9 = 13. Now we find |z|² by squaring the modulus of z: |z|² = (sqrt((-2)^2 + 3^2))^2 = sqrt(4 + 9)^2 = sqrt(13)^2 = 13.

Explanation:
This question tests the application of the formula to a complex number with negative real and imaginary parts.

Common mistake note:
Students might mix up the signs when calculating z·z̄ or make an error in finding the modulus.

Problem 4 (Bnm):
What is the relationship between |z|² and the sum of the squares of the real and imaginary parts of z?
A) They are equal.
B) They differ by a factor of 2.
C) They differ by a factor of i.
D) They have no specific relationship.
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The modulus of a complex number z = a + bi is given by |z| = sqrt(a² + b²). Squaring this gives us |z|² = (sqrt(a² + b²))^2 = a² + b². Therefore, |z|² is equal to the sum of the squares of the real and imaginary parts of z.

Explanation:
This question tests the understanding of the definition of modulus and its connection to the formula z·z̄ = |z|².

Common mistake note:
Students might think that there's a scaling factor involved or混淆 the concept with other operations on complex numbers.

Problem 5 (Ghj):
Given z = 2i, what is the value of z·z̄?
A) 4
B) 0
C) 8
D) -4
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Given z = 2i, its conjugate is z̄ = -2i. The product z·z̄ is (2i)(-2i) = -4i². Since i² = -1, we have -4(-1) = 4. Therefore, z·z̄ = |z|² = 4.

Explanation:
This question tests the application of the formula to a complex number with zero real part.

Common mistake note:
Students might forget that i² = -1 or make an arithmetic error when calculating the product.

Problem 6 (Hjk):
Given z·z̄ = 25, what is the value of |z|?
A) ±5
B) ±sqrt(5)
C) 5
D) sqrt(5)
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
Given z·z̄ = 25, we know that |z|² = 25. Taking the square root of both sides gives us |z| = ±sqrt(25). Since the modulus of a complex number is always non-negative, we take only the positive value: |z| = 5.

Explanation:
This question tests the understanding of how to find the modulus given z·z̄.

Common mistake note:
Students might forget that the modulus is always non-negative and include both positive and negative solutions.

Problem 7 (Uyt):
Given z = -1 + 2i, what is the value of |z|²?
A) 5
B) 9
C) 3
D) 7
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Given z = -1 + 2i, we first find the conjugate z̄ = -1 - 2i. Then we calculate z·z̄ by multiplying z and its conjugate: (-1 + 2i)(-1 - 2i) = 1 - 4i². Since i² = -1, we have 1 - 4(-1) = 1 + 4 = 5. Now we find |z|² by squaring the modulus of z: |z|² = (sqrt((-1)^2 + 2^2))^2 = sqrt(1 + 4)^2 = sqrt(5)^2 = 5.

Explanation:
This question tests the application of the formula to a complex number with both negative real and imaginary parts.

Common mistake note:
Students might make errors when calculating z·z̄ or finding the modulus.
