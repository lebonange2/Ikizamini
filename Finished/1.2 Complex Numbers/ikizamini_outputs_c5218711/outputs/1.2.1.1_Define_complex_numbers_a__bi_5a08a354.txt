Problem 1 (:1):
What is the standard form of a complex number?
A) (b + ai)
B) (a - bi)
C) (a + bi)
D) (bi - a)
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The standard form of a complex number is (a + bi), where 'a' represents the real part and 'b' represents the imaginary part with the imaginary unit i.

Explanation:
Complex numbers are written in the form (a + bi) to clearly distinguish the real and imaginary parts. The other options either have the terms reversed or use incorrect signs, which is not in accordance with the standard form.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Reversing the order of 'a' and 'b', or using a negative sign before 'bi'. This confusion arises from misunderstanding the structure of complex numbers.

Problem 2 (:2):
What is the real part of the complex number 3 + 4i?
A) 7
B) 4
C) 3
D) 0
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The real part of a complex number (a + bi) is 'a'. In the given complex number 3 + 4i, 'a' is 3.

Explanation:
To find the real part of a complex number, you look at the coefficient of the real term without the imaginary unit i. The other choices do not correspond to the correct value for the real part.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing the coefficient of 'i' (4) as the real part. This confusion comes from misunderstanding the distinction between the real and imaginary parts.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 3 (:3):
What is the imaginary part of the complex number -2 + 5i?
A) 7
B) 5
C) -2
D) 0
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The imaginary part of a complex number (a + bi) is 'b'. In the given complex number -2 + 5i, 'b' is 5.

Explanation:
To find the imaginary part of a complex number, you look at the coefficient of the imaginary term with i. The other choices do not correspond to the correct value for the imaginary part.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing the first term (-2) as the imaginary part. This confusion arises from misunderstanding the roles of 'a' and 'b' in a complex number.

Problem 4 (:4):
Which of the following is an example of a pure imaginary number?
A) 3 + 0i
B) 2i
C) 0 + 5i
D) -7 - 4i
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
A pure imaginary number has no real part and only an imaginary part. In the options, 0 + 5i fits this description.

Explanation:
A pure imaginary number is of the form bi, where 'b' is a non-zero real number. The other choices either have a non-zero real part or are not in the correct format.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing numbers with both real and imaginary parts (like 3 + 0i or -7 - 4i). This confusion comes from not recognizing the specific characteristics of pure imaginary numbers.

Problem 5 (:5):
What is the complex conjugate of the number 4 - 6i?
A) -4 + 6i
B) 4 + 6i
C) 6 - 4i
D) 6 + 4i
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The complex conjugate of a number (a + bi) is (a - bi). For the given number 4 - 6i, its conjugate is 4 + 6i.

Explanation:
The complex conjugate switches the sign of the imaginary part. The other choices do not follow this rule or have incorrect real parts.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Switching both the signs of 'a' and 'b'. This confusion occurs when not understanding that only the sign of the imaginary term changes in a complex conjugate.

Problem 6 (:6):
What is the result of multiplying (2 + 3i) by (1 - i)?
A) 5 + i
B) 5 - i
C) -1 - 5i
D) 1 + 5i
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
(2 + 3i)(1 - i) = (2 	imes 1) + (2 	imes -i) + (3i 	imes 1) + (3i 	imes -i) = 2 - 2i + 3i - 3i^2 = 5 + i

Explanation:
To multiply complex numbers, use the distributive property and remember that i^2 = -1. The other choices do not result from the correct multiplication.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Incorrectly multiplying the imaginary parts or forgetting to substitute i^2 with -1. This error occurs when not applying the rules of complex number arithmetic.

Problem 7 (:7):
Which of the following is equivalent to the complex number (5 + 3i)^2?
A) 28 + 6i
B) 28 - 6i
C) 32 + 10i
D) 32 - 10i
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
(5 + 3i)^2 = (5^2) + (2 	imes 5 	imes 3i) + (3i)^2 = 25 + 30i + 9i^2 = 32 + 10i

Explanation:
To square a complex number, apply the binomial theorem or FOIL method and remember that i^2 = -1. The other choices do not result from the correct squaring process.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Incorrectly multiplying the terms or forgetting to substitute i^2 with -1. This confusion arises from not applying the rules of complex number arithmetic when squaring.
