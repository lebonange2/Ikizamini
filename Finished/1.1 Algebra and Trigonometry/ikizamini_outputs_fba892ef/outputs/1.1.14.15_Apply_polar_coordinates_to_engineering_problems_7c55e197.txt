Problem 1 (One-dimensional motion in polar coordinates):
A particle moves along a straight line with polar coordinates given by r = 2t and θ = t, where r is the distance from the origin and θ is the angle measured counterclockwise from the positive x-axis. What is the linear velocity of the particle at time t?
A) 2i + j
B) 2t i + j
C) 2i + 2tj
D) 4ti + tj
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The linear velocity is given by dr/dt * (cosθ, sinθ). For r = 2t and θ = t, we have dr/dt = 2 and dθ/dt = 1. Thus, the velocity vector is (2cos(t), 2sin(t)). At time t, this simplifies to (2, 2t).

Explanation:
The correct choice represents the velocity vector in unit vector form.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to neglect the time dependence of θ when calculating the velocity. Choice A assumes a constant angle, while choices B and D use incorrect scalars.

Problem 2 (Polar equation of a circle):
What is the polar equation for a circle with radius 5 centered at the origin?
A) r = 5cos(θ)
B) r = 5sin(θ)
C) r^2 = 25
D) r = 5
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
The polar equation for a circle centered at the origin with radius r is simply r = r. In this case, r = 5.

Explanation:
Choice C represents the Cartesian form of the circle's equation (x^2 + y^2 = 25) converted to polar coordinates, while choices A and B represent parametric equations for cosine and sine waves, respectively.

Common mistake note:
Students may混淆the polar equation with parametric equations or forget that the simple form r = r represents a circle.

Problem 3 (Converting rectangular to polar coordinates):
Convert the point (3, 4) in rectangular coordinates to polar coordinates.
A) (5, arctan(3/4))
B) (5, π/2 - arctan(4/3))
C) (7, arctan(4/3))
D) (5, arctan(4/3))
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
The polar coordinates (r, θ) are given by r = √(x^2 + y^2) and θ = arctan(y/x). For the point (3, 4), we have r = √(3^2 + 4^2) = 5 and θ = arctan(4/3).

Explanation:
Choice A uses the wrong quadrant for θ, choice B uses π/2 - arctan instead of just arctan, and choice C has an incorrect radius.

Common mistake note:
Students may混淆the quadrants when calculating θ or forget to convert from degrees to radians.

Problem 4 (Angular velocity in polar coordinates):
A wheel is rotating counterclockwise with a constant angular velocity of 2 rad/s. If the point P on the edge of the wheel has polar coordinates (1, θ), what is the linear velocity of P at θ = π/3?
A) 2i - √3j
B) 2√3i + j
C) 2i + 2√3j
D) 4i + 2√3j
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The linear velocity of a point on a rotating wheel is given by v = ω * r * (cosθ, sinθ), where ω is the angular velocity and r is the distance from the origin. At θ = π/3, we have v = 2 * 1 * (cos(π/3), sin(π/3)) = (2/2, √3/2) = (1, √3). Converting to unit vector form gives 2i - √3j.

Explanation:
The correct choice represents the velocity vector in unit vector form.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to use the wrong trigonometric values for cos(π/3) and sin(π/3).

Problem 5 (Work done by a force in polar coordinates):
A particle moves along a curve given by r = 2θ in polar coordinates under a force F = 5r^2i + 3rj. What is the work done by the force as the particle moves from θ = 0 to θ = π/2?
A) 16π
B) 8π
C) 4π
D) 2π
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The work done by a force is given by the line integral W = ∫F·dr. In polar coordinates, dr = r dθ * (cosθ, sinθ), so F·dr = (5r^2i + 3rj)·(r dθ * (cosθ, sinθ)) = (5r^3 + 3r^2)dθ. Integrating from θ = 0 to θ = π/2 gives W = ∫[0, π/2] (5r^3 + 3r^2)dθ = [16r^4/16 + r^3/3]_0^(π/2) = 8π.

Explanation:
The correct choice is obtained by evaluating the integral of the dot product of the force and the differential displacement vector.

Common mistake note:
Students may混淆the integration variable or forget to substitute the polar coordinate expression for r.

Problem 6 (Polar equation of an ellipse):
What is the polar equation for an ellipse with semi-major axis a and semi-minor axis b, centered at the origin?
A) r = a cos(θ)
B) r = b sin(θ)
C) r^2/a^2 + r^2/b^2 = 1
D) r = (a^2/b) * (1 - cos(θ))
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The polar equation for an ellipse with semi-major axis a and semi-minor axis b is given by r^2/a^2 + r^2/b^2 = 1. This is the conversion of the Cartesian form (x^2/a^2 + y^2/b^2 = 1) to polar coordinates.

Explanation:
Choice A represents a circle, choice B represents a sine wave, and choice D represents an incomplete parametric equation for an ellipse.

Common mistake note:
Students may混淆the polar equation with other shapes or forget the correct conversion from Cartesian to polar coordinates.

Problem 7 (Angular acceleration in polar coordinates):
A particle moves along a circle of radius 3 with angular acceleration α = 2 rad/s^2. If its initial angular velocity is ω_0 = 1 rad/s, what is the angular velocity after 2 seconds?
A) 5 rad/s
B) 6 rad/s
C) 7 rad/s
D) 8 rad/s
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Angular velocity ω at time t can be found using ω = ω_0 + αt. Given ω_0 = 1 rad/s and α = 2 rad/s^2, after 2 seconds we have ω = 1 + 2 * 2 = 5 rad/s.

Explanation:
The correct choice is calculated by integrating the angular acceleration with respect to time.

Common mistake note:
Students may混淆angular velocity and linear velocity or forget to multiply α by t when calculating the change in ω.
