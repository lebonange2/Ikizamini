Problem 1 (One):
What is the vertex form of the quadratic function f(x) = x^2 + 4x - 5?
A) (x + 2)^2 - 9
B) (x - 2)^2 + 1
C) (x + 1)^2 - 6
D) (x - 1)^2 - 4
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The vertex form of a quadratic function is given by f(x) = a(x - h)^2 + k, where (h, k) is the vertex. To convert f(x) = x^2 + 4x - 5 to vertex form, we complete the square for the x terms: x^2 + 4x = (x^2 + 4x + 4) - 4 = (x + 2)^2 - 4. Then we substitute back into the function: f(x) = (x + 2)^2 - 4 - 5 = (x + 2)^2 - 9.

Explanation:
The correct answer is A because it represents the vertex form with the vertex at (-2, -9).

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget to subtract the constant term after completing the square, leading to choice B.

Problem 2 (Two):
What is the vertex form of the quadratic function g(x) = 3x^2 + 6x - 9?
A) (3x + 3)^2 - 18
B) (3x - 3)^2 + 6
C) (x + 1)^2 - 4
D) (x - 1)^2 + 1
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
To convert g(x) = 3x^2 + 6x - 9 to vertex form, we first factor out the coefficient of x^2: 3(x^2 + 2x) - 9. Then we complete the square for the x terms inside the parentheses: (x^2 + 2x + 1) - 1 - 9 = (x + 1)^2 - 10. Finally, we multiply everything by 3 to get the vertex form: g(x) = 3[(x + 1)^2 - 10] = 3(x + 1)^2 - 30 = (3x + 3)^2 - 18.

Explanation:
The correct answer is A because it represents the vertex form with the vertex at (-1, -18).

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget to multiply the constant term by the coefficient of x^2 after completing the square, leading to choice B.
