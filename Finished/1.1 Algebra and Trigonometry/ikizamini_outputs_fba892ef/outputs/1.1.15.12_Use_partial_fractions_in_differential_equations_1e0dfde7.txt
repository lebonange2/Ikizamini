Problem 1 (Asdf):
Solve the differential equation dy/dx + y = 6/x by using partial fractions. The correct form of the partial fraction decomposition for 6/x is:
A) A/(x-1) + B/(x+1)
B) C/ x - D/x^2
C) E/ x + F/x^2
D) G/ x - H/x^3
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The correct partial fraction decomposition for 6/x is 6/x = E/x + F/x^2, where E and F are constants. Integrating this will lead to the solution of the differential equation.

Explanation:
To use partial fractions in a differential equation, we must first decompose the rational function into simpler fractions with linear denominators. In this case, 6/x has no common factors and can be directly decomposed as shown.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to assume that the denominator will have linear factors (x - 1) or (x + 1), but in this case, the denominator is simply x, so the decomposition involves powers of x.

Explanation:
