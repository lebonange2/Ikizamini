Problem 1 (:1):
What is the vertex of the parabola given by the equation y = (x - 2)^2 + 3?
A) (0, 3)
B) (2, 0)
C) (2, 3)
D) (3, 2)
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The vertex form of a parabola is y = (x - h)^2 + k, where (h, k) is the vertex. Comparing this to the given equation y = (x - 2)^2 + 3, we can see that h = 2 and k = 3. Therefore, the vertex is at the point (2, 3).

Explanation:
The vertex of a parabola in standard form is directly related to the values of h and k.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to confuse the coordinates with those of a different point on the parabola. Remember that the vertex is always given by (h, k) in the equation.

Problem 2 (2):
The vertex of the parabola y = -3(x + 4)^2 - 1 is at which point?
A) (4, -1)
B) (-4, 1)
C) (-4, -1)
D) (1, -4)
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The vertex form of a parabola is y = a(x - h)^2 + k. Comparing this to the given equation y = -3(x + 4)^2 - 1, we can see that h = -4 and k = -1. Therefore, the vertex is at the point (-4, -1).

Explanation:
The vertex of a parabola in standard form is directly related to the values of h and k.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to forget the negative sign when calculating h or k. The direction of the parabola depends on the sign of 'a'.

Problem 4 (4):
Given the vertex (3, -2) and the parabola equation y = ax^2 + bx + c, what is the value of a?
A) -1
B) 0
C) 1
D) 2
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The vertex form of a parabola is y = a(x - h)^2 + k, where (h, k) is the vertex. Given the vertex (3, -2), we have h = 3 and k = -2. The equation can be rewritten as y = a(x - 3)^2 - 2. To find 'a', we need to expand the equation and match it with the standard form y = ax^2 + bx + c. However, since we are only asked for 'a' and not 'b' or 'c', we can infer that 'a' must be 1 because the coefficient of x^2 is 1 in the vertex form.

Explanation:
The value of 'a' in the vertex form directly corresponds to the coefficient of x^2 in the standard form.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to try to solve for all coefficients, which isn't necessary given the question's scope. Focus on what information is asked for.

Problem 7 (7):
Given a parabola with vertex (0, 0) and equation y = ax^2 + bx + c, which of the following must be true?
A) a = 1
B) b = 0
C) c = 1
D) a = 0
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The vertex form of a parabola is y = a(x - h)^2 + k, where (h, k) is the vertex. Given the vertex (0, 0), we have h = 0 and k = 0. This means that the equation simplifies to y = ax^2 + bx + c. Since there's no linear term (x), the coefficient 'b' must be 0.

Explanation:
When the vertex of a parabola is at the origin, the linear term in the standard form disappears.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to assume that 'a' or 'c' must be zero. However, only the coefficient 'b' is affected by the vertex being at the origin.
