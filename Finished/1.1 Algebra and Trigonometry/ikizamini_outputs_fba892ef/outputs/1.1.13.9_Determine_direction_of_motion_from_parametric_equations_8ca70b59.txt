Problem 1 (Asdf):
Given the parametric equations x(t) = 2t and y(t) = t^2, what is the direction of motion at t=1?
A) Upward
B) Rightward
C) Leftward
D) Downward
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
To determine the direction of motion from parametric equations, we can look at the rates of change of x and y with respect to time. At t=1, dx/dt = 2 and dy/dt = 2. Since both are positive, the object is moving in a positive direction for both x and y, which means it's moving rightward.

Explanation:
The rate of change of x (dx/dt) represents the speed along the x-axis, while the rate of change of y (dy/dt) represents the speed along the y-axis. Both being positive indicates a rightward motion.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to assume that since y(t) = t^2 is increasing, the direction must be upward. However, we need to consider both x and y together to determine the overall direction of motion.

Problem 2 (Asdf):
For the parametric equations x(t) = cos(t) and y(t) = sin(t), what is the direction of motion at t=π/2?
A) Upward
B) Rightward
C) Leftward
D) Downward
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
At t=π/2, dx/dt = -sin(π/2) = -1 and dy/dt = cos(π/2) = 0. Since dy/dt is positive and dx/dt is negative, the object is moving upward but slightly to the left.

Explanation:
In this case, we're dealing with a parametric representation of a circle. The x-component (dx/dt) is decreasing while y-component (dy/dt) remains constant at t=π/2, indicating an upward motion.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to choose 'Rightward' based on the positive value of dy/dt alone. However, dx/dt being negative indicates a leftward component.

Problem 3 (Asdf):
Given x(t) = 3sin(2t) and y(t) = 4cos(2t), what is the direction of motion at t=π?
A) Upward
B) Rightward
C) Leftward
D) Downward
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
At t=π, dx/dt = -6cos(2π) = 0 and dy/dt = -8sin(2π) = 0. Since both are zero, the object is momentarily stationary.

Explanation:
At t=π, both dx/dt and dy/dt are zero, which means the object is not moving in any direction at that instant.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to assume a specific direction based on the trigonometric functions without considering their values at t=π. At this point, they both evaluate to 0.

Problem 4 (Asdf):
For x(t) = 4t - 3 and y(t) = 2t + 1, what is the direction of motion when t increases?
A) Upward
B) Rightward
C) Leftward
D) Downward
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The rates of change are dx/dt = 4 and dy/dt = 2, both positive. This indicates rightward motion.

Explanation:
Here, we can directly see that as t increases, x and y increase at a constant rate, meaning the object is moving rightward along a straight line.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to confuse the direction of motion with the slope of the line. In this case, the positive slopes mean an upward and rightward motion, but the overall direction is rightward.

Problem 5 (Asdf):
The parametric equations are x(t) = 2t^3 - t and y(t) = t^4 + 1. At what value of t does the object change its direction?
A) 0
B) 1
C) sqrt(2)
D) Does not change direction
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
To find when the direction changes, we need to look for points where dx/dt and dy/dt have opposite signs. At t=0, both are 0, and at t=1, dx/dt = 5 and dy/dt = 4, so the direction changes at t=1.

Explanation:
The change in direction occurs when the rates of change (dx/dt and dy/dt) switch from having the same sign to opposite signs or vice versa. At t=1, this transition happens.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to choose 'Does not change direction' based on the fact that both x(t) and y(t) are increasing functions. However, we need to consider their derivatives for direction changes.

Problem 6 (Asdf):
Given x(t) = 3sin(t) + 2cos(t) and y(t) = 4sin(t) - 3cos(t), at what value of t does the object move purely horizontally?
A) π/4
B) π/2
C) 3π/4
D) π
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
For horizontal motion, dy/dt = 0. At t=π/4, dx/dt = 2cos(π/4) + 2sin(π/4) = 2√2 and dy/dt = 4cos(π/4) - 3sin(π/4) = 1, so the object moves purely horizontally at t=π/4.

Explanation:
Horizontal motion occurs when the rate of change in y (dy/dt) is zero. At t=π/4, dy/dt is indeed zero while dx/dt is non-zero, indicating horizontal motion.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to choose 'π/2' based on the trigonometric functions, but at that point, both x(t) and y(t) are at their maximum values, not resulting in horizontal motion.

Problem 7 (Asdf):
The parametric equations are x(t) = 5sin(3t) + 2cos(4t) and y(t) = 7sin(3t) - 4cos(4t). At what interval does the object move in a straight line?
A) (0, π/6)
B) (π/6, π/3)
C) (π/3, π/2)
D) (π/2, π)
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The object moves in a straight line when dx/dt and dy/dt are proportional. At t=π/6, dx/dt = 15cos(3π/2) - 8sin(4π/2) = 0 and dy/dt = 21cos(3π/2) + 16sin(4π/2) = 0, so the object moves in a straight line between t=0 and t=π/3.

Explanation:
A straight line motion occurs when dx/dt and dy/dt are constant multiples of each other. At t=π/6, both derivatives are zero, indicating that the object is momentarily stationary at this point. The interval for straight line motion would be from the start of the movement until this point.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to choose '(0, π/2)' because it covers a larger interval, but the object moves in a circular path after t=π/3.
