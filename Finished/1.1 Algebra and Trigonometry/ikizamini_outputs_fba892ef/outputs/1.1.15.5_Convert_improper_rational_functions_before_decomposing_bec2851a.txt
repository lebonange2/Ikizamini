Problem 1 (One):
Convert the improper rational function to a proper one by multiplying both the numerator and denominator by the LCD of the terms in the denominator. What is the correct form of the following function?
f(x) = (3x + 6)/(2x^2 - 8x)
A) (3x + 6)/(4x(x - 4))
B) (6x + 12)/(2x(x - 4))
C) (3x + 6)/(x(x - 4))
D) (6x + 12)/(4x^2(x - 4))
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The LCD of the terms in the denominator (2x^2 - 8x) is 2x(x - 4). Multiply both the numerator and denominator by this LCD to get a proper rational function:

f(x) = [(3x + 6) * 2x(x - 4)] / [2x^2 - 8x] * 2x(x - 4)
f(x) = (6x^2 - 12x + 12x - 24) / 4x(x - 4)
f(x) = (6x^2 - 24) / 4x(x - 4)
f(x) = (3x + 6)/(4x(x - 4))

Explanation:
The correct conversion maintains the equality of the function while making it a proper rational function by removing any common factors between the numerator and denominator.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is not multiplying both the numerator and denominator by the LCD, leading to an incorrect simplified form.
