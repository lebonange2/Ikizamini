Problem 1 (Asdf):
Which inequality represents the half-plane to the right of the line x = 2?
A) x > 2
B) x < 2
C) y > 2
D) y < 2
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The line x = 2 divides the plane into two half-planes: one to the left (x < 2) and one to the right (x > 2). The inequality x > 2 represents the half-plane to the right of the line.

Explanation:
To identify the half-plane, consider which side of the line the inequality allows for. In this case, x > 2 means that x can take any value greater than 2, which is on the right side of the line.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to choose 'B', as it represents the half-plane to the left of the line.

Problem 3 (Asdf):
Which inequality represents the half-plane that includes the point (1, 2) and is bounded by the line y = x?
A) y > x
B) y < x
C) x > 2y
D) x < 2y
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The line y = x divides the plane into two half-planes: one where y is greater than x (y > x) and one where y is less than x (y < x). Since the point (1, 2) lies on the line, the correct inequality must include this point. The only choice that does so is y > x.

Explanation:
To identify the half-plane, check if the given point satisfies the inequality. In this case, since (1, 2) does not satisfy y < x or x > 2y, and it lies on the line for x < 2y, the correct choice is y > x.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to choose 'B', as it does not include the given point (1, 2).
