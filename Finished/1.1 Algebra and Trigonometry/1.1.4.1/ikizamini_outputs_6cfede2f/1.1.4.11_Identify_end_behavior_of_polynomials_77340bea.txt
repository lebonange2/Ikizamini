Problem 1 (Increasing_fall):
What is the end behavior of the polynomial function f(x) = -3x^4 + 2x^3 - x^2?
A) (Positive infinity, Negative infinity)
B) (Negative infinity, Positive infinity)
C) (Negative infinity, Negative infinity)
D) (Positive infinity, Positive infinity)
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The leading term of the polynomial is -3x^4, which has a negative coefficient and an exponent of 4. This means that as x approaches positive or negative infinity, the function will decrease without bound in the opposite direction. The correct end behavior is thus (Negative infinity, Negative infinity).

Explanation:
The end behavior of a polynomial is determined by its leading term. If the leading coefficient is positive and the exponent is even, the function increases to positive infinity in both directions. If the leading coefficient is negative and the exponent is even, it decreases to negative infinity in both directions. If the exponent is odd, the direction depends on the sign of the coefficient.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to choose (Positive infinity, Negative infinity) or (Negative infinity, Positive infinity), which are incorrect because they represent opposite behaviors. Another mistake might be to focus only on the x^2 term and forget about the higher degree x^4 term, which dominates the end behavior.

Problem 7 (Zero_degree):
What is the end behavior of the polynomial function m(x) = 2x + 1?
A) (Positive infinity, Positive infinity)
B) (Negative infinity, Negative infinity)
C) (Positive infinity, Negative infinity)
D) (Negative infinity, Positive infinity)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The leading term of the polynomial is 2x, which has a positive coefficient and an exponent of 1. This means that as x approaches positive infinity, the function increases without bound. As x approaches negative infinity, it decreases without bound but remains in the same direction (positive). The correct end behavior is thus (Positive infinity, Positive infinity).

Explanation:
The end behavior of a polynomial is determined by its leading term. If the leading coefficient is positive and the exponent is even, the function increases to positive infinity in both directions. If the leading coefficient is negative and the exponent is even, it decreases to negative infinity in both directions. If the exponent is odd, the direction depends on the sign of the coefficient.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to choose (Negative infinity, Negative infinity) or (Negative infinity, Positive infinity), which are incorrect because they represent decreasing behavior. Another mistake might be to forget that the zero-degree term (constant) does not affect the end behavior.
