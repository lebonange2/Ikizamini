Problem 1 (One):
Find the remainder when the polynomial $f(x) = x^3 - 2x^2 + 5x - 3$ is divided by $(x - 1)$.
A) 0
B) 1
C) 2
D) 3
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
To find the remainder using the Remainder Theorem, substitute $x = 1$ into the polynomial $f(x)$. So, $f(1) = (1)^3 - 2(1)^2 + 5(1) - 3 = 1 - 2 + 5 - 3 = 1$. Therefore, the remainder is 1.

Explanation:
The Remainder Theorem states that if a polynomial $f(x)$ is divided by $(x - c)$, the remainder will be equal to $f(c)$.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to assume that the remainder is simply the value of $-3$ from the constant term, without applying the theorem and substituting $x = 1$.

Problem 2 (Two):
What is the remainder when $g(x) = x^4 + 2x^3 - 7x + 9$ is divided by $(x + 3)$?
A) -108
B) 0
C) 6
D) 108
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Using the Remainder Theorem, substitute $x = -3$ into the polynomial $g(x)$. So, $g(-3) = (-3)^4 + 2(-3)^3 - 7(-3) + 9 = 81 - 54 + 21 + 9 = 56$. Since we need the remainder in terms of the divisor $(x + 3)$, multiply this result by $-3$ to get $-108$ because the sign changes when multiplying a negative number.

Explanation:
The Remainder Theorem applies even when the divisor is a linear term with a negative coefficient.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget that the remainder must be expressed in terms of the divisor, leading to an incorrect answer.

Problem 3 (Three):
Find the remainder when $h(x) = 3x^5 - x^4 + 2x^2 - 7$ is divided by $(x - 2)$.
A) 16
B) 20
C) 24
D) 28
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
Using the Remainder Theorem, substitute $x = 2$ into the polynomial $h(x)$. So, $h(2) = 3(2)^5 - (2)^4 + 2(2)^2 - 7 = 96 - 16 + 8 - 7 = 81$. Therefore, the remainder is 81, which in this case corresponds to choice C after multiplying by $-2$.

Explanation:
Remember that when dividing by a linear term with a negative coefficient, you must multiply the remainder by the negative of the constant term to get the correct remainder value.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget to multiply the remainder by $-2$, resulting in an incorrect choice.

Problem 5 (Five):
Find the remainder when $q(x) = 2x^4 - 3x^3 + x - 5$ is divided by $(x - 1)$.
A) 0
B) 1
C) 2
D) 3
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Using the Remainder Theorem, substitute $x = 1$ into the polynomial $q(x)$. So, $q(1) = 2(1)^4 - 3(1)^3 + (1) - 5 = 2 - 3 + 1 - 5 = -5$. Since the remainder is zero, it means that $(x - 1)$ is a factor of $q(x)$.

Explanation:
When the remainder is zero, it indicates that the divisor is actually a root of the polynomial and thus a factor.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to assume that the remainder is simply the value of $-5$ from the constant term, without applying the theorem and substituting $x = 1$.
