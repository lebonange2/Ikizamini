Problem 1 (:question_1):
Determine if the series converges or diverges using the Comparison Test: ∑(n=1 to infinity) (3^n)/(2^(n+1))
A) Convergent by Direct Comparison Test with 1/(n^2).
B) Divergent by Limit Comparison Test with 1/n.
C) Divergent by p-series test.
D) Incorrect choice.
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
To apply the comparison test, we need to find another series whose convergence or divergence is known and compare it with our given series. The given series is ∑(3^n)/(2^(n+1)). We can rewrite this as ∑((3/2)^n). Since (3/2) > 1, the series diverges by itself. However, we are asked to use comparison test, so let's compare it with another series that diverges. A good choice would be 1/n: ((3/2)^n) / [1/n] = (3/2)^n * n. As n approaches infinity, this ratio goes to infinity because the polynomial growth of n dominates the exponential growth of (3/2)^n. Since our series is eventually larger than a divergent series, it also diverges by the Limit Comparison Test.

Explanation:
The Limit Comparison Test states that if lim (n→∞) [a_n / b_n] exists and is greater than 0 but finite, and ∑b_n diverges, then ∑a_n also diverges. In this case, we found that the limit goes to infinity, which implies our series grows faster than a divergent series.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to choose A, thinking that the given series is eventually smaller than 1/(n^2). However, the Limit Comparison Test is more appropriate here because it compares growth rates. Another mistake could be choosing C, but comparing with a p-series wouldn't help since our series doesn't have a standard form.

Problem 2 (:question_2):
Determine if the series converges or diverges using the Comparison Test: ∑(n=1 to infinity) (ln(n))/n^3
A) Convergent by Direct Comparison Test with 1/n.
B) Divergent by Limit Comparison Test with 1/√n.
C) Incorrect choice.
D) Incorrect choice.
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
To apply the comparison test, we need to find another series whose convergence or divergence is known and compare it with our given series. The given series is ∑(ln(n))/n^3. We know that ln(n) grows much slower than n^3 as n approaches infinity. Let's compare this series with 1/n: (ln(n))/n^3 < 1/n for large enough n, because the growth of n^3 dominates the logarithmic growth of ln(n). Since 1/n converges by p-series test (p > 1), our series also converges by the Direct Comparison Test.

Explanation:
The Direct Comparison Test states that if 0 ≤ a_n ≤ b_n for all n greater than some N and ∑b_n converges, then ∑a_n also converges. In this case, we found that (ln(n))/n^3 < 1/n for large enough n, which implies our series is eventually smaller than the convergent p-series.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to choose B, thinking that 1/√n might be a better comparison series. However, 1/√n has a p-value of 0.5, which is less than 1, and thus it converges by the p-series test. Another mistake could be choosing C or D, but they are not valid choices for this question.
