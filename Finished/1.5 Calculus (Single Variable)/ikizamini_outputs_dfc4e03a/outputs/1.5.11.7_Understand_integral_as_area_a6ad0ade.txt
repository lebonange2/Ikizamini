Problem 1 (AsdFg6789):
The definite integral of f(x) from a to b represents the area under the curve of f(x) between x = a and x = b. Which of the following definite integrals represents the area between the x-axis and the curve of f(x) = 2x - 1 from x = 0 to x = 3?
A) ∫[0, 3] (2x - 1) dx
B) ∫[0, 3] (2x + 1) dx
C) ∫[3, 0] (2x - 1) dx
D) ∫[0, 3] 2x dx
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The correct integral is ∫[0, 3] (2x - 1) dx because it represents the area under the curve of f(x) = 2x - 1. The limits of integration, a and b, define the interval over which we calculate the area.

Explanation:
The integral calculates the signed area between the x-axis and the function curve. Since f(x) is positive for x >= 0.5, the area will be positive for our given interval.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to choose option B, which uses (2x + 1), an incorrect function. This would give a different area due to the change in the curve.

Problem 2 (ZxcVbNml):
The integral ∫[1, 4] x^2 dx represents the area of which geometric shape?
A) Triangle
B) Rectangle
C) Parabolic sector
D) Circular sector
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The correct answer is C, a parabolic sector. The integral of x^2 gives the area under the curve y = x^2, which forms a portion of a parabola between x = 1 and x = 4.

Explanation:
When we integrate x^n from a to b, we get the area under the curve y = x^n for that interval. For n = 2, this is a parabolic sector.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to choose option A (Triangle), which arises from integrating x linearly instead of quadratically.

Problem 3 (QweRtYui):
The area under the curve y = 3sin(x) from x = 0 to x = π is given by which definite integral?
A) ∫[0, π] 3sin(x) dx
B) ∫[0, π] 3cos(x) dx
C) ∫[0, π] 2sin(x) dx
D) ∫[0, π] 4sin(x) dx
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The correct integral is ∫[0, π] 3sin(x) dx because it represents the area under the curve y = 3sin(x). The coefficient 3 multiplies the sine function to give the total area.

Explanation:
The area under a sinusoidal curve is still given by integrating the function, but with the appropriate amplitude (in this case, 3).

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to choose option B, which integrates cos(x) instead of sin(x), which would give the area under a cosine curve, not sine.

Problem 4 (BvnMjHgl):
The definite integral ∫[1, 2] e^x dx represents the area between which two curves?
A) y = e^x and y = 1
B) y = e^x and y = x
C) y = e^x and y = e
D) y = e^x and y = e^2
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The correct answer is C, between y = e^x and y = e. The definite integral gives the area between the curve of y = e^x and the x-axis from x = 1 to x = 2. Since we're not subtracting another function, the upper bound for the area is the x-axis.

Explanation:
When integrating a single function without subtraction, the area is above the x-axis and below the curve of that function.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to choose option D, which assumes we're finding the area between y = e^x and y = e^2. This would involve subtracting two functions, but our integral does not include a second function.

Problem 5 (GhjKlPoi):
The definite integral ∫[0, 2] (x - 1)^2 dx represents the area of which shape?
A) Triangle
B) Rectangle
C) Parabolic sector
D) Circular sector
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The correct answer is C, a parabolic sector. The integral of (x - 1)^2 gives the area under the curve y = (x - 1)^2, which forms a portion of a parabola between x = 0 and x = 2.

Explanation:
When integrating a quadratic function like (x - 1)^2, we get the area under the corresponding parabolic curve for the given interval.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to choose option A (Triangle), which arises from integrating a linear function instead of a quadratic one.

Solution:
The correct answer is D, between y = 2cos(2x) and the x-axis. The definite integral gives the area under the curve of y = 2cos(2x) from x = 0 to x = π/4.

Explanation:

Common mistake note:
