Problem 1 (Evaluate e^x integral):
Evaluate the definite integral of e^x from x=0 to x=2.
A) e^2 - 1
B) e^3 - 1
C) e^4 - 1
D) e^5 - 1
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The integral of e^x is e^x. Evaluate at the limits: (e^2 - e^0) = e^2 - 1.

Explanation:
This question tests the ability to integrate an exponential function and apply definite integration.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be forgetting to evaluate the integral at both limits, which would result in an incorrect answer.

Problem 2 (Antiderivative of 3e^(2x)):
Find the antiderivative of 3e^(2x).
A) 3e^(2x)/4
B) 3e^(2x)/2
C) 6e^(2x)
D) 1/6e^(2x)
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The antiderivative of e^(ax) is (1/a)e^(ax). So, for 3e^(2x), the antiderivative is (1/2) * 3e^(2x) = 3e^(2x)/2.

Explanation:
This question tests the ability to find the antiderivative of an exponential function with a constant coefficient.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be forgetting to divide by the coefficient in front of e^(2x), leading to an incorrect answer.

Problem 3 (Integrate 5e^(-x)):
Integrate the function f(x) = 5e^(-x) from x=1 to x=3.
A) 4
B) 6
C) -4
D) -6
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The integral of e^(-x) is -e^(-x). Evaluate at the limits: (-e^(-3) + e^(-1)) = -e^(-3) + 1/e = 4.

Explanation:
This question tests the ability to integrate an exponential function with a negative exponent and apply definite integration.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be forgetting to change the sign when integrating e^(-x), resulting in an incorrect answer.

Problem 4 (Antiderivative of 2e^(3x) + 4e^(-x)):
Find the antiderivative of 2e^(3x) + 4e^(-x).
A) 2/3e^(3x) - 4/ e^x
B) 1/3e^(3x) - 4e^x
C) 2e^(3x) - 4e^x
D) 6e^(3x) - 4/ e^x
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The antiderivative of e^(ax) is (1/a)e^(ax). So, for 2e^(3x), the antiderivative is (1/3) * 2e^(3x) = 2/3e^(3x); for 4e^(-x), it's -4/e^x. Combine: 2/3e^(3x) - 4/ e^x.

Explanation:
This question tests the ability to find the antiderivative of a sum of exponential functions with different exponents.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be forgetting to apply the correct coefficients when integrating each term, leading to an incorrect answer.

Problem 5 (Evaluate e^(2x) integral):
Evaluate the definite integral of e^(2x) from x=-1 to x=1.
A) e^4 - 1/ e^2
B) e^2 - 1/ e^2
C) e^4 - e^2
D) e^2 - 1
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The integral of e^(2x) is (1/2)e^(2x). Evaluate at the limits: [(1/2)e^(2) - (1/2)e^(-2)] = e^2 - 1/ e^2.

Explanation:
This question tests the ability to integrate an exponential function with a power other than 1 and apply definite integration.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be forgetting to divide by the exponent when integrating e^(2x), resulting in an incorrect answer.

Problem 6 (Antiderivative of e^(4x) - 3e^(-2x)):
Find the antiderivative of e^(4x) - 3e^(-2x).
A) 1/4e^(4x) + 3/2e^(-2x)
B) 1/4e^(4x) - 3/2e^(-2x)
C) e^(4x) - 3e^(-2x)
D) 4e^(4x) - 6e^(-2x)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The antiderivative of e^(ax) is (1/a)e^(ax). So, for e^(4x), the antiderivative is (1/4)e^(4x); for -3e^(-2x), it's (-3/2)e^(-2x). Combine: 1/4e^(4x) + 3/2e^(-2x).

Explanation:
This question tests the ability to find the antiderivative of a difference of exponential functions with different exponents.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be forgetting to apply the correct coefficients when integrating each term, leading to an incorrect answer.

Problem 7 (Integrate 6e^(2x)):
Integrate the function f(x) = 6e^(2x) from x=0 to x=ln(3).
A) 9
B) 18
C) 27
D) 54
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The integral of e^(ax) is (1/a)e^(ax). For 6e^(2x), the antiderivative is 3e^(2x). Evaluate at the limits: [3e^(2ln(3)) - 3e^(0)] = 9.

Explanation:
This question tests the ability to integrate an exponential function with a power other than 1 and apply definite integration, using natural logarithms.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be forgetting to change the limits when using natural logarithms or applying the wrong exponent rule, leading to an incorrect answer.
