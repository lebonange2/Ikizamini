Problem 1 (AsdFg67h):
Evaluate the definite integral Ω(x) from -2 to 1 by changing the limits of integration: Ω(x) = x^2 + 1
A) -π(1)^2 - π(-2)^2
B) π(1)^2 - π(-2)^2
C) 3π + 4π
D) -3π + 4π
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
To change the limits of integration, multiply the integrand by -1 and flip the limits. The new integral is Ω(-x) from 1 to -2:
-π(-x)^2 dx = -π(x^2 + 1) dx.
Integrate and evaluate: 
-[π(x^3/3 + x)|_{-2}^{1}] = -[π(1/3 + 1) - π(-8/3 - 2)]
= -[4π/3] + [14π/3]
= -3π + 4π

Explanation:
The correct answer is obtained by flipping the limits and multiplying the integrand by -1.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to forget to multiply the integrand by -1 when changing the limits. Choice A neglects this step.

Problem 2 (BvnMj9kl):
Evaluate Ω(x) from 4 to 7, given that Ω(x) = π(2x - 5)^2 dx by changing the limits of integration.
A) π(16 - 5)^2 - π(28 - 5)^2
B) π(7 - 5)^2 - π(4 - 5)^2
C) π(9)^2 - π(13)^2
D) π(13)^2 - π(9)^2
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
To change the limits, find a substitution that maps 4 to 7 and vice versa. Let u = 2x - 5. Then x = (u + 5)/2. The new limits are from 3 to 11 (when x = 4, u = 3; when x = 7, u = 11). The integral becomes:
π(u^2) * (1/2) du = π(u^2/2) du.
Integrate and evaluate: 
[π(u^3/6)|_3^{11}] = π(11^3/6) - π(3^3/6)
= π(2475/6) - π(27/2)
= π(412.5) - π(13.5)

Explanation:
The correct answer is obtained by finding the appropriate substitution and evaluating the new integral.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to forget to adjust the coefficient of u in the integrand after substituting. Choice B neglects this step.

Problem 3 (CzqXe4rt):
Evaluate Ω(x) from 1 to 2 by changing the limits of integration: Ω(x) = x^3 dx
A) π(1)^3 - π(2)^3
B) -π(1)^3 + π(2)^3
C) 7π/4
D) 9π/4
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
To change the limits, integrate from 2 to 1 and multiply by -1. The new integral is:
-π(x^3) dx from 2 to 1.
Integrate and evaluate: 
-[π(x^4/4)|_2^{1}] = -[-3π/4]
= 7π/4

Explanation:
The correct answer is obtained by integrating from the larger limit to the smaller and multiplying by -1.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to integrate directly from 1 to 2 without flipping the limits. Choice A neglects this step.

Problem 6 (FghJklmn):
Evaluate Ω(x) from -4 to 1 by changing the limits of integration: Ω(x) = sin(x) dx
A) π(sin(1)) - π(sin(-4))
B) -π(sin(1)) + π(sin(-4))
C) 2π
D) -2π
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
To change the limits, integrate from 1 to -4 and multiply by -1. Since sine is an odd function, its definite integral over a symmetric interval around zero is zero. Therefore, the answer is 0.

Explanation:
The correct answer is obtained by recognizing that the integral of an odd function over a symmetric interval around zero is zero.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to integrate directly from -4 to 1 without flipping the limits or considering the symmetry of the sine function. Choice A neglects these steps.

Problem 7 (GhiKlMno):
Evaluate Ω(x) from 6 to 8 by changing the limits of integration: Ω(x) = cos(x) dx
A) π(cos(8)) - π(cos(6))

C) 2π
D) -2π
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
To change the limits, integrate from 8 to 6 and multiply by -1. Since cosine is an even function, its definite integral over any interval is unchanged when the limits are flipped. Therefore, the answer is -2π.

Explanation:
The correct answer is obtained by recognizing that the integral of an even function is unchanged when the limits are flipped.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to integrate directly from 6 to 8 without flipping the limits or considering the symmetry of the cosine function. Choice A neglects these steps.
