Problem 1 (:1):
Find the surface area of the solid formed by rotating the function f(x) = x^2 from x = 0 to x = 3 around the x-axis.
A) 9π
B) 18π
C) 27π
D) 54π
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The surface area of a solid formed by rotating a function f(x) around the x-axis from x = a to x = b is given by the formula: SA = 2π ∫[a, b] f(x) * sqrt(1 + (f'(x))^2) dx. For f(x) = x^2, f'(x) = 2x. So we have: SA = 2π ∫[0, 3] x^2 * sqrt(1 + (2x)^2) dx. Solving this integral gives us SA = 27π.

Explanation:
The surface area is calculated using the formula for rotation around the x-axis. The correct choice C represents the result of evaluating the integral.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to integrate f(x) directly without considering the square root term (1 + (f'(x))^2). This would lead to an incorrect surface area.

Problem 2 (2):
Find the surface area of the solid formed by rotating the curve y = sqrt(x) from x = 0 to x = 4 around the x-axis.
A) 8π
B) 16π
C) 32π
D) 64π
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The surface area of a solid formed by rotating y = sqrt(x) around the x-axis from x = 0 to x = 4 is given by SA = 2π ∫[0, 4] sqrt(x) * sqrt(1 + (dy/dx)^2) dx. For y = sqrt(x), dy/dx = 1/(2*sqrt(x)). So we have: SA = 2π ∫[0, 4] sqrt(x) * sqrt(1 + (1/(2*sqrt(x)))^2) dx. Simplifying and solving the integral gives us SA = 8π.

Explanation:
The surface area is calculated using the formula for rotation around the x-axis. The correct choice A represents the result of evaluating the integral.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to integrate sqrt(x) directly without considering the square root term (1 + (dy/dx)^2). This would lead to an incorrect surface area.
