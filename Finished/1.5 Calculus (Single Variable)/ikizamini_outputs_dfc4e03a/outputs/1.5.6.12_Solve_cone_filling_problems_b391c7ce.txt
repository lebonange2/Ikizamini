Problem 1 (Asdf):
A cone with a radius of 4 cm and height of 8 cm is being filled with water at a rate of 2 cubic cm per second. How long will it take to fill the cone completely?
A) 16 seconds
B) 32 seconds
C) 64 seconds
D) 96 seconds
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The volume of a cone is given by V = (1/3)πr²h, where r is the radius and h is the height. For this problem, V = (1/3)π(4 cm)²(8 cm) = 64π cubic cm. Since the cone is being filled at a rate of 2 cubic cm per second, we can find the time it takes to fill the cone by dividing the volume by the filling rate: t = V / rate = 64π / 2 = 32π seconds. The closest choice to this value is 64 seconds.

Explanation:
The solution involves calculating the volume of the cone using the given radius and height, then dividing that volume by the filling rate to find the time it takes to fill the cone.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget to multiply the radius and height in the volume formula or to use the wrong conversion factor for cubic centimeters. Another possible mistake is to not convert the π value into a decimal when calculating the actual time.

Problem 2 (Asdf):

A) 45 seconds
B) 90 seconds
C) 135 seconds
D) 180 seconds
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
First, we need to find the radius (r) by dividing the diameter by 2: r = 10 inches / 2 = 5 inches. The volume of a cone is V = (1/3)πr²h, where h is the slant height. To fill the cone up to 75% of its volume, we need to calculate 75% of V and then divide by the filling rate. First, find the volume: V = (1/3)π(5 inches)²(12 inches). Then find 75% of V: 0.75 * V = 0.75 * (1/3)π(5 inches)²(12 inches). Finally, divide by the filling rate to find the time: t = (0.75 * V) / 5 cubic inches per second. The closest choice is 135 seconds.

Explanation:
The solution involves calculating the radius from the diameter, finding the volume of the cone using the radius and slant height, determining 75% of that volume, and then dividing by the filling rate to find the time needed.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to use the wrong formula for the volume (e.g., using the area of a circle instead of the cone's volume formula). Another possible mistake is to forget to calculate 75% of the volume before dividing by the filling rate.
