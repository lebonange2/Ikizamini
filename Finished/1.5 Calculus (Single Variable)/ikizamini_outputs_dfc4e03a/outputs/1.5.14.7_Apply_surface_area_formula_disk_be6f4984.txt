Problem 1 (Incremental difficulty 1):
Find the surface area of a disk with radius 5 cm.
A) 25π cm²
B) 75π cm²
C) 100π cm²
D) 125π cm²
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The surface area of a disk is given by the formula A = πr², where r is the radius. For a disk with radius 5 cm, we calculate: A = π(5 cm)² = 25π cm². However, since we're looking for the surface area in square centimeters, we multiply by π: 25π × π = 100π cm².

Explanation:
The correct answer is obtained by correctly applying the surface area formula and multiplying by π.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget to multiply by π after squaring the radius.

Problem 2 (Incremental difficulty 2):
Find the surface area of a disk with diameter 10 inches.
A) 78.5π in²
B) 157π in²
C) 314π in²
D) 628π in²
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Given the diameter is 10 inches, the radius is half of that: r = 5 inches. Using the surface area formula A = πr², we calculate: A = π(5 in)² = 25π in². Multiplying by π gives us: 25π × π = 78.5π in². However, since we're looking for the surface area in square inches, we multiply by π: 78.5π in².

Explanation:
The correct answer is obtained by correctly calculating the radius from the diameter and applying the surface area formula.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to use the diameter instead of the radius in the formula.

Problem 3 (Incremental difficulty 3):
Find the surface area of a solid disk with radius 7 cm and thickness 2 cm.
A) 154π cm²
B) 196π cm²
C) 286π cm²
D) 308π cm²
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The surface area of a solid disk (cylinder) includes the top and bottom circular surfaces, each with radius 7 cm. The total surface area is A = 2πrh + 2πr², where r is the radius and h is the height (thickness). Given r = 7 cm and h = 2 cm, we substitute into the formula: A = 2π(7 cm)(2 cm) + 2π(7 cm)² = 28π cm² + 98π cm² = 126π cm². However, since we're looking for the surface area in square centimeters, we multiply by π: 126π × π = 154π cm².

Explanation:
The correct answer is obtained by correctly applying the surface area formula for a solid disk (cylinder) and multiplying by π.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget that the thickness contributes to the lateral surface area.

Problem 4 (Incremental difficulty 4):
Find the surface area of a disk with radius 3 meters and a hole with radius 1 meter cut out from the center. Assume both parts are separate.
A) 28π m²
B) 36π m²
C) 45π m²
D) 54π m²
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The surface area of the outer disk is A1 = π(3 m)² = 9π m². The surface area of the inner hole is A2 = π(1 m)² = π m². To find the total surface area, we subtract the area of the hole from the area of the outer disk: A_total = A1 - A2 = 9π m² - π m² = 8π m². However, since we're looking for the surface area in square meters, we multiply by π: 8π × π = 28π m².

Explanation:
The correct answer is obtained by correctly calculating the surface areas of both parts and subtracting the hole's area from the outer disk's area.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to add the areas instead of subtracting.

Problem 6 (Incremental difficulty 6):
Find the surface area of a disk with radius 9 cm and thickness 1.5 cm, assuming it is hollow.
A) 282.75π cm²
B) 303.75π cm²
C) 324.75π cm²
D) 345.75π cm²
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The surface area of a hollow disk (cylinder) includes the top and bottom circular surfaces, each with radius 9 cm, and the lateral surface area. The total surface area is A = 2πrh + 2πr², where r is the radius and h is the height (thickness). Given r = 9 cm and h = 1.5 cm, we substitute into the formula: A = 2π(9 cm)(1.5 cm) + 2π(9 cm)² = 27π cm² + 162π cm² = 189π cm². However, since we're looking for the surface area in square centimeters, we multiply by π: 189π × π = 282.75π cm².

Explanation:
The correct answer is obtained by correctly applying the surface area formula for a hollow disk (cylinder) and multiplying by π.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget that the thickness contributes to the lateral surface area.

Problem 7 (Incremental difficulty 7):
Find the surface area of a disk with radius 6 inches and a concentric hole with radius 3 inches, assuming both parts are separate and hollow.
A) 198π in²
B) 204π in²
C) 210π in²
D) 216π in²
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The surface area of the outer hollow disk is A1 = 2π(6 in)(1.5 in) + 2π(6 in)² = 18π in² + 72π in² = 90π in². The surface area of the inner hollow hole is A2 = 2π(3 in)(1.5 in) + 2π(3 in)² = 9π in² + 18π in² = 27π in². To find the total surface area, we add the areas of both parts: A_total = A1 + A2 = 90π in² + 27π in² = 117π in². However, since we're looking for the surface area in square inches, we multiply by π: 117π × π = 198π in².

Explanation:
The correct answer is obtained by correctly calculating the surface areas of both parts and adding them together.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to subtract the area of the hole from the outer disk's area instead of adding.
