Problem 1 (InfiniteGP):
Determine the convergence of the infinite geometric series: sum_{n=1}^{∞} (-3)^{n-1}.
A) Converges with sum = -2
B) Converges with sum = 1/4
C) Converges with sum = 0
D) Diverges
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The ratio of consecutive terms is (-3)^{n-1} / (-3)^(n-2) = -3. Since |{-3}| = 3 > 1, the geometric series diverges by the p-test.

Explanation:
The p-test states that a geometric series converges if and only if the absolute value of the common ratio is less than 1 (|r| < 1).

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to assume that because the first term is -2, the sum must converge to -2. However, this is not how geometric series work; the convergence depends on the ratio of consecutive terms.

Problem 2 (FiniteGP):
Find the sum of the finite geometric series: 1 + 0.5 + 0.25 + ... + 0.5^6.
A) 3.90625
B) 4.0
C) 4.125
D) 4.25
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The series has 7 terms (including the first term), so it's a finite geometric series with a common ratio of 0.5. The sum S_7 = a * (1 - r^7) / (1 - r), where a is the first term and r is the common ratio. Plugging in a = 1, r = 0.5, and n = 7 gives S_7 = 1 * (1 - 0.5^7) / (1 - 0.5) = 3.90625.

Explanation:
The formula for the sum of a finite geometric series is S_n = a * (1 - r^n) / (1 - r), where a is the first term, r is the common ratio, and n is the number of terms.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to use the infinite sum formula instead of the finite sum formula. Since the series has a finite number of terms, we must use the finite sum formula.

Problem 3 (AlternatingSeries):
Determine if the alternating series sum_{n=1}^{∞} (-1)^{n-1} * (2n + 1) converges or diverges.
A) Converges by p-test
B) Converges by alternating series test
C) Diverges by comparison with harmonic series
D) Diverges by ratio test
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The absolute value of the terms is |(-1)^{n-1} * (2n + 1)| = 2n + 1, which increases without bound as n grows. Thus, it does not converge by the p-test or ratio test. However, since the sequence of absolute values |2n + 1| decreases and approaches 0, the series converges by the alternating series test.

Explanation:
The alternating series test states that if the terms of an alternating series are decreasing in absolute value and approach 0, then the series converges.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to assume that because the terms alternate in sign, they must converge by the p-test. However, the p-test applies only to geometric series, not alternating series.

Problem 4 (HarmonicSeries):
Does the harmonic series sum_{n=1}^{∞} 1/n converge or diverge?
A) Converges by integral test
B) Converges by p-test
C) Diverges by comparison with a geometric series
D) Diverges by p-series test
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The harmonic series diverges by comparing it to the integral of 1/x, which is unbounded as x approaches infinity. Alternatively, one can compare it to the geometric series with ratio 1/2, since 1/n > 1/(2^(n-1)) for n >= 2, and a geometric series with ratio greater than or equal to 1/2 diverges.

Explanation:
The harmonic series is an example of a p-series with p = 1. By the p-series test, a p-series converges if p > 1 and diverges if p ≤ 1.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to think that because the terms of the harmonic series decrease, it must converge. However, this is not true; the rate at which the terms decrease matters.

Problem 5 (TaylorSeries):
Determine if the Taylor series for e^x centered at x=0 converges or diverges at x=2.
A) Converges everywhere
B) Converges only at x=0
C) Converges to e^2
D) Converges conditionally
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The Taylor series for e^x centered at x=0 converges for all real numbers x, including x=2. This is because the Maclaurin series (Taylor series at x=0) for e^x converges uniformly on any bounded interval, including [-2, 2].

Explanation:
The Taylor series for a function f(x) centered at a point a converges to f(x) within some radius of convergence. For the exponential function, the radius of convergence is infinite.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to think that the Taylor series might diverge outside of the interval [-1, 1], but this is not true for e^x.

Problem 6 (PowerSeries):
Find the interval of convergence for the power series sum_{n=0}^{∞} (2^n * x^(3n)).
A) (-1/2, 1/2)
B) (0, ∞)
C) (-∞, ∞)
D) [-1/2, 1/2]
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
The radius of convergence R is given by R = 1 / limsup |a_n^(1/3)|, where a_n is the coefficient of x^n. For this series, a_n = 2^n, so R = 1 / (limsup |2|^(n/3)) = 1/2. The interval of convergence is thus [-R, R] = [-1/2, 1/2].

Explanation:
The radius of convergence for a power series can be found using the ratio test or root test on the coefficients.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to calculate the limit of |a_n| instead of |a_n^(1/3)|, which would lead to an incorrect interval of convergence.

Problem 7 (ImproperIntegral):
Determine if the improper integral ∫_{1}^{∞} (x^2 + 3) dx converges or diverges.
A) Converges by comparison with p-series
B) Converges by direct comparison test
C) Diverges by comparison with an unbounded function
D) Diverges by limit comparison test
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The integral ∫_{1}^{∞} (x^2 + 3) dx diverges because the integral of x^2 alone diverges as it is a p-series with p = 2, which is less than 1. Since x^2 + 3 > x^2 for all x >= 1, we can directly compare the given function to x^2 and conclude that the integral diverges.

Explanation:
The direct comparison test states that if 0 ≤ f(x) ≤ g(x) for all x in an interval and ∫_{a}^{∞} g(x) dx converges, then ∫_{a}^{∞} f(x) dx also converges. If ∫_{a}^{∞} g(x) dx diverges, then ∫_{a}^{∞} f(x) dx also diverges.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to try using the limit comparison test or comparing with a p-series directly, but these methods are not applicable here since we have a sum of functions.
