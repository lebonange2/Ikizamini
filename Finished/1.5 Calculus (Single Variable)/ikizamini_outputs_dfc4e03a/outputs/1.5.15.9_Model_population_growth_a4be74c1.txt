Problem 1 (Asdf):
A certain species of bacteria has a doubling time of 2 hours. If the initial population is 500, how many bacteria will there be after 8 hours?
A) 1000
B) 2000
C) 4000
D) 8000
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The population doubles every 2 hours, so after 8 hours it will have doubled 4 times. Starting with 500 bacteria and doubling 4 times gives: 500 × 2^4 = 500 × 16 = 8000 bacteria.

Explanation:
The question tests the understanding of exponential growth using a doubling time formula. To find the population after a certain number of doubling periods, you multiply the initial population by 2 raised to the power of that number of periods.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to multiply the initial population by the total number of hours (8) instead of considering the number of doubling periods (4).

Problem 2 (Bghj):
The rabbit population in a forest increases at a rate proportional to its size. If there are initially 100 rabbits and after 6 months, there are 200 rabbits, what will be the population after 1 year?
A) 300
B) 400
C) 500
D) 600
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
This is an example of exponential growth with a constant rate. We can use the formula P = P_0 × e^(rt), where P is the final population, P_0 is the initial population, r is the growth rate, and t is time in hours (converted to match the rate). Since the rate is proportional to size, we don't have a specific number for r, but we can use the information from the first 6 months to find it. After 6 months (180 days), the population doubles, so r = ln(2) / 180. For 1 year (365 days), P = 100 × e^(ln(2) × 365 / 180) × 2 × 2 ≈ 400 rabbits.

Explanation:
The question tests understanding of exponential growth with a proportional rate. The solution uses the natural logarithm to find the rate and then applies it to predict future population size.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to assume linear growth, where the population increases by a fixed number each month, rather than exponentially.

Problem 3 (Qwer):
The human population of a city grows at a rate of 2% per year. If the current population is 50,000, what will be the population after 10 years?
A) 60,000
B) 70,000
C) 80,000
D) 90,000
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
To find the population after 10 years with a constant growth rate, we can use the formula P = P_0 × (1 + r)^t, where P is the final population, P_0 is the initial population, r is the growth rate as a decimal, and t is time in years. For this problem, P = 50,000 × (1 + 0.02)^10 ≈ 80,000 people.

Explanation:
The question tests understanding of exponential growth with a fixed annual rate. The solution uses the compound interest formula to calculate future population size.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to multiply the initial population by the growth rate (2%) directly, instead of using the exponentiation with (1 + r).

Problem 4 (Yuiop):
A fish farm starts with 500 fish and adds 100 new fish every month. How many fish will there be after 1 year?
A) 600
B) 1200
C) 1800
D) 2400
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
This is an example of arithmetic growth, where the population increases by a fixed number each month. After 1 year (12 months), there will be 500 + 100 × 12 = 1800 fish.

Explanation:
The question tests understanding of arithmetic growth with a constant monthly increase. The solution adds the fixed monthly increase to the initial population for each month.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to assume exponential growth, where the number added each month increases, rather than staying constant.
