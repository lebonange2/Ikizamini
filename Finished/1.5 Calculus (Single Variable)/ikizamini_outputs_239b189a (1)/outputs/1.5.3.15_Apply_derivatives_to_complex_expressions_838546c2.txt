Problem 1 (Asdf):
Find the derivative of f(x) = x^2 * e^x at x=0.
A) 0
B) 1
C) e^0
D) 2 * e^0
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
f'(x) = (x^2)' * e^x + x^2 * (e^x)', using the product rule. f'(x) = 2x * e^x + x^2 * e^x. At x=0, f'(0) = 2*0 * e^0 + 0^2 * e^0 = 0. Therefore, the correct answer is D: 2 * e^0.

Explanation:
The derivative of e^x is itself, and the derivative of x^n is nx^(n-1). Using the product rule, we find the derivative of f(x) and evaluate it at x=0.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget to use the product rule or to incorrectly apply it. Some students might also forget to substitute x=0 into the derivative.

Problem 2 (Bvbn):
Find the derivative of g(x) = sin(x) * cos(x).
A) cos^2(x)
B) sin^2(x)
C) sin(x) * cos(x)
D) sin(2x)
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
g'(x) = (sin(x))' * cos(x) + sin(x) * (cos(x))', using the product rule. g'(x) = cos(x) * cos(x) - sin(x) * sin(x). This simplifies to g'(x) = cos^2(x) - sin^2(x), which is equal to cos(2x) by the double angle identity. Therefore, the correct answer is D: sin(2x).

Explanation:
The derivative of sin(x) is cos(x) and the derivative of cos(x) is -sin(x). Using the product rule, we find the derivative of g(x) and simplify it using trigonometric identities.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget to use the product rule or to incorrectly apply it. Some students might also struggle with recognizing the double angle identity.

Problem 3 (Cvbn):
Find the derivative of h(x) = (x^3 + 2)^4.
A) 12 * x^2 * (x^3 + 2)^3
B) 4 * x^3 * (x^3 + 2)^3
C) 4 * (x^3 + 2)^3
D) 16 * x^3
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
h'(x) = 4 * (x^3 + 2)^3 * (x^3 + 2)', using the chain rule. h'(x) = 4 * (x^3 + 2)^3 * 3x^2 = 12 * x^2 * (x^3 + 2)^3. Therefore, the correct answer is A: 12 * x^2 * (x^3 + 2)^3.

Explanation:
The derivative of u^n is n*u^(n-1) * u'. Here, u = x^3 + 2 and u' = 3x^2. Using the chain rule, we find the derivative of h(x).

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget to use the chain rule or to incorrectly apply it. Some students might also struggle with raising a polynomial to a power.

Problem 4 (Dvbn):
Find the derivative of j(x) = e^(2x) * ln(x).
A) e^(2x) * (1/x)
B) 2 * e^(2x) * ln(x)
C) e^(2x) * (1 + 2ln(x))
D) e^(2x) / x
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
j'(x) = (e^(2x))' * ln(x) + e^(2x) * (ln(x))', using the product rule. j'(x) = 2e^(2x) * ln(x) + e^(2x) * (1/x). This simplifies to j'(x) = e^(2x) * (2ln(x) + 1/x). Therefore, the correct answer is C: e^(2x) * (1 + 2ln(x)).

Explanation:
The derivative of e^u is e^u * u', and the derivative of ln(u) is 1/u. Using the product rule, we find the derivative of j(x) and simplify it.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget to use the chain rule or to incorrectly apply it when differentiating e^(2x). Some students might also struggle with combining the derivatives.

Problem 5 (Evbn):
Find the derivative of k(x) = x^3 * sin(x).
A) 3x^2 * cos(x)
B) 3x^2 * sin(x) + x^3 * cos(x)
C) x^2 * (3sin(x) - 2cos(x))
D) x^2 * (3cos(x) + 2sin(x))
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
k'(x) = (x^3)' * sin(x) + x^3 * (sin(x))', using the product rule. k'(x) = 3x^2 * sin(x) + x^3 * cos(x). Therefore, the correct answer is B: 3x^2 * sin(x) + x^3 * cos(x).

Explanation:
The derivative of x^n is nx^(n-1), and the derivative of sin(x) is cos(x). Using the product rule, we find the derivative of k(x).

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget to use the product rule or to incorrectly apply it. Some students might also struggle with distributing the derivatives.

Problem 6 (Fvbn):
Find the derivative of m(x) = (2x + 1)^5 * (3x - 2)^4.
A) 10(2x+1)^4(3x-2)^3 * (6x-1)
B) 5(2x+1)^4(3x-2)^3 * (6x-1)
C) 5(2x+1)^4(3x-2)^3 * (18x-2)
D) 10(2x+1)^3(3x-2)^4
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
m'(x) = 5(2x+1)^4 * (3x-2)^4 * (2) + (2x+1)^5 * 4(3x-2)^3 * (3), using the product rule. m'(x) = 10(2x+1)^4(3x-2)^3 * (6x-1). Therefore, the correct answer is A: 10(2x+1)^4(3x-2)^3 * (6x-1).

Explanation:
The derivative of u^n is n*u^(n-1) * u', and the derivative of v^m is m*v^(m-1) * v'. Here, u = 2x + 1, u' = 2, v = 3x - 2, and v' = 3. Using the product rule, we find the derivative of m(x).

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget to use the chain rule or to incorrectly apply it when differentiating (2x+1)^5 and (3x-2)^4. Some students might also struggle with multiplying the derivatives.

Problem 7 (Gvbn):
Find the derivative of n(x) = x^2 * ln(3x).
A) 2x * ln(3x) + x^2 / 3
B) 2x * ln(3x) - x^2 / 3
C) x * (2ln(3x) + 1/3)
D) x * (2ln(3x) - 1/3)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
n'(x) = (x^2)' * ln(3x) + x^2 * (ln(3x))', using the product rule. n'(x) = 2x * ln(3x) + x^2 * (1/(3x) * 3). This simplifies to n'(x) = 2x * ln(3x) + x * (1/3), or n'(x) = 2x * ln(3x) + x^2 / 3. Therefore, the correct answer is A: 2x * ln(3x) + x^2 / 3.

Explanation:
The derivative of x^n is nx^(n-1), and the derivative of ln(u) is 1/u * u'. Here, u = 3x. Using the product rule, we find the derivative of n(x) and simplify it.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget to use the chain rule or to incorrectly apply it when differentiating ln(3x). Some students might also struggle with distributing the derivatives.
