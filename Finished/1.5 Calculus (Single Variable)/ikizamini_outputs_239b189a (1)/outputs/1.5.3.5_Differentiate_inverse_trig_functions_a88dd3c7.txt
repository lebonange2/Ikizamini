Problem 1 (Inverse_Trig_Diff_1):
Find the derivative of y = arctan(x):
A) 1 / (x^2 + 1)
B) -1 / (x^2 + 1)
C) 1 / x
D) -1 / x
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The derivative of arctan(x) is dy/dx = 1 / (x^2 + 1). This comes from the differentiation rule for inverse trigonometric functions.

Explanation:
The derivative of arctan(x) can be found using the chain rule and the fact that the derivative of tan^(-1)(u) with respect to u is 1/(u^2 + 1). In this case, u = x.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to choose B, which is the derivative of arctan(x) if it were -x instead of x. Remember that the denominator should always be (x^2 + 1) for arctan(x).

Explanation:
The derivative of arcsin(x^2) can be found using the chain rule and the fact that the derivative of sin^(-1)(u) with respect to u is 1 / √(1 - u^2). In this case, u = x^2.

Common mistake note:
