Problem 1 (Asd123):
A communication satellite is placed in a geostationary orbit around Earth. What is the period of this satellite's orbit, in hours?
A) 6
B) 12
C) 24
D) 48
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The period of a geostationary satellite's orbit is 24 hours because it must match the Earth's rotation to maintain a fixed position above the equator.

Explanation:
Geostationary satellites have an orbital period equal to Earth's rotational period, which is 24 hours. This allows them to stay in the same spot relative to a specific point on Earth's surface.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be choosing option A (6) or B (12), which are half and one-third of the correct value respectively. These durations do not match the Earth's rotation, so the satellite would appear to move in the sky from an observer's perspective.

Problem 2 (Xyz456):
A low-Earth orbiting satellite completes one orbit around Earth in 90 minutes. What is its orbital period, in hours?
A) 1.5
B) 2
C) 3
D) 4
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The orbital period of the satellite is 1.5 hours because 90 minutes is equal to 1.5 hours.

Explanation:
Orbital period is the time it takes for a satellite to complete one full orbit around its host body. In this case, the satellite completes an orbit in 90 minutes, which is equivalent to 1.5 hours.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be choosing option C (3) or D (4), which are multiples of the correct value but do not match the actual time given for one orbit.

Problem 3 (Qwe789):
Which type of satellite orbit is typically used for weather forecasting and has an altitude of about 500-2,000 km above Earth's surface?
A) Geostationary
B) Polar
C) Molniya
D) Low-Earth
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
Low-Earth orbit (LEO) satellites are typically used for weather forecasting and have an altitude range of 500-2,000 km.

Explanation:
Weather forecasting satellites usually operate in low-Earth orbits to provide frequent updates on Earth's surface conditions. Geostationary satellites are too high for this purpose, while polar and Molniya orbits serve different purposes like global coverage or polar regions monitoring.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be choosing option A (Geostationary), which is typically used for communication purposes and has a much higher altitude.

Problem 4 (Rty123):
What is the advantage of using a highly elliptical orbit (HEO) for satellite communications?
A) Reduced interference with other satellites
B) Increased coverage over polar regions
C) Lower fuel consumption for maintaining orbit
D) Greater data transmission speeds
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The advantage of a highly elliptical orbit is increased coverage over polar regions, as the satellite spends more time near the poles.

Explanation:
HEOs are designed to have a large apogee (farthest point from Earth) and perigee (nearest point to Earth), allowing them to cover areas that would otherwise be difficult to reach with traditional orbits. This is particularly useful for monitoring polar regions where other types of orbits might not provide adequate coverage.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be choosing option C (Lower fuel consumption) or D (Greater data transmission speeds), which are not primary advantages of HEOs.

Problem 5 (Uij456):
In a medium Earth orbit (MEO), what is the approximate altitude range?
A) 200-800 km
B) 800-2,000 km
C) 2,000-36,000 km
D) 36,000-42,164 km
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The approximate altitude range for a medium Earth orbit is 800-2,000 km.

Explanation:
MEOs are designed to be between low-Earth orbits (LEO) and geostationary orbits. They typically have altitudes between 800 and 2,000 km above the Earth's surface, providing better coverage than LEOs but with lower latency compared to geostationary satellites.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be choosing option C (2,000-36,000 km), which is the range for geostationary orbits.

Problem 6 (Oop789):
What is the primary purpose of an inclined orbit?
A) To maintain a fixed position relative to Earth's surface
B) To provide global coverage without gaps
C) To minimize atmospheric drag and increase satellite lifetime
D) To avoid collisions with other satellites in similar orbits
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
The primary purpose of an inclined orbit is to avoid collisions with other satellites in similar orbits.

Explanation:
An inclined orbit has an angle with respect to the equatorial plane, which helps distribute satellite traffic and reduces the risk of collisions. Geostationary orbits are a special case where all satellites have the same inclination to maintain a fixed position relative to Earth's surface.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be choosing option A (Maintain a fixed position), which is the purpose of geostationary orbits.

Problem 7 (Kol123):
Which type of orbit is used for global navigation satellite systems like GPS?
A) Geostationary
B) Polar
C) Molniya
D) Medium Earth
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
Medium Earth orbits (MEO) are used for global navigation satellite systems like GPS.

Explanation:
GPS satellites operate in MEO to provide near-global coverage with a relatively low latency. Geostationary orbits would not be suitable for this purpose due to their high altitude and limited coverage, while polar and Molniya orbits have specific advantages but are not used for GPS.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be choosing option A (Geostationary), which is typically used for communication purposes.
