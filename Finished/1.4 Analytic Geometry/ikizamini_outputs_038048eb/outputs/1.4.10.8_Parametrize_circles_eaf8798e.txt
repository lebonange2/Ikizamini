Problem 1 (:1):
What is the standard form equation of a circle with center (h, k) and radius r?
A) (x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2
B) (x + h)^2 + (y + k)^2 = r^2
C) x^2 + y^2 = r^2
D) (h - x)^2 + (k - y)^2 = r^2
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The standard form equation of a circle with center (h, k) and radius r is (x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2. This equation represents the set of all points that are equidistant from the center.

Explanation:
The standard form helps to identify the center and radius directly from the equation.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to reverse the signs in the center coordinates, which would result in choice B. This form does not represent a circle with the given center.

Problem 2 (:2):
What are the parametric equations for a circle with radius 3 and center at the origin?
A) x = 3π, y = 3π
B) x = 3θ cos(θ), y = 3θ sin(θ)
C) x = 3cos(θ), y = 3sin(θ)
D) x = 3sin(θ), y = 3cos(θ)
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The parametric equations for a circle with radius r and center at the origin are x = r cos(θ) and y = r sin(θ). For a circle with radius 3, these equations become x = 3cos(θ) and y = 3sin(θ).

Explanation:
Parametric equations describe the coordinates of points on a curve as functions of a single variable (θ). In this case, θ ranges from 0 to 2π to cover the entire circle.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to use θ instead of cos(θ) and sin(θ), which would result in choice A. This form does not represent a circle.

Problem 3 (:3):
What are the parametric equations for a circle with center (2, 1) and radius 4?
A) x = 2 + 4cos(θ), y = 1 + 4sin(θ)
B) x = 2 - 4cos(θ), y = 1 - 4sin(θ)
C) x = 4cos(θ) + 2, y = 4sin(θ) + 1
D) x = 4cos(θ) - 2, y = 4sin(θ) - 1
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The parametric equations for a circle with center (h, k) and radius r are x = h + r cos(θ) and y = k + r sin(θ). For a circle with center (2, 1) and radius 4, these equations become x = 2 + 4cos(θ) and y = 1 + 4sin(θ).

Explanation:
The parametric equations for a shifted circle include the coordinates of the center in the equation.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to reverse the signs in the center coordinates, which would result in choice B. This form does not represent a circle with the given center.

Problem 4 (:4):
What is the value of θ when the parametric point (x, y) = (5, 3) on a circle with radius 6 and center at the origin corresponds to the quarter where cos(θ) > 0 and sin(θ) > 0?
A) π/4
B) π/2
C) 3π/4
D) π
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The point (5, 3) lies in the first quadrant where both cos(θ) and sin(θ) are positive. The angle π/4 corresponds to this quadrant because cos(π/4) = √2/2 and sin(π/4) = √2/2.

Explanation:
The first quadrant is where both cosine and sine are positive. The angle that corresponds to this quadrant is π/4.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to choose B, which represents the second quadrant where cos(θ) < 0 and sin(θ) > 0.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 5 (:5):
What is the value of r when the parametric equations for a circle are x = 4cos(θ) - 3 and y = 4sin(θ) + 2?
A) 1
B) 2
C) 3
D) 4
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
The parametric equations for a circle with center (h, k) and radius r are x = h + r cos(θ) and y = k + r sin(θ). Comparing the given equations to this form, we see that r is equal to 4.

Explanation:
The coefficient of cos(θ) and sin(θ) in the parametric equations represents the radius of the circle.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to choose C or B, which would be the distance between the origin and the center (3 or 2), not the actual radius.

Problem 6 (:6):
What is the value of θ when the parametric point (x, y) = (-5, -3) on a circle with center at (1, 2) and radius 6 corresponds to the quarter where cos(θ) < 0 and sin(θ) < 0?
A) π
B) 3π/2
C) 5π/4
D) 7π/4
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The point (-5, -3) lies in the third quadrant where both cos(θ) and sin(θ) are negative. The angle 3π/2 corresponds to this quadrant because cos(3π/2) = -√2/2 and sin(3π/2) = -√2/2.

Explanation:
The third quadrant is where both cosine and sine are negative. The angle that corresponds to this quadrant is 3π/2.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to choose A, which represents the fourth quadrant where cos(θ) > 0 and sin(θ) < 0.
