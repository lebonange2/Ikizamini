Problem 1 (:1):
Sketch the ellipse defined by the equation: x^2/9 + y^2/4 = 1
A) [x, y) in (-3, -2)
B) [x, y) in (-∞, -3) U (∞, ∞)
C) [x, y) in (-√5, -√3) U (√3, √5)
D) [x, y) in (-3, ∞)
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The equation of an ellipse is given by (x^2/a^2) + (y^2/b^2) = 1, where a and b are the lengths of the semi-major and semi-minor axes respectively. For x^2/9 + y^2/4 = 1, a = 3 and b = 2.

The ellipse will be centered at the origin (0, 0), with the x-axis stretching from -a to a and the y-axis stretching from -b to b. Therefore, the correct interval for x is (-√9, √9) or (-3, 3) and for y it's (-√4, √4) or (-2, 2).

The correct choice is C: [x, y) in (-√5, -√3) U (√3, √5), which represents the interval for x where the ellipse is defined.

Explanation:
This question tests understanding of the equation of an ellipse and how to determine the intervals for x and y based on the values of a and b.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be selecting choice A, as it only includes the interval for y but not for x. Choice B is incorrect because it represents the entire real number line for x, which is too broad. Choice D omits the lower bound for x.

The correct choice is C: [x, y) in (-√5, -1) U (1, √5), which represents the interval for x where the ellipse is defined after scaling.

Explanation:
This question tests understanding of how to transform an equation to standard form and how to adjust the intervals based on scaling factors.
