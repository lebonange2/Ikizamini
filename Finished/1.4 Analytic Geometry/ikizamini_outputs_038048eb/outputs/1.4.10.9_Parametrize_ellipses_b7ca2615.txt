Problem 1 (:Q1):
What is the standard form of an ellipse with center (h, k), major axis length 2a, and minor axis length 2b?
A) (x - h)^2/a^2 + (y - k)^2/b^2 = 1
B) (x - h)^2/b^2 + (y - k)^2/a^2 = 1
C) (x/h)^2 + (y/k)^2 = 1
D) a(x - h)^2 + b(y - k)^2 = 1
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The standard form of an ellipse with center (h, k), major axis length 2a, and minor axis length 2b is

(x - h)^2/a^2 + (y - k)^2/b^2 = 1

This equation describes the set of points whose distances from the two foci sum up to a constant value (2a).

Explanation:
The correct form includes the squares of the distances from the center to the x and y coordinates divided by the squares of the semi-major and semi-minor axes, respectively. The other choices are either incorrectly ordered or use incorrect variables.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing B because it reverses the order of a^2 and b^2. This would result in an ellipse with swapped major and minor axes.

Problem 2 (:Q2):
An ellipse has vertices at (3, 4) and (-3, 4). What is the equation of the ellipse in standard form?
A) (x - 0)^2/9 + (y - 4)^2/16 = 1
B) (x - 0)^2/16 + (y - 4)^2/9 = 1
C) (x - 3)^2/9 + (y - 4)^2/16 = 1
D) (x - 3)^2/16 + (y - 4)^2/9 = 1
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The vertices of the ellipse are at (3, 4) and (-3, 4), which means the center is at (0, 4) since the x-coordinate is the average of the two vertex x-coordinates. The distance between the center and a vertex is the semi-major axis length, which is 3. Since the y-coordinate remains constant, the semi-minor axis length is equal to the difference between the y-coordinate of the center and one of the vertices, which is 4 - 4 = 0. However, since we cannot have a zero radius, it means that the minor axis is along the x-axis, not the y-axis, so the equation must be flipped. The correct standard form is thus:

(x - 0)^2/9 + (y - 4)^2/16 = 1

Explanation:
The vertices determine the center and semi-major axis length. Since the ellipse is vertical, the minor axis is horizontal, which means we use the x^2 term with the smaller denominator.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing B because it assumes the major axis is along the y-axis. However, the given vertices indicate a vertical ellipse.

Problem 3 (:Q3):
An ellipse has foci at (1, 2) and (5, 2) and passes through the point (3, 4). What is the equation of the ellipse in standard form?
A) (x - 3)^2/9 + (y - 2)^2/4 = 1
B) (x - 3)^2/4 + (y - 2)^2/9 = 1
C) (x - 2)^2/9 + (y - 3)^2/4 = 1
D) (x - 2)^2/4 + (y - 3)^2/9 = 1
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The foci of the ellipse are at (1, 2) and (5, 2), which means the center is at the midpoint between them: (3, 2). The distance between a focus and the center is the focal length, which is 5 - 3 = 2. This is also half the major axis length (a), so a = 4. Since the foci are on the x-axis, the minor axis will be along the y-axis with length b^2 = a^2 - c^2, where c is the focal length. Thus, b^2 = 16 - 4 = 12 and b = 2√3. The standard form of the ellipse is:

(x - 3)^2/4 + (y - 2)^2/(2√3)^2 = 1

Explanation:
The foci determine the center and focal length, which leads to the semi-major axis length. Since the foci are on the x-axis, the minor axis is along the y-axis.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing A because it assumes the major axis is along the y-axis. However, the foci indicate a horizontal ellipse.

Problem 4 (:Q4):
A circle with radius 5 has its center at (3, -2). What is the equation of the circle in standard form?
A) (x - 3)^2 + (y + 2)^2 = 25
B) (x + 3)^2 + (y - 2)^2 = 25
C) (x - 3)^2 + (y - 2)^2 = 10
D) (x + 3)^2 + (y + 2)^2 = 10
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The equation of a circle with center (h, k) and radius r is given by:

(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2
Given the center at (3, -2) and radius 5, the standard form of the circle's equation is:

(x - 3)^2 + (y + 2)^2 = 25

Explanation:
A circle is a special case of an ellipse with equal semi-major and semi-minor axes. The center and radius determine the equation.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing B because it reverses the signs on the x and y terms. This would result in a circle centered at (-3, 2), not (3, -2).

Problem 5 (:Q5):
An ellipse has vertices at (4, 0) and (-4, 0) and passes through the point (0, 3). What is the equation of the ellipse in standard form?
A) (x - 0)^2/16 + (y - 0)^2/9 = 1
B) (x - 0)^2/9 + (y - 0)^2/16 = 1
C) (x - 4)^2/16 + (y - 0)^2/9 = 1
D) (x - 4)^2/9 + (y - 0)^2/16 = 1
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The vertices of the ellipse are at (4, 0) and (-4, 0), which means the center is at (0, 0). The distance between the center and a vertex is the semi-major axis length, which is 4. Since the x-coordinate remains constant, the minor axis is along the y-axis with length b^2 = a^2 - c^2, where c is the focal length. However, since the ellipse has no foci (it's a circle), c = 0 and b^2 = a^2 = 16. The standard form of the ellipse is:

(x - 4)^2/16 + (y - 0)^2/16 = 1

Explanation:
The vertices determine the center and semi-major axis length. Since there are no foci, it's a circle with equal major and minor axes.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing B because it assumes the major axis is along the y-axis. However, the given vertices indicate a horizontal ellipse.
