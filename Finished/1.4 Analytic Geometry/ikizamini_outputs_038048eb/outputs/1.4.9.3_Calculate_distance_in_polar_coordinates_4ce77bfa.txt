Problem 1 (:1):
Find the distance between the points (2, π/3) and (-1, -π/6) in polar coordinates.
A) sqrt(5)
B) sqrt(7)
C) sqrt(3)
D) sqrt(2)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The distance between two points in polar coordinates can be found using the formula: d = sqrt(r1^2 + r2^2 - 2r1r2cos(theta_diff)), where (r1, theta1) and (r2, theta2) are the polar coordinates of the two points, and theta_diff is the difference between their angles. For the given points (2, π/3) and (-1, -π/6), we have r1 = 2, theta1 = π/3, r2 = 1 (since |-1| = 1), and theta2 = -π/6. The angle difference is |theta1 - theta2| = |π/3 - (-π/6)| = π/2. Plugging these values into the formula: d = sqrt(2^2 + 1^2 - 2*2*1*cos(π/2)) = sqrt(4 + 1 - 0) = sqrt(5). The correct answer is A.

Explanation:
The correct answer is found by applying the distance formula in polar coordinates. A common mistake would be to forget to take the square root of the final result after using the distance formula.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Not taking the square root of the result from the distance formula.

Problem 2 (:2):
Calculate the distance between the points (4, 5π/6) and (-3, -π/3) in polar coordinates.
A) sqrt(18)
B) sqrt(27)
C) sqrt(30)
D) sqrt(39)
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
Using the distance formula: d = sqrt(r1^2 + r2^2 - 2r1r2cos(theta_diff)), where (r1, theta1) and (r2, theta2) are the polar coordinates of the two points, and theta_diff is the difference between their angles. For the given points (4, 5π/6) and (-3, -π/3), we have r1 = 4, theta1 = 5π/6, r2 = 3 (since |-3| = 3), and theta2 = -π/3. The angle difference is |theta1 - theta2| = |5π/6 - (-π/3)| = 7π/6. Plugging these values into the formula: d = sqrt(4^2 + 3^2 - 2*4*3*cos(7π/6)) = sqrt(16 + 9 - 24*(-1/2)) = sqrt(25 + 12) = sqrt(37). The correct answer is C.

Explanation:
The correct answer is found by applying the distance formula in polar coordinates. A common mistake would be to misapply the cosine function or incorrectly calculate the angle difference.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Incorrectly calculating the angle difference or misapplying the cosine function.

Problem 3 (:3):
Find the distance between the origin (0, 0) and the point (3, π/2) in polar coordinates.
A) sqrt(9)
B) sqrt(18)
C) sqrt(27)
D) sqrt(36)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The distance from the origin to any point (r, θ) in polar coordinates is simply the radius r. For the given point (3, π/2), the distance is r = 3. The correct answer is A.

Explanation:
The correct answer is found by recognizing that the distance from the origin to any point in polar coordinates is equal to its radius. A common mistake would be to apply the distance formula unnecessarily.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Applying the distance formula when it's not needed for points on the origin.

Problem 4 (:4):
Calculate the distance between the points (1, π/2) and (-1, -π/2) in polar coordinates.
A) sqrt(2)
B) 2
C) sqrt(3)
D) sqrt(5)
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The distance between two points with the same radius but opposite angles (like these, both having r = 1 and an angle difference of π) is simply twice their radius. In this case, the distance is 2r = 2*1 = 2. The correct answer is B.

Explanation:
The correct answer is found by recognizing that when points have equal radii and opposite angles, the distance between them is twice the radius. A common mistake would be to apply the distance formula incorrectly.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Applying the distance formula without considering the symmetry of the points.
