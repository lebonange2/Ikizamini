Problem 1 (:1):
A triangle is rotated 90 degrees clockwise around the origin. What is the angle of rotation in radians?
A) π/2
B) π
C) 3π/2
D) 2π
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The angle of rotation from a 90-degree clockwise rotation in degrees to radians is π/2. In radians, one full revolution is 2π, and a quarter revolution (90 degrees) is π/2.

Explanation:
When converting angles from degrees to radians, remember that 180 degrees equals π radians.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing B (π) which represents a half-revolution (180 degrees). The question specifies a 90-degree rotation.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 2 (2):
A parallelogram is rotated 135 degrees counterclockwise around the origin. What is the angle of rotation in radians?
A) π/4
B) π/2
C) 3π/4
D) π
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
A 135-degree counterclockwise rotation is equivalent to a 45-degree clockwise rotation from the positive x-axis. Since π/4 represents a 45-degree angle, we add it to π (half a revolution) to get the total angle of rotation in radians: π + π/4 = 5π/4, which simplifies to 3π/2.

Explanation:
When rotating counterclockwise, subtract the angle from π. In this case, 180 - 45 = 135 degrees, which is equivalent to 3π/4 radians.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing A (π/4) which represents a 45-degree rotation but not accounting for the direction of rotation.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 3 (3):
A rectangle is flipped vertically about the y-axis. What is the angle of rotation required to achieve this transformation?
A) 0
B) π/2
C) π
D) 3π/2
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
Flipping an object vertically about the y-axis is equivalent to a 180-degree rotation, which in radians is π.

Explanation:
A vertical flip can be thought of as a full rotation around the y-axis. This corresponds to a π radian angle.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing B (π/2) which represents a 90-degree rotation, but not considering that flipping requires a complete revolution.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 4 (4):
A circle is dilated by a scale factor of 2 while maintaining the origin as the center. What angle of rotation would result in the same dilation?
A) 0
B) π/2
C) π
D) 3π/2
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Dilation by a scale factor does not involve any rotation; thus, the angle of rotation is 0.

Explanation:
Dilation only changes the size of an object and does not affect its orientation. Therefore, no rotation is needed to achieve the same dilation.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing any other option, assuming that dilation involves a change in orientation.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 5 (5):
A regular hexagon is reflected across the line y = x. What is the angle of rotation required to achieve this reflection?
A) 0
B) π/2
C) π
D) 3π/2
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Reflecting an object across the line y = x is equivalent to a 90-degree rotation, which in radians is π/2.

Explanation:
A reflection across the line y = x can be thought of as a quarter turn counterclockwise. This corresponds to a π/2 radian angle.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing C (π) which represents a half-revolution, but not considering that reflections are like rotations by 180 degrees about an axis perpendicular to the reflection line.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 6 (6):
A pentagon is translated 5 units right and 3 units up, then reflected across the x-axis. What angle of rotation would result in the same transformation?
A) 0
B) π/2
C) π
D) 3π/2
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Translation and reflection do not involve any rotation; thus, the angle of rotation is 0.

Explanation:
Translation and reflection are independent transformations that do not affect an object's orientation. Therefore, no rotation is needed to achieve the same transformation.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing any other option, assuming that translation or reflection involves a change in orientation.

Problem 7 (7):
A square is rotated around its center such that one of its sides aligns with the positive x-axis. What is the angle of rotation required for this alignment?
A) π/4
B) π/2
C) 3π/4
D) π
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
To align one side of a square with the positive x-axis, we need to rotate it 45 degrees clockwise or counterclockwise. In radians, this is π/4.

Explanation:
Rotating a square so that one of its sides lines up with the x-axis requires a quarter turn. This corresponds to a π/4 radian angle.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing B (π/2) which represents a 90-degree rotation, but not considering that the square can be aligned in either direction.
