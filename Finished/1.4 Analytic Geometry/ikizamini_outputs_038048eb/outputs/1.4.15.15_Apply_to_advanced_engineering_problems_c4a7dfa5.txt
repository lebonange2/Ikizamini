Problem 1 (:1):
A bridge is designed to support a maximum load of 200,000 N. A truck with a weight of 180,000 N approaches the bridge. Is it safe for the truck to cross the bridge?
A) Yes, the bridge can handle the weight.
B) No, the bridge is not designed for this much weight.
C) It depends on other factors.
D) The question does not provide enough information.
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The bridge is designed to support a maximum load of 200,000 N and the truck weighs 180,000 N. Since the truck's weight is less than the bridge's capacity, it is safe for the truck to cross.

Explanation:
This problem tests the application of engineering principles in assessing the safety of structures under load.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to choose B, assuming that any weight above zero would make the bridge unsafe. However, understanding the maximum capacity is crucial for making this determination.

Problem 2 (:2):
An engineer needs to design a heat exchanger for a chemical process operating at 150°C and 10 bar. Which of the following materials would be the most suitable?
A) Copper
B) Stainless steel
C) Titanium
D) Aluminum
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
Titanium is a suitable material for high-temperature and pressure applications due to its corrosion resistance, strength, and thermal stability.

Explanation:
This question assesses the selection of materials based on their properties and application requirements in engineering.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to choose A (Copper) or D (Aluminum), which are not suitable for high-temperature and pressure conditions.

Problem 3 (:3):
A hydraulic system uses a pump with an output of 20 liters per minute at 100 bar. If the system's pipe diameter is reduced by half, what will be the new flow rate?
A) 40 L/minute
B) 20 L/minute
C) 10 L/minute
D) 5 L/minute
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
Using the principle of continuity (A1V1 = A2V2), where A is cross-sectional area and V is velocity, a reduction in pipe diameter by half will increase the velocity fourfold. Since flow rate is directly proportional to velocity, the new flow rate would be 1/4 of the original, or 5 L/minute.

Explanation:
This question tests understanding of fluid dynamics principles in hydraulic systems.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to choose A (40 L/minute), assuming that the flow rate remains constant despite the change in pipe diameter.

Problem 4 (:4):
In a reinforced concrete beam, what is the primary function of steel reinforcement?
A) To increase the beam's compressive strength.
B) To increase the beam's tensile strength.
C) To improve the beam's fire resistance.
D) To reduce the weight of the beam.
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Steel reinforcement in a reinforced concrete beam is primarily used to increase its tensile strength, as concrete is strong in compression but weak in tension.

Explanation:
This question evaluates knowledge of structural engineering and materials science principles.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to choose A (increase compressive strength), which is the natural property of concrete without reinforcement.
