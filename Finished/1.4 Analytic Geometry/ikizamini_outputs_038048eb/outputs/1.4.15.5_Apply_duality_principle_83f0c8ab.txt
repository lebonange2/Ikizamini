Problem 1 (Asd123):
Consider the primal linear program: maximize 2x + 3y subject to x + y ≤ 4, x ≥ 0, y ≥ 0. What is the objective function of its dual?
A) -4z
B) 4z
C) z
D) 2z + 3w
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The dual of a linear program maximizes the constraints' coefficients, multiplied by their corresponding variables, subject to non-negativity. The primal has constraints x + y ≤ 4 and non-negative variables x and y. So, in the dual, z will correspond to the constraint with coefficient -4 (flipped sign due to duality) and w is not present since there's no free variable in the primal. Thus, the dual objective function is maximize 4z.

Explanation:
The duality principle states that the optimal value of a linear program and its dual are equal.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to forget to flip the sign of the constraint coefficients when forming the dual objective.

Problem 2 (Xcv123):
Given the primal LP: minimize 5x + 7y subject to 2x + 3y ≥ 6, x ≤ 4, y ≤ 3. What is the variable associated with constraint x ≤ 4 in its dual?
A) w
B) z
C) u
D) v
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
In the dual of a linear program, each constraint from the primal becomes a variable. Since x is bounded by 4 in the primal, its corresponding variable in the dual will be u.

Explanation:
The duality principle involves mapping variables and constraints between the primal and dual problems.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to confuse the variable associated with y ≤ 3 instead of x ≤ 4.

Problem 3 (Zxc123):
For the primal LP: maximize 3x + 5y subject to 2x + 4y ≤ 10, x ≥ 0, y ≥ 0. What is the non-negativity constraint for the dual variable associated with x?
A) u ≥ 0
B) v ≥ 0
C) w ≥ 0
D) z ≥ 0
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
In the dual of a linear program, each primal variable becomes a non-negativity constraint for its corresponding dual variable. Since x is in the primal, its associated dual variable u will have the constraint u ≥ 0.

Explanation:
The duality principle requires that dual variables are non-negative.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to choose the wrong variable (v or w) due to confusion between x and y.

Problem 4 (Sdf123):
What is the relationship between the optimal values of a primal linear program and its dual?
A) They are equal.
B) The primal's value is always greater than the dual's.
C) The dual's value is always greater than the primal's.
D) They have no specific relationship.
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
According to the duality principle, the optimal values of a linear program and its dual are equal.

Explanation:
Duality ensures that both problems have the same optimal value.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to think that one problem's value can be greater than the other without understanding the fundamental theorem of duality.

Problem 5 (Qwe123):
In a primal LP with an unbounded feasible region, what can be said about its dual?
A) The dual is infeasible.
B) The dual has no optimal solution.
C) The dual is unbounded.
D) The dual has the same optimal value as the primal.
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
If a primal linear program has an unbounded feasible region, its dual must be unbounded. The duality principle does not apply in this case.

Explanation:
Unboundedness in one problem implies unboundedness in the other under duality.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to think that both problems have no optimal solution when only one of them is unbounded.

Problem 6 (Rty123):
Given a primal LP with an infeasible feasible region, what can be said about its dual?
A) The dual is unbounded.
B) The dual has no optimal solution.
C) The dual is infeasible.
D) The dual has the same optimal value as the primal.
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
If a primal linear program is infeasible, its dual has no feasible solution and thus no optimal solution. The duality principle does not apply when both problems are infeasible.

Explanation:
Infeasibility in one problem implies infeasibility or unboundedness in the other under duality.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to confuse infeasibility with unboundedness, leading to selecting the wrong answer.

Problem 7 (Ghi123):
What is an application of the duality principle in linear programming?
A) Finding a feasible solution quickly.
B) Solving large-scale problems more efficiently.
C) Determining if a problem has no optimal solution or is unbounded.
D) Comparing different optimization algorithms.
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The duality principle can be used to determine whether a linear program has no feasible solution, an unbounded feasible region, or if it has an optimal solution. It does not directly help with finding solutions or comparing algorithms.

Explanation:
Duality provides insights into the existence and properties of optimal solutions in linear programming.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to think that duality helps with finding solutions or comparing optimization methods, which it does not.
