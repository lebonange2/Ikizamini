Problem 1 (Intrinsic_Curvature):
A surface has an intrinsic curvature of -1 at a particular point. Which of the following best describes this curvature?
A) The surface is locally like a plane.
B) The surface is locally like a sphere.
C) The surface is locally like a hyperbolic plane.
D) The surface is locally like a cylinder.
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
Intrinsic curvature of -1 indicates that the surface is negatively curved, which means it is locally similar to a hyperbolic plane. A sphere has positive curvature, a plane has zero curvature, and a cylinder does not have intrinsic curvature since it is a flat surface along one direction.

Explanation:
Differential geometry studies properties of surfaces independent of their embedding in higher-dimensional spaces. Intrinsic curvature refers to how much the surface bends or curves at a given point without considering external factors.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing B (sphere) because it is curved, but forgetting that intrinsic curvature distinguishes between positive and negative curvatures.

Problem 2 (Geodesic_Between_Points):
Find the geodesic between two points A(1,0,0) and B(0,2,0) on a sphere of radius 3 in Euclidean space. Which of the following equations best describes this shortest path?
A) x = 3sin(t), y = 3cos(t), z = 0
B) x = 3t, y = 3(1-t), z = 0
C) x = 3cos(t), y = 3sin(t), z = 0
D) x = 3sqrt(t), y = 3sqrt(1-t), z = 0
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The geodesic on a sphere is the shortest path between two points, which corresponds to a great circle. The parametric equation of a great circle on a sphere with radius r passing through points (x1, y1, z1) and (x2, y2, z2) can be expressed as x = r cos(t) * (x1 - x2) + r sin(t) * sqrt(1 - ((x1 - x2)^2 + (y1 - y2)^2) / 4r^2), y = r cos(t) * (y1 - y2) + r sin(t) * sqrt(1 - ((x1 - x2)^2 + (y1 - y2)^2) / 4r^2), z = r cos(t) * (z1 - z2). For A(1,0,0) and B(0,2,0), the equation simplifies to x = 3cos(t), y = 3sin(t), z = 0.

Explanation:
Geodesics in differential geometry are paths that locally minimize distance. On a sphere, they are great circles.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing A or D because of the use of sin(t) and sqrt(t), which might be associated with parametric equations for circles, but forgetting to consider the specific form required for a geodesic on a sphere.

Problem 3 (Parallel_Transport):
When parallel transporting a vector along a curve on a surface, which of the following statements is true?
A) The vector's magnitude and direction both change.
B) The vector's magnitude changes but its direction remains constant.
C) The vector's direction changes but its magnitude remains constant.
D) The vector's magnitude and direction both remain constant.
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
Parallel transport is the process of moving a vector along a curve on a surface while keeping it tangent to the surface at each point. This preserves both the magnitude and direction of the vector.

Explanation:
In differential geometry, parallel transport is used to study how vectors behave as they are moved along curves on curved surfaces without changing their intrinsic properties.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing C or B because of misconceptions about how vectors might change when transported along a curve, forgetting that the key requirement of parallel transport is preserving the vector's intrinsic properties.

Problem 4 (Christoffel_Symbols):
Calculate the Christoffel symbol Γ<sup>1</sup><sub>21</sub> for the 2D metric g = dx^2 + dy^2. Which of the following is the correct value?
A) 0
B) 1/2
C) dy/dx
D) dx/dy
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The Christoffel symbols are defined as Γ<sup>μ</sup><sub>αβ</sub> = (1/2) g<sup>μν</sup>(∂g<sub>να</sub>/∂x<sup>β</sup> + ∂g<sub>νβ</sub>/∂x<sup>α</sup> - ∂g<sub>αβ</sub>/∂x<sup>ν</sup>). For the metric g = dx^2 + dy^2, all partial derivatives of g are zero, so all Christoffel symbols are also zero.

Explanation:
Christoffel symbols are used in differential geometry to describe how the coordinate system behaves on a curved surface and play a crucial role in tensor calculus.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing C or D because of confusion with derivatives, but forgetting that for this specific metric, all partial derivatives are zero.

Problem 5 (Riemann_Curvature_Tensor):
Calculate the Riemann curvature tensor R<sup>μν</sup><sub>αβ</sub> for a 2D Euclidean space with metric g = dx^2 + dy^2. Which of the following is the correct value?
A) 0
B) g<sup>μν</sup>
C) g<sub>αβ</sub>
D) g<sup>μν</sup>g<sub>αβ</sub>
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The Riemann curvature tensor is given by R<sup>μν</sup><sub>αβ</sub> = ∂Γ<sup>μ</sup><sub>αβ</sub>/∂x<sup>ν</sup> - ∂Γ<sup>μ</sup><sub>αν</sub>/∂x<sup>β</sup> + Γ<sup>λ</sup><sub>αβ</sub>Γ<sup>μ</sup><sub>λν</sub> - Γ<sup>λ</sup><sub>αν</sub>Γ<sup>μ</sup><sub>λβ</sub>. In a 2D Euclidean space, all Christoffel symbols are zero, so the Riemann curvature tensor is also identically zero.

Explanation:
The Riemann curvature tensor describes the curvature of a manifold at a given point and how it affects parallel transport. In flat spaces like 2D Euclidean space, the curvature tensor vanishes.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing B or C because of confusion with metric tensors, but forgetting that in a flat space, the Riemann curvature tensor is zero.

Problem 7 (Tensor_Calculus):
Given a 2D tensor field T = (x^2 + y^2)dx^2 - xy dy^2, calculate its divergence ∇·T. Which of the following is the correct value?
A) 0
B) x + y
C) 2x + 2y
D) x^2 + y^2
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The divergence of a tensor field T<sup>μν</sup> is given by ∇·T = ∂T<sup>μν</sup>/∂x<sup>μ</sup>. For T = (x^2 + y^2)dx^2 - xy dy^2, the divergence is ∂(x^2 + y^2)/∂x - ∂(xy)/∂y = 2x + 0 = x + y.

Explanation:
Tensor calculus is an extension of vector calculus to higher-dimensional spaces and curved manifolds. The divergence measures the change in density or flux through a surface element.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing C because of confusion with the gradient operator, but forgetting that for a 2D tensor field, only one partial derivative is involved.
