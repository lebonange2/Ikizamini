Problem 1 (:1):
What is the equation of a parabola with vertex (2, -3) and focus at (2, -5)?
A) (x  - 2)^2 = 4(y  + 3)
B) (y  - 2)^2 = 4(x  + 3)
C) (x  - 2)^2 = 4(y  - 5)
D) (y  - 2)^2 = 4(x  - 5)
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
(x  - h)^2 = 4p(y  - k) is the standard form of a parabola with vertex (h, k). Given vertex (2, -3), we have h = 2 and k = -3. The distance p between the vertex and focus is half the difference between the y-coordinates of the focus (-5) and the vertex (-3): p = |-5 - (-3)| / 2 = |-2| / 2 = 1. So, the equation becomes (x  - 2)^2 = 4(1)(y  - 5), which simplifies to (x  - 2)^2 = 4(y  - 5).

Explanation:
The correct equation uses the vertex form and has the focus at a distance of p units below the vertex.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to use the wrong sign for the y-coordinate in the equation, which would result in choice B. This happens when the focus is above the vertex, but that's not the case here.
