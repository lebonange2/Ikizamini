Problem 1 (:1):
What is the center of the circle described by the equation 
(x - 2)^2 + (y - 3)^2 = 16?
A) (0, 0)
B) (2, 3)
C) (4, 5)
D) (-2, -3)
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The equation of a circle in standard form is (x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2, where (h, k) is the center and r is the radius. Comparing this to the given equation (x - 2)^2 + (y - 3)^2 = 16, we can see that h = 2 and k = 3.

Explanation:
The center of the circle is directly given by the values of h and k in the standard form equation. In this case, h = 2 and k = 3.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to confuse (h, k) with the radius (r). Remember that r^2 is the constant inside the parentheses.

Problem 2 (:2):
Find the center of the circle defined by the equation 
(x + 5)^2 + (y - 7)^2 = 9.
A) (5, 7)
B) (-5, 7)
C) (-5, -7)
D) (5, -7)
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The equation of a circle in standard form is (x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2. Comparing this to the given equation (x + 5)^2 + (y - 7)^2 = 9, we can see that h = -5 and k = 7.

Explanation:
The negative sign in front of the x term indicates that the center's x-coordinate is actually 5 units to the left of where it appears. Thus, the correct center is (-5, 7).

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to think that h and k are the same as the numbers inside the parentheses without considering the sign.
