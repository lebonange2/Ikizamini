Problem 1 (Asdf):
A camera has a lens with a focal length of 50mm and an aperture of f/2.8. What is the approximate focal width (in mm) when taking a photo?
A) 17.9mm
B) 23.4mm
C) 35.7mm
D) 67.0mm
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The formula to calculate the approximate focal width (also known as depth of field) is: focal width ≈ focal length / aperture. Given a focal length of 50mm and an aperture of f/2.8, we can plug these values into the formula: focal width ≈ 50mm / 2.8 ≈ 17.9mm. However, since we're looking for the approximate focal width, we round up to the nearest whole number, which is 35.7mm.

Explanation:
The correct answer is found by applying the formula that relates focal length and aperture to focal width. The other choices are incorrect because they either use the wrong formula or do not round appropriately.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to directly divide the focal length by the f-number without rounding, leading to choice A (17.9mm), which is the exact value rather than the approximate one.

Problem 2 (Bghj):
A photographer sets up a camera with a 35mm lens and an aperture of f/4 for a landscape shot. What is the approximate focal width (in mm) if the subject is 10 meters away?
A) 7.1mm
B) 9.5mm
C) 21.2mm
D) 35.7mm
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
To calculate the approximate focal width for a given distance, we can use the hyperfocal distance formula: hyperfocal distance = (focal length² / aperture²) + focal length. Then, the focal width is approximately equal to the hyperfocal distance - focal length. Given a focal length of 35mm and an aperture of f/4, we calculate the hyperfocal distance first: hyperfocal distance ≈ (35² / 4²) + 35 ≈ 106.75mm. Then, we find the focal width: focal width ≈ 106.75 - 35 ≈ 71.75mm. Rounding to the nearest whole number gives us 9.5mm.

Explanation:
The correct answer is derived from applying the hyperfocal distance formula and then calculating the focal width based on that. The other choices are incorrect because they either use the wrong formula or do not round appropriately.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to directly calculate the focal width without considering the hyperfocal distance, leading to an underestimation of the actual focal width.

Problem 7 (Jklm):
A videographer is using a 28mm lens with an aperture of f/4 for a landscape video. What is the approximate focal width (in mm) if the subject is at infinity?
A) 7.1mm
B) 9.5mm
C) 14.3mm
D) 21.2mm
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
At infinity, the hyperfocal distance is approximately equal to the focal length squared divided by the aperture squared. Given a focal length of 28mm and an aperture of f/4, we calculate the hyperfocal distance: hyperfocal distance ≈ (28² / 4²) ≈ 196mm. The focal width at infinity is approximately equal to half the hyperfocal distance: focal width ≈ 196mm / 2 ≈ 98mm. Rounding to the nearest whole number gives us 7.1mm.

Explanation:
The correct answer is calculated by using the relationship between focal length, aperture, and hyperfocal distance at infinity. The other choices are incorrect because they either use the wrong formula or do not round appropriately.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to assume that the focal width at infinity is equal to the focal length, leading to an overestimation of the actual focal width.
