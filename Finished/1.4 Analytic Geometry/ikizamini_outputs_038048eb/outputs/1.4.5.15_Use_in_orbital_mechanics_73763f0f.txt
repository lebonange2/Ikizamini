Problem 1 (Asd123):
A satellite orbits Earth at a distance of 40,000 km from the center. Calculate its orbital velocity. (Assume Earth's mass = 5.97 × 10^24 kg and G = 6.674 × 10^-11 m^3/kg s^2)
A) 1.8 km/s
B) 3.1 km/s
C) 7.9 km/s
D) 9.8 km/s
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Orbital velocity (v) = √(GM/r), where G is the gravitational constant, M is Earth's mass, and r is the distance from Earth's center. v ≈ 3.1 km/s

Explanation:
The correct answer is found by applying the orbital velocity formula.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to use Earth's surface gravity (9.8 m/s^2) instead of G.

Problem 2 (Xyz456):
Determine the period of an Earth satellite in a circular orbit 7,000 km above the Earth's surface. (Assume Earth's mean radius = 6,371 km and G = 6.674 × 10^-11 m^3/kg s^2)
A) 1 hour
B) 4 hours
C) 8 hours
D) 24 hours
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
Period (T) = 2π√(r^3/GM), where r is the orbit radius, G is the gravitational constant, and M is Earth's mass. T ≈ 8 hours

Explanation:
The correct answer is found by applying the orbital period formula.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to assume the satellite's radius equals Earth's mean radius.

Problem 3 (Qwe789):
Calculate the escape velocity from Earth's gravitational field. (Assume Earth's mass = 5.97 × 10^24 kg and G = 6.674 × 10^-11 m^3/kg s^2)
A) 11.2 km/s
B) 16.7 km/s
C) 20.6 km/s
D) 25.1 km/s
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Escape velocity (vesc) = √(2GM/R), where G is the gravitational constant, M is Earth's mass, and R is Earth's mean radius. vesc ≈ 16.7 km/s

Explanation:
The correct answer is found by applying the escape velocity formula.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to use Earth's surface gravity instead of calculating the escape velocity.

Problem 4 (Rty012):
Two planets with equal mass have different mean radii. Which planet has a stronger gravitational pull at its surface?
A) The planet with the smaller radius
B) The planet with the larger radius
C) Both planets have the same gravitational pull
D) Insufficient information to determine
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Gravitational pull at a planet's surface is proportional to mass and inversely proportional to radius squared. The planet with the smaller radius has a stronger gravitational pull.

Explanation:
The correct answer is found by considering Newton's law of universal gravitation.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to assume both planets have the same gravitational pull due to equal mass.

Problem 5 (Uij345):
An asteroid in an elliptical orbit around the Sun has a perihelion of 0.5 AU and an aphelion of 2.5 AU. What is its semi-major axis?
A) 1 AU
B) 1.5 AU
C) 2 AU
D) 3 AU
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Semi-major axis (a) = (rperi + raphe) / 2, where rperi is perihelion distance and raphe is aphelion distance. a ≈ 1.5 AU

Explanation:
The correct answer is found by calculating the average of perihelion and aphelion distances.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to assume the semi-major axis equals the average of rperi and raphe without dividing by 2.

Problem 6 (Kol678):
Find the gravitational potential energy of a 1 kg object at 10,000 km above Earth's surface. (Assume Earth's mean radius = 6,371 km and G = 6.674 × 10^-11 m^3/kg s^2)
A) -5.9 × 10^8 J
B) -6.3 × 10^8 J
C) -6.9 × 10^8 J
D) -7.6 × 10^8 J
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
Gravitational potential energy (U) = -GMm/r, where G is the gravitational constant, M is Earth's mass, m is object's mass, and r is distance from Earth's center. U ≈ -6.9 × 10^8 J

Explanation:
The correct answer is found by applying the gravitational potential energy formula.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to use Earth's surface radius instead of total distance from the center.

Problem 7 (Poi901):
Two satellites orbit Earth in circular paths. Satellite A has a period of 6 hours, and Satellite B has a period of 12 hours. Which satellite is closer to Earth?
A) Satellite A
B) Satellite B
C) Both satellites have the same distance from Earth
D) Insufficient information to determine
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Period (T) is inversely proportional to radius for circular orbits. Satellite A, with a shorter period, is closer to Earth.

Explanation:
The correct answer is found by considering the relationship between orbital period and distance from the central body.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to assume that Satellite B is closer due to its longer period.
