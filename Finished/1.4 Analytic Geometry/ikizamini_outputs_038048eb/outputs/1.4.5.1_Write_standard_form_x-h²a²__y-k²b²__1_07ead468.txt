Problem 1 (:1):
What is the standard form equation of a hyperbola centered at (h, k) with transverse axis along the x-axis and asymptotes y = ±(b/a)(x-h)?
A) (x-h)²/(a²) + (y-k)²/(b²) = 1
B) (x-h)²/(b²) + (y-k)²/(a²) = 1
C) (y- k)²/(a²) + (x-h)²/(b²) = 1
D) (y- k)²/(b²) + (x-h)²/(a²) = 1
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The standard form of a hyperbola centered at (h, k), with the transverse axis along the x-axis is given by (x-h)²/a² + (y-k)²/b² = 1. Choice A correctly represents this equation.

Explanation:
In a hyperbola, if the transverse axis is along the x-axis, the term involving x will have a positive coefficient (a²), and the term involving y will have a negative coefficient (b²).

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to switch the coefficients for x and y, which would result in choice B or D. This leads to an ellipse rather than a hyperbola.

Problem 3 (:3):
What is the standard form equation of a hyperbola centered at (0, 0) with vertices at (±a, 0)?
A) (x-h)²/(a²) + (y-k)²/(b²) = 1
B) (x-h)²/(b²) + (y-k)²/(a²) = 1
C) x²/a² - y²/b² = 1
D) y²/a² - x²/b² = 1
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The standard form of a hyperbola centered at (0, 0) with vertices at (±a, 0) is given by x²/ a² - y²/b² = 1. Choice C correctly represents this equation.

Explanation:
In a hyperbola centered at the origin, if the transverse axis is along the x-axis, the equation will be x²/a² - y²/b² = 1. If it's along the y-axis, the equation would be y²/a² - x²/b² = 1.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to choose choice A or B, which are for ellipses rather than hyperbolas. Another mistake could be to mix up the signs in choices D, which represents a hyperbola with transverse axis along the y-axis.
