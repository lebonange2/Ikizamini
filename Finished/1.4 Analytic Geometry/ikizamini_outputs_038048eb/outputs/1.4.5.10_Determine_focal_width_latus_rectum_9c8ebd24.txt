Problem 1 (One):
Find the latus rectum of the parabola with equation y = x^2.
A) 1
B) 2
C) 4
D) 8
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The formula for the focal width (latus rectum) of a parabola in standard form y = ax^2 is L = 4p, where p is the distance from the vertex to the focus. For y = x^2, p = 1/4. Thus, L = 4 * (1/4) = 1.

Explanation:
The latus rectum of a parabola is twice the focal length, which for y = x^2 with 'a' equal to 1, is 2p. Since p = 1/4, L = 2 * (1/4) = 1/2.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to confuse the focal length with the latus rectum.

Problem 2 (Two):
Find the latus rectum of the parabola with equation x = 2y^2.
A) 1/8
B) 1/4
C) 1
D) 2
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The formula for the latus rectum of a parabola in standard form x = ay^2 is L = 4p, where p is the distance from the vertex to the focus. For x = 2y^2, p = 1/(4 * (1/2)) = 1/2. Thus, L = 4 * (1/2) = 2.

Explanation:
The latus rectum of a parabola is twice the focal length. For x = 2y^2, convert to standard form y = ax^2 and find 'a'. After converting, we find that 'a' is 1/4, so p = 1/(4 * (1/4)) = 1. Therefore, L = 4p = 4.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to forget to convert the equation to standard form before finding 'a'.

Problem 3 (Three):
Find the latus rectum of the parabola with focus at (3, 0) and directrix y = -2.
A) 1
B) 2
C) 4
D) 8
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The distance from the vertex to the focus is p, which can be found using the formula p = (1/4) * (distance between focus and directrix). The distance between (3, 0) and y = -2 is 2 units. Thus, p = (1/4) * 2 = 1/2, and L = 4p = 2.

Explanation:
The latus rectum of a parabola is twice the focal length. Given the focus and directrix, calculate the distance between them to find p. Then use L = 4p.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to confuse the distance from the vertex to the directrix with the distance between the focus and directrix.

Problem 4 (Four):
Find the latus rectum of the parabola with equation y = 3(x - 2)^2 + 1.
A) 4/3
B) 8/3
C) 16/3
D) 32/3
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The parabola is in the form y = a(x - h)^2 + k, where (h, k) is the vertex. Expand and simplify: y = 3x^2 - 12x + 15. Thus, a = 3. The latus rectum L = 4p, where p = 1/(4 * 3) = 1/12. So, L = 4 * (1/12) = 1/3.

Explanation:
The latus rectum of a parabola is twice the focal length. Convert to standard form y = ax^2 and find 'a'. Then calculate p and use L = 4p.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to forget to expand and simplify the given equation before finding 'a'.

Problem 5 (Five):
Find the latus rectum of the parabola with vertex at (1, 2) and focus at (4, 2).
A) 3
B) 6
C) 9
D) 12
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The distance between the vertex (1, 2) and the focus (4, 2) is p. Since both points have the same y-coordinate, p = |x焦点 - x顶点| = |4 - 1| = 3. Thus, L = 4p = 12.

Explanation:
The latus rectum of a parabola is twice the focal length. Given the vertex and focus coordinates, calculate the distance between them to find p. Then use L = 4p.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to forget that the distance between the vertex and focus is along the axis of symmetry.

Problem 7 (Seven):
Find the latus rectum of the parabola with vertex at (0, 0) and focus at (3, 0).
A) 1/3
B) 2/3
C) 4/3
D) 8/3
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The distance between the vertex (0, 0) and the focus (3, 0) is p. Since both points have the same x-coordinate, p = |y焦点 - y顶点| = |0 - 0| = 0. However, for a parabola with vertex at (0, 0) and focus at (3, 0), 'a' is negative. The standard form is x = ay^2 with a = -1/4. Thus, p = 1/(4 * (-1/4)) = -1, and L = 4p = -4.

Explanation:
The latus rectum of a parabola is twice the focal length. Given the vertex and focus coordinates, calculate the distance between them to find p. For negative 'a', use the standard form x = ay^2 with a = -1/4. Then use L = 4p.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to assume that all parabolas have positive 'a', which leads to an incorrect focal length and latus rectum.
