Problem 1 (AsdFgHjK):
A rectangle with vertices at (0, 0), (3, 0), (3, 2), and (0, 2) is subjected to a shear transformation. The new coordinates of the vertex (3, 2) are (4, 2). What are the new coordinates of the vertex (0, 0)?
A) (1, 0)
B) (0, -1)
C) (1, 1)
D) (4, 0)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The shear transformation shifts the rectangle horizontally by 1 unit without changing the vertical positions. The new coordinates of (0, 0) are thus (1, 0).

Explanation:
Shear transformations preserve parallel lines and change only the relative distances between them in a specified direction.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to assume that both horizontal and vertical positions change, but in this case, only the horizontal position changes.

Problem 2 (XyzWvUtR):
Given a square with vertices at (0, 0), (1, 0), (1, 1), and (0, 1) that undergoes a shear transformation, the new coordinates of the vertex (1, 1) are (2, 1). What is the new x-coordinate of the vertex (0, 0)?
A) 1
B) 2
C) 0.5
D) 1.5
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The shear transformation shifts all points in the square horizontally by 1 unit without changing their vertical positions. The new x-coordinate of (0, 0) is thus 1.

Explanation:
Shear transformations preserve parallel lines and change only the relative distances between them in a specified direction.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to think that the vertex (0, 0) will not move at all, but it does shift horizontally by the same amount as the other points.

B) (6, 10)
C) (-1, 7)
D) (-5, 3)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
A shear transformation with a factor of -2 shifts the point (3, 5) horizontally by multiplying its x-coordinate by -2 while keeping the vertical position unchanged. The new coordinates are thus (1, 3).

Explanation:
Shear transformations preserve parallel lines and change only the relative distances between them in a specified direction.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to think that both horizontal and vertical positions change, but with a shear factor, only the horizontal position changes.
