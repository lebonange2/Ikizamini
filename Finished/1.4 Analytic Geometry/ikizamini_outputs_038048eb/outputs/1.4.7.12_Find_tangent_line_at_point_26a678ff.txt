Problem 1 (:1):
Find the equation of the tangent line to the curve f(x) = x^2 + 3x - 4 at the point (1, 0):
A) (x - 1) = 6(y - 0)
B) (x - 1) = 4(y - 0)
C) (x - 1) = 2(y - 0)
D) (x - 1) = (y - 0)
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The slope of the tangent line at a point is given by the derivative of the function evaluated at that point. The derivative of f(x) = x^2 + 3x - 4 is f'(x) = 2x + 3. To find the slope at x=1, we evaluate f'(1): f'(1) = 2(1) + 3 = 5. The equation of the tangent line is y - y1 = m(x - x1), where m is the slope and (x1, y1) is the point on the curve. Plugging in m=5 and (x1, y1) = (1, 0): y - 0 = 5(x - 1), which simplifies to (x - 1) = 5(y - 0). The correct answer is C.

Explanation:
The tangent line's slope at a given point is the derivative of the function evaluated at that point. Choice A has an incorrect slope, choice B has a smaller slope than the correct value, and choice D has a slope that does not match the derivative.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Using the wrong formula for the tangent line equation or forgetting to evaluate the derivative at the given point.
