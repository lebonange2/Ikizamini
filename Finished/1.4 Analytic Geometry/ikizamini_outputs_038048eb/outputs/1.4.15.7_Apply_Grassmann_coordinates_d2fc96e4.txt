Problem 1 (OneDimGrassmannCoord):
Given a one-dimensional subspace in θ-space spanned by the vector v = (1, 2), what are its Grassmann coordinates?
A) [1, 2]
B) [1/2, 1]
C) [1, 1/2]
D) [-1, -2]
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The Grassmann coordinates of a one-dimensional subspace are given by the coefficients of the Plücker embedding. For a single vector v = (a, b), the Grassmann coordinates are simply [a, b]. Thus, for v = (1, 2), the coordinates are [1, 2].

Explanation:
The Grassmann coordinates represent the subspace in terms of its spanning vectors.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to confuse Grassmann coordinates with vector components. The Grassmann coordinates are not the components of a single vector but rather the coefficients of the Plücker embedding.

Problem 2 (TwoDimGrassmannCoord):
Find the Grassmann coordinates of the two-dimensional subspace in θ-空间 spanned by the vectors v1 = (1, 2) and v2 = (3, 4).
A) [1, 2, 3, 4]
B) [1, 3, 2, 4]
C) [1 × 3 - 2 × 4, 2 × 3 - 1 × 4]
D) [-1, -3, -2, -4]
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The Grassmann coordinates for a two-dimensional subspace spanned by v1 = (a1, b1) and v2 = (a2, b2) are given by the coefficients of the Plücker embedding, which is the determinant of the matrix formed by the vectors: [a1, b1; a2, b2]. For v1 = (1, 2) and v2 = (3, 4), the coordinates are [1 × 3 - 2 × 4, 2 × 3 - 1 × 4] = [-5, 4].

Explanation:
The Grassmann coordinates for a two-dimensional subspace are derived from the determinant of the matrix formed by its spanning vectors.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to think that the Grassmann coordinates are just the concatenation of the vector components. Instead, they involve taking the determinant of the matrix formed by the vectors.

Problem 3 (ThreeDimGrassmannCoord):
Calculate the Grassmann coordinates of a three-dimensional subspace in θ-空间 spanned by the vectors v1 = (1, 2, 3), v2 = (4, 5, 6), and v3 = (7, 8, 9).
A) [1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9]
B) [1, 4, 7, 2, 5, 8, 3, 6, 9]
C) [det(v1, v2, v3)]
D) [-1, -4, -7, -2, -5, -8, -3, -6, -9]
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The Grassmann coordinates for a three-dimensional subspace spanned by three vectors are given by the determinants of the 3x3 matrices formed by taking two vectors at a time. However, in this case, we're asked to find only one coordinate, which is typically not possible for a three-dimensional subspace. The correct answer should be the determinant of any 3x3 matrix formed by the given vectors. For example, [det(v1, v2, v3)].

Explanation:
Grassmann coordinates for a three-dimensional subspace involve determinants of 3x3 matrices formed from its spanning vectors.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to assume that there are nine Grassmann coordinates for a three-dimensional subspace. In reality, the number of coordinates depends on the dimensionality of the ambient space.

Problem 4 (ZeroDimGrassmannCoord):
What are the Grassmann coordinates of the zero-dimensional subspace (a single point) in θ-空间?
A) [0, 0, ...] (an infinite number of zeros)
B) [1, 0, 0, ...] (one non-zero coordinate followed by an infinite number of zeros)
C) undefined
D) [0]
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
Grassmann coordinates are used to represent linear subspaces, and the zero-dimensional subspace is a single point. There are no coordinates needed to describe a single point in a vector space; thus, the Grassmann coordinates are undefined.

Explanation:
Zero-dimensional subspaces do not have Grassmann coordinates because they consist of a single point, which does not require any coordinates for representation.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to think that a zero-dimensional subspace would have one coordinate representing the point. However, this is not how Grassmann coordinates work; they describe linear subspaces, not individual points.

Problem 5 (NonLinearSubspaceCoord):
Find the Grassmann coordinates of a non-linear subspace in θ-空间 spanned by the vectors v1 = (1, 2), v2 = (2, 4), and v3 = (3, 6).
A) [det(v1, v2, v3)]
B) undefined
C) [1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6]
D) [0, 0]
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Grassmann coordinates are defined for linear subspaces only. Since the subspace spanned by v1, v2, and v3 is non-linear (as it forms a line), its Grassmann coordinates are undefined.

Explanation:
Grassmann coordinates apply to linear subspaces; they cannot be used to describe non-linear combinations of vectors.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to attempt to calculate the Grassmann coordinates for a non-linear subspace, which is not valid. Non-linear subspaces do not have well-defined Grassmann coordinates.

Problem 6 (IntersectionSubspaceCoord):
Calculate the Grassmann coordinates of the intersection of two one-dimensional subspaces in θ-空间 spanned by v1 = (1, 2) and v2 = (3, 4), and w1 = (2, 4) and w2 = (6, 8).
A) [det(v1, w1)]
B) undefined
C) [0, 0]
D) [1, 2, 3, 4]
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The Grassmann coordinates of the intersection of two subspaces are not directly calculable from their individual coordinates. Moreover, in this case, both one-dimensional subspaces are identical (spanned by parallel vectors), so the intersection is a single point, which has undefined Grassmann coordinates.

Explanation:
Grassmann coordinates for the intersection of subspaces are not straightforward; they do not simply combine the coordinates of each subspace. Additionally, zero-dimensional subspaces have undefined coordinates.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to try to find a formula or operation to combine the Grassmann coordinates of two intersecting subspaces. This is not a well-defined process.

Problem 7 (SumSubspaceCoord):
Find the Grassmann coordinates of the sum of two one-dimensional subspaces in θ-空间 spanned by v1 = (1, 2) and w1 = (2, 4).
A) [det(v1 + w1)]
B) [1, 2, 2, 4]
C) undefined
D) [0, 0]
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The Grassmann coordinates of the sum of two subspaces are not directly calculable from their individual coordinates. The sum of one-dimensional subspaces in θ-空间 results in a two-dimensional subspace, which requires more than two coordinates to represent. Thus, the operation is undefined.

Explanation:
Grassmann coordinates for the sum of subspaces do not follow a simple formula; they depend on the ambient space and the dimensions of the sum. In this case, the sum results in a higher-dimensional subspace with an indeterminate number of coordinates.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to try to find a way to combine the Grassmann coordinates of two subspaces when taking their sum. This operation is not well-defined.
