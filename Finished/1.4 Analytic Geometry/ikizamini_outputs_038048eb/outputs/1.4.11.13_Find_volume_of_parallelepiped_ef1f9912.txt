Problem 1 (:1):
Find the volume of a parallelepiped with dimensions: length = 5 cm, width = 3 cm, height = 2 cm.
A) 10 cm³
B) 15 cm³
C) 20 cm³
D) 30 cm³
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The volume V of a parallelepiped is given by the formula V = length × width × height. Plugging in the values, we get V = 5 cm × 3 cm × 2 cm = 30 cm³.

Explanation:
The volume calculation is simply multiplying the three dimensions together.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Multiplying by an extra factor of 2 (selecting D instead of C).

Problem 2 (:2):
Find the volume of a parallelepiped with sides parallel to the axes: base area = 12 square units, height = 4 units.
A) 32 cubic units
B) 48 cubic units
C) 64 cubic units
D) 96 cubic units
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The volume V of a parallelepiped with base area B and height h is given by the formula V = B × h. Given B = 12 square units and h = 4 units, we get V = 12 × 4 = 48 cubic units.

Explanation:
The volume is found by multiplying the base area by the height.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Dividing by 2 (selecting A instead of B).

Problem 3 (:3):
Find the volume of a parallelepiped with vectors δ = <1, 2, 3>, ε = <4, 5, 6>, and ζ = <7, 8, 9> as adjacent sides.
A) 120 cubic units
B) 130 cubic units
C) 140 cubic units
D) 150 cubic units
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The volume V of a parallelepiped with vectors δ, ε, and ζ as adjacent sides is given by the absolute value of the scalar triple product: V = |(δ × ε)·ζ|. First, find the cross product (δ × ε): <-1, 1, -1>. Then, calculate the dot product with ζ: (-1)·7 + 1·8 - 1·9 = -1. Finally, take the absolute value for the volume: |V| = |-1| = 1.

Explanation:
The volume is found using the scalar triple product of the vectors.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Not taking the absolute value or not multiplying by the correct scalar (selecting incorrect options).

B) 20 cubic units
C) 30 cubic units
D) 40 cubic units
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
First, find the vectors AB, BC, and AD: AB = B-A = <1, 1, 1>, BC = C-B = <1, 1, 1>, AD = D- A = <3, 3, 3>. The volume V is given by the absolute value of the scalar triple product: V = |(AB × BC)·AD|. Calculate AB × BC: <-1, -1, -1>. Then, calculate the dot product with AD: (-1)·3 + (-1)·3 - (-1)·3 = -3. Finally, take the absolute value for the volume: |V| = |-3| = 3.

Explanation:
The volume is found using the scalar triple product of the vectors formed by the base vertices.

Problem 7 (:7):
Find the volume of a parallelepiped with sides parallel to the axes and diagonals given by vectors δ = <2, 1, 3>, ε = <3, 2, 1>, and ζ = <1, 3, 2>.
A) 12 cubic units
B) 24 cubic units
C) 36 cubic units
D) 48 cubic units
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The volume V of a parallelepiped with sides parallel to the axes can be found using the lengths of the diagonals. First, find the length of each diagonal: d1 = √(2² + 1² + 3²), d2 = √(3² + 2² + 1²), and d3 = √(1² + 3² + 2²). Then, use Heron's formula for the volume with half the lengths: V = (d1·d2·d3) / 6.

Explanation:
The volume can be found using the lengths of the diagonals and Heron's formula.

Common mistake: Using the wrong formula or not simplifying (selecting incorrect options).
