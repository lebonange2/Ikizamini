Problem 1 (:1):
Find the symmetric equation of the line passing through points (3, -2) and (-1, 4).
A) (x + 3)(y - 2) = 0
B) (x - 3)(y + 2) = 8
C) (x + 1)(y - 4) = 6
D) (x - 1)(y + 4) = 0
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The symmetric form of a line's equation is given by (x - x1)(x - x2) = (y - y1)(y - y2), where (x1, y1) and (x2, y2) are two points on the line. For points (3, -2) and (-1, 4): (x - 3)(x + 1) = (y + 2)(y - 4). Expanding this gives us: x^2 - 2x - 3 = y^2 - 2y - 8. Rearranging terms to match the format in choice B, we get: (x - 3)(y + 2) = 8.

Explanation:
The symmetric form helps visualize the symmetry of a line about its midpoint. The correct equation should balance out the x and y terms when expanded.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake is to incorrectly expand or rearrange the equation, leading to choice A or D.

Problem 2 (:2):
Find the symmetric equation of the line passing through points (0, 5) and (6, -3).
A) (x + 5)(y - 6) = 0
B) (x - 5)(y + 6) = 18
C) (x - 0)(y + 5) = 12
D) (x - 6)(y + 3) = 18
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
Using the symmetric form: (x - x1)(x - x2) = (y - y1)(y - y2), with points (0, 5) and (6, -3): (x - 0)(x - 6) = (y - 5)(y + 3). Expanding this gives us: x^2 - 6x = y^2 - 2y - 15. Rearranging terms to match the format in choice D, we get: (x - 6)(y + 3) = 18.

Explanation:
The symmetric form emphasizes the symmetry of a line about its midpoint. The correct equation should balance out the x and y terms when expanded.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake is to mix up the coordinates or signs, leading to choices A, B, or C.
