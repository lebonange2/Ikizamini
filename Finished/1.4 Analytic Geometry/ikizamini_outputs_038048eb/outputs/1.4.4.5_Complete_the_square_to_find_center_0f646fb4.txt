Problem 1 (?):
Find the center of the circle given by the equation: x^2 + y^2 - 6x + 8y = 0
A) (3, -4)
B) (6, 8)
C) (1.5, -2)
D) (2, -4)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
To complete the square for x and y, we need to add and subtract terms that are half of the coefficient squared for both x and y. The equation is already in standard form (x^2 + y^2 + ax + by = c). Here's how we complete the square:
x^2 - 6x + y^2 + 8y = 0
(1) Add (6/2)^2 to both sides: x^2 - 6x + 9 + y^2 + 8y = 9
(2) Rewrite as perfect squares and separate the constants: (x - 3)^2 + (y + 4)^2 = 9
The center of the circle is at the point where both squared terms are zero, so the center is (3, -4).

Explanation:
We complete the square by adding and subtracting the same value to maintain equality. The correct choice reflects the coordinates of the center after completing the square.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is forgetting to add the same value inside and outside the squared terms when completing the square.

(2x - 4)^2 + (2y + 2)^2 = 25

The center of the circle is at the point where both squared terms are zero, so the center is (4, -2).

Explanation:
