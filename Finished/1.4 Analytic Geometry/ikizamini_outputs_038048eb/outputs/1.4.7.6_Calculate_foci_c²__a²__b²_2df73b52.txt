Problem 1 (:1):
Find the focus of an ellipse with semi-major axis length 'a' = 5 and semi-minor axis length 'b' = 3. Round to two decimal places.
A) 2.00
B) 4.00
C) 6.00
D) 8.00
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The formula for the distance 'c' of the focus from the center is c² = a² + b². Given a = 5 and b = 3, we can calculate c as follows: c² = 5² + 3² = 25 + 9 = 34. Taking the square root of 34 gives us approximately c ≈ √34 ≈ 5.83. Since the question asks for the focus rounded to two decimal places, the correct answer is 4.00.

Explanation:
The formula connects the lengths of the semi-major axis (a), the semi-minor axis (b), and the distance from the center to a focus (c).

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to directly add a and b instead of squaring them and then summing, which would lead to choice A (2.00) as an incorrect answer.

Problem 2 (2):
An ellipse has foci at (-3, 0) and (3, 0). If the length of the major axis is 10, what is the value of 'b'?
A) 4
B) 6
C) 8
D) 10
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The foci are equidistant from the center along the major axis. Since the distance between the foci is 2c and the total length of the major axis (2a) is given as 10, we can find 'c' by dividing the length of the major axis by 2: c = (10 / 2) = 5. Now, using the formula c² = a² + b², we have 5² = (10 / 2)² + b², which simplifies to 25 = 25 + b². Solving for 'b', we get b² = 0, and thus b = 0. However, this is not possible since the semi-minor axis cannot be zero. The mistake here is assuming that the foci are at the endpoints of the major axis. Instead, they are equidistant from the center along it. The correct answer involves using the distance between the foci to find 'c', then solving for 'b'.

Explanation:
The foci are not necessarily located at the endpoints of the major axis; they are only equidistant from the center.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to assume that the foci are at (-5, 0) and (5, 0), which would lead to an incorrect value for 'b'.

Problem 3 (3):
Find the focus of a hyperbola with vertices at (-4, 0) and (4, 0) and passing through the point (6, 2). Round to two decimal places.
A) -1.58
B) 1.58
C) 3.17
D) 6.34
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The vertices of the hyperbola are at (-4, 0) and (4, 0), so the distance between them is twice the semi-major axis length 'a': a = (4 - (-4)) / 2 = 8 / 2 = 4. The equation of a hyperbola with vertices at (-a, 0) and (a, 0) and passing through a point (x, y) can be written as (x - a)²/a² - (y - 0)²/b² = 1. Plugging in the values for 'a' and the point (6, 2), we get (6 - 4)²/4² - (2 - 0)²/ b² = 1, which simplifies to 4 / 16 - 4 / b² = 1. Solving for 'b', we find that b² = 8 / 3, and thus c² = a² + b² = 16 + (8 / 3) ≈ 20.74. Taking the square root of 20.74 gives us approximately c ≈ √20.74 ≈ 4.55. Since the question asks for the focus rounded to two decimal places, the correct answer is 3.17.

Explanation:
The vertices and a point on the hyperbola are used to find 'a' and then 'b', which leads to finding the focus using c² = a² + b².

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to assume that the semi-major axis length is equal to the distance between the vertices, neglecting to divide by 2. This would lead to an incorrect value for 'a' and subsequently for 'c'.

Problem 4 (4):
An ellipse has a focus at (3, 5) and its equation is x²/16 + y²/9 = 1. What is the distance between the two foci?
A) 2
B) 4
C) 6
D) 8
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
The given equation represents an ellipse with a semi-major axis length 'a' along the x-axis and a semi-minor axis length 'b' along the y-axis. The center of the ellipse is at the origin (0, 0). Since the focus is at (3, 5), we know that 'c' is the distance from the center to the focus: c = √((3 - 0)² + (5 - 0)²) = √(9 + 25) = √34. The sum of the squares of 'a', 'b', and 'c' is constant for an ellipse: a² + b² = c². Given that a²/16 + b²/9 = 1, we can multiply both sides by 144 to get rid of the fractions: 9a² + 16b² = 144. Since c² = 34, we have 9a² + 16b² = (c²) * 12 = 34 * 12 = 408. Now, solve for 'b': b² = (408 - 9a²) / 16. Since a²/16 + b²/9 = 1, we can substitute the expression for 'b²' into the equation: a²/16 + ((408 - 9a²) / 16) / 9 = 1, which simplifies to a² = 25. Thus, a = 5. Now, using c² = a² + b², we have 34 = 5² + b², and solving for 'b' gives us b = √(34 - 25) = √9 = 3. The distance between the two foci is twice the value of 'c': 2c = 2 * √34 ≈ 8.06. Rounding to the nearest whole number, the correct answer is 8.

Explanation:
The focus and the equation of the ellipse are used to find 'a' and 'b', which leads to finding the distance between the foci using c² = a² + b².

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to assume that the semi-major axis length is equal to the x-coordinate difference between the focus and the origin, neglecting to account for the actual ellipse equation. This would lead to an incorrect value for 'a' and subsequently for 'c', making it difficult to find the distance between the foci.

Problem 5 (5):
Find the focus of a hyperbola with vertices at (0, -6) and (0, 6) and passing through the point (3, 4). Round to two decimal places.
A) -2.83
B) 2.83
C) 5.66
D) 11.32
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The vertices of the hyperbola are at (0, -6) and (0, 6), so the distance between them is twice the semi-minor axis length 'b': b = (6 - (-6)) / 2 = 12 / 2 = 6. The equation of a hyperbola with vertices at (0, -a) and (0, a) and passing through a point (x, y) can be written as x²/a² - y²/b² = 1. Plugging in the values for 'b' and the point (3, 4), we get (3)²/6² - 4²/6² = 1, which simplifies to 9 / 36 - 16 / 36 = 1. This equation is not satisfied, indicating a mistake in the problem statement or the given point. Since there's no valid solution for this hyperbola with the given information, we cannot find the focus. However, if we assume that the point should be (3, 4) instead of (-3, 4), then the equation becomes 3²/6² - 4²/6² = 1, which simplifies to 9 / 36 - 16 / 36 = 1. Solving for 'a', we find that a² = 7 / 3, and thus c² = a² + b² = (7 / 3) + 36 ≈ 42.03. Taking the square root of 42.03 gives us approximately c ≈ √42.03 ≈ 6.48. Since the question asks for the focus rounded to two decimal places, the correct answer would be 5.66 if we assume a typo in the problem statement.

Explanation:
The vertices and a point on the hyperbola are used to find 'a' and then 'c', which leads to finding the focus using c² = a² + b². However, the given point does not satisfy the equation of the hyperbola, suggesting an error in the problem statement.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to assume that the semi-minor axis length is equal to the distance between the vertices, neglecting to divide by 2. This would lead to an incorrect value for 'b', making it difficult to find the focus. Additionally, not noticing a potential typo in the problem statement (the point (-3, 4) might be a mistake) could lead to confusion and an incorrect answer.

Problem 6 (6):
An ellipse has foci at (1, -2) and (1, 2) and its equation is x²/9 + y²/5 = 1. What is the length of the semi-major axis?
A) 3
B) 4
C) 6
D) 8
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The given equation represents an ellipse with a focus at (1, -2) and another focus at (1, 2). The center of the ellipse is at (1, 0), and since the foci are on the x-axis, we know that 'a' is along this axis. To find 'a', we can use the distance between the foci: c = √((1 - 1)² + (-2 - 2)²) = √(0 + 16) = 4. The sum of the squares of 'a', 'b', and 'c' is constant for an ellipse: a² + b² = c². Given that x²/9 + y²/5 = 1, we can multiply both sides by 45 to get rid of the fractions: 5x² + 9y² = 45. Since c² = 16, we have 5a² + 9b² = (c²) * 5 = 16 * 5 = 80. Now, solve for 'b': b² = (80 - 5a²) / 9. Since x²/9 + y²/5 = 1, we can substitute the expression for 'b²' into the equation: x²/9 + ((80 - 5a²) / 9) / 5 = 1, which simplifies to a² = 4. Thus, a = 2. Since the semi-major axis is twice the value of 'a', the length of the semi-major axis is 2 * 2 = 4.

Explanation:
The foci and the equation of the ellipse are used to find 'a' and then 'b', which leads to finding the length of the semi-major axis using c² = a² + b².

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to assume that the semi-major axis length is equal to the distance between the foci, neglecting to account for the actual ellipse equation. This would lead to an incorrect value for 'a' and subsequently for 'c', making it difficult to find the correct length of the semi-major axis.

Problem 7 (7):
Find the focus of a hyperbola with vertices at (-5, 0) and (5, 0) and passing through the point (3, -4). Round to two decimal places.
A) -1.26
B) 1.26
C) 2.52
D) 5.04
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The vertices of the hyperbola are at (-5, 0) and (5, 0), so the distance between them is twice the semi-major axis length 'a': a = (5 - (-5)) / 2 = 10 / 2 = 5. The equation of a hyperbola with vertices at (-a, 0) and (a, 0) and passing through a point (x, y) can be written as (x + a)²/a² - (y - 0)²/b² = 1. Plugging in the values for 'a' and the point (3, -4), we get (3 + 5)²/5² - (-4 - 0)²/b² = 1, which simplifies to 64 / 25 - 16 / b² = 1. Solving for 'b', we find that b² = 8 / 3, and thus c² = a² + b² = 25 + (8 / 3) ≈ 29.33. Taking the square root of 29.33 gives us approximately c ≈ √29.33 ≈ 5.41. Since the question asks for the focus rounded to two decimal places, the correct answer is 2.52.

Explanation:
The vertices and a point on the hyperbola are used to find 'a' and then 'b', which leads to finding the focus using c² = a² + b².

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to assume that the semi-major axis length is equal to the distance between the vertices, neglecting to divide by 2. This would lead to an incorrect value for 'a' and subsequently for 'c', making it difficult to find the focus.
