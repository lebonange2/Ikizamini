Problem 1 (IncrementalID_1):
Convert the Cartesian equation y = 2x + 4 to parametric form.
A) (x, y) = (t, 2t + 4)
B) (x, y) = (2t, t + 4)
C) (x, y) = (t, 4t + 2)
D) (x, y) = (t + 2, 2t)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The parametric form is given by x = t and y = f(t). In the Cartesian equation y = 2x + 4, we can set y = f(t) and solve for x. Setting y = t, we get x = (y - 4)/2. So, the parametric equations are x = t/2 and y = t. The correct answer is (A).

Explanation:
The parametric form expresses both x and y as functions of a single variable t. In this case, we express x in terms of t by solving for x from the Cartesian equation.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing (B) because it's a rearrangement of the original equation but does not follow the parametric form where both x and y are functions of t.

Problem 2 (IncrementalID_2):
Convert the Cartesian equation x^2 + y^2 = 16 to parametric form.
A) (x, y) = (4cos(t), 4sin(t))
B) (x, y) = (cos(t), sin(t))
C) (x, y) = (4t, 4cos(t))
D) (x, y) = (4cos(t), t)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The Cartesian equation represents a circle with radius 4. In parametric form for a circle, x = r*cos(t) and y = r*sin(t). So, the parametric equations are x = 4*cos(t) and y = 4*sin(t). The correct answer is (A).

Explanation:
The equation of a circle in Cartesian form can be converted to polar coordinates, where x = r*cos(t) and y = r*sin(t), with r as the radius.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing (B) because it's the parametric form for a unit circle, but not for the given circle with radius 4.

Problem 4 (IncrementalID_4):
Convert the Cartesian equation 2x + 3y = 6 to parametric form.
A) (x, y) = (3t, 2 - 2t)
B) (x, y) = (2t, 3 - 3t)
C) (x, y) = (t, 2t + 3)
D) (x, y) = (t + 3, 2t)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
To convert the Cartesian equation to parametric form, we can solve for one variable in terms of the other. Let x = t, then 2t + 3y = 6, which gives y = (6 - 2t)/3. The parametric equations are x = t and y = (6 - 2t)/3, or equivalently x = t and y = 2 - 2t/3. The correct answer is (A).

Explanation:
The parametric form expresses both x and y as functions of a single variable t. Here, we express y in terms of t by solving for y from the Cartesian equation.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing (B) because it's a rearrangement of the original equation but does not follow the parametric form where both x and y are functions of t.

Problem 7 (IncrementalID_7):
Convert the Cartesian equation 4x^2 + 9y^2 = 36 to parametric form.
A) (x, y) = (3cos(t), 2cos(t))
B) (x, y) = (2cos(t), 3cos(t))
C) (x, y) = (2cos(t), 3cos^2(t))
D) (x, y) = (3t, 2t)
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The Cartesian equation represents an ellipse with semi-major axis 3 and semi-minor axis 2. In parametric form for an ellipse, x = a*cos(t) and y = b*sin(t). So, the parametric equations are x = 2*cos(t) and y = 3*sin(t). The correct answer is (B).

Explanation:
The equation of an ellipse in Cartesian form can be converted to polar coordinates, where x = a*cos(t) and y = b*sin(t), with a and b as the semi-major and semi-minor axes.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing (A) because it's a rearrangement of the original equation but does not follow the parametric form where both x and y are functions of t, or choosing (C) because it uses cos(t) twice but does not express y correctly.
