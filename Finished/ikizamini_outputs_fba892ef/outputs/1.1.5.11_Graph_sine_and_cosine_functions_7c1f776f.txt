Problem 1 (:1):
Sketch the graph of y = 3sin(x) for one period starting at x=0.
A) A sinusoidal curve with amplitude 3 and period π
B) A sinusoidal curve with amplitude 1 and period 2π
C) A sinusoidal curve with amplitude 3 and period 2π
D) A sinusoidal curve with amplitude 1 and period π
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The function y = 3sin(x) has an amplitude of 3, which means the graph oscillates between -3 and 3. The period is determined by the coefficient inside the sine function (not counting the multiplier outside). Since there's no coefficient inside the sine function, the period is the same as for sin(x), which is 2π. So, the correct graph will have an amplitude of 3 and a period of 2π.

Explanation:
The key points to consider when sketching a sine or cosine function are its amplitude and period. The amplitude determines how far the curve goes above and below the x-axis, while the period determines the width of one full cycle of the wave.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing A because students might forget that the period is 2π for the basic sine function. Choice B is incorrect because it has the wrong amplitude.

Problem 2 (:2):
Sketch the graph of y = cos(2x) for one full cycle.
A) A sinusoidal curve with amplitude 1 and period π
B) A sinusoidal curve with amplitude 2 and period π
C) A sinusoidal curve with amplitude 1 and period 2π
D) A sinusoidal curve with amplitude 2 and period 2π
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The function y = cos(2x) has an amplitude of 1, as the cosine function itself has an amplitude of 1. The period is determined by the coefficient inside the cosine function, which in this case is 2. So, the period is halved from the standard 2π to π. Therefore, the correct graph will have an amplitude of 1 and a period of π.

Explanation:
When there's a coefficient inside the cosine function, it affects the period by dividing the standard period (2π) by that coefficient. The amplitude remains unchanged at 1 for the basic cosine function.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing D because students might misinterpret the coefficient as affecting the amplitude instead of the period.

Problem 3 (:3):
Sketch the graph of y = -2sin(πx) for two periods starting at x=0.
A) A sinusoidal curve with amplitude 2 and period π
B) A sinusoidal curve with amplitude -2 and period π
C) A sinusoidal curve with amplitude 2 and period 2π
D) A sinusoidal curve with amplitude -2 and period 2π
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
The function y = -2sin(πx) has an amplitude of -2, which means the graph oscillates between -2 and 2. The period is determined by the coefficient inside the sine function, which is π in this case. So, one full cycle will take 2π units on the x-axi... (truncated for brevity) ...e. Thus, the correct graph will have an amplitude of -2 and a period of 2π.

Explanation:
The negative sign outside the sine function changes the direction of the curve but does not affect the period. The period is still determined by the coefficient inside the sine function.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing B because students might forget that the amplitude includes the sign, and C because they might misinterpret the period as π instead of 2π.

Problem 4 (:4):
Sketch the graph of y = 1/2cos(3x) for one full cycle.
A) A sinusoidal curve with amplitude 1/2 and period π
B) A sinusoidal curve with amplitude 3/2 and period π
C) A sinusoidal curve with amplitude 1/2 and period 2π
D) A sinusoidal curve with amplitude 3/2 and period 2π
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The function y = 1/2cos(3x) has an amplitude of 1/2, as the cosine function itself has an amplitude of 1. The period is determined by the coefficient inside the cosine function, which is 3 in this case. So, the period is divided by 3 from the standard 2π to π/3. However, since we are asked for one full cycle, we need to multiply this by 2 (to get a total of 2π/3), which still fits within the given domain of one full cycle. Therefore, the correct graph will have an amplitude of 1/2 and a period of π.

Explanation:
When there's a coefficient inside the cosine function, it affects the period by dividing the standard period (2π) by that coefficient. The amplitude is multiplied by the outside coefficient in this case.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing C because students might forget to multiply the reduced period by 2 to get one full cycle.

Problem 5 (:5):
Sketch the graph of y = sin(x) + 4 for one period starting at x=0.
A) A sinusoidal curve with amplitude 1 and vertical shift 4 units up
B) A sinusoidal curve with amplitude 4 and no vertical shift
C) A sinusoidal curve with amplitude 5 and no vertical shift
D) A sinusoidal curve with amplitude 1 and no vertical shift
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The function y = sin(x) + 4 is a sine wave with an amplitude of 1 (from the sine function), shifted vertically up by 4 units (due to the addition of 4). The period remains unchanged at 2π. So, the correct graph will have an amplitude of 1 and be shifted 4 units above the x-axis.

Explanation:
When adding a constant value to a trigonometric function, it shifts the entire curve vertically without affecting the amplitude or period.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing B because students might overlook the vertical shift.

Problem 6 (:6):
Sketch the graph of y = 4cos(x - π/2) for one full cycle.
A) A sinusoidal curve with amplitude 4 and phase shift π/2 to the right
B) A sinusoidal curve with amplitude 4 and phase shift π/2 to the left
C) A sinusoidal curve with amplitude 1 and phase shift π/2 to the right
D) A sinusoidal curve with amplitude 1 and phase shift π/2 to the left
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The function y = 4cos(x - π/2) has an amplitude of 4, as the cosine function itself has an amplitude of 1. The negative sign inside the cosine function indicates a phase shift to the left by π/2 units. So, the correct graph will have an amplitude of 4 and be shifted π/2 units to the left from the standard cosine curve.

Explanation:
When there's a horizontal shift in a trigonometric function, it is represented by a change inside the argument (e.g., x - π/2). A negative value shifts the graph to the left, while a positive value shifts it to the right. The amplitude remains unchanged at 4 for this function.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing A because students might overlook the phase shift direction.

Problem 7 (:7):
Sketch the graph of y = sin(x) - cos(x) for one full cycle.
A) A sinusoidal curve with amplitude 2 and no vertical or horizontal shifts
B) A sinusoidal curve with amplitude √2 and no vertical or horizontal shifts
C) A sinusoidal curve with amplitude 1 and phase shift π/4 to the right
D) A sinusoidal curve with amplitude 1 and phase shift π/4 to the left
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The function y = sin(x) - cos(x) can be rewritten as √2sin(x - π/4), using the trigonometric identity sin(a) - cos(b) = √2sin(a - (b + π/4)). This new form shows that the amplitude is √2, and there's a phase shift to the left by π/4 units. So, the correct graph will have an amplitude of √2 and be shifted π/4 units to the left.

Explanation:
When combining sine and cosine functions, it can sometimes be helpful to use trigonometric identities to simplify the expression. In this case, we used the identity to rewrite the function in a form that reveals its amplitude and phase shift.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing A because students might not recognize the combined sine and cosine as a shifted sine wave.
