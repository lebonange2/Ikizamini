Problem 1 (:Q1):
Convert the rectangular equation x^2 + y^2 = 9 to polar form.
A) (r*cosθ)^2 + (r*sinθ)^2 = 3
B) r^2 = 9cosθ + 9sinθ
C) r = 3
D) r^2 = 9
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
The rectangular equation x^2 + y^2 = 9 represents a circle with radius 3. In polar form, this is represented as r^2 = a^2, where a is the radius. Therefore, the correct conversion is r^2 = 9.

Explanation:
In converting from rectangular to polar form, remember that x = r*cosθ and y = r*sinθ. Squaring both sides of these equations and adding them together gives us x^2 + y^2 = r^2.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to square the trigonometric functions separately (e.g., (r*cosθ)^2 + (r*sinθ)^2), which results in an incorrect equation. Remember that you need to square the entire expression (x or y) before substituting with r*cosθ and r*sinθ.

Problem 5 (:Q5):
Convert the rectangular equation x + y = 4 to polar form.
A) rcosθ + rsinθ = 4
B) r = rac{4}{cosθ + sinθ}
C) r^2 = rac{16}{cosθ + sinθ}
D) r = 4sin(θ + π/4)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
To convert the equation x + y = 4 to polar form, substitute x with r*cosθ and y with r*sinθ. We get r*cosθ + r*sinθ = 4.

Explanation:
When converting a linear equation, substitute y with rsinθ and x with r*cosθ. The resulting equation is already in polar form.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to try to solve for r, which isn't necessary for this type of equation. Remember that the sum of trigonometric functions directly corresponds to a polar form.
