Problem 1 (Asdf):
Convert the point (3, π/6) to polar form.
A) (2√3, π/3)
B) (3, π/6)
C) (3√3, π/6)
D) (√3, π/2)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The point (3, π/6) in polar form is given by the radius r and angle θ. The radius can be found using the formula r = √(x^2 + y^2), where x is the horizontal component and y is the vertical component of the Cartesian coordinate. For (3, π/6), x = 3 cos(π/6) = 3 * (√3/2) = (3√3)/2 and y = 3 sin(π/6) = 3 * (1/2) = 3/2. So, r = √(((3√3)/2)^2 + ((3/2)^2)) = √(9/4 + 9/4) = √(18/4) = √(9/2) = 3/√2 = 2√3. The angle θ is the same as the given angle in polar form, π/6. Therefore, the point (3, π/6) in polar form is (2√3, π/3).

Explanation:
The correct conversion from Cartesian to polar coordinates involves finding the radius using the Pythagorean theorem and keeping the angle unchanged.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to mix up the trigonometric ratios when calculating the radius. Remember that x = r cos(θ) and y = r sin(θ), so you need to use the appropriate ratio for each coordinate.

Problem 2 (Asdf):
Graph the point (4, 5π/3) in the polar coordinate system.
A) A point on the positive x-axis
B) A point in the fourth quadrant
C) A point in the second quadrant
D) A point in the third quadrant
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
The point (4, 5π/3) in polar form has a radius r = 4 and an angle θ = 5π/3. The angle 5π/3 is equivalent to 180° + 2π/3 or 240°. This angle lies in the third quadrant of the polar coordinate system, where both x and y coordinates are negative. Therefore, when graphing (4, 5π/3), you'll find a point in the third quadrant.

Explanation:
In polar coordinates, the angle determines the direction from the origin, and the radius determines the distance from the origin. The quadrants are defined by the signs of x and y: first quadrant (+x, +y), second quadrant (-x, +y), third quadrant (-x, -y), and fourth quadrant (+x, -y).

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to forget that angles in polar coordinates are measured counterclockwise from the positive x-axis, not from the positive y-axis. Make sure you understand how the quadrants are defined by the signs of x and y.

Problem 3 (Asdf):
Convert the point (-2, -√3) in Cartesian coordinates to polar form.
A) (1, 5π/6)
B) (2, π/3)
C) (2, 7π/6)
D) (4, 4π/3)
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The point (-2, -√3) in Cartesian coordinates has an x-coordinate of -2 and a y-coordinate of -√3. To convert to polar form, we need the radius r and angle θ. The radius can be found using the formula r = √(x^2 + y^2), where x = -2 and y = -√3. So, r = √((-2)^2 + (-√3)^2) = √(4 + 3) = √7. To find θ, we use the inverse tangent function (tan^-1) with y/x: θ = tan^-1(y/x) = tan^-1(-√3/-2) = tan^-1(√3/2). This angle is equivalent to π/6 in the first quadrant. However, since both x and y are negative in our original point, we must add π to θ to get the correct angle in the third quadrant: θ = π/6 + π = 7π/6. The polar form of the point (-2, -√3) is thus (2√7, 7π/6).

Explanation:
When converting from Cartesian to polar coordinates, it's essential to consider the signs of x and y to determine the correct quadrant for the angle.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to forget that when both x and y are negative in Cartesian coordinates, the angle in polar form will be in the third quadrant. Make sure you adjust the angle accordingly.

Problem 4 (Asdf):
Find the rectangular coordinates of the point (2, π/4) in polar form.
A) (√2, √2)
B) (1, 1)
C) (2√2, 2√2)
D) (-√2, -√2)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The point (2, π/4) in polar form has a radius r = 2 and an angle θ = π/4. To convert to rectangular coordinates, we use the formulas x = r cos(θ) and y = r sin(θ). For our point, x = 2 * cos(π/4) = 2 * (√2/2) = √2 and y = 2 * sin(π/4) = 2 * (√2/2) = √2. Therefore, the rectangular coordinates of (2, π/4) are (√2, √2).

Explanation:
When converting from polar to Cartesian coordinates, use the trigonometric ratios cos(θ) and sin(θ) with the given radius.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to mix up the values of x and y when calculating them using cosine and sine. Remember that x corresponds to r cos(θ) and y corresponds to r sin(θ).

Problem 6 (Asdf):
Convert the point (5√3, -π/6) in polar form to rectangular coordinates.
A) (5, 5)
B) (2.5, 2.5√3)
C) (2.5√3, 2.5)
D) (-2.5√3, -2.5)
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
The point (5√3, -π/6) in polar form has a radius r = 5√3 and an angle θ = -π/6. To convert to rectangular coordinates, we use the formulas x = r cos(θ) and y = r sin(θ). For our point, x = 5√3 * cos(-π/6) = 5√3 * (√3/2) = (15/2) = 7.5 and y = 5√3 * sin(-π/6) = 5√3 * (-1/2) = -5√3 / 2 = -2.5√3. Therefore, the rectangular coordinates of (5√3, -π/6) are (-7.5, -2.5√3). Note that since both x and y are negative in polar form, we must also make them negative in rectangular form.

Explanation:
When converting from polar to Cartesian coordinates, use the trigonometric ratios cos(θ) and sin(θ) with the given radius. Be mindful of the signs when both x and y are negative in polar form.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to forget that when both x and y are negative in polar coordinates, the corresponding rectangular coordinates will also be negative. Make sure you adjust the signs accordingly.

Problem 7 (Asdf):
Find the polar form of the point (2√3, -√3) in rectangular coordinates.
A) (4, 7π/6)
B) (2, π/3)
C) (2, 5π/3)
D) (2√3, 4π/3)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The point (2√3, -√3) in rectangular coordinates has an x-coordinate of 2√3 and a y-coordinate of -√3. To convert to polar form, we need the radius r and angle θ. The radius can be found using the formula r = √(x^2 + y^2), where x = 2√3 and y = -√3. So, r = √(((2√3)^2) + ((-√3)^2)) = √(12 + 3) = √15. To find θ, we use the inverse tangent function (tan^-1) with y/x: θ = tan^-1(y/x) = tan^-1(-√3/(2√3)) = -π/6. However, since both x and y are negative in our original point, we must add π to θ to get the correct angle in the third quadrant: θ = -π/6 + π = 5π/6. The polar form of the point (2√3, -√3) is thus (√15, 5π/6).

Explanation:
When converting from rectangular to polar coordinates, it's essential to consider the signs of x and y to determine the correct quadrant for the angle.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to forget that when both x and y are negative in rectangular coordinates, the angle in polar form will be in the third quadrant. Make sure you adjust the angle accordingly.
