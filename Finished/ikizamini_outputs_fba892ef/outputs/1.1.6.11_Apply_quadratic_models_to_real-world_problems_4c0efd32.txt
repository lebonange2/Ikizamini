Problem 1 (What is the maximum height of a ball thrown vertically upwards with an initial velocity of 20 m/s, assuming no air resistance? The equation for the height h(t) in meters after t seconds is h(t) = -4.9t^2 + 20t.):
What is the maximum height of the ball?
A) 5.8 m
B) 10 m
C) 15.2 m
D) 20 m
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The maximum height occurs at the vertex of the parabola represented by the quadratic equation h(t) = -4.9t^2 + 20t. The x-coordinate of the vertex can be found using the formula t = -b / (2a), where a = -4.9 and b = 20. Plugging in these values gives us t = -20 / (2 	imes -4.9) = 20 / 9.8 ≈ 2.04 seconds. To find the height at this time, we substitute t back into the equation: h(2.04) = -4.9 	imes (2.04)^2 + 20 	imes 2.04 ≈ 15.17 m, which rounds to 15.2 m.

Explanation:
The quadratic formula helps us find the time at which the ball reaches its maximum height. Then we plug that time into the equation to get the actual height.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to assume that the maximum height occurs when t = 0, but that would only be true if the ball were simply dropped from rest, not thrown upwards with an initial velocity.

Problem 2 (An archer aims at a target 40 meters away. The quadratic equation for the height h(x) in meters of the arrow after it has traveled x meters is h(x) = -5x^2 + 30x. At what distance from the archer does the arrow hit the ground?):
At what distance from the archer does the arrow hit the ground?
A) 10 m
B) 20 m
C) 30 m
D) 40 m
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The arrow hits the ground when its height is zero. To find this point, we set h(x) = 0 and solve for x: -5x^2 + 30x = 0. Factoring out 5x gives us 5x(x - 6) = 0, so either x = 0 (which represents the archer's position) or x = 6. Since we're looking for the distance from the archer, we subtract the archer's position from 40 meters: 40 - 6 = 34 meters. However, since x is measured from the archer, the correct answer is actually 34 / 2 = 17 meters.

Explanation:
Setting the height equation to zero and solving for x gives us the distance at which the arrow hits the ground. Remember that x represents the horizontal distance from the archer, so we divide by 2 to get the actual distance from the archer's position.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to forget to divide the final answer by 2, assuming that x represents the total distance instead of the distance from the archer.

Problem 3 (A rectangular garden with a length twice its width has an area of 100 square meters. What are the dimensions of the garden?):
What is the width of the garden?
A) 5 m
B) 10 m
C) 15 m
D) 20 m
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Let's call the width w meters. Then the length is 2w meters. The area A of a rectangle is given by A = lw, so we have 100 = w 	imes 2w = 2w^2. Solving for w, we get w^2 = 50, which means w = √50 ≈ 7.07. Since the width must be a whole number, we round down to 5 meters.

Explanation:
Set up an equation for the area of the rectangle in terms of the width and solve for the width. Remember that dimensions should be integers for real-world scenarios.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to forget to check if the quadratic solution is a whole number, which it must be for practical garden dimensions.

Problem 4 (A company's profit P in thousands of dollars after t months can be modeled by the equation P(t) = -2t^2 + 16t - 24. At what month does the company first make a profit?):
At which month does the company start making a profit?
A) 1 month
B) 2 months
C) 3 months
D) 4 months
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The company starts making a profit when its profit P(t) is greater than zero. To find this point, we set P(t) > 0 and solve for t: -2t^2 + 16t - 24 > 0. Factoring the quadratic gives us (2t - 4)(t - 6) < 0. The solution to this inequality is 2 < t < 6, meaning the company starts making a profit in the third month (since we're looking for whole months).

Explanation:
Set the profit equation greater than zero and solve for the time when the company first makes a profit.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to misinterpret the inequality, thinking that t = 4 is the answer because it's one of the roots of the quadratic. However, we need to consider the intervals where the profit is positive.

Problem 5 (A ball is thrown horizontally from a height of 10 meters with an initial velocity of 25 m/second. How far does the ball travel horizontally before hitting the ground?):
What is the horizontal distance traveled by the ball?
A) 30 m
B) 40 m
C) 50 m
D) 60 m
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
Since the ball is thrown horizontally, its vertical motion is governed by a quadratic equation. The horizontal distance x can be found by ignoring the vertical component and using the formula for constant velocity: x = v 	imes t, where v is the initial velocity (25 m/s) and t is the time it takes for the ball to hit the ground. To find t, we use the quadratic equation for the height h(t) = -4.9t^2 + 10, setting h(t) to zero: -4.9t^2 + 10 = 0. Solving for t gives us t = √(10 / 4.9) ≈ 1.43 seconds. Now we can find the horizontal distance: x = 25 	imes 1.43 ≈ 36.25 m, which rounds to 36 meters.

Explanation:
Separate the horizontal and vertical components of motion. Solve for the time when the ball hits the ground using the quadratic equation for height, then use that time to find the horizontal distance with constant velocity.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to forget to separate the horizontal and vertical motions or to mix up the equations for each direction.

Problem 6 (A rectangular swimming pool has a surface area of 100 square meters. If the length is twice the width, what is the depth of the pool if it's uniform?):
What is the depth of the pool in meters?
A) 1 m
B) 2 m
C) 3 m
D) 4 m
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Let's call the width w meters. Then the length is 2w meters. The surface area A of a rectangular pool with uniform depth d is given by A = lw + 2wh, where l and w are the length and width, respectively. We have 100 = (2w) 	imes w + 2w 	imes d. Simplifying and solving for d gives us 2w^2 + 2wd = 100, or d = (100 - 2w^2) / (2w). Since the width must be a whole number, we can try different values of w until we find one that makes d a whole number as well. For example, if w = 5, then d = (100 - 2 	imes 5^2) / (2 	imes 5) = 2 meters.

Explanation:
Set up an equation for the surface area of the pool in terms of width and depth, then solve for the depth using whole numbers for width.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to assume that any solution to the quadratic equation will work, but the depth must also be a whole number.

Problem 7 (A parabola with vertex (3, 4) and passing through the point (1, 2) has the form y = ax^2 + bx + c. What is the value of a?):
What is the coefficient 'a' in the quadratic equation?
A) -1
B) 0
C) 1
D) 2
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Given that the vertex is (3, 4), we can write the equation of the parabola in vertex form as y = a(x - h)^2 + k, where h and k are the x- and y-coordinates of the vertex. Plugging in h = 3 and k = 4 gives us y = a(x - 3)^2 + 4. To find 'a', we can use the point (1, 2) that lies on the parabola: 2 = a(1 - 3)^2 + 4, or 2 = 4a + 4. Solving for a gives us a = -1.

Explanation:
Use the vertex form of the quadratic equation and substitute the given vertex coordinates. Then use one of the points on the parabola to solve for 'a'.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to forget to use the vertex form of the equation or to plug in the wrong values for h and k.
