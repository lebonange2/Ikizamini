Problem 1 (:Q1):
How many ways can you choose 4 distinct fruits from a basket containing 6 apples, 5 oranges, and 3 bananas if you must pick at least one apple?
A) 20
B) 45
C) 60
D) 126
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
To solve this problem, use the principle of inclusion-exclusion. First, calculate total combinations without restrictions (C(14, 4)), then subtract combinations with no apples (C(9, 4)), and add back combinations with exactly 0 apples (C(8, 4)). Adjust for overcounting by subtracting cases with no oranges and no bananas.

Total combinations = C(14, 4) = 1001
Combinations without any apples = C(9, 4) = 126
Combinations with exactly 0 apples (not allowed) = C(8, 4) = 70
Overcounted cases to subtract:
Combinations with no oranges and at least one apple = C(6+3, 4) = C(9, 4) = 126
Combinations with no bananas and at least one apple, one orange = C(6+5, 4) = C(11, 4) = 330
Final combinations = 1001 - 126 + 70 - 126 - 330 = 489
The correct answer is 489, which corresponds to choice C.

Explanation:
This question assesses the student's ability to apply inclusion-exclusion when counting combinations with restrictions. Common mistakes include forgetting to adjust for overcounted cases or misapplying the principle.

Common mistake note:
Students may forget to subtract overcounted cases (no apples, no oranges, and no bananas) or incorrectly apply the principle of inclusion-exclusion.

Problem 2 (:Q2):
How many ways can you select a committee of 3 students from a group of 5 boys and 4 girls if at least one girl must be included?
A) 30
B) 35
C) 40
D) 45
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Use the principle of inclusion-exclusion. First, calculate total combinations without restrictions (C(9, 3)), then subtract combinations with no girls (C(5, 3)), and add back combinations with exactly 0 girls (C(5, 3)). Adjust for overcounting by subtracting cases with all boys.

Total combinations = C(9, 3) = 84
Combinations without any girls = C(5, 3) = 10
Combinations with exactly 0 girls (not allowed) = C(5, 3) = 10
Overcounted cases to subtract:
Combinations with all boys = C(5, 3) = 10
Final combinations = 84 - 10 + 10 - 10 = 74
The correct answer is 74, which corresponds to choice B.

Explanation:
This question tests the student's ability to apply inclusion-exclusion when counting combinations with restrictions. Common mistakes include not considering all possible overcounted cases (all boys) or misapplying the principle.

Common mistake note:
Students may forget to consider selecting all boys as a separate case, leading to an incorrect result when adjusting for overcounting.

Problem 3 (:Q3):
How many ways can you arrange 5 books on a shelf if two specific books must be next to each other?
A) 120
B) 240
C) 480
D) 720
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Treat the two specific books as one unit. Arrange 4 units (combined and 3 individual books) in C(4, 1) ways, then arrange the combined unit and other books within their positions (5! / 2!). Correct for double counting by dividing by 2.

Total arrangements = C(4, 1) 	imes (5! / 2!) = 4 	imes 120 = 480
Corrected total arrangements = 480 / 2 = 240
The correct answer is 240, which corresponds to choice B.

Explanation:
This question tests the student's ability to handle permutations with restrictions. Common mistakes include not treating the specific books as a unit or forgetting to divide by 2 for double counting.

Common mistake note:
Students may either not consider the two specific books as one unit, leading to an overcount of arrangements, or forget to divide by 2 when correcting for double counting within the combined unit.

Problem 5 (:Q5):
How many ways can you select 3 desserts from a menu of 7 different options if you must choose at least one ice cream and one cake?
A) 28
B) 35
C) 42
D) 56
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Use the principle of inclusion-exclusion. First, calculate total combinations without restrictions (C(7, 3)), then subtract combinations with no ice cream or no cake, and add back combinations with exactly 0 ice creams or cakes. Adjust for overcounting by subtracting cases with only one type of dessert.

Total combinations = C(7, 3) = 35
Combinations without any ice cream = C(4, 3) = 4
Combinations without any cake = C(4, 3) = 4
Combinations with exactly 0 ice creams or cakes (not allowed) = C(4, 3) = 4
Overcounted cases to subtract:
Combinations with only ice cream = C(3, 3) = 1
Combinations with only cake = C(4, 3) = 4
Final combinations = 35 - (4 + 4) + (4 + 4) - (1 + 4) = 26
The correct answer is 26, which corresponds to choice B.

Explanation:
This question tests the student's ability to apply inclusion-exclusion when counting combinations with restrictions. Common mistakes include not considering all possible overcounted cases (only one type of dessert) or misapplying the principle.

Common mistake note:
Students may forget to consider selecting only one type of dessert as a separate case, leading to an incorrect result when adjusting for overcounting.

Problem 6 (:Q6):
How many ways can you select 4 students from a group of 8 if at least two boys and one girl must be included?
A) 70
B) 91
C) 126
D) 168
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Use the principle of inclusion-exclusion. First, calculate total combinations without restrictions (C(8, 4)), then subtract combinations with fewer than two boys or no girls, and add back combinations with exactly one boy or 0 girls. Adjust for overcounting by subtracting cases with all boys.

Total combinations = C(8, 4) = 70
Combinations with fewer than two boys = C(5, 4) + C(5, 3) = 5 + 10 = 15
Combinations with no girls = C(5, 4) = 5
Combinations with exactly one boy and no girls (not allowed) = C(5, 3) = 10
Overcounted cases to subtract:
Combinations with all boys = C(3, 0) = 1
Final combinations = 70 - (15 + 5) + (10 + 10) - 1 = 60
The correct answer is 60, which corresponds to choice B.

Explanation:
This question assesses the student's ability to apply inclusion-exclusion when counting combinations with restrictions. Common mistakes include not considering all possible overcounted cases (all boys) or misapplying the principle.

Common mistake note:
Students may forget to consider selecting only boys as a separate case, leading to an incorrect result when adjusting for overcounting.

Problem 7 (:Q7):
How many ways can you select 6 students from a group of 10 if at least two students must be selected from each of two specific groups (Group A and Group B), with no restrictions on the third group?
A) 210
B) 350
C) 420
D) 560
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
First, select two students from each of Group A and B (C(5, 2) 	imes C(5, 2)). Then, choose the remaining 3 students from the third group (C(5, 3)). Correct for overcounting by dividing by 2 since order within Group A and Group B does not matter.

Total combinations = C(5, 2) 	imes C(5, 2) 	imes C(5, 3) = 10 	imes 10 	imes 10 = 1000
Corrected total combinations = 1000 / 2 = 500
The correct answer is 500, which corresponds to choice A.

Explanation:
This question tests the student's ability to handle permutations with restrictions and account for overcounting. Common mistakes include not realizing that selecting students from Group A and Group B can be done in multiple orders.

Common mistake note:
Students may not account for the fact that choosing two students from each group can be done in different orders, leading to overcounting the total number of combinations.
