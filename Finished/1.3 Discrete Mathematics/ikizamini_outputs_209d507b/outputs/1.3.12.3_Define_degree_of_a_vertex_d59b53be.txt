Problem 1 (Asdf):
In a graph, what is the degree of a vertex?
A) The number of edges connected to it.
B) The number of vertices in the graph.
C) The sum of the degrees of all vertices.
D) The number of cycles in the graph.
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The degree of a vertex in a graph is the number of edges incident to that vertex. Answer A accurately defines this concept. Answers B, C, and D refer to different properties: B is the total count of vertices, C is the sum of degrees for all vertices, and D refers to cycles, which are unrelated to individual vertex degree.

Explanation:
Understanding the degree of a vertex is fundamental in graph theory as it describes the connectivity of nodes within a network.

Common mistake note:
A common misconception is to confuse the degree with the number of vertices (B), the sum of all degrees (C), or cycles (D).

Problem 2 (Asdf2):
What is the degree of an isolated vertex in a graph?
A) 0
B) 1
C) The total number of edges in the graph.
D) The number of vertices in the graph minus 1.
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
An isolated vertex has no connections to other vertices. Its degree is 0, as there are no incident edges. Answer A correctly reflects this. Answers B, C, and D pertain to vertices with at least one edge.

Explanation:
Isolated vertices play a role in understanding graph structure and connectivity patterns.

Common mistake note:
A common error is to select B, assuming that every vertex has at least one connection. However, isolated vertices have none.

Problem 3 (Asdf3):
In a complete graph with 5 vertices, what is the degree of each vertex?
A) 1
B) 2
C) 4
D) 5
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
In a complete graph, every pair of distinct vertices is connected. In a 5-vertex complete graph, each vertex connects to all other 4 vertices, giving a degree of 4. Answer C is correct. Answers A, B, and D represent incorrect degrees.

Explanation:
Complete graphs are used to study connectivity and edge properties in graph theory.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake might be choosing D, assuming the degree equals the total number of vertices. However, this would only hold if each vertex had exactly one connection.

Problem 4 (Asdf4):
What is the sum of degrees for all vertices in a simple undirected graph with 6 edges?
A) 6
B) 12
C) 18
D) 24
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
In a simple undirected graph, the sum of vertex degrees equals twice the number of edges (Handshaking Theorem). With 6 edges, the sum is 2 * 6 = 12. Answer B is correct. Answers A, C, and D do not follow the theorem.

Explanation:
The Handshaking Theorem reveals a fundamental relationship between edge counts and vertex degrees.

Common mistake note:
A common error might be to choose A or D, assuming that the sum equals the number of edges or twice the number of vertices. This is incorrect.

Problem 5 (Asdf5):
In a directed graph with 8 vertices, where each vertex has exactly one outgoing edge, what is the maximum possible degree for any vertex?
A) 1
B) 2
C) 7
D) 8
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
In a directed graph, degree includes in-degree (incoming edges) and out-degree (outgoing edges). With one outgoing edge per vertex, the maximum in-degree is 7 (all other vertices minus self). Total degree is thus 1 + 7 = 8. Answer C is correct. Answers A, B, and D represent incorrect degrees.

Explanation:
Directed graphs have unique properties; understanding in- and out-degrees is crucial for analysis.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake might be to choose A or B, assuming the maximum degree is limited by outgoing edges. However, incoming edges also contribute.

Problem 6 (Asdf6):
In a tree with 10 vertices, what is the sum of degrees for all vertices?
A) 18
B) 20
C) 24
D) 30
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
In a tree (a connected graph with no cycles), the Handshaking Theorem applies. With 10 vertices and n-1 edges, the sum of degrees is 2 * (n-1) = 18. Answer A is correct. Answers B, C, and D do not follow the relationship between edges and vertex degrees in a tree.

Explanation:
Trees have specific properties related to connectivity and degree distribution.

Common mistake note:
A common error might be to choose B or D, assuming that the sum equals twice the number of vertices or edges. This is incorrect for trees.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 7 (Asdf7):
What is the degree of a vertex in a cycle graph with 6 vertices?
A) 1
B) 2
C) 3
D) 4
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
In a cycle graph, each vertex is connected to exactly two others. Thus, the degree of each vertex in a 6-vertex cycle is 2. Answer B is correct. Answers A, C, and D represent incorrect degrees.

Explanation:
Cycle graphs are examples of simple undirected graphs where all vertices have equal degree forming a closed loop.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake might be to choose A or C, assuming the degree is limited by outgoing edges or increases with vertices. In cycle graphs, each vertex has exactly two connections.
