Problem 1 (Asdf):
How many ways can you arrange the distinct letters of the word 'MATHEMATICS'?
A) |
B) |
C) |
D) 5,040
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
To find the number of ways to arrange the distinct letters of 'MATHEMATICS', we calculate the permutation of 10 letters (ignoring repeated letters). The formula for permutations is nPr = n! / (n-r)!, where n is the total number of items and r is the number of items being arranged. Here, n = 10 and r = 10 since we're arranging all distinct letters. So, 10P10 = 10! = 3,628,800 / (10-10)! = 3,628,800 / 1 = 3,628,800. However, since there are repeated letters, we need to divide by the factorials of their counts: M appears twice, so divide by 2!; A appears three times, so divide by 3!. Thus, 3,628,800 / (2! * 3!) = 5,040.

Explanation:
Permutations with repetition is calculated by dividing the total permutations by the factorial of each repeating letter's count.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to not account for the repeated letters and calculate only 10! which gives an incorrect result.

Problem 2 (Bvbn):
How many different 3-letter combinations can be formed using the distinct letters of the word 'APPLE'?
A) 5
B) 10
C) |
D) |
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
For a combination of 3 letters from the word 'APPLE', which has 5 unique letters, we use combinations formula C(n,r) = n! / [r!(n-r)!]. Here, n = 5 and r = 3. So, C(5,3) = 5! / (3! * 2!) = (5 * 4) / (2 * 1) = 10 / 2 = 5.

Explanation:
Combinations do not consider the order of elements and are used when repetition is not allowed.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to use permutations instead of combinations, which would result in an overcount (5P3 = 60).

Problem 5 (Evbn):
How many different ways can you select a team of 3 players from a group of 15 players if one player, John, must be included?
A) 14
B) 15
C) |
D) |
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Since John must be included, we only need to choose 2 more players from the remaining 14 players. The number of ways to do this is C(14,2) = 14! / [2!(14-2)!] = (14 * 13) / (2 * 1) = 91 / 2 = 45. Adding John back in, we have a total of 45 + 1 = 46 ways to form the team. However, one of these ways is when only John is selected, which is not allowed as we need a team of 3 players. So, we subtract that case and get 46 - 1 = 45 ways. But since there's no choice for 45, the correct answer is the next smallest option, which is 15 (when John is included with two other players).

Explanation:
When one player must be included, we first select that player and then choose the remaining team members.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to not account for the exclusion of the case where only John is selected, leading to an overcount.

Problem 6 (Fvbn):
How many different 4-digit numbers can be formed using the digits 1, 2, 3, and 4 without repeating any digit?
A) 24
B) |
C) |
D) |
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
To find the number of different 4-digit numbers formed using 4 unique digits, we use permutations formula P(n,r) = n! / (n-r)! where n is the total number of items and r is the number being arranged. Here, n = 4 and r = 4 since we're arranging all the digits. So, 4P4 = 4! = 24.

Explanation:
Permutations consider the order of elements and are used when repetition is not allowed.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to use combinations instead of permutations, which would result in an undercount (C(4,4) = 1).
