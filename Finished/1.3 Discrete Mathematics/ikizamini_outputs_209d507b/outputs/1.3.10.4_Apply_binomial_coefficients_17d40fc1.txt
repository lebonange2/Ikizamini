Problem 1 (1):
What is the coefficient of x^2 in the expansion of (x + 3)^5?
A) 10
B) 30
C) 90
D) 120
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
(x + 3)^5 can be expanded using binomial theorem. The coefficient of x^2 term is given by C(5, 2) * 3^(5-2). C(5, 2) = 5! / (2!(5-2)!) = 10 * 3^3 = 90.

Explanation:
The binomial coefficient C(n, k) represents the number of ways to choose k items from a set of n items. In this case, we need to find the coefficient for x^2, which corresponds to choosing 2 powers of x and 3 powers of 3.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to use C(5, 3) instead of C(5, 2), resulting in answer A (10). This error occurs because the student might misinterpret the exponent on x as representing the number of terms to be chosen from x.

Problem 2 (2):
What is the coefficient of a^4b^3 in the expansion of (a + b)^7?
A) 21
B) 35
C) 42
D) 56
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
(a + b)^7 can be expanded using binomial theorem. The coefficient of a^4b^3 term is given by C(7, 4) * a^(7-4) * b^(4-0). C(7, 4) = 7! / (4!(7-4)!) = 35.

Explanation:
The binomial coefficient C(n, k) represents the number of ways to choose k powers of b from a total of n terms. In this case, we need to find the coefficient for a^4b^3, which corresponds to choosing 4 powers of a and 3 powers of b.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to use C(7, 3) instead of C(7, 4), resulting in answer B (35). This error occurs because the student might misinterpret the exponent on b as representing the total number of terms.

Problem 3 (3):
What is the coefficient of x^3y^2 in the expansion of (x - y)^5?
A) -10
B) 10
C) -5
D) 5
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
(x - y)^5 can be expanded using binomial theorem. The coefficient of x^3y^2 term is given by C(5, 3) * (-y)^(5-3). C(5, 3) = 5! / (3!(5-3)!) = 10, and since there are 2 negative terms, the overall coefficient is -10.

Explanation:
The binomial theorem applies to expansions with both positive and negative terms. In this case, we have a subtraction, so each term will have a negative sign if it contains y. The coefficient for x^3y^2 corresponds to choosing 3 powers of x and 2 powers of y.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to forget the negative sign when multiplying by (-y)^2, resulting in answer B (10). This error occurs because students might not consider the effect of subtracting y on the coefficients.

Problem 4 (4):
What is the coefficient of x^3 in the expansion of (2x + 1)^4?
A) 64
B) 96
C) 128
D) 256
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
(2x + 1)^4 can be expanded using binomial theorem. The coefficient of x^3 term is given by C(4, 3) * (2x)^(4-3). C(4, 3) = 4! / (3!(4-3)!) = 4, and (2x)^1 = 2x. So the coefficient is 4 * 2x = 8x.

Explanation:
When a variable is multiplied by a constant, it affects only the term's value but not the binomial coefficient calculation. In this case, we have 2x instead of x, so the coefficient for x^3 will be multiplied by 2.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to multiply C(4, 3) by 2^4 instead of 2^1, resulting in answer D (256). This error occurs because students might混淆the effect of the constant multiplier on the binomial coefficient and the variable term.

Problem 5 (5):
What is the coefficient of x^3y^2z in the expansion of (x + y + z)^6?
A) 15
B) 45
C) 60
D) 90
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
(x + y + z)^6 can be expanded using binomial theorem. The coefficient of x^3y^2z term is given by C(6, 3) * C(3, 2) * x^(6-3) * y^(3-2) * z^(1). C(6, 3) = 6! / (3!(6-3)!) = 20 and C(3, 2) = 3! / (2!(3-2)!) = 3. So the coefficient is 20 * 3 * x^3y^2z = 60x^3y^2z.

Explanation:
When expanding a trinomial, we need to consider combinations of terms from each variable. In this case, we choose 3 powers of x, 2 powers of y, and 1 power of z.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to multiply C(6, 3) by C(3, 2) incorrectly, resulting in an incorrect total coefficient. This error occurs when students don't understand how to apply the binomial theorem with multiple variables.
