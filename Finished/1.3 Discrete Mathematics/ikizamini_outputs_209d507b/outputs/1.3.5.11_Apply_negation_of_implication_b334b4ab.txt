Problem 1 (:Q1):
If not all dogs are cats, which of the following statements is true?
A) All cats are dogs.
B) No dogs are cats.
C) Some cats are not dogs.
D) Some dogs are cats.
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The negation of 'If all dogs are cats' is 'There exists a dog that is not a cat', which is equivalent to 'Some cats are not dogs.' Answers A and D assert the converse, which is not necessarily true. Answer B is too strong and contradicts the given statement.

Explanation:
This question tests understanding of negation applied to categorical statements. The correct answer reflects the contrapositive of the original statement.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be choosing A or D, which are converses of the given statement but not necessarily true. Another mistake could be selecting B, which is too strong and contradicts the information provided.

Problem 2 (:Q2):
If it's not the case that if you study, you will pass the exam, which of the following must be true?
A) You do not study.
B) You will not pass the exam.
C) Studying does not guarantee passing.
D) Passing the exam does not require studying.
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The negation of 'If you study, you will pass the exam' is 'There exists a case where you study and do not pass the exam', implying that studying isn't a guarantee for passing. Answers A and B make specific claims about actions or outcomes, while D is too strong as it suggests studying is unnecessary in all cases.

Explanation:
This question assesses understanding of negation applied to conditional statements. The correct answer highlights the uncertainty introduced by the negated implication.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be choosing A or B, which are specific situations and not necessarily true for every case. Choosing D could also be a mistake as it makes a blanket statement about studying's necessity.

Problem 3 (:Q3):
If it's not the case that if you exercise, you will lose weight, then which of the following must be false?
A) Exercising helps with weight loss.
B) Losing weight requires exercising.
C) There are other factors affecting weight loss besides exercise.
D) You can lose weight without exercising.
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The negation of 'If you exercise, you will lose weight' doesn't imply that exercising has no effect on weight loss or that there are no other factors involved. It only means that exercising isn't a guaranteed way to lose weight. Therefore, the statement 'Losing weight requires exercising' must be false because it implies a necessary condition contradicted by the negated implication. Answers A, C, and D are either true or neutral given the negated implication.

Explanation:
This question tests understanding of negation in the context of cause- and-effect relationships. The correct answer identifies the statement that is logically inconsistent with the negated implication.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be choosing A, C, or D, which are either true or neutral given the information provided. Choosing B is a mistake because it makes a strong claim about weight loss that is contradicted by the negated implication.

Problem 4 (:Q4):
If not all apples are red, then which of the following must be false?
A) All red fruits are apples.
B) Some apples are not red.
C) There exists a non-red apple.
D) All non-red fruits are apples.
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
The negation of 'All apples are red' means there is at least one apple that isn't red. This doesn't imply anything about other fruits or the color of all apples. Therefore, the statement 'All non-red fruits are apples' must be false because it implies no non-apples can be non-red. Answers A, B, and C are either true or neutral given the negated implication.

Explanation:
This question assesses understanding of negation applied to universal statements. The correct answer identifies the statement that is logically inconsistent with the negated universal claim.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be choosing A, B, or C, which are either true or neutral given the information provided. Choosing D is a mistake because it makes a strong claim about non-red fruits that is contradicted by the negated universal statement.
