Problem 1 (Asd123):
A fair coin is flipped twice. What is the probability of getting exactly one head?
A) 0.125
B) 0.25
C) 0.375
D) 0.5
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The probability of flipping a heads on a fair coin is 0.5, and the probability of flipping tails is also 0.5. Since the coin is flipped twice, we can have four possible outcomes: HH, HT, TH, TT. The event of getting exactly one head corresponds to two of these outcomes (HT and TH). So, the probability is calculated as follows: P(1H) = P(HT) + P(TH) = 0.5 * 0.5 + 0.5 * 0.5 = 0.25.

Explanation:
This question tests understanding of independent events and calculating probabilities for multiple flips of a fair coin.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to assume that since there are two heads outcomes (HH, HT), the probability would be 0.5, but this neglects the possibility of tails coming up first (TH).

Problem 2 (Xyz456):
In a bag, there are 3 red balls and 2 blue balls. If one ball is drawn at random, what is the probability that it will be red?
A) 0.25
B) 0.333
C) 0.667
D) 0.75
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
There are a total of 3 + 2 = 5 balls in the bag, and 3 of them are red. The probability of drawing a red ball is therefore P(red) = Number of red balls / Total number of balls = 3/5 = 0.6.

Explanation:
This question tests understanding of basic probability with replacement and counting principles.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to divide by the number of blue balls instead of considering all balls in the bag, which would result in an incorrect probability (2/3).

Problem 3 (Qwe789):
An urn contains 4 white and 6 black marbles. If two marbles are drawn without replacement, what is the probability that both are white?
A) 0.125
B) 0.2
C) 0.375
D) 0.5
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The probability of drawing a white marble on the first draw is P(W1) = 4/10 = 0.4. After one white marble has been drawn, there are now 3 white marbles and 9 total marbles left in the urn. The probability of drawing another white marble on the second draw is P(W2|W1) = 3/9 = 0.333. To find the probability of both events happening, we multiply the probabilities: P(both W) = P(W1) * P(W2|W1) = 0.4 * 0.333 ≈ 0.133 or 0.125.

Explanation:
This question tests understanding of conditional probability and drawing without replacement.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to assume that the second draw has the same probability as the first, neglecting that a marble was already drawn, which changes the total number of marbles.

Problem 4 (Rty123):
A student takes a multiple-choice test with 5 questions, each having 4 choices. If all answers are chosen randomly, what is the probability that the student gets exactly 3 questions correct?
A) 0.0625
B) 0.125
C) 0.25
D) 0.375
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The probability of getting a single question right is 1/4, and the probability of getting it wrong is 3/4. We want to find the probability of getting exactly 3 questions correct out of 5, which can be calculated using combinations: P(3 correct) = C(5,3) * (1/4)^3 * (3/4)^2 = 10 * 1/64 * 9/16 ≈ 0.125.

Explanation:
This question tests understanding of binomial probability and combinations.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to use the formula for finding the probability of getting all questions correct or none correct, which would lead to an incorrect answer.

Problem 5 (Uij456):
An experiment has a 20% chance of success and an 80% chance of failure. If the experiment is performed 3 times independently, what is the probability that it will succeed exactly once?
A) 0.008
B) 0.064
C) 0.192
D) 0.256
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The probability of success is 0.2, and the probability of failure is 0.8. The probability of getting exactly one success in three trials can be calculated using combinations: P(1 success) = C(3,1) * (0.2)^1 * (0.8)^2 = 3 * 0.2 * 0.64 ≈ 0.192.

Explanation:
This question tests understanding of independent events and binomial probability.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to assume that the probabilities for each trial will be multiplied, neglecting that they are added together using combinations.

Problem 6 (Oop789):
A bag contains 2 red, 3 green, and 4 blue marbles. If two marbles are drawn without replacement, what is the probability that both marbles have different colors?
A) 0.5
B) 0.6
C) 0.7
D) 0.8
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
There are two ways to draw marbles of different colors: red-green, red-blue, green-red, green-blue, blue-red, or blue-green. The total number of ways to choose 2 marbles out of 9 is C(9,2) = 36. So, the probability is P(different colors) = Number of favorable outcomes / Total number of outcomes = 6/36 = 0.167 or 0.17.

Explanation:
This question tests understanding of combinations and counting different color combinations without replacement.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to consider only one pair of colors (e.g., red-green) and multiply by the number of marbles in each group, neglecting other possible pairs.

Problem 7 (Kol123):
A box contains 5 white and 7 black socks. If two socks are drawn at random without replacement, what is the probability that both socks will be the same color?
A) 0.469
B) 0.531
C) 0.625
D) 0.75
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
There are two ways to draw socks of the same color: both white or both black. First, we calculate the probability of drawing two white socks: P(both white) = (5/12) * (4/11) ≈ 0.167. Then, we calculate the probability of drawing two black socks: P(both black) = (7/12) * (6/11) ≈ 0.375. Adding these probabilities together gives the total probability of drawing socks of the same color: P(same color) = P(both white) + P(both black) ≈ 0.167 + 0.375 = 0.542 or 0.531.

Explanation:
This question tests understanding of combinations, conditional probability, and drawing without replacement.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to assume that the probabilities for each color are independent, neglecting that the first draw affects the second draw.
