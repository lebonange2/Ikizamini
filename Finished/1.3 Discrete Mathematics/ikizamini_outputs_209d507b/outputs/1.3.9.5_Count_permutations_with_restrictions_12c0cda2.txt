Problem 1 (One-Slot-Restriction):
How many ways can you arrange the letters of the word "MATHEMATICS" if the two M's must be next to each other?
A) 120
B) 720
C) 1440
D) 5040
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
To solve this problem, treat the two adjacent M's as a single unit (MM). There are 8 units to arrange: MM, A, T, H, E, I, C, S. These can be arranged in 8! ways. Since the two M's within the unit can also be arranged in 2! ways, multiply by this factor. Total arrangements: 8! * 2! = 1440.

Explanation:
The word "MATHEMATICS" has 11 letters with two M's treated as one unit. Account for the restriction and calculate permutations of combined units.

Common mistake note:
Not accounting for the double arrangement of the two M's within MM, which leads to overcounting.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 2 (Two-Slot-Restriction):
How many ways can you arrange the letters of the word "COMPUTER" if the C and R must be at the ends?
A) 12
B) 24
C) 36
D) 48
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Given C and R at ends, 7 letters remain. Place C in 2 ways; R fills the other end. Then arrange remaining 6 letters in 6! ways. Total arrangements: 2 * 6! = 24.

Explanation:
With C and R fixed at ends, arrange the remaining 7 letters subject to those constraints.

Common mistake note:
Overcounting by not considering the fixed positions of C and R.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 3 (Restricted-Positions):
How many ways can you arrange the numbers 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 if 1 must be before 2 and 3 must be after 4?
A) 720
B) 360
C) 180
D) 90
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
Arrange 1, 3, 5, and 6 in 4! ways. Given constraints, place 2 and 4 in 2 slots (2! ways). Total arrangements: 4! * 2! = 180.

Explanation:
With relative positions of 1, 2, 3, and 4 fixed, arrange the non-restricted numbers first then place the restricted ones.

Common mistake note:
Overlooking constraint that 3 must follow 4.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 4 (Restricted-Adjacent):
How many ways can you arrange the letters of the word "BANANA" if no two A's are adjacent?
A) 120
B) 240
C) 360
D) 720
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Consider 5 slots between and around A's for non-A letters (4 choices). Arrange these in 3! ways. Then arrange all 6 letters in 6! ways. Total arrangements: 3! * 6! = 240.

Explanation:
With restriction on A's, consider non-A letter positions and calculate permutations without violating the rule.

Common mistake note:
Assuming all 6 letters can be arranged freely, leading to overcounting with adjacent A's.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 5 (Restricted-Count):
How many ways can you arrange a set of 5 distinct objects, including 2 identical red balls and 3 distinct blue balls?
A) 120
B) 60
C) 40
D) 20
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
With 2 identical red balls, divide total permutations (5!) by arrangements of these (2!). Total arrangements: 5! / 2! = 60.

Explanation:
Account for overcounting due to indistinguishable red balls by dividing by their arrangements.

Common mistake note:
Forgetting to divide by factorial of identical objects, leading to incorrect count.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 6 (Restricted-Permutations):
How many ways can you arrange 3 boys and 4 girls in a row if no two boys are adjacent?
A) 288
B) 192
C) 144
D) 72
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
Consider 3 slots between and around boys for girls (4 choices). Arrange girls in 4! ways. Then arrange boys in 3! ways within slots. Total arrangements: 4! * 3! = 144.

Explanation:
Given no adjacent boys, consider girl positions and calculate permutations without violating the restriction.

Common mistake note:
Assuming all 7 children can be arranged freely, leading to overcounting with adjacent boys.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 7 (Restricted-Combination):
How many ways can you choose a committee of 3 students from a group of 5 boys and 4 girls if at least one girl must be included?
A) 28
B) 36
C) 40
D) 48
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Choose 1 girl, 2 boys: 4C1 * 5C2. Choose 2 girls, 1 boy: 4C2 * 5C1. Total arrangements (divided by 2 for double counting): (4 * 10 + 6 * 5) / 2 = 30 = 5 * 6.

Explanation:
With at least one girl, consider two cases: 1 girl and 2 boys or 2 girls and 1 boy. Combine counts accounting for overcounting.

Common mistake note:
Not adjusting for double counting when combining cases, leading to incorrect total count.
