Problem 1 (Asdf):
A light ray hits a mirror at an angle of incidence of 30°. What is the angle of reflection?
A) 15°
B) 30°
C) 60°
D) 90°
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The reflective property states that the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection. Therefore, if the angle of incidence is 30°, the angle of reflection will also be 30°.

Explanation:
This question tests the understanding of the basic principle of reflection where angles of incidence and reflection are equal.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to choose A (15°), which is half the angle of incidence. This error occurs because the student might confuse it with the concept of dividing an angle in half, rather than reflecting it.

Problem 2 (Asdf2):
An object is placed 10 cm in front of a plane mirror. How far is its image from the mirror?
A) 5 cm
B) 10 cm
C) 20 cm
D) 30 cm
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
According to the reflective property, the distance from the object to the mirror is equal to the distance from the image to the mirror. So if the object is 10 cm away, the image will also be 10 cm away.

Explanation:
This question assesses the understanding of how the distance between an object and its image relates to the position of the object in front of a plane mirror.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to choose A (5 cm), assuming that the image is half the distance. This error occurs due to misunderstanding the reflection principle, which does not halve distances but duplicates them.

Problem 3 (Asdf3):
When a light ray reflects off a smooth surface like glass, what happens to the angle of incidence and reflection?
A) Both angles increase.
B) Both angles decrease.
C) They remain the same.
D) One increases while the other decreases.
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The reflective property states that the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection. So, when a light ray reflects off a smooth surface, both angles remain the same.

Explanation:
This question tests the understanding of how angles behave during reflection on a smooth surface.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to choose A or B (angles increase or decrease), which is incorrect because the reflective property maintains equality between the two angles.

Problem 4 (Asdf4):
An object is placed in front of a concave mirror. If the image formed is real and inverted, where must the object be located relative to the focus?
A) Inside the focus
B) At the focus
C) Between the focus and the mirror
D) Beyond the focal point
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
According to the reflective property of concave mirrors, real and inverted images are formed when an object is placed beyond the focal point.

Explanation:
This question checks the understanding of image formation in a concave mirror based on the position of the object relative to the focus.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to choose C (between the focus and the mirror), which would result in an upright, real image, not inverted.

Problem 6 (Asdf6):
What happens when light rays strike a flat piece of paper?
A) They are refracted
B) They are reflected
C) They pass through without change
D) They are absorbed
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
Light rays pass through a flat piece of paper without significant refraction, reflection, or absorption. They may bend slightly at the edges but continue in a straight line.

Explanation:
This question assesses the understanding of how light behaves when encountering different surfaces.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to choose A (refracted), which might happen if the surface is not flat or transparent, like glass. The paper's transparency and flatness make refraction minimal.
