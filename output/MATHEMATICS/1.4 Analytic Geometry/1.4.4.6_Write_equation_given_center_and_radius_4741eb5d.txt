Problem 1 (Q1):
What is the equation of a circle with center (3, -2) and radius 5?
A) (x - 3)^2 + (y + 2)^2 = 25
B) (x + 3)^2 + (y - 2)^2 = 25
C) (x - 3)^2 + (y + 2)^2 = 5^2
D) (x + 3)^2 + (y - 2)^2 = 49
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The equation of a circle with center (h, k) and radius r is given by 
(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2. For the given circle, h = 3, k = -2, and r = 5. Substituting these values into the formula gives us 
(x - 3)^2 + (y + 2)^2 = 5^2.

Explanation:
The correct equation uses the center coordinates and squares the radius correctly.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to use the wrong signs in the center coordinates, which would result in choice A. Another mistake might be not squaring the radius, as shown in choice D.
