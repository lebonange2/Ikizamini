Problem 1 (Andromeda Galaxy Shape):
What shape does the Andromeda Galaxy appear as when viewed in spherical coordinates?
A) Circle
B) Sphere
C) Ellipsoid
D) Cylinder
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
In spherical coordinates, the Andromeda Galaxy appears as a sphere due to the uniform distribution of stars across its surface. So, the correct answer is B: Sphere.

Explanation:
Spherical coordinates describe points on a sphere or in 3D space. The galaxy's shape in these coordinates reflects its overall structure rather than its spiral arms.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: selecting A (Circle), which represents the projection of the galaxy onto a plane, not its shape in spherical coordinates.

Problem 2 (Sphere Surface Area):
Calculate the surface area of a sphere with radius 5 units using the formula for surface area in spherical coordinates: A = 2πr².
A) 100π
B) 75π
C) 25π
D) 50π
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Using the formula, A = 2π(5)² = 100π. So, the correct answer is A: 100π.

Explanation:
The surface area of a sphere is directly proportional to the square of its radius.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: forgetting to square the radius or using an incorrect formula (e.g., volume formula instead).

Problem 3 (Cone Volume):
Find the volume of a cone with height 6 units and base radius 3 units. Use the standard formula for volume, V = πr²h / 3, not spherical coordinates.
A) 18π
B) 54π
C) 36π
D) 72π
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The volume is V = π(3)²(6) / 3 = 18π. So, the correct answer is A: 18π.

Explanation:
The volume of a cone is unrelated to spherical coordinates; use the standard formula.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: attempting to use a spherical coordinate formula or applying concepts incorrectly.

Problem 4 (Sphere Volume):
Calculate the volume of a sphere with radius 4 units using the formula for volume in spherical coordinates: V = πr³ / 3.
A) 256π
B) 128π
C) 64π
D) 32π
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
Using the formula, V = π(4)³ / 3 = 64π. So, the correct answer is C: 64π.

Explanation:
The volume of a sphere is directly proportional to the cube of its radius.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: forgetting to cube the radius or using an incorrect formula (e.g., surface area formula instead).

Problem 5 (Spherical Cap Volume):
Find the volume of a spherical cap with height 3 units and base radius 5 units. The cap is cut from a sphere with radius 8 units. Use the formula V = πh(R² + h²) / 3, where R is the radius of the sphere.
A) 12π
B) 48π
C) 72π
D) 96π
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The volume is V = π(3)(8² + 3²) / 3 = 48π. So, the correct answer is B: 48π.

Explanation:
The volume of a spherical cap depends on its height and the radius of the sphere it's cut from.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: using the wrong formula or mixing up variables (e.g., using base radius instead of sphere's radius).

Problem 6 (Spherical Shell Volume):
Find the volume of a spherical shell with outer radius 10 units and inner radius 6 units. Use the standard formula for volume, V = π(R² - r²)h, where R is the outer radius and r is the inner radius.
A) 128π
B) 192π
C) 256π
D) 320π
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The volume is V = π(10² - 6²)(4) = 192π. So, the correct answer is B: 192π.

Explanation:
The volume of a spherical shell depends on the difference in radii and height between them.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: using the wrong formula or mixing up variables (e.g., using radius of a sphere instead of shell's radii).

Problem 7 (Spherical Sector Volume):
Find the volume of a spherical sector with central angle 60° and radius 7 units. Use the standard formula for volume, V = πr²h / 3, where h is the height of the sector.
A) 49π
B) 147π
C) 294π
D) 882π
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The volume is V = π(7)²(7π / 6) / 3 = 49π. So, the correct answer is A: 49π.

Explanation:
The volume of a spherical sector depends on its central angle and radius.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: using the wrong formula or not converting the central angle to radians.
