Problem 1 (:1):
What is the standard form of a hyperbola centered at (h, k) with transverse axis along the y-axis and vertices at (h ± a, k)?
A) (y - k)²/a² + (x - h)²/b² = 1
B) (y - k)²/b² + (x - h)²/a² = 1
C) (y - k)²/a² - (x - h)²/b² = 1
D) (x - h)²/a² - (y - k)²/b² = 1
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The standard form of a hyperbola with its transverse axis along the y-axis is given by (y - k)²/a² - (x - h)²/b² = 1. This equation represents a hyperbola centered at (h, k), with vertices at (h ± a, k).

Explanation:
The key feature of this form is that the y-term has a positive sign and the x-term has a negative sign.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to confuse the transverse axis with the conjugate axis. If the transverse axis is along the x-axis, the equation would be (x - h)²/a² - (y - k)²/b² = 1.
