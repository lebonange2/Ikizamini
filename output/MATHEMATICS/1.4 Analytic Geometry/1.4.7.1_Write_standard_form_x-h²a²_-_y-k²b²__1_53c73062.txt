Problem 1 (:1):
What is the standard form equation of a hyperbola centered at (h, k), with horizontal transverse axis and vertices located at (h ± a, k)?
A) (x - h)²/a² + (y - k)²/b² = 1
B) (x - h)²/b² + (y - k)²/a² = 1
C) (x - h)²/a² - (y - k)²/b² = 1
D) (x - h)²/b² - (y - k)²/a² = 1
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The standard form for a hyperbola with a horizontal transverse axis is (x-h)²/a² - (y-k)²/b² = 1. This equation represents the relationship between the distances from the center (h, k) to any point on the hyperbola and the vertices.

Explanation:
The key difference between this form and other forms is the sign before the (y- k)² term, which determines whether the hyperbola opens horizontally (-) or vertically (+).

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing A, which represents an ellipse instead of a hyperbola due to the positive sign before both terms.

Problem 3 (:3):
What is the standard form equation of a hyperbola centered at the origin with vertices at (±a, 0)?
A) (x - h)²/a² + (y - k)²/b² = 1
B) (x - h)²/b² + (y - k)²/a² = 1
C) (x - h)²/a² - (y - k)²/b² = 1
D) (x - h)²/b² - (y - k)²/a² = 1
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The standard form for a hyperbola centered at the origin with vertices at (±a, 0) is x²/a² - y²/b² = 1. This can be simplified from (x-h)²/a² - (y-k)²/b² = 1 by setting h=k=0.

Explanation:
When the hyperbola is centered at the origin, the coordinates of the center are (0, 0), and the equation simplifies accordingly.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing D, which represents a vertical hyperbola at the origin instead of a horizontal one due to the positive sign before the y² term.

Problem 5 (:5):
Given the hyperbola with vertices at (3, 4) and (7, 4), what is its standard form equation?
A) (x - h)²/a² + (y - k)²/b² = 1
B) (x - h)²/b² + (y - k)²/a² = 1
C) (x - h)²/a² - (y - k)²/b² = 1
D) (x - h)²/b² - (y - k)²/a² = 1
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The hyperbola is centered horizontally between the vertices, at (5, 4). The distance between the center and a vertex is the semi-major axis, which is 2. So, a=2. The standard form equation is thus (x - 5)²/2² - (y - 4)²/b² = 1.

Explanation:
Since the vertices are horizontally aligned, we have a horizontal hyperbola with h=5 and a=2. b can be found from other information or left as a variable in the equation.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing A, which represents an ellipse instead of a hyperbola due to the positive sign before both terms.

Problem 7 (:7):
Given the hyperbola with foci at (3, 0) and (7, 0), what is its standard form equation?
A) (x - h)²/a² + (y - k)²/b² = 1
B) (x - h)²/b² + (y - k)²/a² = 1
C) (x - h)²/a² - (y - k)²/b² = 1
D) (x - h)²/b² - (y - k)²/a² = 1
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The distance between the foci is 4, which is twice the value of c. The center is at (5, 0). Since the foci are horizontally aligned, it's a horizontal hyperbola with h=5 and c=2. a² = c² + b², so a² = 2² + b², and b² = a² - 4. The standard form equation is thus (x - 5)²/(a² - 4) - y²/4² = 1.

Explanation:
The foci determine the value of c, which helps find a and b using the relationship a² = c² + b². Since the foci are horizontally aligned, we have a horizontal hyperbola with h=5.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing A, which represents an ellipse instead of a hyperbola due to the positive sign before both terms.
