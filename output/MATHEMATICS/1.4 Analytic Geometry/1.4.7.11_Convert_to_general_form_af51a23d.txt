Problem 1 (Asdf):
What is the general form of the quadratic equation with roots -3 and 2?
A) -x^2 + x + 6
B) x^2 + x - 6
C) x^2 - x - 6
D) -x^2 - x + 6
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The general form of a quadratic equation with roots $r_1$ and $r_2$ is given by $(x - r_1)(x - r_2) = 0$. For roots -3 and 2, the equation would be $(x + 3)(x - 2) = x^2 + 3x - 2x - 6 = x^2 + x - 6$. However, since we're looking for the general form with a positive leading coefficient, we can simplify it to $x^2 - x - 6$.

Explanation:
The correct answer is obtained by multiplying the factors $(x + 3)$ and $(x - 2)$. The common mistake is not simplifying the equation to have a positive leading coefficient, which results in choice A (-x^2 + x + 6).

Common mistake note:
Failing to simplify the quadratic equation to have a positive leading coefficient.

Problem 3 (Asdf):
What is the general form of the equation with vertex at (3, -4) and opens downward?
A) -2(x - 3)^2 - 4
B) 2(x - 3)^2 + 4
C) -(x - 3)^2 - 4
D) (x - 3)^2 + 4
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The vertex form of a quadratic equation with vertex (h, k) and opens downward is given by $y = -(x - h)^2 + k$. For the vertex at (3, -4), the equation would be $y = -(x - 3)^2 - 4$. Since we're looking for the general form, we can drop the 'y' and keep only the equation in terms of x.

Explanation:
The correct answer is obtained by plugging the vertex coordinates into the vertex form and simplifying. The common mistake is not accounting for the direction the parabola opens or misplacing the negative sign.

Common mistake note:
Not considering the direction the parabola opens (downward) or incorrectly placing the negative sign in the equation.

What is the general form of the quadratic equation with vertex at (-2, 5) and passes through the point (1, -3)?
A) (x + 2)^2 - 8
B) -(x + 2)^2 + 8
C) (x + 2)^2 + 8
D) -(x + 2)^2 - 8
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The vertex form of a quadratic equation with vertex (h, k) is given by $y = a(x - h)^2 + k$. We know the vertex is (-2, 5), so we have $y = a(x + 2)^2 + 5$. To find 'a', we can use the point (1, -3). Plugging in x = 1 and y = -3 gives us $-3 = a(1 + 2)^2 + 5$, which simplifies to $-8 = 9a$ and thus $a = -rac{8}{9}$. The general form is then $y = -(x + 2)^2 + 8$.

Explanation:
The correct answer is obtained by using the vertex form, plugging in the given point to find 'a', and simplifying. The common mistake is not accounting for the vertex or making errors when solving for 'a'.

Common mistake note:

Problem 5 (Asdf):
What is the general form of the equation with zeros at 1 and -4?
A) (x + 1)(x + 4)
B) (x - 1)(x + 4)
C) (x - 1)(x - 4)
D) (x + 1)(x - 4)
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The zeros of a quadratic equation give us the factors. Since the zeros are 1 and -4, the factors would be (x - 1) and (x + 4). The general form is obtained by multiplying these factors: $(x - 1)(x + 4) = x^2 + 4x - x - 4 = x^2 + 3x - 4$.

Explanation:
The correct answer is found by multiplying the factors corresponding to the given zeros. The common mistake is reversing the signs of the factors or not simplifying the equation.

Common mistake note:
Reversing the signs of the factors or failing to simplify the quadratic equation.

Problem 6 (Asdf):
Convert the quadratic equation 3x^2 + 6x - 9 = 0 into general form.
A) (3x + 3)^2
B) (x + 1)^2
C) (3x - 3)^2
D) $(x - 1)^2
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
To convert the quadratic equation to general form, we need to factor it. First, divide all terms by 3: $x^2 + 2x - 3 = 0$. Factoring this gives us $(x + 3)(x - 1) = 0$. The general form is obtained by writing this as $(x + 1)^2$ since we're looking for a perfect square trinomial.

Explanation:
The correct answer is found by factoring the quadratic equation and recognizing it as a perfect square. The common mistake is not factoring correctly or failing to recognize the resulting factors as a perfect square.

Common mistake note:
Not factoring the quadratic equation correctly or not recognizing it as a perfect square trinomial.

Problem 7 (Asdf):

A) (x - 3)^2 + 6
B) (x + 3)^2 + 6
C) (x - 3)^2 - 6
D) $(x + 3)^2 - 6
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The vertex form of a quadratic equation with vertex (h, k) and opens to the right is given by $y = a(x - h)^2 + k$. For the vertex at (0, 9), we have $y = a(0 - 0)^2 + 9$, which simplifies to $y = ax^2 + 9$. Since the parabola opens to the right, 'a' must be positive. The general form is thus $(x - 3)^2 + 6$ after solving for 'a'.

Explanation:
The correct answer is found by using the vertex form and determining the sign of 'a' based on the direction the parabola opens. The common mistake is not accounting for the direction the parabola opens or making errors when solving for 'a'.

Common mistake note:
Not considering the direction the parabola opens (to the right) or incorrectly determining the sign of the coefficient 'a'.
