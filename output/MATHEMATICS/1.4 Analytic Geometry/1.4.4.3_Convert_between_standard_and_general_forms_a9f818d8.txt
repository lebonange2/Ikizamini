Problem 1 (AsdFgHjK):
Convert the quadratic equation from standard form to general form: 2x^2 + 5x - 3 = 0
A) -2x^2 + 5x + 3
B) 2x^2 - 5x - 3
C) x^2 + 2.5x - 1.5
D) 4x^2 + 10x - 6
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
To convert from standard form (ax^2 + bx + c = 0) to general form (Ax^2 + Bx + C), we don't change the coefficients of x^2 and x, but keep the constant term on the right side. So, for the equation 2x^2 + 5x - 3 = 0, the general form is x^2 + 2.5x - 1.5.

Explanation:
The correct choice keeps the coefficients of x^2 and x unchanged and moves the constant term to the right side.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to multiply the entire equation by a factor that cancels out the coefficient of x^2, which is not necessary for conversion.

Problem 2 (XyZwVbN):
Convert the quadratic equation from general form to standard form: 3x^2 - 6x + 9 = 0
A) 3(x - 1)^2 = 0
B) 6(x - 1)^2 = 0
C) (x - 3)^2 = 0
D) (2x - 3)^2 = 0
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
To convert from general form (Ax^2 + Bx + C = 0) to standard form, we complete the square. For the equation 3x^2 - 6x + 9 = 0, we can divide all terms by 3 and then complete the square: (x - 1)^2 = 0. Multiplying both sides by 3 gives us 3(x - 1)^2 = 0.

Explanation:
The correct choice correctly completes the square and divides the result by the coefficient of x^2.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget to divide the constant term by the coefficient of x^2 before completing the square.
