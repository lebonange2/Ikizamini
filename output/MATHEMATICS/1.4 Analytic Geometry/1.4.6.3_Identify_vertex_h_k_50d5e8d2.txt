Problem 1 (:Q1):
What are the coordinates of the vertex for the parabola given by the equation y = 2(x - 3)^2 + 4?
A) (0, 4)
B) (3, 0)
C) (3, 4)
D) (4, 3)
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The vertex form of a parabola is y = a(x - h)^2 + k, where (h, k) represents the vertex. Comparing this to the given equation y = 2(x - 3)^2 + 4, we can see that h = 3 and k = 4. Therefore, the vertex is at (3, 4).

Explanation:
The vertex form directly provides the coordinates of the vertex.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to choose (0, 4) because it's the y-intercept, but the vertex is not necessarily the y-intercept.

Problem 2 (:Q2):
What are the coordinates of the vertex in the equation y = -3(x + 2)^2 - 1?
A) (2, -1)
B) (-2, -1)
C) (-1, 2)
D) (-1, -2)
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The vertex form of a parabola is y = a(x - h)^2 + k. Comparing this to the given equation y = -3(x + 2)^2 - 1, we can see that h = -2 and k = -1. Therefore, the vertex is at (-2, -1).

Explanation:
The coefficient of x^2 determines whether the parabola opens up or down, but does not affect the h value.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to choose (2, -1) because it's the opposite of h, but remember that the vertex is at (-h, k).

Problem 4 (:Q4):
What are the coordinates of the vertex in the equation y = x^2 - 6x + 13?
A) (0, 13)
B) (3, 4)
C) (3, -4)
D) (-3, 4)
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The standard form of a parabola is y = ax^2 + bx + c. To find the vertex, we need to use the formula h = -b/(2a) and k = c - b^2/(4a). For the given equation, a = 1, b = -6, and c = 13. Plugging these values into the formulas, we get h = 3 and k = 4. Therefore, the vertex is at (3, 4).

Explanation:
We need to convert the standard form to find the vertex coordinates.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to choose (0, 13) because it's the y-intercept, but the vertex is not necessarily the y-intercept.
