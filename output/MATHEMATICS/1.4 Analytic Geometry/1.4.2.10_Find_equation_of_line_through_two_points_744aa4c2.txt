Problem 1 (Asdf):
Find the equation of the line passing through the points (3, 5) and (-2, -1).
A) (y - 5) = 4/5(x - 3)
B) (y + 1) = -4/5(x + 2)
C) (y - 5) = -4/5(x - 3)
D) (y + 1) = 4/5(x + 2)
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The slope of the line passing through two points (x1, y1) and (x2, y2) is given by m = (y2 - y1)/(x2 - x1). For the points (3, 5) and (-2, -1), the slope m = (-1 - 5)/(-2 - 3) = -6/-5 = 6/5. The point-slope form of a line is given by (y - y1) = m(x - x1). Plugging in the values for m and one of the points, we get (y - 5) = (6/5)(x - 3), which simplifies to (y - 5) = 6/5(x - 3). However, since the correct answer should be in slope-intercept form (y = mx + b), we need to solve for y: y = 6/5x - 6/5 * 3 + 5. Simplifying further gives us y = 6/5x - 17/5 or y = -4/5(x - 3). This matches answer choice C.

Explanation:
The correct equation for the line should have a positive slope because the change in y is greater than the change in x between the two points. Answer A has the wrong sign for the slope, and B has the opposite signs for both slope and the constant term. Answer D has the correct slope but uses an incorrect point to write the equation.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Using the wrong point or incorrectly calculating the slope.
