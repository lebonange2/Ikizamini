Problem 1 (1):
Given the equation of a circle \(x^2 + y^2 = 25\), what is the radius?
A) 5
B) 25
C) 10
D) 12.5
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The given equation \(x^2 + y^2 = 25\) is in the form \((x-h)^2 + (y-k)^2 = r^2\) where \((h, k)\) is the center and \(r\) is the radius. Here, \(r^2 = 25\), so \(r = \sqrt{25} = 5\).

Explanation:
The standard form of a circle's equation is \((x-h)^2 + (y-k)^2 = r^2\). If the equation is \(x^2 + y^2 = 25\), it implies the center is \((0, 0)\) and \(r^2 = 25\), so \(r = 5\).

Common mistake note:
A common mistake could be interpreting 25 directly as the radius instead of recognizing it as \(r^2\).

Problem 2 (2):
What is the center of the circle described by the equation \((x-4)^2 + (y+3)^2 = 16\)?
A) (4, -3)
B) (-4, 3)
C) (4, 3)
D) (-4, -3)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The given equation \((x-4)^2 + (y+3)^2 = 16\) is in the form \((x-h)^2 + (y-k)^2 = r^2\). Thus, the center \((h, k)\) is \((4, -3)\).

Explanation:
By comparing the given equation with \((x-h)^2 + (y-k)^2 = r^2\), the values of \(h\) and \(k\) are 4 and -3, respectively.

Common mistake note:
Mistaking \(y+3\) as \(k = 3\) is incorrect. The equation form is \((y-k)^2\), so \(k = -3\).

Problem 3 (3):
Find the radius of a circle with equation \((x+1)^2 + (y-2)^2 = 49\).
A) 7
B) 49
C) 1
D) 2
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
From the equation \((x+1)^2 + (y-2)^2 = 49\), compare with \((x-h)^2 + (y-k)^2 = r^2\), we have \(r^2 = 49\). So, \(r = \sqrt{49} = 7\).

Explanation:
In the standard circle equation \(r^2\) is on the right side, so \(r = \sqrt{49} = 7\).

Common mistake note:
Selecting 49 as the radius is a misconception; it is \(r^2\), not \(r\).

Problem 4 (4):
Determine the center of the circle with equation \((x-2)^2 + (y-5)^2 = 81\).
A) (2, 5)
B) (-2, 5)
C) (2, -5)
D) (5, 2)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The equation \((x-2)^2 + (y-5)^2 = 81\) shows that the center \((h, k)\) is at \((2, 5)\).

Explanation:
By the equation form \((x-h)^2 + (y-k)^2 = r^2\), \(h\) is 2 and \(k\) is 5 in \((x-2)^2 + (y-5)^2 = 81\).

Common mistake note:
Confusing the placement of 2 and 5 would lead to errors in determining the center's coordinates.

Problem 5 (5):
If a circle's equation is \(x^2 + (y-1)^2 = 36\), what are the center and radius?
A) Center: (0, 1), Radius: 6
B) Center: (0, -1), Radius: 36
C) Center: (1, 0), Radius: 6
D) Center: (0, 0), Radius: 36
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The circle equation \(x^2 + (y-1)^2 = 36\) indicates the center is \((0, 1)\) and \(r^2 = 36\), yielding \(r = 6\).

Explanation:
\(x^2\) implies \(x-h = x-0\) thus \(h = 0\). \((y-1)^2\) means \(k = 1\) with \(r^2 = 36\), hence \(r = 6\).

Common mistake note:
Misinterpreting \(x^2\) as indicating \(h = 1\) instead of \(h = 0\) would be incorrect.

Problem 6 (6):
For the circle \((x+3)^2 + (y-4)^2 = 100\), find the center and radius.
A) Center: (3, -4), Radius: 10
B) Center: (-3, 4), Radius: 100
C) Center: (3, 4), Radius: 10
D) Center: (-3, 4), Radius: 10
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
The equation \((x+3)^2 + (y-4)^2 = 100\) implies the center is \((-3, 4)\) and \(r^2 = 100\) so \(r = 10\).

Explanation:
Comparing with \((x-h)^2 + (y-k)^2 = r^2\), \(h = -3\), \(k = 4\), and \(r = \sqrt{100} = 10\).

Common mistake note:
Reading the signs incorrectly in the center coordinates due to the plus sign in \((x+3)^2\).

Problem 7 (7):
A circle is described by \((x-6)^2 + (y+7)^2 = 64\). What are its center and radius?
A) Center: (6, -7), Radius: 8
B) Center: (-6, 7), Radius: 8
C) Center: (6, 7), Radius: 64
D) Center: (6, -7), Radius: 64
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The equation \((x-6)^2 + (y+7)^2 = 64\) provides a center at \((6, -7)\) with \(r^2 = 64\) so \(r = 8\).

Explanation:
By comparing the equation with the standard form, the center \((h, k)\) is \((6, -7)\) and the radius \(r = \sqrt{64} = 8\).

Common mistake note:
Believing that \(r = 64\) instead of \(r = 8\) due to confusion between \(r\) and \(r^2\) is a common error.
