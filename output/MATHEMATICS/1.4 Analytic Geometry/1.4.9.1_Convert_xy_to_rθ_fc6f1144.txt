Problem 1 (Asdf):
Convert the point (3, 4) in Cartesian coordinates to polar coordinates (r, θ). Round to two decimal places.
A) (5.00, 0.93)
B) (7.00, 0.62)
C) (5.00, 1.25)
D) (7.00, 1.41)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The formula to convert from Cartesian coordinates (x, y) to polar coordinates (r, θ) is r = √(x² + y²) and θ = arctan(y/x). For the point (3, 4), we calculate: r ≈ 5.00 and θ ≈ 0.93 radians. So the polar coordinates are approximately (5.00, 0.93).

Explanation:
The conversion from Cartesian to polar coordinates involves finding the distance (r) from the origin and the angle (θ) with respect to the positive x-axis.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to forget to convert θ from radians to degrees, which would result in choice B. Another mistake might be using the wrong order of the coordinates when calculating arctan.

Problem 3 (Qwer):
Convert the point (0, 5) in Cartesian coordinates to polar coordinates (r, θ). Round to two decimal places.
A) (5.00, 1.57)
B) (7.00, 2.36)
C) (5.00, 0.00)
D) (7.00, 3.14)
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The formula to convert from Cartesian coordinates (x, y) to polar coordinates (r, θ) is r = √(x² + y²) and θ = arctan(y/x). For the point (0, 5), we calculate: r = 5.00 and θ = π or 180°. So the polar coordinates are (5.00, 0.00) in radians. However, it's important to note that θ can also be expressed as π or any other equivalent angle like 2π - π.

Explanation:
When x is zero, arctan(y/x) is undefined because of division by zero. In this case, the point lies on the positive y-axis, so θ is π (or 180°).

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to choose an angle that's not coterminal with π, like choice A or D, which would result in a different quadrant. Another mistake might be forgetting to consider the special case when x=0.
