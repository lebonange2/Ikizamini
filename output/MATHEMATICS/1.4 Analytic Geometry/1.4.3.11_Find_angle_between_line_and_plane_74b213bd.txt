Problem 1 (1):
Find the angle between the line r = <2, 3, 4> + t<1, -1, 0> and the plane x + y - z = 5.
A) 30°
B) 60°
C) 45°
D) 90°
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The direction vector of the line is <1, -1, 0>. The normal vector of the plane is <1, 1, -1>. To find the angle between them, we use the dot product and the magnitudes of these vectors. The dot product is 0, and the magnitudes are sqrt(2), sqrt(3). The angle θ is arccos(0 / (sqrt(2)*sqrt(3))) = π/4 or 45°.

Explanation:
The dot product of two perpendicular vectors is zero, which indicates a 90° angle between them. Since the dot product is zero, the line and plane intersect at an angle of 45°.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to assume that since the normal vector of the plane and the direction vector of the line are perpendicular (dot product is zero), the angle between the line and the plane must also be 90°. However, this is not always true; in this case, the line intersects the plane at a 45° angle.

Problem 2 (2):
Find the acute angle between the line y = 3x + 1 and the xy-plane.
A) 30°
B) 45°
C) 60°
D) 90°
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The line is in slope-intercept form y = mx + b, where m = 3. The angle θ between the line and the xy-plane can be found using the tangent function: tan(θ) = |m| = 3. Thus, θ = arctan(3), which is approximately 71.56°. However, since we're looking for an acute angle, we choose the smaller positive value of arctan(-3), which is 45°.

Explanation:
The slope of a line in the xy-plane determines its angle with the x-axis. An acute angle with the xy-plane means that the angle must be less than 90°. The given line has a steep slope, but since we're looking for an acute angle, it's not 90°.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to assume that the angle between any line and the xy-plane is always 90°. This is only true if the line is perpendicular to the plane, which is not the case here.

Solution:

Since v has a non-zero z-component (6), the line does not intersect the xy-plane at all; it lies entirely above or below it. Therefore, the angle is 0°.

Explanation:
The angle between a line and the xy-plane is zero if the line does not intersect the plane. In this case, the direction vector of the line has a non-zero z-component, indicating that the line does not touch the xy-plane.

Common mistake note:

Problem 5 (5):
Find the angle between the line y = x + 1 and the plane y - z = 2.
A) 30°
B) 45°
C) 60°
D) 90°
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The line is in slope-intercept form y = mx + b, where m = 1. The normal vector of the plane is <0, 1, -1>. The angle θ between them can be found using the dot product and magnitudes: (1)(0) + (1)(1) + (0)(-1) = 1, sqrt(2), sqrt(2). The angle θ is arccos(1 / (sqrt(2)*sqrt(2))) = π/4 or 45°.

Explanation:
The slope of the line and the normal vector of the plane are used to find the angle between them. Since both vectors have a component in the y-direction, we can calculate the angle directly using the dot product and magnitudes.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to assume that since one of the variables is eliminated (y), the line must be perpendicular to the plane. However, this is not always true; in this case, the line intersects the plane at a 45° angle.
