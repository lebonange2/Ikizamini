Problem 1 (:1):
Which shape has reflection symmetry?
A) A triangle with one angle measuring 90 degrees
B) A square with sides of equal length
C) A circle (note: circles have infinite lines of rotational symmetry, but no reflection symmetry)
D) A parallelogram with unequal sides
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
A square has four lines of reflection symmetry running through its center and connecting opposite vertices. This makes option B the correct choice. The other options either have no reflection symmetry (circle), only one line of symmetry (triangle), or are not symmetrical at all (parallelogram).

Explanation:
Reflection symmetry refers to a shape that can be divided into two identical halves by a line or plane.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing C, as some might think a circle has no reflection symmetry. However, a circle actually has infinite lines of rotational symmetry but not reflection symmetry.

Problem 2 (:2):
Does the following figure have rotational symmetry? If yes, how many degrees does it rotate to match its original position?
A) No
B) Yes, 90 degrees
C) Yes, 180 degrees
D) Yes, 360 degrees
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
The figure is a regular hexagon, which has rotational symmetry of 360 degrees. It can be rotated in a full circle and still match its original position. Therefore, the correct answer is D.

Explanation:
Rotational symmetry refers to a shape that looks the same after rotating around its center by a certain angle.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing B or C, as some might think it has only 90-degree or 180-degree rotational symmetry. However, a regular hexagon can be rotated in increments of 60 degrees up to a full rotation.

Problem 3 (:3):
Identify the line(s) of symmetry for this letter: A
A) One vertical line
B) Two horizontal lines
C) No lines of symmetry
D) One diagonal line
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The letter 'A' has no lines of symmetry. None of the options provided accurately describe its symmetry properties.

Explanation:
Lines of symmetry refer to a line that divides a shape into two identical halves when reflected across it.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing A or B, as some might mistakenly think there is symmetry in the letter 'A.' However, it does not have any lines of symmetry.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 4 (:4):
Which figure has both reflection and rotational symmetry?
A) A rhombus with acute angles
B) A rectangle with opposite sides parallel
C) An equilateral triangle with equal sides
D) A parallelogram with one pair of parallel sides
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
A rectangle has both reflection symmetry (two horizontal and two vertical lines) and rotational symmetry (180 degrees). The other options either lack reflection symmetry or have only rotational symmetry.

Explanation:
Both reflection and rotational symmetries can be present in a shape, meaning it can be divided into identical halves by a line or plane and also looks the same after being rotated around its center.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing C, as some might think an equilateral triangle has both symmetries. However, it only has rotational symmetry of 120 degrees.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 5 (:5):
Does a regular pentagon have reflection symmetry?
A) Yes
B) No
C) It depends on the size of the pentagon
D) It depends on the orientation of the pentagon
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
A regular pentagon has no reflection symmetry. It only has rotational symmetry.

Explanation:
Reflection symmetry requires a shape to be identical after being reflected across a line, and a regular pentagon does not have this property.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing A, as some might think that because it's symmetrical, there must be reflection symmetry. However, the only symmetries present in a regular pentagon are rotational.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 6 (:6):
Which shape has the most lines of reflection symmetry?
A) A square
B) A hexagon
C) A decagon
D) A dodecagon
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
A dodecagon (12-sided polygon) has 12 lines of reflection symmetry, more than a square (4), hexagon (6), or decagon (2).

Explanation:
The number of lines of reflection symmetry in a regular polygon is equal to the number of sides it has.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing A, B, or C, as some might think that more complex shapes have fewer lines of symmetry. However, the number of lines increases with the number of sides.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 7 (:7):
Identify the type of symmetry exhibited by this figure: a circle with a dot in the center.
A) Reflection symmetry
B) Rotational symmetry
C) Both reflection and rotational symmetry
D) No symmetry
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The figure has only rotational symmetry, as it can be rotated around its center without changing appearance. The dot in the center does not affect this property.

Explanation:
Rotational symmetry refers to a shape that looks the same after being rotated around its center by a certain angle.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing A or C, as some might think there is reflection symmetry due to the dot. However, the dot does not create any lines of reflection symmetry.
