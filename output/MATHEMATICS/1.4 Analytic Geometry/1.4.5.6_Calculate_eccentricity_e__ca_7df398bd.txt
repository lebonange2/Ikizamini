Problem 1 (:1):
What is the eccentricity of an ellipse with a focus at (3, 0) and a vertex at (5, 0)?
A) 0.25
B) 0.4
C) 0.6
D) 0.8
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The distance from the focus to the center is c = √((3 - 5)^2) = 2, and the semi-major axis a is equal to the distance from the vertex to the center, which is √((5 - 3)^2) = 2. Using the formula e = c/a, we get e = 2/2 = 1. However, for an ellipse, eccentricity should be between 0 and 1. In this case, there might be an error in the given information or calculation. The correct eccentricity for an ellipse is between 0 and 1.

Explanation:
The eccentricity of an ellipse should be between 0 and 1. A value of 1 indicates a circle, not an ellipse. The given coordinates suggest that the shape might be a hyperbola instead of an ellipse.

Common mistake: Assuming the focus and vertex coordinates are correct for an ellipse, which leads to an eccentricity greater than 1.

Problem 3 (:3):
Find the eccentricity of an ellipse with semi-major axis 10 and semi-minor axis 6.
A) 0.4
B) 0.6
C) 0.8
D) 1
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The formula for eccentricity is e = √(1 - (b^2/a^2)), where a is the semi-major axis and b is the semi-minor axis. Plugging in the values, we get e = √(1 - (6^2/10^2)) = √(1 - 0.36) = √(0.64) = 0.8.

Explanation:
The eccentricity of an ellipse is calculated using the semi-major and semi-minor axes, as given by the formula e = √(1 - (b^2/a^2)).

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Forgetting to square the semi-minor axis or the semi-major axis in the calculation.

Problem 4 (:4):

A) 1
B) 2
C) 3
D) 4
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The formula for eccentricity is e = c/a, where c is the distance from the center to the focus and a is the semi-major axis. Given that e = 0.6 and a = √((5 - 3)^2) = 2, we can solve for c: 0.6 = c/2 => c = 0.6 * 2 = 1.2. However, since eccentricity is usually rounded to two decimal places, the exact value of c might be slightly different from 1.2, but it's close enough for this question.

Explanation:
The distance from the center to the focus (c) can be found by rearranging the formula e = c/a and plugging in the given eccentricity and semi-major axis values.

Common mistake: Rounding the eccentricity value before calculating c, leading to a slightly different result for c.
