Problem 1 (Q1):
Which of the following is a vector function that represents a helix in space?
A) r(t) = (cos(t), sin(t), t)
B) r(t) = (t^2, t^3, t^4)
C) r(t) = (e^t, e^(-t), t)
D) r(t) = (t, t*sin(t), t*cos(t))
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The helix is defined by r(t) = (cos(t), sin(t), t) where the circular motion is in the xy-plane and the linearly increasing t represents the upward movement along the z-axis.

Explanation:
A helix is characterized by a circular path along with linear progression in another direction. Option A satisfies this condition.

Common mistake note:
Students might confuse option D as a helix due to sine and cosine components, but their t-dependent scaling leads to a different shape.

Problem 2 (Q2):
What is the curvature of the space curve r(t) = (t, t^2, t^3)?
A) 0
B) 1
C) |1 + 4t^2|^(-3/2)
D) |2t + 4t^5|^(-3/2)
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
First, compute r'(t) = (1, 2t, 3t^2) and r''(t) = (0, 2, 6t). The curvature κ(t) is given by κ(t) = |r'(t) × r''(t)| / |r'(t)|^3. The cross-product is (4t^2, -6t, 2), and thus, |r'(t) × r''(t)| = √((4t^2)^2 + (-6t)^2 + 2^2) = √(16t^4 + 36t^2 + 4). The magnitude |r'(t)| = √(1 + 4t^2 + 9t^4). Thus, κ(t) = √(16t^4 + 36t^2 + 4) / (1 + 4t^2 + 9t^4)^(3/2).

Explanation:
Curvature measures how quickly a curve deviates from a straight line. Compute curvature using κ(t) = |r'(t) × r''(t)| / |r'(t)|^3.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to neglect computing the magnitude of the cross product properly, leading to errors in the curvature formula.

Problem 3 (Q3):
Consider the curve r(t) = (3t, 2sqrt(2)t, t^2). What is its parametrization at t=2?
A) (6, 4sqrt(2), 4)
B) (4, 4sqrt(2), 2)
C) (2, 2sqrt(2), 2)
D) (6, 4, 4)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Substitute t = 2 into r(t): r(2) = (3(2), 2sqrt(2)(2), (2)^2) = (6, 4sqrt(2), 4).

Explanation:
Parametrization involves finding specific points on the curve for given values of t.

Common mistake note:
Students might forget the correct application of the square root term, leading to inaccurate results.

Problem 4 (Q4):
Which formula provides the torsion τ of a space curve given r(t)?
A) τ = (r'(t) × r''(t)) • r'''(t) / |r'(t) × r''(t)|^2
B) τ = |r'(t)| / |r(t)|
C) τ = |r''(t) × r'''(t)| / |r'(t)|
D) τ = (r'(t) • r''(t))^2 / |r(t)|^3
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The torsion of a space curve is defined by τ = (r'(t) × r''(t)) • r'''(t) / |r'(t) × r''(t)|^2, which indicates twisting of the curve.

Explanation:
Torsion measures how a curve twists out of the plane of curvature, computed from derivatives of the curve's vector function.

Common mistake note:
Confusion might arise by ignoring the need for the cross product and focusing only on dot product terms.

Problem 5 (Q5):
In a space curve r(t) = (t, t^2, t^3), what is the unit tangent vector T(t) at t=1?
A) (1/√14, 2/√14, 3/√14)
B) (1/3, 2/3, 1)
C) (1, 1, 1)
D) (1/3, 1/3, 1/3)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Calculate r'(t) = (1, 2t, 3t^2). At t=1, r'(1) = (1, 2, 3). The magnitude |r'(1)| = √(1^2 + 2^2 + 3^2) = √14. Thus, T(t) = (1/√14, 2/√14, 3/√14).

Explanation:
The unit tangent vector is found by normalizing the derivative at a given point, providing directionality on the curve.

Common mistake note:
Students may miscalculate the magnitude by simply adding instead of taking the square root of the sum of squares.

Problem 6 (Q6):
What is the geometric significance of the normal vector to a space curve at a given point?
A) It indicates the axial symmetry of the curve.
B) It shows the direction towards the center of curvature.
C) It is parallel to the tangent vector.
D) It describes the twisting of the curve.
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The normal vector at a given point in a curve is perpendicular to the tangent vector and points towards the circle of curvature.

Explanation:
The normal vector helps in understanding how sharply a curve is bending at a given point.

Common mistake note:
Confusion may occur confusing the normal vector with torsion, but torsion is related to twisting, not direct curvature.

Problem 7 (Q7):
For the curve r(t) = (sin(t), cos(t), t), what are the Frenet-Serret equations used to describe?
A) The equations of motion in free space.
B) The relationship between curvature, torsion, and spatial orientation.
C) The trajectory of projectile motion.
D) The solution to the Laplace's equation.
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The Frenet-Serret equations describe the derivative relations of the tangent, normal, and binormal vectors in terms of curvature and torsion, thus explaining the curve’s spatial twisting and bending.

Explanation:
Their significance lies in providing a complete description of the curve's geometry in three-dimensional space.

Common mistake note:
Mixing up with equations of motion like Newton's laws might lead students to incorrectly associate Frenet-Serret equations with mechanics.
