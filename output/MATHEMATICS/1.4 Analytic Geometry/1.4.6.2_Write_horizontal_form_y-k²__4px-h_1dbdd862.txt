Problem 1 (Q1):
Rewrite the equation in horizontal form given that it represents a parabola with vertex (h, k):
A) (x-k)² = 4p(y-h)
B) (y-k)² = -4p(x-h)
C) (x-h)² = 4p(y-k)
D) (x-h)² = -4p(y-k)
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The standard form of a horizontal parabola with vertex (h, k) is π(x - h)² = 4p(y - k), where p is the focus-directrix distance. The correct answer matches this form with x and y switched.

Explanation:
In a horizontal parabola, the axis of symmetry is parallel to the x-axis, so the equation should have (x-h)² on one side and 4p(y-k) on the other side.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing A, which represents a vertical parabola with vertex (k, h). Remember that the vertex form for a horizontal parabola has x and y switched compared to a vertical parabola.

Problem 2 (Q2):
Write the equation of a horizontal parabola with vertex at (3, -2) and focus at (-1, -2):
A) (x-3)² = 4p(y+2)
B) (y+2)² = 4p(x-3)
C) (x-3)² = 4(-6)(y+2)
D) (x-3)² = 4(6)(y+2)
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The focus-directrix distance (p) can be found using the distance formula between the vertex and focus. The distance is |-1 - 3| = 4, so p = 4 / 2 = 2. Plugging in h = 3, k = -2, and p = 2 gives us (x-3)² = 4(-6)(y+2).

Explanation:
The focus-directrix distance is half the difference between the x-coordinates of the vertex and focus. Once we have p, we substitute it into the horizontal form equation.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing A or B, which are for vertical parabolas. Ensure that you use the correct form based on the orientation of the parabola.

Problem 4 (Q4):
Given the equation (y-4)² = 16(x+2), what is the standard form of the equation in horizontal orientation?
A) (x+2)² = -4(y-4)
B) (x+2)² = 4(y-4)
C) (x-4)² = -16(y+2)
D) (x-4)² = 16(y+2)
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
To convert the equation to horizontal form, switch x and y, then adjust the coefficients accordingly. The new equation becomes (x-4)² = 16(y+2).

Explanation:
Switching x and y in the original equation gives us (x-4)² = 16(y+2), which is the standard form for a horizontal parabola.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing A or B, which represent vertical parabolas. Ensure that you switch x and y correctly when converting to horizontal form.

Problem 5 (Q5):
What is the equation of a horizontal parabola with vertex at (0, 0) and focus at (3, 0)?
A) (x-3)² = -4p(y)
B) (y)² = 4p(x+3)
C) (x)² = 4p(y+3)
D) (x)² = 4(3)(y)
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
The focus-directrix distance (p) is the same as the x-coordinate of the focus, so p = 3. Plugging in h = 0, k = 0, and p = 3 gives us (x)² = 4(3)(y).

Explanation:
In a parabola with vertex at the origin and focus on the positive x-axis, the focus-directrix distance is equal to the x-coordinate of the focus. Once we have p, we substitute it into the horizontal form equation.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing A or B, which are for vertical parabolas. Remember that the vertex form for a horizontal parabola has x and y switched compared to a vertical parabola.
