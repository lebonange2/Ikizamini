Problem 1 (InfiniteTangent):
Find the slope of the tangent line to the curve y = x^3 - 2x at the point (1, -1).
A) -1
B) 0
C) 1
D) Infinite
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
The derivative of y = x^3 - 2x is y' = 3x^2 - 2. At the point (1, -1), the derivative is y'(1) = 3(1)^2 - 2 = 1. However, as x approaches 1 from both sides, y approaches infinity, indicating a vertical tangent line with infinite slope.

Explanation:
This question assesses understanding of finding the derivative to determine the slope of a tangent line and recognizing when the slope becomes infinite due to a vertical tangent.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to calculate the derivative and plug in x = 1, which gives a finite value (1). However, this does not account for the behavior of the function near the point where the tangent line is actually vertical.

Problem 2 (ZeroDerivative):
Find the slope of the tangent line to the curve y = x^2 sin(x) at the point (0, 0).
A) 0
B) 1
C) -1
D) π
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The derivative of y = x^2 sin(x) is y' = 2x sin(x) + x^2 cos(x). At the point (0, 0), the derivative is y'(0) = 0. This means that the tangent line at this point is horizontal.

Explanation:
This question tests the ability to find the derivative and apply it to a specific point on the curve, even when the function value itself is zero.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to assume that since the function value is zero at (0, 0), the slope of the tangent line must also be zero. However, this neglects to consider the derivative, which determines the local behavior of the curve.

Problem 3 (QuadraticFunction):
Find the equation of the tangent line to the parabola y = x^2 + 3x - 4 at the point (1, 0).
A) y = -2x + 2
B) y = 2x - 2
C) y = x - 1
D) y = -x + 1
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
The derivative of y = x^2 + 3x - 4 is y' = 2x + 3. At the point (1, 0), the slope of the tangent line is m = 5. The equation of the tangent line in point-slope form is y = 5(x - 1) or y = -x + 1.

Explanation:
This question assesses understanding of finding the derivative to determine the slope and using point-slope form to write the equation of a tangent line.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to forget to evaluate the derivative at the given point, resulting in an incorrect slope. Another mistake might be to incorrectly apply the point-slope formula, leading to an incorrect equation.

Problem 4 (CubicFunction):
Find the equation of the tangent line to the curve y = x^3 - 2x^2 + 1 at the point (1, 0).
A) y = -x + 1
B) y = x - 1
C) y = 2x - 2
D) y = 3x - 3
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The derivative of y = x^3 - 2x^2 + 1 is y' = 3x^2 - 4x. At the point (1, 0), the slope of the tangent line is m = -1. The equation of the tangent line in point-slope form is y = -x + 1.

Explanation:
This question tests the ability to find the derivative, determine the slope at a specific point, and write the equation of the tangent line using point-slope form.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to misapply the power rule when finding the derivative, leading to an incorrect slope. Another possible error is in applying the point-slope formula incorrectly.

Problem 5 (ExponentialFunction):
Find the equation of the tangent line to the curve y = e^x at the point (0, 1).
A) y = x + 1
B) y = x
C) y = 2x - 1
D) y = 0
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The derivative of y = e^x is y' = e^x. At the point (0, 1), the slope of the tangent line is m = 1. The equation of the tangent line in point-slope form is y = x + 1.

Explanation:
This question assesses understanding of differentiating an exponential function and using the derivative to find the slope of a tangent line at a specific point.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to forget that e^0 is equal to 1, resulting in an incorrect slope. Another possible error is in applying the point-slope formula incorrectly.

Problem 7 (TrigonometricFunction):
Find the equation of the tangent line to the curve y = sin(x) at the point (π/2, 1).
A) y = x - π/2
B) y = x + π/2
C) y = 0
D) y = cos(π/2)x
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The derivative of y = sin(x) is y' = cos(x). At the point (π/2, 1), the slope of the tangent line is m = 0. The equation of the tangent line in point-slope form is y = 1, which is a horizontal line.

Explanation:
This question evaluates understanding of differentiating a trigonometric function and using the derivative to find the slope of a tangent line at a specific point.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to forget that cos(π/2) is equal to 0, resulting in an incorrect slope. Another possible error is in applying the point-slope formula incorrectly.
