Problem 1 (Asdf):
Which pair of planes has the same normal vector and constant term, indicating parallelism?
A) [0, 2, 1], [4, 6, 3]
B) [1, 0, 2], [5, 1, 3]
C) [2, 1, 0], [6, 3, 4]
D) [3, 2, 1], [7, 5, 6]
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Parallel planes have the same normal vector and constant term. Choices A and C share the same normal vector, but choice C has a different constant term. Only choice A has both the same normal vector and constant term, indicating parallelism.

Explanation:
To determine if two planes are parallel, compare their normal vectors and ensure they have the same constant term. If these conditions are met, the planes are parallel.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to assume that if two planes have the same normal vector, they are automatically parallel without checking the constant terms.

Problem 2 (Bghj):
What is the relationship between the planes defined by 2x + 3y - z = 5 and 4x + 6y - 2z = 10?
A) Parallel
B) Intersecting at a line
C) Intersecting at a point
D) Coincident
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The normal vectors for both planes are [2, 3, -1] and [4, 6, -2], respectively. These vectors are scalar multiples of each other (with a factor of 2), indicating that the planes are parallel.

Explanation:
Parallelism between planes is determined by comparing their normal vectors. If they are scalar multiples of each other, the planes are parallel. In this case, the normal vectors differ only by a scale factor, so the planes are parallel.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to assume that different constant terms imply non-parallel planes, but this does not affect parallelism.

Problem 3 (Qwer):
Are the planes defined by x + y + z = 1 and 2x + 2y + 2z = 2 parallel?
A) Yes
B) No
C) Cannot be determined without more information
D) Depends on the specific values of x, y, and z
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The normal vectors for both planes are [1, 1, 1] and [2, 2, 2], respectively. These vectors are not scalar multiples of each other, so the planes are not parallel.

Explanation:
To determine if two planes are parallel, compare their normal vectors. If they are scalar multiples of each other, the planes are parallel. In this case, the normal vectors have different components and are not scalar multiples, indicating that the planes intersect or are skew.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to think that equal coefficients imply parallelism, but it's the direction vector (normal vector) that determines parallelism.

Problem 4 (Yuiop):
Find a value for k so that the planes 3x + 4y - 5z = 6 and 6x + 8y - kz = 12 are parallel.
A) k = 10
B) k = 9
C) k = 15
D) No value of k will make the planes parallel
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
For the planes to be parallel, their normal vectors must be scalar multiples. The normal vectors are [3, 4, -5] and [6, 8, -k]. To find k, set the second vector as a multiple of the first: 6 = 3m, 8 = 4m, and -k = -5m. Solving for m and k gives m = 2 and k = 10.

Explanation:
To make two planes parallel, their normal vectors must be scalar multiples of each other. By setting the second vector as a multiple of the first and solving for k, we find that k = 10 will result in parallel planes.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to assume that equal coefficients imply parallelism without setting up a system of equations to solve for k.

Problem 5 (Bvnmm):
Which pair of normal vectors corresponds to parallel planes?

D) [4, 8, 12], [6, 12, 18]
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The normal vectors for each choice are compared. Choice A has different components, choice B is a scalar multiple of choice A (not parallel), and choice D is also a scalar multiple of choice C. Only choice C has normal vectors that are not scalar multiples of each other.

Explanation:
Parallel planes have the same normal vector. In this case, only choice C has normal vectors that are not scalar multiples, indicating that no pair in the choices is parallel.

Common mistake note:

Problem 6 (Xcvbn):
Find the equation of a plane parallel to 4x - 3y + 2z = 7 and passing through (1, 2, 3).
A) 4x - 3y + 2z = 16
B) 8x - 6y + 4z = 28
C) 4x - 3y + 2z = 7
D) 12x - 9y + 6z = 21
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The normal vector for the given plane is [4, -3, 2]. To find a parallel plane, we can use the point-normal form: A(x - x0) + B(y - y0) + C(z - z0) = 0. Plugging in the normal vector and the point (1, 2, 3), we get 4(x - 1) - 3(y - 2) + 2(z - 3) = 0, which simplifies to 4x - 3y + 2z = 16.

Explanation:
To find a plane parallel to another given plane and passing through a specific point, use the point-normal form of the equation. The normal vector remains the same, and the point is used to solve for the constant term.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to assume that the constant term in the new equation will be the same as the original plane. Instead, it's determined by plugging in the given point into the point-normal form.

Problem 7 (Poiuy):
Are the planes defined by 3x + 6y - 9z = 1 and 2(3x + 6y - 9z) = 2 parallel?
A) Yes
B) No
C) Cannot be determined without more information
D) Depends on the specific values of x, y, and z
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The second equation simplifies to 6x + 12y - 18z = 2. The normal vectors for both planes are [3, 6, -9] and [6, 12, -18], respectively. These vectors are scalar multiples of each other (with a factor of 2), indicating that the planes are parallel.

Explanation:
Parallelism between planes is determined by comparing their normal vectors. If they are scalar multiples of each other, the planes are parallel. In this case, the second equation simplifies to reveal that both planes have the same normal vector, making them parallel.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to think that because one equation is a multiple of the other, the planes must be different or non-parallel. However, when the equations are simplified, they may reveal parallelism.
