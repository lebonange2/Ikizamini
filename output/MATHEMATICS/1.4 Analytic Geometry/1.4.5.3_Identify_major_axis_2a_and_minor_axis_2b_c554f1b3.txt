Problem 1 (?):
What are the lengths of the major and minor axes in an ellipse with equation 4x^2 + 9y^2 = 36?
A) Major: 3, Minor: 2
B) Major: 2, Minor: 3
C) Major: 6, Minor: 4
D) Major: 4, Minor: 6
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The equation of an ellipse in standard form is (x^2/a^2) + (y^2/b^2) = 1. Comparing this to the given equation 4x^2 + 9y^2 = 36, we have a^2 = 9/4 and b^2 = 4/9. Taking square roots gives us the lengths of the major axis (2a) and minor axis (2b): Major: 2√(9/4) = 3, Minor: 2√(4/9) = 2.

Explanation:
The major axis is the longest diameter of an ellipse, while the minor axis is the shortest. The standard form equation provides the squares of the semi-major and semi-minor axes, which are then doubled to get the actual lengths.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to confuse a^2 with b^2 or to forget to double the square roots when finding the actual axis lengths.

Problem 2 (?):
In the equation 9x^2 + 16y^2 = 144, which value represents the length of the major axis?
A) 9
B) 16
C) 12
D) 8
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The equation of an ellipse in standard form is (x^2/a^2) + (y^2/b^2) = 1. Comparing this to the given equation 9x^2 + 16y^2 = 144, we have a^2 = 144/9 and b^2 = 144/16. The length of the major axis is 2√(a^2) = 2√(16) = 12.

Explanation:
The major axis is directly related to the coefficient of x^2 in the standard form equation, after adjusting for the constant term on the right side of the equation. Once a^2 is found, double the square root to get the actual length.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to confuse a^2 with b^2 or to forget to multiply by 2 when finding the major axis length.

Problem 3 (?):
What are the lengths of the major and minor axes for the ellipse described by the equation (x - 3)^2 + 4(y - 2)^2 = 16?
A) Major: 4, Minor: 2
B) Major: 8, Minor: 4
C) Major: 2, Minor: 1
D) Major: 6, Minor: 4
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The equation of an ellipse in standard form is (x - h)^2/a^2 + (y - k)^2/b^2 = 1, where (h, k) is the center of the ellipse. Comparing this to the given equation, we have a^2 = 16 and b^2 = 1/4. The lengths of the major and minor axes are thus Major: 2√(a^2) = 8, Minor: 2√(b^2) = 4.

Explanation:
The standard form equation with a shifted center still provides the squares of the semi-major and semi-minor axes. After finding a^2 and b^2, double the square roots to get the actual lengths of the major and minor axes.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to forget that the given equation has a shifted center, which means the standard form must be adjusted accordingly.

A) Major: 10, Minor: 5

Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The equation of an ellipse in standard form is (x^2/a^2) + (y^2/b^2) = 1. Comparing this to the given equation 25x^2 + y^2 = 100, we have a^2 = 100/25 and b^2 = 100. The lengths of the major and minor axes are thus Major: 2√(a^2) = 10, Minor: 2√(b^2) = 10.

Explanation:
The major axis is determined by the coefficient of x^2, while the minor axis is determined by the coefficient of y^2. After finding a^2 and b^2, double the square roots to get the actual lengths of the axes.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to mix up the roles of a^2 and b^2 or to forget to multiply by 2 when finding the axis lengths.

Problem 5 (?):
For the ellipse with equation (x - 1)^2/9 + (y - 2)^2/4 = 1, which value represents the length of the minor axis?
A) 3
B) 4
C) 6
D) 8
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The equation of an ellipse in standard form is (x - h)^2/a^2 + (y - k)^2/b^2 = 1, where (h, k) is the center of the ellipse. Comparing this to the given equation, we have b^2 = 4. The length of the minor axis is thus 2√(b^2) = 2√4 = 4.

Explanation:
The standard form equation with a shifted center still provides the square of the semi-minor axis. After finding b^2, double the square root to get the actual length of the minor axis.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to forget that the given equation has a shifted center, which means the standard form must be adjusted accordingly.
