Problem 1 (:1):
Find the equation of the surface defined by the function f(x, y) = x^2 + y^2 at point (3, 4).
A) (x - 3)^2 + (y - 4)^2 = 0
B) (x - 9)^2 + (y - 16)^2 = 25
C) x^2 + y^2 = 9
D) x^2 + y^2 = 16
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The equation of the surface at a specific point is found by plugging in the coordinates of that point into the function. So, we substitute x with 3 and y with 4 in f(x, y) = x^2 + y^2: (3)^2 + (4)^2 = 9 + 16 = 25. The equation becomes (x - 3)^2 + (y - 4)^2 = 25.

Explanation:
The correct answer represents the surface equation evaluated at the point (3, 4).

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to forget to square the differences in the resulting equation. Answer A lacks this step and thus gives an incorrect result.

Problem 2 (:2):
Find the equation of the surface z = x^2 + y^2 at point (1, 2).
A) z = 5
B) z - 5 = 0
C) x^2 + y^2 = 3
D) x + y = 3
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The equation of the surface at a specific point is found by plugging in the coordinates of that point into the function. So, we substitute x with 1 and y with 2 in z = x^2 + y^2: z = (1)^2 + (2)^2 = 5. The equation becomes z - 5 = 0.

Explanation:
The correct answer represents the surface equation evaluated at the point (1, 2).

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to write only the value of z instead of setting it equal to zero as an implicit equation. Answer A does this and gives a different type of equation.

C) x^2 - 2xy + y^2 = 2
D) x^2 - 2xy + y^2 = 1
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The equation of the surface at a specific point is found by plugging in the coordinates of that point into the function. So, we substitute x with 1 and y with 1 in f(x, y) = x^2 - 2xy + y^2: (1)^2 - 2(1)(1) + (1)^2 = 0. The equation becomes (x - 1)^2 - 2(x - 1)(y - 1) + (y - 1)^2 = 0.

Explanation:
The correct answer represents the surface equation evaluated at the point (1, 1).

Common mistake note:
