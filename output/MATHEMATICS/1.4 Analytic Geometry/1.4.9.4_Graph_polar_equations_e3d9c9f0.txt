Problem 1 (:1):
Sketch the graph of the polar equation: r = 2 + sin(θ)
A) A circle with radius 3 centered at (0, 0)
B) A cardioid with a loop around the origin
C) A straight line passing through the pole at an angle of π/6
D) A spiral starting from the pole and winding outwards
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The graph of r = 2 + sin(θ) is a cardioid because it can be derived from the general form r = a + bsin(θ), which represents a cardioid. The loop around the origin indicates that there's a cusp at θ = π.

Explanation:
Cardioids are heart-shaped curves formed by the intersection of a cone with a plane parallel to its side. In this case, since a > b (a = 2 and b = 1), the cardioid has a loop around the origin.

Common mistake note:
Choosing A might be due to adding the radius (2) and the maximum value of sin(θ) (1), which would result in a circle with radius 3. However, this is not correct because the equation combines r and sin(θ) linearly.

Problem 2 (:2):
Sketch the graph of the polar equation: r = 4cos(2θ)
A) A circle with radius 4 centered at (0, 0)
B) An Archimedean spiral starting from the pole and winding outwards
C) A concentric series of circles with decreasing radii
D) A cardioid without a loop around the origin
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The graph of r = 4cos(2θ) represents a family of concentric circles because it can be derived from the general form r = Acos(nθ), which forms concentric circles when n is an integer greater than 1. The radius varies as cos(2θ) changes.

Explanation:
Archimedean spirals are formed by equations where r is a function of θ, not its multiple like in this case. A cardioid would have sinθ instead of cosθ, and a circle would have no dependence on θ inside the trigonometric function.

Common mistake note:
Choosing B might be due to confusion with polar equations that create spirals (e.g., r = aθ). However, in this case, the presence of 2θ makes it a series of concentric circles.

Problem 3 (:3):
Sketch the graph of the polar equation: r = 3sin(3θ)
A) A circle with radius 3 centered at (0, 0)
B) An Archimedean spiral starting from the pole and winding outwards
C) A rose curve with petal count 6
D) A cardioid with a loop around the origin
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The graph of r = 3sin(3θ) is a rose curve because it can be derived from the general form r = a sin(nθ), which forms petal-likeshapes called roses. The number of petals is determined by n, in this case, 6.

Explanation:
Rose curves have an even number of petals when n is odd and an infinite number of petals when n is even. In this case, with n = 3, there are 6 petals. A circle would not have a trigonometric function inside r, and a cardioid would use cosθ.

Common mistake note:
Choosing D might be due to confusion with the form of a cardioid (r = a + bsin(θ)), but in this case, the sine function indicates a rose curve.

Problem 4 (:4):
Sketch the graph of the polar equation: r = 5e^(2θ)
A) A logarithmic spiral starting from the pole and winding outwards
B) An Archimedean spiral starting from the pole and winding outwards
C) A circle with radius 5 centered at (0, 0)
D) A cardioid without a loop around the origin
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The graph of r = 5e^(2θ) is a logarithmic spiral because it involves an exponential function. Logarithmic spirals have a continuously changing radius and angle, creating a spiral shape.

Explanation:
Logarithmic spirals are formed by equations where r is an exponential function of θ. An Archimedean spiral would involve a trigonometric function like cosθ or sinθ, and a circle would not have any variable inside the function.

Common mistake note:
Choosing B might be due to confusion with Archimedean spirals, but in this case, the exponential function (e^(2θ)) creates a logarithmic spiral.

Explanation:
Cardioids are heart-shaped curves formed by the intersection of a cone with a plane parallel to its side. In this case, since a > b (a = 1 and b = 1), the cardioid does not have a loop around the origin.

Common mistake note:

D) A cardioid without a loop around the origin

Solution:
