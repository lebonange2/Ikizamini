Problem 1 (:1):
A shape is transformed by scaling with a factor of 2. How does the area change?
A) Stays the same
B) Increases by a factor of 2
C) Increases by a factor of 4
D) Decreases by a factor of 1/2
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
When scaling a shape with a factor of 2, each dimension is multiplied by 2. Area is calculated as length times width, so multiplying both dimensions by 2 results in an area increase of 4 (2^2). Therefore, the correct answer is C: Increases by a factor of 4.

Explanation:
Scaling affects area differently than it does perimeter or shape; remember that area scales with the square of the scaling factor.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing A, thinking that area would stay the same. This is incorrect because area increases proportionally to the square of the scale factor.

Problem 2 (:2):
A rectangle has a length of 6 units and width of 3 units. After scaling by a factor of 1/3, what is its new area?
A) 54 units^2
B) 18 units^2
C) 6 units^2
D) 2 units^2
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
The new length after scaling by 1/3 is 6 / 3 = 2 units, and the new width is 3 / 3 = 1 unit. The new area is 2 * 1 = 2 units^2. So, the correct answer is D: 2 units^2.

Explanation:
Scaling reduces both dimensions proportionally; calculate the new dimensions and multiply them to find the new area.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Multiplying the original area by 1/3. This would be correct for perimeter, but not for area which scales with the square of the scale factor.

Problem 3 (:3):
A triangle has an area of 24 square units. If it is scaled up by a factor of 5, what will its new area be?
A) 120 square units
B) 600 square units
C) 1200 square units
D) 300 square units
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The new area after scaling by a factor of 5 is 24 * (5^2) = 24 * 25 = 600 square units. So, the correct answer is B: 600 square units.

Explanation:
Area scales with the square of the scale factor; multiply the original area by the square of the scaling factor.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Multiplying the original area by 5. This would be correct for linear dimensions, but not for area which scales with the square of the scale factor.

Problem 4 (:4):
A circle has a radius of 4 units. If it is scaled down by a factor of 2, what will its new circumference be?
A) 8π units
B) 16π units
C) 32π units
D) 64π units
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The new radius after scaling by a factor of 2 is 4 / 2 = 2 units. The circumference of a circle is 2πr, so the new circumference is 2π * 2 = 8π units. So, the correct answer is A: 8π units.

Explanation:
Circumference scales linearly with the scale factor; calculate the new radius and multiply by 2π.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Multiplying the original circumference by 1/2. This would be correct for area, but not for circumference which scales linearly with the scale factor.

Problem 5 (:5):
A square has an area of 36 square units. If it is scaled up by a factor of 4, what will its new perimeter be?
A) 12 units
B) 24 units
C) 48 units
D) 96 units
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The new side length after scaling by a factor of 4 is 	extsqrt{36 * 4} = 12 units. The perimeter of a square is 4s, so the new perimeter is 4 * 12 = 48 units. So, the correct answer is C: 48 units.

Explanation:
Perimeter scales linearly with the scale factor; calculate the new side length and multiply by 4.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Multiplying the original perimeter by 4. This would be correct for area, but not for perimeter which scales linearly with the scale factor.

Problem 6 (:6):
A cube has a volume of 64 cubic units. If it is scaled down by a factor of 3, what will its new volume be?
A) 216 cubic units
B) 72 cubic units
C) 27 cubic units
D) 8 cubic units
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
The new side length after scaling by a factor of 3 is 	extsqrt[3]{64 / 3} = 4/3 units. The volume of a cube is s^3, so the new volume is (4/3)^3 = 8 cubic units. So, the correct answer is D: 8 cubic units.

Explanation:
Volume scales with the cube of the scale factor; calculate the new side length and raise it to the power of 3.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Multiplying the original volume by 1/3. This would be correct for linear dimensions, but not for volume which scales with the cube of the scale factor.

Problem 7 (:7):
A parallelogram has a base of 8 units and height of 6 units. If it is scaled up by a factor of 2, what will its new area be?
A) 96 square units
B) 48 square units
C) 24 square units
D) 12 square units
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The new base after scaling by a factor of 2 is 8 * (1/2) = 4 units, and the new height is 6 * (1/2) = 3 units. The new area is 4 * 3 = 12 square units. So, the correct answer is B: 12 square units.

Explanation:
Area scales with the product of scaling factors for base and height; calculate the new dimensions and multiply them.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Multiplying the original area by 2. This would be correct if only one dimension was scaled, but not when both are scaled independently.
