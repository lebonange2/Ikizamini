Problem 1 (Asymmetric key encryption):
Which of the following is an example of asymmetric key encryption?
A) AES
B) RSA
C) MD5
D) SHA-256
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
RSA is a well-known example of asymmetric encryption. AES, MD5, and SHA-256 are hashing or symmetric encryption algorithms.

Explanation:
AES and SHA-256 use the same key for encryption and decryption (symmetric), while MD5 is a hash function. RSA uses pairs of keys (asymmetric).

Common mistake note:
Choosing A  (AES) or C  (MD5) as they are symmetric or hashing, not asymmetric.

Problem 2 (Digital signatures):
What is the primary purpose of digital signatures in cryptography?
A) Securing data during transmission
B) Verifying the authenticity of a message
C) Encrypting large amounts of data quickly
D) Establishing secure connections between devices
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Digital signatures ensure the authenticity and integrity of digital messages, confirming their origin and preventing tampering.

Explanation:
Encryption and secure connections are general goals, not specific to digital signatures. MACs provide data integrity, but not authentication like digital signatures.

Common mistake note:
Choosing A  (Securing data) as it's related to cryptography but not digital signature purpose.

Problem 3 (Key exchange protocols):
Which protocol is commonly used for securely exchanging cryptographic keys?
A) SSL/TLS
B) PGP
C) Diffie-Hellman
D) RSA
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The Diffie-Hellman key exchange allows two parties to establish a shared secret without explicitly exchanging the key.

Explanation:
SSL/TLS and RSA are for encryption, PGP is an encryption software. None of these are primarily for key exchange.

Common mistake note:
Choosing A  (SSL/TLS) or D  (RSA), as they are related to cryptography but not key exchange.

Problem 4 (Message Authentication Codes):
What do Message Authentication Codes  (MACs) provide in addition to encryption?
A) Data compression
B) Integrity and authenticity
C) Decryption of data
D) Anonymity of the sender
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
MACs ensure data integrity and authenticity, complementing encryption.

Explanation:
MACs do not compress, decrypt, or anonymize; these functions are unrelated to their purpose.

Common mistake note:
Choosing A  (Compression), C  (Decryption), or D  (Anonymity) as they are not MAC functions.

Problem 5 (Public key infrastructure):
What is the main purpose of a Public Key Infrastructure  (PKI)?
A) Storing private keys on a central server
B) Enabling secure communication without sharing secret keys
C) Encrypting large amounts of data quickly
D) Verifying authenticity of software updates
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
PKI manages public-private key pairs for secure communication without sharing secrets.

Explanation:
PKI is not for storing private keys, encrypting data, or verifying software updates; it focuses on key management.

Common mistake note:
Choosing A  (Storing keys) as it's related to key management but not PKI's main purpose.

Problem 6 (Symmetric vs. Asymmetric encryption):
Which type of encryption is generally faster and uses less computational resources?
A) Asymmetric
B) Symmetric
C) Both are equally efficient
D) It depends on specific algorithms
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Symmetric encryption, like AES, is faster and more efficient than asymmetric encryption, like RSA.

Explanation:
Asymmetric encryption is resource-intensive due to complex operations. Symmetric encryption is efficient for data encryption but not key exchange.

Common mistake note:
Choosing A  (Asymmetric) as it's considered more secure, but speed and efficiency differ.

Problem 7 (Hash functions in cryptography):
What is the primary purpose of a hash function in cryptography?
A) Encrypting data for transmission
B) Verifying integrity of digital signatures
C) Establishing secure connections between devices
D) Creating a unique representation of input data
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
Hash functions create a fixed-size, unique summary  (hash) of input data, useful for verifying integrity and authenticity.

Explanation:
Hashes are not encryption, do not establish connections, or verify signatures directly. They generate compact, unique representations of data.

Common mistake note:
Choosing A  (Encrypting data) as it's related to cryptography but not hash functions' main purpose.
