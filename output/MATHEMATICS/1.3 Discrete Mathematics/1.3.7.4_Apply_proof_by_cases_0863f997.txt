Problem 1 (:1):
Prove that the sum of two even integers is always an even integer.
A) Let x and y be odd integers. Then, x + y = 2k + 2m = 2(k+m), where k and m are integers. Thus, the sum of two even integers is even.
B) Let x and y be even integers. Then, x + y = 2k + 2m = 2(k+1) + 2(m+1), where k and m are integers. This is incorrect because it uses odd integers instead of even integers in the proof.
C) Let x and y be odd integers. Then, x + y = 2k + 2m = 2(k+m) + 1, where k and m are integers. Thus, the sum of two odd integers is odd.
D) Let x and y be even integers. Then, x + y = 2k + 2m + 1, where k and m are integers. This is incorrect because it incorrectly concludes that the sum of two even integers is odd.
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The correct proof uses even integers (not odd) for x and y. The sum of two even integers will always result in an even number because you can factor out a 2 from their sum. Choice A uses odd integers, which does not prove the statement. Choice C is about the sum of two odd integers, and choice D incorrectly concludes that the sum of two even integers is odd.

Explanation:
This question tests the understanding of proof by cases and how to correctly apply it to demonstrate that the sum of two even integers is always an even integer.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing A or D, which use odd integers instead of even integers in the proof.

Solution:

Explanation:

Correct Answer: A

Solution:
