Problem 1 (Asdf):
A differential equation is given as dy/dx = 2x + y. To find the initial condition, we are given that when x=0, y=? What is the value of y at x=0?
A) 1
B) 2
C) 3
D) 4
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
To find the initial condition, set x = 0 in the differential equation and solve for y. So, dy/dx at x=0 is 2(0) + y = y. Since dy/dx represents the rate of change of y with respect to x, when x=0, there's no change (dy/dx = 0), so y remains constant. Therefore, y = 2.

Explanation:
The initial condition gives us the value of y at a specific point, usually when x = 0. In this case, we are given that dy/dx = 0 when x=0, which means y is constant and equal to its initial value.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to assume that since dy/dx is zero at x=0, y must also be zero. However, this is not correct because the differential equation represents a rate of change, not an actual value.

Problem 2 (Qwer):
Given the differential equation dx/dt = 3t^2 + 4t and the initial condition when t=0, x=5, what is the value of x when t=1?
A) 6
B) 7
C) 8
D) 9
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Integrate the differential equation: ∫dx/dt dt = ∫(3t^2 + 4t) dt. Integrating, we get x(t) = t^3 + 2t^2 + C. Using the initial condition x(0) = 5, solve for C: 5 = 0^3 + 2*0^2 + C, so C = 5. The equation becomes x(t) = t^3 + 2t^2 + 5. Plug in t=1 to find x(1): x(1) = 1^3 + 2*1^2 + 5 = 8.

Explanation:
To find the value of x at a specific time t, integrate the differential equation and use the initial condition to determine the constant of integration. Then, substitute the given time into the resulting function.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget to include the constant of integration C when integrating the differential equation. Without it, the solution will not match the initial condition.

Problem 3 (Yuiop):
The population growth of a city is modeled by dP/dt = 0.1P + 500, where P is the population in thousands and t is time in years. If the population was 2000 thousand at t=0, what will be the population after 3 years?
A) 4000
B) 4500
C) 5000
D) 5500
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
Integrate the differential equation: ∫dP/dt dt = ∫(0.1P + 500) dt. Integrating, we get P(t) = 0.1Pt + 500t + C. Use the initial condition P(0) = 2000 to solve for C: 2000 = 0.1*2000*0 + 500*0 + C, so C = 2000. The equation becomes P(t) = 0.1Pt + 500t + 2000. Plug in t=3 to find P(3): P(3) = 0.1*2000*3 + 500*3 + 2000 = 5000.

Explanation:
To find the population after a specific time, integrate the differential equation and use the initial condition to determine the constant of integration. Then, substitute the given time into the resulting function.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget that the rate of change is 0.1P, not just P, when integrating the differential equation. This would lead to an incorrect population growth rate.

Problem 4 (Bvnmm):
The position of a particle moving along a line is given by s(t) = t^3 - 6t^2 + 9t + C, where s is in meters and t is in seconds. If the particle's initial position when t=0 is 5 meters, what is its position after 2 seconds?
A) 14
B) 16
C) 18
D) 20
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The initial condition gives us the value of s at t=0: s(0) = C. Since s(0) = 5, we have C = 5. The position function becomes s(t) = t^3 - 6t^2 + 9t + 5. Plug in t=2 to find s(2): s(2) = 2^3 - 6*2^2 + 9*2 + 5 = 16.

Explanation:
To find the position at a specific time, use the given position function and substitute the time into it. The initial condition helps determine the constant of integration.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget to include the constant of integration C when using the position function. Without it, the solution will not match the initial position.

Problem 5 (Ghjkl):
The temperature change of a metal rod is modeled by dT/dt = -k(T - T_0), where T is the temperature in degrees Celsius, t is time in minutes, k is a constant, and T_0 is the surrounding temperature. If the initial temperature of the rod when t=0 is 100°C and after 30 minutes it reaches 50°C, what was the initial temperature T_0?
A) 25
B) 50
C) 75
D) 100
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
Separate variables and integrate: ∫dT/(T - T_0) = ∫(-k dt). Integrating, we get ln|T - T_0| = -kt + C. Use the initial condition T(0) = 100 to solve for C: ln|100 - T_0| = 0, so C = 0 and T_0 = 75.

Explanation:
To find the surrounding temperature T_0, integrate the differential equation with respect to T and use the initial condition to determine the constant of integration. Then, solve for T_0.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget to take the natural logarithm when separating variables in the differential equation. This would lead to an incorrect temperature relationship.

Problem 6 (Bnmnb):
The decay of a radioactive substance follows the equation dN/dt = -kN, where N is the number of atoms remaining and t is time in seconds. If the initial amount of the substance when t=0 is 1000 atoms and after 30 seconds it has decayed to 500 atoms, what is the decay constant k?
A) 0.02
B) 0.04
C) 0.06
D) 0.08
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Separate variables and integrate: ∫dN/N = ∫(-k dt). Integrating, we get ln|N| = -kt + C. Use the initial condition N(0) = 1000 to solve for C: ln|1000| = 0, so C = 0. The equation becomes ln|N| = -kt. Plug in t=30 and N=500 to find k: ln|500| = -k*30, which gives k ≈ 0.04.

Explanation:
To find the decay constant k, integrate the differential equation with respect to N and use the initial condition to determine the constant of integration. Then, solve for k using the given time and number of atoms remaining.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget to take the natural logarithm when separating variables in the differential equation. This would lead to an incorrect decay rate.

Problem 7 (Hgfhj):

A) 0.1
B) 0.2
C) 0.3
D) 0.4
Correct Answer: B

Solution:

Explanation:
To find the rate constant k, integrate the differential equation with respect to C and use the initial condition to determine the constant of integration. Then, solve for k using the given time and concentration.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget to take the natural logarithm when separating variables in the differential equation. This would lead to an incorrect rate constant.
