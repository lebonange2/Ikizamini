Problem 1 (: 1):
What is the coefficient of $x^2y^3$ in the expansion of $(x + y)^5$?
A) 5
B) 10
C) 15
D) 20
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
(x+y)^5 can be expanded using the multinomial theorem. The coefficient for $x^2y^3$ is found by choosing 2 x's from the 5 terms and 3 y's from the remaining 3 terms. This can be calculated as:

C(5, 2) * C(3, 3) = (5! / (2!(5-2)!)) * (3! / (3!(3-3)!))

C(5, 2) = 10 and C(3, 3) = 1, so the coefficient is:

10 * 1 = 10

Explanation:
The multinomial theorem helps to distribute powers among multiple variables. The correct answer is found by calculating combinations of x's and y's that sum up to the desired power.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to choose A (5), which is the coefficient for $x^5$, or C (15), which is the combination of choosing 2 x's from 5, but not considering the remaining y terms. Remember that each variable has its own exponent in the multinomial expansion.

Problem 2 (2):
What is the coefficient of $a^3b^2c$ in the expansion of $(a + b + c)^6$?
A) 15
B) 20
C) 30
D) 45
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
(a+b+c)^6 can be expanded using the multinomial theorem. The coefficient for $a^3b^2c$ is found by choosing 3 a's, 2 b's, and 1 c from the 6 terms. This can be calculated as:

C(6, 3) * C(3, 2) * C(1, 1)

C(6, 3) = 20, C(3, 2) = 3, and C(1, 1) = 1, so the coefficient is:

20 * 3 * 1 = 60

Explanation:
The multinomial theorem distributes powers among multiple variables. The correct answer is found by calculating combinations of a's, b's, and c's that sum up to the desired power.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to choose A (15), which is the combination for choosing 3 a's from 6 but not considering the remaining b's and c. Remember that each variable has its own exponent in the multinomial expansion.
