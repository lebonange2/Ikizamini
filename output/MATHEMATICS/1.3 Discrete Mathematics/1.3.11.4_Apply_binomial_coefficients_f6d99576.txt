Problem 1 (Asdf):
What is the coefficient of x^2 in the expansion of (x + 3)^5?
A) 5
B) 10
C) 30
D) 120
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
(x + 3)^5 can be expanded using the binomial theorem. The term with x^2 will come from choosing x twice and 3 three times. The coefficient is C(5, 2) = (5!)/(2!(5-2)!) = 10.

Explanation:
Binomial coefficients are calculated using the combination formula: C(n, k) = n! / (k!(n-k)!).

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to use the factorial of 5 directly, which would give 5! = 120. This is incorrect because it does not account for the correct distribution of x and 3 terms.

What is the coefficient of a^3b^2 in the expansion of (a + b)^5?
A) 10
B) 20
C) 30
D) 40
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
(a + b)^5 can be expanded using the binomial theorem. The term with a^3b^2 will come from choosing a three times and b two times. The coefficient is C(5, 3) = (5!)/(3!(5-3)!) = 10.

Explanation:
Binomial coefficients are calculated using the combination formula: C(n, k) = n! / (k!(n-k)!).

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to confuse the powers of a and b with the binomial coefficient. The power of a term in the expansion does not determine the coefficient directly.

Problem 3 (Asdf3):
What is the coefficient of x^4y in the expansion of (2x + y)^6?
A) 15
B) 60
C) 120
D) 240
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
(2x + y)^6 can be expanded using the binomial theorem. The term with x^4y will come from choosing 2x four times and y once. The coefficient is C(6, 4) * (2^4) = (6!)/(4!(6-4)!)*16 = 60.

Explanation:
Binomial coefficients are calculated using the combination formula: C(n, k) = n! / (k!(n-k)!). Since we have 2x instead of x, we multiply by (2^4) to account for the powers of 2.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to forget to include the power of 2 raised to the number of times it appears in the term.

Problem 4 (Asdf4):
What is the coefficient of x^3y^2z in the expansion of (x + y + z)^5?
A) 10
B) 20
C) 30
D) 40
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
(x + y + z)^5 can be expanded using the binomial theorem. The term with x^3y^2z will come from choosing x three times, y two times, and z zero times. The coefficient is C(5, 3) * C(2, 2) = (5!)/(3!(5-3)!)*1 = 30.

Explanation:
Binomial coefficients are calculated using the combination formula: C(n, k) = n! / (k!(n-k)!). Since z does not appear in the term, we use C(2, 2) = 1 to account for its absence.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to forget that terms with zero powers do not contribute to the binomial coefficient calculation.

Problem 5 (Asdf5):
What is the coefficient of x^3 in the expansion of (x - 2)^4?
A) 6
B) 12
C) 24
D) 48
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
(x - 2)^4 can be expanded using the binomial theorem. The term with x^3 will come from choosing x three times and (-2) one time. The coefficient is C(4, 3) * (-2)^1 = (4!)/(3!(4-3)!)*(-2) = -8.

Explanation:
Binomial coefficients are calculated using the combination formula: C(n, k) = n! / (k!(n-k)!). Since we have a negative term, we multiply by (-2).

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to forget that when there's a negative coefficient in the binomial, it affects the final coefficient.

Problem 6 (Asdf6):
What is the coefficient of x^3y^2 in the expansion of (x - y)^5?
A) 10
B) 20
C) 30
D) 40
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
(x - y)^5 can be expanded using the binomial theorem. The term with x^3y^2 will come from choosing x three times and (-y) two times. The coefficient is C(5, 3) * (-1)^2 = (5!)/(3!(5-3)!)*1 = 10.

Explanation:
Binomial coefficients are calculated using the combination formula: C(n, k) = n! / (k!(n-k)!). Since we have a negative term in the binomial, we multiply by (-1)^2 to account for two occurrences of -y.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to forget that when there's a negative coefficient in the binomial, it affects the final coefficient only if the corresponding variable appears an odd number of times.

Problem 7 (Asdf7):
What is the coefficient of x^4y^2z^3 in the expansion of (x + y + z)^7?
A) 105
B) 210
C) 315
D) 420
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
(x + y + z)^7 can be expanded using the binomial theorem. The term with x^4y^2z^3 will come from choosing x four times, y two times, and z three times. The coefficient is C(7, 4) * C(3, 2) * C(1, 1) = (7!)/(4!(7-4)!)* (3!)/(2!(3-2)!)*1 = 315.

Explanation:
Binomial coefficients are calculated using the combination formula: C(n, k) = n! / (k!(n-k)!). Since each variable appears a different number of times, we multiply their respective binomial coefficients.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to forget to multiply the binomial coefficients for each variable when they appear with different exponents.
