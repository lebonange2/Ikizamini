Problem 1 (?):
Given the statement: (A ∪ B)', which of the following is its equivalent using De Morgan's laws?
A) (A' ∩ B')
B) (A' ∪ B)
C) (A ∩ B')'
D) (A ∩ B)'
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
De Morgan's law states that the complement of a union is equal to the intersection of the complements. Therefore, (A ∪ B)' is equivalent to (A' ∩ B')

Explanation:
De Morgan's laws are used to manipulate logical expressions involving negation, union, and intersection.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing option B, which represents the complement of one set without applying it to both sets in the union. The law requires the complement to be applied to each set within the union.

Problem 3 (?):
Given the statement: (¬X ∧ ¬Y) , which of the following is its equivalent using De Morgan's laws?
A) (X ∨ Y)'
B) (X ∨ Y)
C) (¬X ∨ ¬Y)'
D) (¬X ∨ ¬Y)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
De Morgan's law states that the negation of 'and' (conjunction) is equivalent to 'or' (disjunction) with negated terms. Therefore, (¬X ∧ ¬Y) is equivalent to (X ∨ Y)'

Explanation:
Applying De Morgan's laws correctly negates and switches between conjunction and disjunction.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing option B, which represents the original statement without applying De Morgan's laws. The law requires negation and a change in operation.
