Problem 1 (Inequality_Simplification_1):
Simplify the expression: (x > 3) AND (x < 7)
A) (x >= 3) OR (x <= 7)
B) (x >= 3) AND (x <= 7)
C) (3 < x < 7)
D) False
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The simplified form of the expression is (3 < x < 7), which means x is greater than 3 and less than 7. Options A, B, and D are incorrect because they either allow for x to be equal to 3 or 7, or they represent a different relationship.

Explanation:
When simplifying logical expressions involving inequalities, we keep the strict inequality (>) and (<) signs intact.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to mistakenly include 'equal to' in the simplified form, which would make it less precise.

Problem 2 (Conjunction_ Simplification_2):
Simplify the expression: ((p AND q) OR (r AND s))
A) (p OR r) AND (q OR s)
B) p OR q OR r OR s
C) p AND q AND r AND s
D) False
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The expression simplifies to (p OR r) AND (q OR s), using the distributive property of logical operators. Options B, C, and D are incorrect because they either represent a different relationship or do not follow the correct operator precedence.

Explanation:
When simplifying expressions with conjunctions (AND) and disjunctions (OR), we can use the distributive property to group terms.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to forget the distributive property, leading to an incorrect simplification.

Problem 4 (Identity_Laws_4):
Simplify the expression: p AND True
A) False
B) p
C) True
D) p OR True
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The expression simplifies to True, as any value ANDed with True results in True. Options A, B, and D are incorrect because they represent a different relationship.

Explanation:
In logical expressions, the identity laws state that any value ANDed with True or ORed with False results in the other value.

Common mistake note:

Problem 5 (Implication_Simplification_5):
Simplify the expression: NOT(p) OR q
A) p AND NOT(q)
B) NOT( p) AND q
C) p OR NOT(q)
D) False
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The expression simplifies to NOT(p) AND q, which is equivalent to the original expression due to the material implication rule. Options A, C, and D are incorrect because they represent a different relationship.

Explanation:
In logical expressions, the material implication (p implies q) can be represented as NOT(p) OR q, and vice versa.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to confuse the material implication with other logical relationships, leading to an incorrect simplification.

Problem 6 (Exclusive_Or_Simplification_6):
Simplify the expression: (p XOR q) AND NOT(p)
A) q
B) NOT(q)
C) False
D) p
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The expression simplifies to q, as p XOR q AND NOT(p) means either p is false and q is true (exclusive OR), and since we know NOT(p) is true, q must be true. Options B, C, and D are incorrect because they represent a different relationship.

Explanation:
When simplifying expressions involving exclusive OR (XOR), we consider the truth table for XOR to determine the simplified form.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to misinterpret the exclusive OR, leading to an incorrect simplification.

Problem 7 (Converse_Implies_Biconditional_7):
Simplify the expression: (p implies q) AND (q implies p)
A) p XOR q
B) p AND q
C) p OR q
D) False
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The expression simplifies to p AND q, as both implications imply a biconditional relationship (p if and only if q). Options A, C, and D are incorrect because they represent different relationships.

Explanation:
When combining two implications in opposite directions, the result is a biconditional statement (p AND q) or (q AND p), depending on the order of the variables.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to overlook the relationship between converse implications and biconditionals, leading to an incorrect simplification.
