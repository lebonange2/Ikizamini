Problem 1 (:Q1):
Express the statement 'If it is raining, then I will stay at home' in logical notation.
A) R -> H
B) H -> R
C) R & H
D) ¬R v ¬H
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The statement 'If it is raining, then I will stay at home' can be translated to the logical implication 'Raining (R) implies Staying at Home (H)', which is represented as R -> H.

Explanation:
An implication has the form 'P -> Q', where P is the antecedent and Q is the consequent. In this case, P is 'it is raining' and Q is 'I will stay at home'.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing B (H -> R), which means 'Staying at Home implies It is Raining', reversing the relationship.

Problem 2 (:Q2):
Translate the sentence 'Either it is sunny or I am playing tennis' into logical notation.
A) S v T
B) S & T
C) ¬S -> T
D) T  -> ¬S
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The statement 'Either it is sunny or I am playing tennis' can be translated to the logical disjunction 'Sunny (S) or Playing Tennis (T)', which is represented as S v T.

Explanation:
A disjunction has the form 'P v Q', meaning either P or Q, or both are true. In this case, P is 'it is sunny' and Q is 'I am playing tennis'.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing C (¬S -> T), which means 'If it's not sunny, then I'm playing tennis', or D (T  -> ¬S), which means 'If I'm playing tennis, then it's not sunny', both of which express different relationships.

Problem 3 (:Q3):
Express the statement 'I will go to the gym if and only if I have time' in logical notation.
A) G <-> T
B) G & T
C) G v ¬T
D) ¬G -> ¬T
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The statement 'I will go to the gym if and only if I have time' can be translated to the biconditional 'Going to the Gym (G) is equivalent to Having Time (T)', which is represented as G <-> T.

Explanation:
A biconditional has the form 'P <-> Q', meaning P is true if and only if Q is true, and vice versa. In this case, P is 'I will go to the gym' and Q is 'I have time'.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing B (G & T), which means 'Going to the gym and Having Time simultaneously', or C (G v ¬T), which means 'Either Going to the Gym or Not Having Time', both of which do not express the equivalence relationship.

Problem 4 (:Q4):
Translate the sentence 'It is not the case that both I am studying and it is noisy' into logical notation.
A) ¬(S & N)
B) (S v N) -> F
C) S -> ¬N
D) N -> S
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The statement 'It is not the case that both I am studying (S) and it is noisy (N)' can be translated to the negation of their conjunction, which is represented as ¬(S & N).

Explanation:
Negating a conjunction means 'not both P and Q' and is written as ¬(P & Q). In this case, P is 'I am studying' and Q is 'it is noisy'.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing B (S v N) -> F, which means 'If either Studying or Noisy, then False', or C (S -> ¬N), which means 'Studying implies Not Noisy', or D (N -> S), which means 'Noisy implies Studying', all of which express different relationships.

Problem 5 (:Q5):
Express the statement 'I will go to the movies only if I finish my homework' in logical notation.
A) M & H
B) M v H
C) H -> M
D) M -> ¬H
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The statement 'I will go to the movies (M) only if I finish my homework (H)' can be translated to the conditional 'Finishing Homework implies Going to the Movies', which is represented as H -> M.

Explanation:
A conditional has the form 'P -> Q', where P is the antecedent and Q is the consequent. In this case, P is 'I finish my homework' and Q is 'I go to the movies'.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing A (M & H), which means 'Going to the movies and Finishing Homework simultaneously', or B (M v H), which means 'Either Going to the Movies or Finishing Homework', or D (M -> ¬H), which means 'If I go to the movies, then I did not finish my homework', all of which express different relationships.

Problem 6 (:Q6):
Translate the sentence 'It is raining and either it is cold or it is snowing' into logical notation.
A) R & (C v S)
B) (R v C) & S
C) R & C v S
D) R v (C & S)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The statement 'It is raining (R) and either it is cold (C) or it is snowing (S)' can be translated to the conjunction of R with the disjunction of C and S, which is represented as R & (C v S).

Explanation:
A conjunction has the form 'P & Q', meaning both P and Q are true. In this case, P is 'It is raining' and Q is '(It is cold or It is snowing)', which is a disjunction expressed as C v S.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing B (R v C) & S, which means 'Either Raining or Cold and Snowing', or C (R & C v S), which means 'Raining and (Cold or Snowing)', or D (R v (C & S)), which means 'Raining or Both Cold and Snowing', all of which express different relationships.

Problem 7 (:Q7):
Express the statement 'I will go to bed if it is past 10 PM, but only if I finish my reading' in logical notation.
A) (T > 10) & (F -> B)
B) (T > 10) & (B -> F)
C) T > 10 v (F -> B)
D) T > 10 & (B v F)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The statement 'I will go to bed (B) if it is past 10 PM (T > 10), but only if I finish my reading (F)' can be translated to the conjunction of 'It's past 10' with the conditional 'Finishing Reading implies Going to Bed', which is represented as (T > 10) & (F -> B).

Explanation:
We need both conditions to be true: it must be past 10 PM and finishing reading should imply going to bed. This can be expressed as a conjunction of the two conditions.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing B (T > 10) & (B -> F), which means 'Finishing Reading implies Going to Bed, given it's past 10', but does not require finishing reading; C (T > 10 v (F -> B)), which combines the two conditions with a disjunction and is incorrect; or D (T > 10 & (B v F)), which means 'It's past 10 and either Going to Bed or Finishing Reading', but does not require both conditions to be met simultaneously.
