Problem 1 (:Q1):
In a club with 20 members, there are 15 who play chess and 12 who play tennis. If 8 members play both games, how many members do not play either game?
A) As
B) Bs
C) Cs
D) Ds
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
Using the inclusion-exclusion principle: Total number of members who play chess or tennis = Members playing chess + Members playing tennis - Members playing both. So, 15 (chess) + 12 (tennis) - 8 (both) = 29 - 8 = 21 members play either game. Members not playing either game = Total members - Members playing either game = 20 - 21 = 1 member does not play either game.

Explanation:
The inclusion-exclusion principle helps avoid overcounting when combining sets. In this case, we must subtract the number of members who play both games twice to correct for double-counting.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Adding the number of members playing both games instead of subtracting it.

Problem 2 (:Q2):
There are 30 students in a class. 15 study math, 18 study physics, and 9 study both. How many students study neither math nor physics?
A) As
B) Bs
C) Cs
D) Ds
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
Using the inclusion-exclusion principle: Total number of students studying math or physics = Students studying math + Students studying physics - Students studying both. So, 15 (math) + 18 (physics) - 9 (both) = 24. Since there are 30 students in total, the number not studying either subject is: Total students - Students studying at least one subject = 30 - 24 = 6.

Explanation:
The inclusion-exclusion principle helps avoid overcounting when combining sets. In this case, we must subtract the number of students who study both subjects twice to correct for double-counting.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Adding the number of students studying both math and physics instead of subtracting it.

Problem 3 (:Q3):
A library has 50 books on history, 40 on economics, and 20 on both subjects. How many books are there that are not on either history or economics?
A) As
B) Bs
C) Cs
D) Ds
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Using the inclusion-exclusion principle: Total number of books on history or economics = Books on history + Books on economics - Books on both. So, 50 (history) + 40 (economics) - 20 (both) = 70. Since there are 110 books in total, the number not on either subject is: Total books - Books on at least one subject = 110 - 70 = 40. However, this result includes the 20 books that are both history and economics, so we must subtract them to get the correct answer: 40 - 20 = 20.

Explanation:
The inclusion-exclusion principle helps avoid overcounting when combining sets. In this case, we must subtract the number of books on both subjects twice to correct for double-counting and then remove the overlap again.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Not accounting for the overlap between history and economics a second time.

A) As

D) Ds

Explanation:
The inclusion-exclusion principle helps avoid overcounting when combining sets. In this case, we must subtract the number of people who like both fruits twice to correct for double-counting.
