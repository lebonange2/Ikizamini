Problem 1 (One):
How many combinations are there for choosing 3 desserts from a menu of 7 options?
A) {21}
B) {35}
C) {42}
D) {56}
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The number of combinations is calculated using the combination formula: C(n, r) = n! / (r!(n-r)!). For 7 desserts and choosing 3, we have C(7, 3) = 7! / (3!(7-3)!) = 35. The correct answer is 42.

Explanation:
This problem involves counting combinations of 7 objects taken 3 at a time using the combination formula.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to use permutations instead of combinations, which would result in an incorrect count (permutations consider order while combinations do not).

Problem 2 (Two):
How many ways can you form a committee of 4 people from a group of 10?
A) {20}
B) {35}
C) {56}
D) {70}
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
Using the combination formula, C(10, 4) = 10! / (4!(10-4)!) = 210. Dividing by 4! (since there are 4! ways to arrange 4 people), we get 56 unique combinations of people for the committee.

Explanation:
This question asks for the number of ways to choose 4 people from a group of 10 without considering their order on the committee.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to divide by 10! instead of 4!, which would result in an overcount because it considers all permutations rather than unique combinations.
