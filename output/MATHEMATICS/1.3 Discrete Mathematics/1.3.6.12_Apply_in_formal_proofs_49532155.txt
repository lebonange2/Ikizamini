Problem 1 (x1):
In a formal proof, what is the first step after stating the given information?
A) Provide a conclusion without justification.
B) Assume what needs to be proven and work backwards.
C) Use logical reasoning to derive new statements from the given information.
D) Introduce unrelated premises to support the conclusion.
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
In a formal proof, the first step after stating the given information is to use logical reasoning to derive new statements from those given facts. This helps build a chain of reasoning that eventually leads to the desired conclusion.

Explanation:
Logical reasoning allows one to make valid deductions based on the initial premises. Options A and D introduce flaws in the proof, while option B describes a non-standard approach called 'proof by contradiction'.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing A or D may happen when students misunderstand the structure of a formal proof, thinking it can start with an unsupported conclusion or unrelated premises.

Problem 2 (x2):
Which of the following is an example of a valid step in a formal proof using modus ponens?
A) If A implies B and B implies C, then A implies C.
B) If A implies B and C implies D, then A implies D.
C) If A implies B and not-A implies C, then B or C.
D) If A implies B and C implies A, then B implies C.
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Modus ponens is a valid deductive rule that states if 'A implies B' is true and 'A' is also true, then 'B' must be true. In the given examples, only option A directly applies modus ponens.

Explanation:
Option A correctly uses modus ponens to derive 'C' from 'A implies B' and 'A'. Options B, C, and D involve different logical relationships that are not modus ponens.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing B may occur when students confuse modus ponens with hypothetical syllogism (B→C→D). Choosing C could result from confusion with disjunctive syllogism, and choosing D might be due to misunderstanding the contrapositive.

Problem 3 (x3):
In a formal proof, what is the purpose of using a conditional statement?
A) To introduce unrelated premises.
B) To provide an immediate conclusion without justification.
C) To express a relationship between two propositions where one implies the other.
D) To contradict the given information.
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
Conditional statements in formal proofs are used to express a relationship between two propositions, where if the first proposition (antecedent) is true, then the second proposition (consequence) must also be true.

Explanation:
Conditional statements allow for logical connections between premises and conclusions. Options A, B, and D describe actions that would not contribute to a valid proof structure.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing A or B may happen when students misunderstand the role of conditional statements in proofs, thinking they can introduce unrelated information or immediate conclusions without reasoning.

Problem 4 (x4):
In a formal proof, how do you introduce a new assumption for the sake of contradiction?
A) State it as a given fact.
B) Assume it and derive a conclusion that contradicts a known fact or premise.
C) Introduce it as an intermediate step without any justification.
D) Use a conditional statement to link it to another assumption.
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
In a formal proof, introducing a new assumption for the sake of contradiction involves assuming the opposite of what needs to be proven and then showing that this leads to a contradiction with a known fact or premise.

Explanation:
Proof by contradiction is a valid method where one assumes the negation of the conclusion and derives a contradiction. Options A, C, and D do not accurately describe this technique.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing A may occur when students misunderstand the purpose of introducing an assumption for contradiction, thinking it can be treated as a given fact. Choosing C or D might result from confusion about how to connect assumptions in a proof.

Problem 5 (x5):
Which of the following is an example of a valid direct proof?
A) Assume what needs to be proven and work backwards.
B) Prove the contrapositive of the statement to be proven.
C) Derive a conclusion by showing that the opposite would lead to a contradiction.
D) Use a combination of conditional statements and known facts to reach the desired conclusion.
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
A direct proof involves using logical reasoning, conditional statements, and known facts to directly show that a statement is true without resorting to contradictions or contrapositives.

Explanation:
Option D accurately describes a direct proof. Options A and C describe methods other than direct proof (proof by contradiction and proof of the contrapositive, respectively). Option B is not an example of a direct proof since it proves the contrapositive instead.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing A or C may happen when students混淆证明方法，误以为它们是直接证明。选择B可能是由于对直接证明和证明逆否命题之间的区别混淆。

Problem 6 (x6):
In a formal proof, how do you use the law of detachment?
A) By assuming two premises and concluding their conjunction.
B) By assuming two conditional statements and concluding their disjunction.
C) By assuming a conditional statement and one of its antecedents, then concluding the consequence.
D) By assuming a conclusion and working backwards to derive the given information.
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The law of detachment (modus ponens) is used in formal proofs by assuming a conditional statement (A implies B) and one of its antecedents (A), then concluding the consequence (B).

Explanation:
The law of detachment allows for drawing a conclusion when both the antecedent and the conditional are true. Options A, B, and D describe actions that do not involve the law of detachment.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing A or B may occur when students混淆逻辑规则，误以为它们与 detachment 相关。选择D可能是由于对证明过程的误解，认为结论是先验的。

Problem 7 (x7):
In a formal proof, how do you use the law of syllogism?
A) By assuming two premises and concluding their disjunction.
B) By assuming two conditional statements and concluding their conjunction.
C) By assuming two propositions with the same antecedent, then concluding the conjunction of their consequences.
D) By assuming a conclusion and working backwards to derive the given information.
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The law of syllogism is used in formal proofs by assuming two propositions with the same antecedent (A implies B and A implies C), then concluding the conjunction of their consequences (B and C).

Explanation:
The law of syllogism allows for combining two conditional statements with a common antecedent to derive a new conclusion. Options A, B, and D describe actions that do not involve the law of syllogism.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing A or B may occur when students混淆逻辑规则，误以为它们与 syllogism 相关。选择D可能是由于对证明过程的误解，认为结论是先验的。
