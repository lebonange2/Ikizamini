Problem 1 (:1):
Given the following set of logical expressions, which one is dominated by (implies) the expression X AND Y?
A) (X OR Y)
B) (NOT X OR NOT Y)
C) (X AND NOT Y)
D) (NOT X AND Y)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The domination law states that 'X AND Y' is dominated by 'X OR Y', meaning that if 'X OR Y' is true, then 'X AND Y' must also be true. Therefore, (X OR Y) dominates (X AND Y). The other options do not have this implication.

Explanation:
Domination law states that if A implies B, then A is said to dominate B.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to choose (X AND NOT Y), which is equivalent to 'X' but does not imply 'X AND Y'.

Problem 2 (:2):
Which of the following logical expressions dominates (implies) the expression NOT(X OR Y)?
A) (NOT X AND NOT Y)
B) (X AND Y)
C) (X OR NOT Y)
D) (NOT X OR Y)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The domination law states that 'NOT( X OR Y)' is dominated by '(NOT X AND NOT Y)', meaning that if '(NOT X AND NOT Y)' is true, then 'NOT(X OR Y)' must also be true. Therefore, (NOT X AND NOT Y) dominates (NOT(X OR Y)). The other options do not have this implication.

Explanation:
Using De Morgan's laws, we can rewrite 'NOT( X OR Y)' as '(NOT X AND NOT Y)', which is dominated by itself.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to choose (X AND Y), which is the negation of 'NOT(X OR Y)' but does not imply it.

Explanation:
NAND is the negation of AND, so 'X NAND Y' is equivalent to '(NOT( X AND Y))', which implies '(NOT X OR Y)' according to De Morgan's laws.
