Problem 1 (In how many ways can a committee of 4 people be chosen from a group of 9?):
In how many ways can a committee of 4 people be chosen from a group of 9?
A) 126
B) 36
C) 56
D) 18
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The number of ways to choose 4 people out of 9 can be calculated using the combination formula: C(n, k) = n! / (k!(n-k)!), where n is the total number of items and k is the number of items to choose. For this problem, n = 9 and k = 4. Plugging these values into the formula gives us: C(9, 4) = 9! / (4!(9-4)!) = (9*8*7*6) / (4*3*2*1) = 126.

Explanation:
This question tests the understanding of combinations and how to calculate them using the combination formula.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to use permutations instead of combinations, which would result in a higher number because permutations account for order. Another mistake could be not simplifying the factorial calculation correctly.

Problem 2 (How many ways can you arrange 6 distinct books on a shelf?):
How many ways can you arrange 6 distinct books on a shelf?
A) 720
B) 360
C) 120
D) 48
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The number of ways to arrange n distinct items is given by the factorial of n. In this case, n = 6, so the arrangement possibilities are: 6! = 6*5*4*3*2*1 = 720.

Explanation:
This question tests the understanding of permutations and how to calculate them using factorials.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to use combinations instead of permutations, which would not account for the order in which the books are arranged. Another mistake could be forgetting to multiply the numbers correctly or miscounting the factorial.
