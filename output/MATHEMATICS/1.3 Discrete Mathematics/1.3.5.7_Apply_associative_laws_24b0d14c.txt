Problem 1 (InPlaceAssociativeLaw):
(2 + 3) + 4
A) (2 + 4) + 3
B) 2 + (3 + 4)
C) 5 + 4
D) 2 + 7
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Using the associative law of addition, we can rearrange the terms without changing the value. So, (2 + 3) + 4 is equal to 2 + (3 + 4).

Explanation:
The associative property states that when adding three or more numbers, the grouping of the numbers does not affect the sum.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to choose A, which rearranges the terms but keeps the parentheses, which is incorrect because it implies a different order of operations. Choice D adds the result of (2 + 3) to 4 instead of grouping them correctly.

Problem 3 (MixedAssociativeLaw):
(4 + 2) × (3 + 1)
A) (4 × 3) + (2 × 1)
B) 4 × (3 + 1) + 2 × (3 + 1)
C) 6 × 4
D) 10 × 2
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Using the associative law for both addition and multiplication, we can rearrange the terms without changing the value. So, (4 + 2) × (3 + 1) is equal to 4 × (3 + 1) + 2 × (3 + 1).

Explanation:
The associative property applies to both addition and multiplication. Here, we can group terms separately for addition and then multiply the results.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to choose A, which applies the distributive law instead of the associative law. Choice C multiplies the sum of the first two numbers with the sum of the last two numbers, while choice D incorrectly multiplies 10 by 2.

Problem 4 (AssociativeLawWithParentheses):
((7 + 3) × 2) + (4 × 5)
A) (7 + (3 × 2)) + (4 × 5)
B) (10 × 2) + (4 × 5)
C) 21 + 20
D) 14 + 20
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Using the associative law for both addition and multiplication, we can rearrange the terms without changing the value. So, ((7 + 3) × 2) + (4 × 5) is equal to (10 × 2) + (4 × 5).

Explanation:
The associative property applies within parentheses as well. Here, we first perform addition inside the first set of parentheses and then multiply by 2.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to choose A, which incorrectly distributes multiplication before applying the associative law for addition. Choice C combines the results without considering the operations within the parentheses. Choice D adds the results of each operation separately rather than combining them according to the order of operations.

Problem 5 (AssociativeLawWithNegativeNumbers):
(-2 + 3) + (-4)
A) (-2 + -4) + 3
B) -2 + (-4 + 3)
C) -1 + -4
D) -2 + (-1)
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
Using the associative law of addition, we can rearrange the terms without changing the value. So, (-2 + 3) + (-4) is equal to -2 + (-1).

Explanation:
The associative property holds for both positive and negative numbers. Here, we combine the two positive numbers first and then add the negative number.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to choose A, which incorrectly groups the negative numbers together first. Choice B adds -4 and 3 before considering the associative law. Choice C combines the results of adding -2 and -4 without considering the correct grouping.
