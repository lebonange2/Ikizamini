Problem 1 (OneBallMulticolored):
How many ways are there to arrange 5 balls of different colors in a row if we have 3 red, 2 blue, and 1 green ball?
A) 60
B) 30
C) 15
D) 120
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
To find the number of permutations with repetition, multiply the number of choices for each position: 3 (red) × 2 (blue) × 1 (green) = 6. There are 6 ways to arrange the balls.

Explanation:
The order matters as it's a permutation, not a combination. Repetition comes from multiple balls of the same color.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is treating this as a combination problem and dividing by the factorial of the number of balls (e.g., 5! / (3! × 2!)), which gives an incorrect result.

Problem 2 (ThreeBooksDifferent):
How many ways can you arrange 3 different books on a shelf?
A) 6
B) 12
C) 18
D) 24
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
With 3 distinct books, the first book has 3 choices, the second has 2, and the third has 1. Total arrangements: 3 × 2 × 1 = 6.

Explanation:
This is a simple permutation problem without repetition since each book is unique.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is multiplying by the number of books (e.g., 3!) again, which results in an incorrect count.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 3 (TwoPencilsThreeMarkers):
You have 2 identical pencils and 3 different markers. How many ways can you arrange them on a desk if you place all the pencils together?
A) 6
B) 12
C) 18
D) 24
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Considering the pencils as one group, we have 4 items (pencils + markers). First position: 4 ways, second: 3, third: 2. Dividing by 2! for identical pencils: arrangements = (4 × 3 × 2) / 2! = 6.

Explanation:
Problem involves repetition and indistinguishable items; treat pencils as one group to avoid overcounting.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is forgetting to divide by the factorial of identical items (e.g., 2!) when grouping, which gives an incorrect result.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 4 (FourFruitsTwoEach):
You have 4 different fruits: apples, oranges, bananas, and pears. You want to pick two of each fruit. How many ways can you choose the fruits?
A) 64
B) 32
C) 16
D) 96
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
With 4 different fruits, pick 2 of each: 4 × 3 × (2!)^2 = 24. Multiply by factorials for identical pairs within kinds: total arrangements = 24 × 2! × 2! = 64.

Explanation:
Problem involves repetition and indistinguishable items within groups; multiply by factorial of identical items per group.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is forgetting to multiply by factorials of identical items (e.g., 2!) for each group, which gives an incorrect result.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 5 (FiveStudentsTeams):
In a class with 5 students, how many ways can they form two teams of 2 and 3 students if all students are distinct?
A) 10
B) 20
C) 30
D) 40
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Choose 2 out of 5 for team of 2: C(5,2) = (5!)/(2!(5-2)!) = 10. The remaining students form the other team automatically. Total arrangements = 10.

Explanation:
Combination followed by permutation; teams are distinguishable but order within each doesn't matter.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is treating the entire process as a single permutation problem, which results in overcounting.
