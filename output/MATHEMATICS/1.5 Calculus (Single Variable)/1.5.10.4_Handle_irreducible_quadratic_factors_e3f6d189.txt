Problem 1 (?):
Factor the expression: x^2 + 6x + 9
A) (x + 3)^2
B) x(x + 6) + 9
C) (x + 1)(x + 5)
D) (x + 3)(x - 3)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The expression is a perfect square trinomial, which can be factored as (x + 3)^2. The other choices do not correctly factor the given expression.

Explanation:
Perfect square trinomials have the form x^2 + 2ax + a^2, where a is the square root of the constant term divided by 2. In this case, a = 3, so the factored form is (x + 3)^2.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to try to factor it as (x + 3)(x + 3), which is correct but can be simplified further to (x + 3)^2.

Problem 2 (?):
Factor the expression: x^2 - 5x + 6
A) (x - 2)(x - 3)
B) (x - 1)(x - 6)
C) (x + 2)(x + 3)
D) (x - 1)^2
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The expression factors into (x - 2)(x - 3), since the product of the first and last coefficients is equal to the constant term, and the product of their middle terms is equal to the coefficient of x.

Explanation:
To factor a quadratic trinomial, find two numbers that multiply to give the constant term (6) and add to give the coefficient of x (-5). In this case, -2 and -3 are those numbers.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to try to factor it as (x + 2)(x + 3), which does not work because the product of these factors does not match the original expression.
