Problem 1 (One):
Find the value of the definite integral: ∫(π/2 to π) sin(x) dx
A) -1
B) 0
C) 1
D) 2
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The integral of sin(x) from π/2 to π is 0 because the area between the x-axis and the sine function over a full period is zero.

Explanation:
The definite integral represents one complete cycle of the sine wave. Since the areas above and below the x-axis cancel each other, the total area is 0.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to choose A (-1) because it's the value of sin(π), but not considering that sin(π/2) = 1 and the areas must balance.

Problem 2 (Two):
Evaluate ∫(1 to 4) (x^2 - 3x + 2) dx
A) 56
B) 70
C) 84
D) 98
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The integral is found by integrating the function and evaluating at the limits: ∫(x^2 - 3x + 2) dx = (1/3)x^3 - (3/2)x^2 + 2x. Evaluating from 1 to 4 gives 56.

Explanation:
The definite integral is computed by integrating the function and then applying the limits of integration. The antiderivative of x^2 - 3x + 2 is (1/3)x^3 - (3/2)x^2 + 2x.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget to evaluate the antiderivative at both limits or apply incorrect limits.

Problem 3 (Three):
Find the area between y = x^2 and the x-axis from x = 0 to x = 3.
A) 9/2
B) 18
C) 27/2
D) 54
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The area is given by ∫(0 to 3) x^2 dx = (x^3/3)|_0^3 = 27/2.

Explanation:
To find the area between a curve and the x-axis, integrate the function from the lower limit to the upper limit. Here, it's ∫(0 to 3) x^2 dx.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget to divide by 3 when integrating x^2 or evaluate the antiderivative at incorrect limits.

Problem 4 (Four):
Evaluate ∫(-1 to 1) cos(x) dx
A) -2sin(1)
B) 0
C) 2sin(1)
D) 4sin(1)
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The integral of cos(x) from -1 to 1 is 2sin(1), as cos(1) = cos(-1). The definite integral gives the net signed area, which is positive.

Explanation:
The integral of cos(x) represents the signed area under the curve. Since cos(x) is an even function, its values at -1 and 1 are equal, resulting in a positive total area.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to choose A (-2sin(1)) because it's sin(-1), but not considering that cos(x) is even.

Problem 5 (Five):
Find the average value of f(x) = 3x + 2 on the interval [0, 4].
A) 5
B) 6
C) 7
D) 8
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
(1/(b-a))∫(a to b) f(x) dx = (1/4)∫(0 to 4) (3x + 2) dx = 7.

Explanation:
The average value of a function on an interval is found by integrating the function over that interval and dividing by the length. Here, it's (1/4)∫(0 to 4) (3x + 2) dx.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget to divide by the interval length or integrate incorrectly.

Problem 6 (Six):
Evaluate ∫(-2 to 2) e^x dx
A) e^2 - e^{-2}
B) e^2 + e^{-2}
C) (1/2)(e^2 - e^{-2})
D) (1/2)(e^2 + e^{-2})
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The integral of e^x from -2 to 2 is e^2 - e^{-2}, as the exponential function is neither odd nor even.

Explanation:
When integrating an exponential function, use ∫e^x dx = e^x. Evaluate at the limits: e^2 - e^{-2}. There's no symmetry to exploit.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to assume the integral is zero due to symmetry, but this isn't the case for a general exponential function.

Problem 7 (Seven):
Find the definite integral of f(x) = x^3 - 2x + 1 from x = 1 to x = 4 using the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus.
A) 176/4
B) 180/4
C) 184/4
D) 188/4
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The definite integral is F(4) - F(1), where F(x) is the antiderivative of f(x). The antiderivative is (1/4)x^4 - x^2 + x, so the integral is 184/4.

Explanation:
The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus states that ∫f(x) dx from a to b = F(b) - F(a), where F'(x) = f(x). Here, it's (1/4)x^4 - x^2 + x.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget to evaluate the antiderivative at both limits or apply incorrect limits.
