Problem 1 (:1):
A bridge is designed to support a maximum load of 20,000 pounds. If a truck carrying 15,000 pounds of cargo crosses the bridge, what percentage of the bridge's capacity is being used?
A) 75%
B) 60%
C) 37.5%
D) 25%
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
To find the percentage of the bridge's capacity being used, divide the weight of the truck by the maximum load and multiply by 100: (15,000 / 20,000) * 100 = 75%.

Explanation:
This question assesses the student's ability to calculate a percentage of capacity used in an engineering context.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Dividing by the weight of the truck instead of the bridge's maximum load. This would result in a percentage greater than 100, which is not possible.

Problem 2 (:2):
An engineer needs to design a support beam for a building with a span of 30 feet. The maximum deflection allowed is 0.5 inches. If the beam is made of steel with an elastic modulus of 29,000ksi, what is the minimum required cross-sectional area (in square inches) for the beam?
A) 1.8
B) 3.6
C) 7.2
D) 14.4
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The maximum deflection formula is: Δ = (5 * L^4 * w) / (384 * E * I), where Δ is the deflection, L is the span, w is the load, E is the elastic modulus, and I is the area moment of inertia. For a rectangular beam, I = b * h^3 / 12, where b is the width and h is the height. Assuming b = h, solve for I using given values: L = 30 ft (360 inches), Δ = 0.5 in, E = 29,000ksi.

Explanation:
This question assesses the student's ability to apply beam deflection formulas in an engineering context.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Not converting units to consistent values before plugging them into the formula. This would lead to incorrect results.

Problem 3 (:3):
A water pump is designed to deliver 10 gallons of water per minute at a pressure of 50 psi. If the pipe diameter is reduced from 2 inches to 1 inch, what will be the new flow rate (in gallons per minute) assuming the same pressure?
A) 5
B) 10
C) 20
D) 40
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The flow rate is related to the pipe diameter and pressure by the continuity equation: Q = A * v, where Q is the flow rate, A is the cross-sectional area of the pipe, and v is the velocity. Assuming incompressible flow, the velocity remains constant when the pipe diameter changes. Since the area A is proportional to the square of the diameter, reducing the diameter by half will halve the flow rate.

Explanation:
This question assesses the student's ability to apply fluid dynamics principles in an engineering context.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Assuming that pressure affects the flow rate directly. In this case, the pressure remains constant, so the change in flow rate is solely due to the change in pipe area.

Problem 4 (:4):
A solar panel system generates 200 watts of power under optimal conditions. If the system's efficiency is 15%, how much sunlight energy (in joules per second) hits the panel?
A) 1,333
B) 8,333
C) 12,000
D) 20,000
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The efficiency of a solar panel is the ratio of electrical power output to incident sunlight energy. To find the incident sunlight energy, divide the electrical power by the efficiency: E = P / η, where E is the sunlight energy and η is the efficiency. In this case: E = 200 W / 0.15.

Explanation:
This question assesses the student's ability to calculate incident solar energy based on panel efficiency.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Dividing the electrical power by 100 instead of finding the inverse of the efficiency. This would result in an incorrect value.

Problem 5 (:5):
A suspension bridge has two main cables, each supporting a load of 40,000 pounds. The cables are made of steel wire with a cross-sectional area of 0.1 square inches per cable. If the maximum allowable stress for the steel is 30,000 psi, what should be the minimum number of wires in each cable to ensure safety?
A) 2
B) 4
C) 8
D) 16
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The maximum allowable stress is the force per unit area. To find the total force each wire can handle, multiply the stress by the cross-sectional area of one wire: F = A * σ. Then, divide the load by this value to find the number of wires needed: N = P / (A * σ). Given values: P = 40,000 pounds per cable, σ = 30,000 psi.

Explanation:
This question assesses the student's ability to calculate the minimum number of wires required for a suspension bridge based on stress and load requirements.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Not considering that there are two cables supporting the load. This would lead to an incorrect calculation of the force per wire.

Problem 6 (:6):
A wind turbine generates electricity with a rated power output of 2 megawatts under optimal wind conditions. If the turbine's rotor diameter is 100 feet and its efficiency is 45%, what is the average wind speed (in miles per hour) required to achieve this power output?
A) 12
B) 18
C) 24
D) 30
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The power output of a wind turbine is related to the wind speed by the Betz limit, which states that the maximum efficiency of a wind turbine is 59.3%. Since the given efficiency is 45%, we can assume that the actual wind speed is higher than what would be required for 59.3% efficiency. The power output formula is: P = (1/2) * ρ * A * v^3, where P is power, ρ is air density (approximately 0.076 lb/ft³ or 1.225 kg/ m³), A is the swept area of the rotor (π * D^2 / 4), and v is the wind speed. Rearrange the formula to solve for v: v = ((2 * P) / (ρ * A))^(1/3). Given values: P = 2,000,000 W, D = 100 ft.

Explanation:
This question assesses the student's ability to calculate wind speed based on turbine power output and efficiency.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Assuming that the given efficiency is equal to the Betz limit. This would lead to an underestimation of the required wind speed.

Problem 7 (:7):
A structural engineer needs to design a concrete column with a height of 15 feet and a diameter of 1 foot to support a load of 60,000 pounds. If the compressive strength of the concrete is 4,000 psi and the safety factor is 2, what should be the minimum thickness (in inches) of the column?
A) 3
B) 6
C) 9
D) 12
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The compressive strength formula for a cylindrical column is: P = π * d^2 * h * f / 4, where P is the maximum load the column can support (factored by safety), d is the diameter, h is the height, and f is the compressive strength. To find the minimum thickness, rearrange the formula for thickness: t = (P * 4) / (π * d^2 * h * f). Given values: P = 60,000 lb * 2, d = 1 ft, h = 15 ft, and f = 4,000 psi. Convert units to consistent units: d = 12 inches, h = 180 inches.

Explanation:
This question assesses the student's ability to calculate the minimum thickness of a concrete column based on load requirements and compressive strength.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Not factoring in the safety factor when calculating the maximum load the column can support. This would lead to an insufficient column thickness.
