Problem 1 (Asdf1):
Find the equation of the line that is normal to the curve y = x^3 - 4x + 2 at the point (1, -1).
A) (y + 1) = -(1/3)(x - 1)
B) (y + 1) = (1/3)(x - 1)
C) (y + 1) = -3(x - 1)
D) (y + 1) = 3(x - 1)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The slope of the tangent line at a point on the curve is given by the derivative of the function evaluated at that point. So, we first find the derivative of y = x^3 - 4x + 2: f'(x) = 3x^2 - 4. Now, we evaluate the derivative at x = 1: f'(1) = 3(1)^2 - 4 = -1. The slope of the normal line is the negative reciprocal of the tangent line's slope, so m_normal = 1 / (-1) = -1. Since we have a point (1, -1) on the normal line and its slope, we can use the point-slope form to find the equation: y - (-1) = -1(x - 1). Simplifying gives us (y + 1) = -(x - 1).

Explanation:
The normal line is perpendicular to the tangent line at a given point. The slope of the normal line is the negative reciprocal of the tangent line's slope.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to use the derivative directly as the slope of the normal line without taking its negative reciprocal.

Problem 2 (Asdf2):
Find the equation of the line that is normal to the curve y = 2sin(x) at the point (π/6, √3/2).
A) (y - √3/2) = -(3√3/4)(x - π/6)
B) (y - √3/2) = (3√3/4)(x - π/6)
C) (y - √3/2) = -(√3/4)(x - π/6)
D) (y - √3/2) = (√3/4)(x - π/6)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The derivative of y = 2sin(x) is f'(x) = 2cos(x). At x = π/6, the slope of the tangent line is f'(π/6) = 2cos(π/6) = √3. The slope of the normal line is the negative reciprocal of this, so m_normal = -1 / √3 = -(√3/3). Using the point-slope form with the point (π/6, √3/2) and slope -(√3/3) gives us (y - √3/2) = -(√3/3)(x - π/6).

Explanation:
The normal line is perpendicular to the tangent line at a given point. The slope of the normal line is the negative reciprocal of the tangent line's slope.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget that the sine function has a period of 2π, which might lead to an incorrect derivative calculation.

Problem 3 (Asdf3):
Find the equation of the line normal to the curve y = e^x at the point (0, 1).
A) (y - 1) = -e(x - 0)
B) (y - 1) = e(x - 0)

D) (y - 1) = -√e(x - 0)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The derivative of y = e^x is f'(x) = e^x. At x = 0, the slope of the tangent line is f'(0) = e^0 = 1. The slope of the normal line is the negative reciprocal of this, so m_normal = -1 / 1 = -1. Using the point-slope form with the point (0, 1) and slope -1 gives us (y - 1) = -1(x - 0).

Explanation:
The normal line is perpendicular to the tangent line at a given point. The slope of the normal line is the negative reciprocal of the tangent line's slope.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget that the derivative of e^x is simply e^x itself.

Problem 5 (Asdf5):
Find the equation of the line normal to the curve y = x^2 + 3x - 4 at the point (-2, -6).
A) (y + 6) = (1/7)(x + 2)
B) (y + 6) = -(1/7)(x + 2)
C) (y + 6) = -7(x + 2)
D) (y + 6) = 7(x + 2)
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The derivative of y = x^2 + 3x - 4 is f'(x) = 2x + 3. At x = -2, the slope of the tangent line is f'(-2) = 2(-2) + 3 = -1. The slope of the normal line is the negative reciprocal of this, so m_normal = -1 / (-1) = 1. Using the point-slope form with the point (-2, -6) and slope 1 gives us (y + 6) = 1(x + 2). However, since we're looking for a normal line, which is perpendicular to the tangent, we need to use the negative reciprocal of this slope: (y + 6) = -(1/7)(x + 2).

Explanation:
The normal line is perpendicular to the tangent line at a given point. The slope of the normal line is the negative reciprocal of the tangent line's slope.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget that we need to use the negative reciprocal of the tangent line's slope for the normal line.

Problem 7 (Asdf7):
Find the equation of the line normal to the curve y = 2cos(3x) at the point (π/6, √3).
A) (y - √3) = -(1/(6√3))(x - π/6)
B) (y - √3) = (1/(6√3))(x - π/6)
C) (y - √3) = -(6√3)(x - π/6)

Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The derivative of y = 2cos(3x) is f'(x) = -6sin(3x). At x = π/6, the slope of the tangent line is f'(π/6) = -6sin(3(π/6)) = -6sin(π/2) = -6. The slope of the normal line is the negative reciprocal of this, so m_normal = -1 / (-6) = 1/6. Using the point-slope form with the point (π/6, √3) and slope 1/6 gives us (y - √3) = (1/6)(x - π/6). However, since we're looking for a normal line, which is perpendicular to the tangent, we need to use the negative reciprocal of this slope: (y - √3) = -(1/(6√3))(x - π/6).

Explanation:
The normal line is perpendicular to the tangent line at a given point. The slope of the normal line is the negative reciprocal of the tangent line's slope.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget that when differentiating cos(3x), the derivative involves sin(3x) and the chain rule.
