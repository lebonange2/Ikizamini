Problem 1 (Asdf):
Find the derivative of the function F(x) = ∫ from 0 to x sin(t^2) dt. What is F'(x)?
A) cos(x^2)
B) 2x cos(x^2)
C) sin(x^2)
D) 2x cos(x)
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
By the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, Part 1 (FTC), the derivative of an integral F(x) evaluated from a constant to x is the integrand evaluated at x. So F'(x) = sin(x^2). Since the integral has limits from 0 to x, we multiply by the derivative of the upper limit, which is 2x.

Explanation:
The FTC Part 1 states that d/dx ∫f(t)dt from a to x = f(x). In this case, f(t) = sin(t^2), so F'(x) = sin(x^2). The additional factor of 2x comes from differentiating the upper limit x with respect to x.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to forget to multiply by the derivative of the upper limit (2x in this case). This error would lead to answer A, which is only the integrand evaluated at x without accounting for the change in the upper limit.
