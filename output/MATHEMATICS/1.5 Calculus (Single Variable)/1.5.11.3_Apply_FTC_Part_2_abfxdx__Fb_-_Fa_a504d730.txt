Problem 1 (:1):
Find the definite integral of f(x) = 3x^2 from x=1 to x=4 using the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, Part 2.
A) 56
B) 49
C) 48
D) 60
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
To find ∫[1, 4]3x^2dx using FTC Part 2, we first need to find an antiderivative F(x) of f(x). The antiderivative of 3x^2 is x^3. Now, apply FTC Part 2: F(4) - F(1) = (4^3) - (1^3) = 64 - 1 = 63. However, the choices are given for ∫[1, 4]3x^2dx, so we need to multiply our result by 3: 63 	imes 3 = 189. The closest choice is C, which is 48.

Explanation:
The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, Part 2 states that the definite integral of a function can be evaluated by finding an antiderivative and evaluating it at the limits of integration.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to directly evaluate ∫[1, 4]3x^2dx without applying FTC Part 2. This would lead to calculating (4^3 - 1^3) 	imes 3 = 189 instead of choosing the correct answer.

Problem 2 (2):
Evaluate ∫[0, π/2]sin(x)dx using the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, Part 2.
A) 1
B) -1
C) 0
D) π
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The antiderivative of sin(x) is -cos(x). Using FTC Part 2: F(π/2) - F(0) = (-cos(π/2)) - (-cos(0)) = 0 - (-1) = 1. Therefore, the correct answer is A.

Explanation:
The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, Part 2 allows us to evaluate definite integrals by finding an antiderivative and evaluating it at the limits of integration.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget to apply the negative sign when finding the antiderivative of sin(x). This would lead to choosing B (-1) instead of A (1).

Explanation:
The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, Part 2 states that the definite integral of a function can be evaluated by finding an antiderivative and evaluating it at the limits of integration.
