Problem 1 (One):
Evaluate the following improper integral by splitting it at its discontinuity: ∫<sub>1</sub><sup>3</sup> (x<sup>2</sup> - 4) / (x - 2) dx
A) (9/2) ln(2) - 5
B) (9/2) ln(3) - 7
C) (15/2) ln(2) - 8
D) (15/2) ln(3) - 10
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
To evaluate the integral, we split it at x = 2, which is a discontinuity in the integrand. The integral becomes two separate integrals: ∫<sub>1</sub><sup>2</sup> (x<sup>2</sup> - 4) / (x - 2) dx + ∫<sub>2</sub><sup>3</sup> (x<sup>2</sup> - 4) / (x - 2) dx. The first integral is 0 because the integrand is undefined at x = 2, and the second integral can be simplified by factoring out (x - 2): ∫(x + 2)(x - 2)/(x - 2) dx = ∫(x + 2) dx. Integrating this gives us (1/2)x<sup>2</sup> + 2x evaluated from 2 to 3. Plugging in the limits, we get [(1/2)(3)<sup>2</sup> + 2(3)] - [(1/2)(2)<sup>2</sup> + 2(2)] = (9/2) + 6 - (4/2) - 4 = (15/2) - 2 = (15/2) ln(2) - 8.

Explanation:
The correct answer is obtained by splitting the integral at its discontinuity and evaluating each part separately. Choice A is incorrect because it uses the natural logarithm of 3 instead of 2, choice B has an incorrect constant term, and choice D has an extra term (-10) that does not belong in the solution.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to forget to split the integral at the discontinuity or to integrate the entire expression without considering the behavior at x = 2.

Problem 2 (Two):
Evaluate the improper integral ∫<sub>0</sub><sup>π/2</sup> (sin(x) - cos(x)) / sin(x) dx by splitting it at its discontinuity.
A) 1
B) π/2
C) π - 2
D) π + 2
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The integral has a discontinuity at x = π/2, where sin(x) = 0. We split the integral into two parts: ∫<sub>0</sub><sup>π/2</sup> (sin(x) - cos(x)) / sin(x) dx = ∫<sub>0</sub><sup>π/2</sup> 1 - cos(x)/sin(x) dx. The first integral is π/2 from x = 0 to π/2, and the second integral simplifies to ∫<sub>0</sub><sup>π/2</sup> csc(x) dx, which evaluates to ln|csc(x) - cot(x)| evaluated from 0 to π/2. This gives us ln(1 - 0) - ln(0 - (-1)) = 0 - ∞, which is undefined. However, since the original integrand is continuous on [0, π/2), we can ignore this singularity and focus on the first integral, which equals π/2. Adding these together gives us π/2 + 0 = π.

Explanation:
The correct answer is obtained by splitting the integral at its discontinuity and evaluating each part separately. Choice A neglects the contribution from the second integral, choice B only considers one part of the integral, and choice D includes an extra term that does not belong in the solution.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to overlook the singularity at x = π/2 and integrate the entire expression without considering its behavior there.

Problem 3 (Three):
Evaluate the improper integral ∫<sub>1</sub><sup>3</sup> (x - 1) / (x + 1) dx by splitting it at its discontinuity.
A) 2 ln(4)
B) 2 ln(5)
C) 3 ln(4)
D) 3 ln(5)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The integral has a discontinuity at x = -1, but since the limits of integration are from 1 to 3, we can ignore this singularity and integrate the entire expression. Integrating (x - 1) / (x + 1), we get ln|x + 1| evaluated from 1 to 3, which gives us ln(4) - ln(2) = ln(2). Multiplying by 2 gives us 2 ln(2) or 2 ln(4).

Explanation:
The correct answer is obtained by integrating the entire expression without splitting it at the discontinuity because the discontinuity does not affect the interval of integration. Choice B has an incorrect constant term, and choices C and D use the natural logarithm of 5 instead of 4.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to split the integral at x = -1 even though it falls outside the limits of integration.

Problem 4 (Four):
Evaluate the improper integral ∫<sub>0</sub><sup>2</sup> (x^3 - 4x) / (x - 2) dx by splitting it at its discontinuity.
A) (8/3)
B) (16/3)
C) (32/3)
D) (64/3)
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The integral has a discontinuity at x = 2, which we must split the integral at: ∫<sub>0</sub><sup>2</sup> (x^3 - 4x) / (x - 2) dx = ∫<sub>0</sub><sup>2</sup> (x^3 - 4x)/(x - 2) dx + ∫<sub>2</sub><sup>2</sup> (x^3 - 4x)/(x - 2) dx. The first integral is 0 because the integrand is undefined at x = 2, and the second integral simplifies to ∫(x^3 - 4x)/(x - 2) dx with limits from 2 to 2, which is also 0. Thus, the total integral is 0.

Explanation:
The correct answer is obtained by splitting the integral at its discontinuity and recognizing that one part of the integral evaluates to 0 due to the singularity at x = 2. Choice A has an incorrect constant term, and choices C and D are much larger than the actual value of the integral.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to integrate the entire expression without considering the behavior at x = 2.

Problem 5 (Five):
Evaluate the improper integral ∫<sub>1</sub><sup>3</sup> (x^2 - 4) / (x + 2) dx by splitting it at its discontinuity.
A) (9/2)
B) (15/2)
C) (21/2)
D) (27/2)
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The integral has a discontinuity at x = -2, which we must split the integral at: ∫<sub>1</sub><sup>3</sup> (x^2 - 4) / (x + 2) dx = ∫<sub>1</sub><sup>3</sup> (x^2 - 4)/(x + 2) dx + ∫<sub>-2</sub><sup>-2</sup> (x^2 - 4)/(x + 2) dx. The first integral is evaluated from 1 to 3, and the second integral is 0 because the limits are the same, so the integrand integrates to zero. Integrating the first integral, we get ∫(x - 2)(x + 2)/(x + 2) dx = ∫(x - 2) dx, which evaluates to (1/2)x^2 - 2x evaluated from 1 to 3. Plugging in the limits, we get [(1/2)(3)^2 - 2(3)] - [(1/2)(1)^2 - 2(1)] = (9/2) - 6 - (1/2) + 2 = (9/2) - (1/2) = 4.

Explanation:
The correct answer is obtained by splitting the integral at its discontinuity and evaluating each part separately. Choice A has an incorrect constant term, and choices C and D are too large for the given limits of integration.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to integrate the entire expression without considering the behavior at x = -2.

Problem 6 (Six):
Evaluate the improper integral ∫<sub>0</sub><sup>π/2</sup> (cos(x) - sin(x)) / cos(x) dx by splitting it at its discontinuity.
A) 1
B) π/2
C) π
D) π + 1
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The integral has a discontinuity at x = π/2, where cos(x) = 0. We split the integral into two parts: ∫<sub>0</sub><sup>π/2</sup> (cos(x) - sin(x)) / cos(x) dx = ∫<sub>0</sub><sup>π/2</sup> 1 - sin(x)/cos(x) dx. The first integral is π/2 from x = 0 to π/2, and the second integral simplifies to ∫<sub>0</sub><sup>π/2</sup> sec(x) dx, which evaluates to ln|sec(x) + tan(x)| evaluated from 0 to π/2. This gives us ln(1 + 0) - ln(1 + 1) = 0 - ln(2) = -ln(2). Adding these together gives us π/2 - ln(2), which simplifies to 1 because ln(2) ≈ 0.693.

Explanation:
The correct answer is obtained by splitting the integral at its discontinuity and evaluating each part separately. Choice B neglects the contribution from the second integral, choice C integrates the entire expression without considering its behavior at x = π/2, and choice D includes an extra term that does not belong in the solution.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to overlook the singularity at x = π/2 and integrate the entire expression without considering its behavior there.

Problem 7 (Seven):
Evaluate the improper integral ∫<sub>1</sub><sup>3</sup> (x - 1) / (x - 1) dx by splitting it at its discontinuity.
A) 0
B) 1
C) 2
D) 3
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The integrand is undefined at x = 1, which falls within the limits of integration. However, since the function is continuous on (1, 3], we can integrate it directly: ∫<sub>1</sub><sup>3</sup> (x - 1) / (x - 1) dx = ∫<sub>1</sub><sup>3</sup> 1 dx. Integrating this gives us x evaluated from 1 to 3, which is 3 - 1 = 2. However, since the integrand simplifies to 1 everywhere except at x = 1, the integral evaluates to 0 because the singularity contributes nothing.

Explanation:
The correct answer is obtained by recognizing that the singularity does not affect the integration on (1, 3]. Choice A correctly accounts for the singularity, while choices B, C, and D integrate the entire expression without considering its behavior at x = 1.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to integrate the entire expression without considering the behavior at x = 1.
