Problem 1 (InfiniteSeriesConvergence_01):
Determine if the infinite series converges or diverges: Σ (n / (n+1)) from n=1 to infinity.
A) Convergent
B) Divergent - p-series with p = 1
C) Divergent - harmonic series
D) Divergent - integral test fails
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The series converges by the limit comparison test, comparing it to the convergent p-series Σ (1/n) with p=1.

Explanation:
The limit comparison test states that if 0 ≤ a_n / b_n ≤ M for all n beyond some point and b_n converges, then a_n also converges. Here, a_n = n / (n+1) and b_n = 1/n.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to think it's divergent due to similarity with the harmonic series, but the extra term in the denominator changes its behavior.

Problem 2 (InfiniteSeriesConvergence_02):
Determine if the infinite series converges or diverges: Σ (-1)^n / n from n=1 to infinity.
A) Convergent - alternating series test
B) Divergent - harmonic series
C) Divergent - integral test fails
D) Convergent - geometric series
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The series converges by the alternating series test, as the terms (-1)^n / n decrease monotonically to zero.

Explanation:
The alternating series test states that if a series has terms (-1)^n * u_n where u_n is decreasing and approaches zero, then the series converges.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to think it's divergent due to similarity with the harmonic series, but the alternating sign makes it converge.

Problem 3 (InfiniteSeriesConvergence_03):
Determine if the infinite series converges or diverges: Σ (2^n / 3^(n+1)) from n=0 to infinity.
A) Convergent - geometric series
B) Divergent - p-series with p = 1
C) Divergent - integral test fails
D) Convergent - ratio test
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The series converges as it is a geometric series with common ratio (2/3)^n and |r| < 1.

Explanation:
A geometric series with first term a and common ratio r converges if |r| < 1. Here, a = 2/3 and r = 2/3.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to apply the ratio test or integral test unnecessarily; it's clearly a geometric series.

Problem 4 (InfiniteSeriesConvergence_04):
Determine if the infinite series converges or diverges: Σ (ln(n) / n^2) from n=1 to infinity.
A) Convergent - integral test
B) Divergent - p-series with p = 1
C) Divergent - harmonic series
D) Convergent - comparison test
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The series converges by the integral test, as the integral of ln(x) / x^2 from 1 to infinity is finite.

Explanation:
The integral test states that if f(x) is positive, continuous, and decreasing on [1, ∞), and its improper integral converges, then the series Σ f(n) converges. Here, f(x) = ln(x) / x^2.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to compare it to p-series or harmonic series without a clear conclusion.

Problem 5 (InfiniteSeriesConvergence_05):
Determine if the infinite series converges or diverges: Σ (n! / 2^n) from n=1 to infinity.
A) Convergent - ratio test
B) Divergent - p-series with p = 1
C) Divergent - integral test fails
D) Convergent - comparison test
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The series converges by the ratio test, as lim (n→∞) |a_n+1 / a_n| < 1.

Explanation:
The ratio test states that if lim (n→∞) |a_n+1 / a_n| < 1, then the series Σ a_n converges. Here, the limit goes to zero, so the series converges.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to try other tests or comparisons without a direct conclusion.

Problem 6 (InfiniteSeriesConvergence_06):
Determine if the infinite series converges or diverges: Σ (sin(n) / n^2) from n=1 to infinity.
A) Convergent - alternating series test
B) Divergent - p-series with p = 1
C) Divergent - integral test fails
D) Convergent - Dirichlet's test
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
The series converges by Dirichlet's test, as |sin(n)| is bounded and 1 / n^2 decreases monotonically to zero.

Explanation:
Dirichlet's test states that if the terms |a_n| are bounded and b_n decreases to zero, then Σ a_n * b_n converges. Here, a_n = sin(n) and b_n = 1 / n^2.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to use other tests like alternating series or integral test without direct applicability.
