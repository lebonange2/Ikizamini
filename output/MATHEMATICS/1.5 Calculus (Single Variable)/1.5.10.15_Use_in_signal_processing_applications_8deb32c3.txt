Problem 1 (Inverse Fourier Transform usage):
What is the primary use of the Inverse Fourier Transform in signal processing?
A) Converting a continuous-time signal to a discrete-time signal.
B) Determining the frequency content of a signal.
C) Reconstructing a time-domain signal from its frequency domain representation.
D) Filtering out unwanted noise from a signal.
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The Inverse Fourier Transform is used to reconstruct a time-domain signal from its frequency domain representation. It helps in understanding how the signal varies over time based on its frequency components.

Explanation:
A: The Continuous-Time Fourier Transform does that, not the Inverse Fourier Transform. B: The Fourier Transform determines the frequency content. D: Filtering is an application but not the primary use of the Inverse Fourier Transform.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake is to confuse the roles of Fourier Transform and Inverse Fourier Transform.

Problem 2 (Filter Design):
Which type of filter can be designed using frequency domain information obtained from a signal?
A) Butterworth Filter
B) Mean Filter
C) Median Filter
D) Moving Average Filter
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Butterworth Filters are designed based on the desired frequency response, which can be obtained from the Fourier Transform or Inverse Fourier Transform of a signal.

Explanation:
B: Mean and Median Filters are non-parametric filters that operate in the time domain. C: Same as B. D: Moving Average Filter is also a time-domain filter.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake is to choose time-domain filters instead of frequency-domain designed ones.

Problem 3 (Signal Compression):
What technique can be used to compress signals in the context of signal processing?
A) Quantization
B) Amplitude Modulation
C) Frequency Domain Sampling
D) Time-Shifting
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Quantization is a process that reduces the number of bits required to represent a signal, which helps in compression.

Explanation:
B: Amplitude Modulation changes the amplitude of a carrier wave. C: Frequency Domain Sampling does not necessarily lead to compression. D: Time-Shifting doesn't change the size of the signal.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake is to confuse compression techniques with modulation or sampling methods.

Problem 5 (Signal Smoothing):
What technique can be used to smooth noisy signals in the time domain?
A) Savitzky-Golay Filter
B) Discrete Cosine Transform
C) Fast Fourier Transform
D) Z-Transform
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Savitzky-Golay Filters are used for smoothing time-domain signals by applying a moving average with polynomial fitting.

Explanation:
B: Discrete Cosine Transform is a frequency domain transform. C: Fast Fourier Transform is also a frequency domain transform. D: Z-Transform is a mathematical tool, not a signal smoothing method.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake is to choose frequency domain transforms or unrelated methods.

Problem 6 (Signal Bandpass):
In a communication system, what type of filter would be used to allow only specific frequencies to pass through?
A) Low-Pass Filter
B) High-Pass Filter
C) Bandpass Filter
D) All-Pass Filter
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
A Bandpass Filter is designed to allow signals within a certain frequency range to pass while attenuating frequencies outside that range.

Explanation:
A: Low-Pass Filter allows low frequencies only. B: High-Pass Filter allows high frequencies only. D: All-Pass Filter passes all frequencies equally, without attenuation.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake is to choose filters that allow passage of only one type of frequency.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 7 (Signal Demodulation):
What process in signal processing involves converting a modulated signal back into its original form?
A) Sampling
B) Quantization
C) Decimation
D) Demodulation
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
Demodulation is the process of extracting the original information from a carrier wave that has been modulated.

Explanation:
A: Sampling involves taking measurements at regular intervals. B: Quantization reduces the number of bits to represent a signal. C: Decimation is reducing the sampling rate.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake is to confuse demodulation with other processes related to signal representation.
