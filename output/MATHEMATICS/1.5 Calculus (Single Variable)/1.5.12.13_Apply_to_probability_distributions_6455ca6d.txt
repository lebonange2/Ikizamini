Problem 1 (AsdFg6789):
A fair six-sided die is rolled once. What is the probability of rolling an odd number?
A) 1/2
B) 1/3
C) 2/3
D) 1/6
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The probability of rolling an odd number on a fair six-sided die is 3 out of 6, because there are three odd numbers (1, 3, and 5) and six possible outcomes in total. This simplifies to 1/2.

Explanation:
When calculating probabilities for discrete events, like rolling a die, you divide the number of desired outcomes by the total number of possible outcomes.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to assume that each outcome has an equal probability, which leads to choosing B (1/3), but this only applies if all outcomes are equally likely.

Problem 2 (ZxcvbNmlk):
A bag contains 5 red balls and 7 blue balls. If one ball is drawn at random, what is the probability that it will be a red ball?
A) 2/3
B) 1/2
C) 5/12
D) 7/12
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The probability of drawing a red ball from the bag is calculated by dividing the number of red balls (5) by the total number of balls in the bag (5 + 7 = 12). So, the probability is 5/12.

Explanation:
When calculating probabilities with replacement, each draw does not affect the subsequent draws. The total outcomes remain constant, and you only consider the desired outcome for each question.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to add the number of red balls (5) to the number of blue balls (7), assuming that's the total probability, which leads to choosing D (7/12). However, this method would be correct if you were calculating the probability of drawing a red ball and then another red ball without replacement.

Problem 3 (QwertyUij):
A standard deck of 52 cards is shuffled. What is the probability of drawing a heart as the first card?
A) 1/4
B) 1/2
C) 3/4
D) 1/13
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
There are 52 cards in a deck, and 13 of them are hearts. The probability of drawing a heart as the first card is thus 13 out of 52, which simplifies to 1/4.

Explanation:
When drawing from a well-shuffled deck, each card has an equal chance of being drawn. Since there are four suits and one of them is hearts, the probability of drawing a heart is 1/4.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to confuse the number of hearts with the total number of cards, leading to choosing D (1/13), which would be the probability of drawing a specific card from the deck.

Problem 4 (BnmLkjhGf):
A fair coin is flipped twice. What is the probability of getting at least one head?
A) 1/4
B) 3/4
C) 1/2
D) 7/8
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
There are four possible outcomes when flipping a coin twice: HH, HT, TH, and TT. Three out of these four have at least one head (HH, HT, and TH). So the probability is 3/4.

Explanation:
When considering events that can happen in multiple trials, like flipping a coin twice, it's important to count all outcomes with the desired property. In this case, we're looking for any outcome with at least one head.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to only consider the probability of getting exactly one head (HT or TH), which would be 1/2, and not including the probability of getting two heads (HH). This leads to choosing C.

Problem 5 (PoiuytRtf):
A box contains 3 red marbles, 4 blue marbles, and 5 green marbles. If two marbles are drawn without replacement, what is the probability that both will be red?
A) 1/20
B) 9/10
C) 6/45
D) 3/10
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
First, we calculate the probability of drawing a red marble on the first draw: 3 red marbles out of 12 total (3 + 4 + 5). After drawing one red marble, there are now 2 red marbles left and 11 total. The probability of drawing another red is 2/11. Multiply these probabilities together to get 3/12 	imes 2/11 = 6/132, which simplifies to 6/45.

Explanation:
When drawing without replacement, the probability of each draw changes based on previous draws. Here, we first find the probability of drawing a red marble and then multiply it by the probability of drawing another red after one has been drawn.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to assume that the probability remains constant for both draws (3/12), leading to choosing A (1/20). However, this neglects the reduction in total marbles after the first draw.

Problem 6 (BvcxZqweR):
An urn contains 7 white balls and 5 black balls. If two balls are drawn without replacement, what is the probability that one will be white and one will be black?
A) 2/3
B) 1/2
C) 14/35
D) 7/15
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
There are two ways to draw one white ball and one black ball: first white then black or first black then white. The probability of drawing a white ball first is 7/12, followed by a black ball with probability 5/11. The other way around has a probability of 5/12 	imes 7/11. Adding these probabilities together gives us (7/12 	imes 5/11) + (5/12 	imes 7/11) = 35/66, which simplifies to 7/15.

Explanation:
When calculating the probability of two events happening in a specific order without replacement, you need to consider both orders and sum their probabilities. Here, we're looking for one white and one black ball, regardless of the order.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to assume that each event has an equal probability (7/12 	imes 5/12), leading to choosing C (14/35). However, this method would be correct if both balls were drawn with replacement.

Problem 7 (SdfghJkl):
A fair six-...

B) 5/36

D) 1/6
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
There are six possible outcomes for each die roll, so there are a total of 6 	imes 6 = 36 possible combinations. The sums that equal 7 are (1, 6), (2, 5), (3, 4), (4, 3), and (5, 2). So, the probability is 5 out of 36.

Explanation:
When rolling two dice, we're looking for all combinations that result in a sum of 7. We then divide the number of successful outcomes by the total number of possible outcomes.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to assume that each roll has an equal probability and multiply those probabilities (1/6 	imes 1/6), leading to choosing A (1/36). This would be correct if we were looking for two specific numbers, but not for a sum.
