Problem 1 (Asdf):
Find the antiderivative of f(x) = x^3 π using integration by substitution. Let u = x^2. What is the appropriate value for du?
A) du = 2x^3 dx
B) du = 2x dx
C) du = x^4 dx
D) du = x dx
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
To use integration by substitution, we need to express the integrand in terms of u and its derivative du. Given u = x^2, we differentiate both sides with respect to x: du/dx = 2x. Thus, du = 2x dx. So, the correct choice is B.

Explanation:
The key here is to recognize that when u = x^2, du/dx is the derivative of u with respect to x, which is 2x. This leads to du = 2x dx.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to square the derivative (i.e., du = 2x^3 dx), but that's not correct because we're looking for the differential of u with respect to x, not the value of u itself.

Problem 2 (Asdf):
Integrate f(x) = e^(2x) using integration by substitution. Let u = 2x. What is the appropriate value for du?
A) du = 2e^(2x) dx
B) du = e^(2x) dx
C) du = e^x dx
D) du = x dx
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
To use integration by substitution, we need to express the integrand in terms of u and its derivative du. Given u = 2x, we differentiate both sides with respect to x: du/dx = 2. Thus, du = 2 dx. However, since we're integrating e^(2x), we must multiply du by the exponential term's coefficient (1/2) to get the correct expression: du = e^(2x) dx. So, the correct choice is B.

Explanation:
The key here is to remember that when u = 2x, du/dx is 2, but we must also account for the exponential term's coefficient (1/2) in front of e^(2x). This leads to du = e^(2x) dx.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget about the coefficient (i.e., du = 2e^(2x) dx), but that's not correct because we need to maintain the original function's form in terms of u.
