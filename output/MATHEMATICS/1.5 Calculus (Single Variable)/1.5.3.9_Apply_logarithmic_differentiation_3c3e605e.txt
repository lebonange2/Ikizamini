Problem 1 ([1.5.3.9]Q1):
Find the derivative of f(x) = x^3 * e^(2x) using logarithmic differentiation.
A) 3x^2 * e^(2x)
B) x^3 * 2e^(2x) + 3x^2 * e^(2x)
C) 6x^2 * e^(2x)
D) 2x * e^(2x) + x^3 * 2e^(2x)
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Take the natural logarithm of both sides: ln(f(x)) = ln(x^3 * e^(2x)). Use properties of logarithms to separate the terms: ln(f(x)) = 3ln(x) + 2x. Differentiate both sides with respect to x, remembering that the left side is f'(x)/f(x): f'(x)/f(x) = 3/x + 2. Multiply by f(x) to get f'(x): f'(x) = (3/x + 2)x^3 * e^(2x). Simplify: f'(x) = x^3 * 2e^(2x) + 3x^2 * e^(2x).

Explanation:
Logarithmic differentiation helps to simplify the derivative calculation by taking the logarithm of both sides and using properties of logarithms. The correct answer is found after differentiating and simplifying.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is forgetting to multiply f'(x) by f(x) after differentiating the left side, leading to an incorrect simplified form.

Problem 2 ([1.5.3.9]Q2):
Find the derivative of g(x) = (sin(x))^4 * cos(x) using logarithmic differentiation.
A) 4(sin(x))^3 * cos^2(x) - (sin(x))^4 * sin(x)
B) (sin(x))^3 * cos^2(x) + 4(sin(x))^4 * cos(x)
C) 4(sin(x))^3 * cos(x) + (sin(x))^4 * sin(x)
D) 4(sin(x))^3 * cos^2(x) + (sin(x))^4 * cos(x)
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
Take the natural logarithm of both sides: ln(g(x)) = ln((sin(x))^4 * cos(x)). Use properties of logarithms to separate the terms: ln(g(x)) = 4ln(sin(x)) + ln(cos(x)). Differentiate both sides with respect to x, remembering that the left side is g'(x)/g(x): g'(x)/g(x) = 4cos(x)/(sin(x)) - sin(x)/cos(x). Simplify and multiply by g(x) to get g'(x): g'(x) = (4(sin(x))^3 * cos^2(x) + (sin(x))^4 * cos(x)).

Explanation:
Logarithmic differentiation simplifies the derivative calculation. The correct answer is found after differentiating, simplifying, and multiplying by g(x).

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is not simplifying the derivative before multiplying by g(x), leading to a more complicated expression.

Problem 3 ([1.5.3.9]Q3):
Find the derivative of h(x) = (2x + 1)^5 / (3x - 2)^3 using logarithmic differentiation.
A) 5(2x + 1)^4 * (3x - 2)^2 / (2x + 1)^5
B) 5(2x + 1)^4 * (3x - 2)^2 - 3(3x - 2)^2 / (2x + 1)^5
C) 5(2x + 1)^4 * (3x - 2) - 3(3x - 2)^3 / (2x + 1)^4
D) 5(2x + 1)^4 * (3x - 2)^2 - 3(3x - 2)^3 / (2x + 1)^5
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
Take the natural logarithm of both sides: ln(h(x)) = ln((2x + 1)^5 / (3x - 2)^3). Use properties of logarithms to separate the terms: ln(h(x)) = 5ln(2x + 1) - 3ln(3x - 2). Differentiate both sides with respect to x, remembering that the left side is h'(x)/h(x): h'(x)/h(x) = (5/(2x + 1)) * (2) - (3/(3x - 2)) * (3). Simplify and multiply by h(x) to get h'(x): h'(x) = 5(2x + 1)^4 * (3x - 2)^2 - 3(3x - 2)^3 / (2x + 1)^5.

Explanation:
Logarithmic differentiation simplifies the derivative calculation. The correct answer is found after differentiating, simplifying, and multiplying by h(x).

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is not distributing the negative sign when differentiating the denominator term, leading to an incorrect expression.

Problem 4 ([1.5.3.9]Q4):
Find the derivative of p(x) = e^(x^2 + 3x) using logarithmic differentiation.
A) e^(x^2 + 3x) * (2x + 3)
B) e^(x^2 + 3x) * (x^2 + 3x)^2
C) 2xe^(x^2 + 3x) + 3e^(x^2 + 3x)
D) 2xe^(x^2 + 3x) - 3e^(x^2 + 3x)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Take the natural logarithm of both sides: ln(p(x)) = ln(e^(x^2 + 3x)). Use properties of logarithms to simplify: ln(p(x)) = x^2 + 3x. Differentiate both sides with respect to x, remembering that the left side is p'(x)/p(x): p'(x)/p(x) = 2x + 3. Multiply by p(x) to get p'(x): p'(x) = e^(x^2 + 3x) * (2x + 3).

Explanation:
Logarithmic differentiation simplifies the derivative calculation for an exponential function with a composite base. The correct answer is found after differentiating and multiplying by p(x).

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is not recognizing that e^(x^2 + 3x) can be simplified before taking its logarithm, leading to unnecessary complexity.

Problem 5 ([1.5.3.9]Q5):
Find the derivative of q(x) = (ln(x))^4 / x using logarithmic differentiation.
A) 4(ln(x))^3 * (1/x^2)
B) 4(ln(x))^3 * (1/x) - (ln(x))^4 / x^2
C) (ln(x))^3 * (1/x) + 4(ln(x))^4 / x
D) 4(ln(x))^3 * (1/x) - 4(ln(x))^4 / x
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Take the natural logarithm of both sides: ln(q(x)) = ln((ln(x))^4 / x). Use properties of logarithms to separate the terms: ln(q(x)) = 4ln(ln(x)) - ln(x). Differentiate both sides with respect to x, remembering that the left side is q'(x)/q(x): q'(x)/q(x) = (4/(ln(x))) * (1/x) - (1/x). Simplify and multiply by q(x) to get q'(x): q'(x) = 4(ln(x))^3 * (1/x) - (ln(x))^4 / x^2.

Explanation:
Logarithmic differentiation simplifies the derivative calculation for a quotient with a logarithm in the numerator. The correct answer is found after differentiating, simplifying, and multiplying by q(x).

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is not distributing the negative sign when differentiating the denominator term, leading to an incorrect expression.

A) -3(x^2 + 1)^{-5/2} * 2x
B) -3(x^2 + 1)^{-5/2} * (x^2 + 1)
C) -(x^2 + 1)^{-5/2} * (x^2 + 1)^2
D) -3(x^2 + 1)^{-5/2} * x
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Take the natural logarithm of both sides: ln(r(x)) = ln((x^2 + 1)^(-3/2)). Use properties of logarithms to simplify: ln(r(x)) = -3/2ln(x^2 + 1). Differentiate both sides with respect to x, remembering that the left side is r'(x)/r(x): r'(x)/r(x) = (-3/2) * (1/(x^2 + 1)) * (2x). Simplify and multiply by r(x) to get r'(x): r'(x) = -3(x^2 + 1)^{-5/2} * 2x.

Explanation:
Logarithmic differentiation simplifies the derivative calculation for a power with a composite base. The correct answer is found after differentiating, simplifying, and multiplying by r(x).

Common mistake note:

Problem 7 ([1.5.3.9]Q7):
Find the derivative of s(x) = (x + 2)^3 * e^(-x) using logarithmic differentiation.
A) 3(x + 2)^2 * e^(-x) - (x + 2)^3 * e^(-x)
B) 3(x + 2)^2 * e^(-x) + (x + 2)^3 * e^(-x)
C) (x + 2)^3 * (-e^(-x))
D) 3(x + 2)^2 * e^(-x) - x(x + 2)^2 * e^(-x)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Take the natural logarithm of both sides: ln(s(x)) = ln((x + 2)^3 * e^(-x)). Use properties of logarithms to separate the terms: ln(s(x)) = 3ln(x + 2) - x. Differentiate both sides with respect to x, remembering that the left side is s'(x)/s(x): s'(x)/s(x) = (3/(x + 2)) * (1) - e^(-x). Simplify and multiply by s(x) to get s'(x): s'(x) = 3(x + 2)^2 * e^(-x) - (x + 2)^3 * e^(-x).

Explanation:
Logarithmic differentiation simplifies the derivative calculation for a product with different bases. The correct answer is found after differentiating, simplifying, and multiplying by s(x).

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is not distributing the negative sign when differentiating the exponential term, leading to an incorrect expression.
