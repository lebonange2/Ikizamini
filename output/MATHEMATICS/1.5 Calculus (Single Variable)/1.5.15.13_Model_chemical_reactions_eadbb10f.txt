Problem 1 (AsF9cGdJ):
What is the balanced equation for the reaction between hydrochloric acid (HCl) and sodium hydroxide (NaOH)?
A) HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H2O
B) 2HCl + NaOH → 2NaCl + H2O
C) HCl + 2NaOH → NaCl + 2H2O
D) 2HCl + 2NaOH → 4NaCl + 2H2O
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The balanced equation for the reaction between HCl and NaOH is HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H2O. The number of atoms for each element must be equal on both sides of the equation.

Explanation:
Balancing chemical equations involves ensuring that the number of atoms for each element is the same on both the reactant and product sides.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to forget to balance the equation, resulting in an unequal number of atoms for one or more elements. Choice B is a common error where twice as many HCl molecules are used but only one NaOH molecule, leading to an imbalance.

Problem 2 (x7cE6tVz):
What is the product formed when iron (Fe) reacts with sulfuric acid (H2SO4)?
A) FeSO4 + H2
B) Fe2(SO4)3 + H2
C) Fe(OH)2 + SO2
D) FeCl3 + H2O
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The product formed when iron reacts with sulfuric acid is FeSO4 and hydrogen gas (H2). The balanced equation is Fe + H2SO4 → FeSO4 + H2.

Explanation:
This reaction involves the displacement of hydrogen from an acid by a more reactive metal, resulting in the formation of a salt (iron sulfate) and hydrogen gas.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to choose option B, which represents iron(III) sulfate instead of iron(II) sulfate. The correct product should have iron in its +2 oxidation state.

Problem 3 (vZdLpR5W):
What is the balanced equation for the combustion of methane (CH4)?
A) CH4 + O2 → CO2 + H2O
B) 2CH4 + 3O2 → 2CO2 + 4H2O
C) CH4 + 2O2 → CO2 + 2H2O
D) 4CH4 + 7O2 → 4CO2 + 8H2O
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The balanced equation for the combustion of methane is 2CH4 + 3O2 → 2CO2 + 4H2O. The number of atoms for each element must be equal on both sides.

Explanation:
Combustion reactions involve complete燃烧 of a hydrocarbon with oxygen, producing carbon dioxide and water. In this case, two methane molecules react with three oxygen molecules to produce two carbon dioxide molecules and four water molecules.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to forget to balance the equation, resulting in an unequal number of atoms for one or more elements. Choice C has only one oxygen molecule on the right side, which would lead to an imbalance.

Problem 4 (nKqLX9pJ):
What is the balanced equation for the reaction between silver nitrate (AgNO3) and sodium chloride (NaCl)?
A) AgNO3 + NaCl → AgCl + NaNO3
B) 2AgNO3 + 2NaCl → 2AgCl + 2NaNO3
C) AgNO3 + 2NaCl → 2AgCl + NaNO3
D) 4AgNO3 + 4NaCl → 4AgCl + 4NaNO3
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The balanced equation for the reaction between silver nitrate and sodium chloride is AgNO3 + NaCl → AgCl + NaNO3. The number of atoms for each element must be equal on both sides.

Explanation:
This is a double displacement reaction, where the cations and anions switch places to form new compounds. In this case, silver chloride (AgCl) and sodium nitrate (NaNO3) are formed.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to choose option B or C, which involve multiplying both reactants and products by 2 or 1, respectively, without ensuring the balance of atoms. Choice D multiplies everything by 4, leading to an imbalance.

Problem 5 (rLJ9sY8P):
What is the product formed when magnesium (Mg) reacts with hydrochloric acid (HCl)?
A) MgCl + H2O
B) MgCl2 + H2
C) Mg(OH)2 + HCl
D) MgSO4 + H2

Solution:

Explanation:
This reaction involves the displacement of hydrogen from an acid by a more reactive metal, resulting in the formation of a salt (magnesium chloride) and hydrogen gas.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to choose option A, which represents magnesium hydroxide instead of magnesium chloride. The correct product should have magnesium in its +2 oxidation state.

Problem 6 (tGKX9fPz):
What is the balanced equation for the reaction between aluminum (Al) and copper sulfate (CuSO4)?
A) Al + CuSO4 → AlSO4 + Cu
B) 2Al + 3CuSO4 → Al2(SO4)3 + 3Cu
C) Al + CuSO4 → Al2(SO4)3 + Cu
D) 2Al + CuSO4 → Al2(SO4)3 + 2Cu
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The balanced equation for the reaction between aluminum and copper sulfate is 2Al + 3CuSO4 → Al2(SO4)3 + 3Cu. The number of atoms for each element must be equal on both sides.

Explanation:
This is a single displacement reaction, where aluminum replaces copper in the compound. In this case, two aluminum atoms react with three copper sulfate molecules to produce aluminum sulfate and three copper atoms.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to choose option A or C, which do not balance the equation correctly. Choice D has an imbalance in the number of aluminum atoms.

Problem 7 (e9xLH5tV):
What is the product formed when calcium carbonate (CaCO3) decomposes into calcium oxide (CaO) and carbon dioxide (CO2)?
A) CaO + CO2
B) 2CaO + 2CO2
C) CaCO3 → CaO + CO2
D) CaO + 2CO2
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The product formed when calcium carbonate decomposes is calcium oxide (CaO) and carbon dioxide (CO2). The balanced equation is CaCO3 → CaO + CO2.

Explanation:
Decomposition reactions involve the breakdown of a compound into simpler substances. In this case, heating calcium carbonate results in the formation of calcium oxide and release of carbon dioxide gas.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to choose option A or B, which represent incorrect stoichiometry. Choice D has an imbalance in the number of oxygen atoms.
