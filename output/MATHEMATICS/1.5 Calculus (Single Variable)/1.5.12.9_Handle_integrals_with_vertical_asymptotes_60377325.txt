Problem 1 (:question_1):
Evaluate the definite integral π∫(x/(x^2 - 4))dx from -3 to 5.
A) -π/2
B) π/2
C) 0
D) Does not converge
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
The integrand has a vertical asymptote at x = 2, which is within the integration interval. The integral does not converge due to the singularity.

Explanation:
Vertical asymptotes in integrands require splitting the interval and checking convergence separately on each part.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to integrate without considering the vertical asymptote.

Problem 2 (:question_2):
Evaluate the definite integral π∫((x^2 + 1)/(x - 5))dx from -3 to 4.
A) 6π
B) -6π
C) Does not converge
D) Indeterminate
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The integrand has a removable singularity at x = 5, but no vertical asymptote. Integrating: π∫((x + 5)(x - 4)/(x - 5))dx from -3 to 4 simplifies to π∫((x + 5)dx) from -3 to 4, which evaluates to 6π. However, since the integrand has a vertical asymptote at x = 5 within the integration interval, the integral does not converge.

Explanation:
Removable singularities do not affect convergence; we can integrate as if the singularity were not there. But the presence of a vertical asymptote at x = 5 causes the integral to diverge.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to overlook the vertical asymptote at x = 5 and think that the removable singularity is the only factor affecting convergence.
