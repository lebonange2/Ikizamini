Problem 1 (q1):
Simplify: \( \sqrt{18} + \sqrt{8} - \sqrt{2} \)
A) 4\sqrt{2}
B) 5\sqrt{2}
C) 3\sqrt{2}
D) 6\sqrt{2}
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
First, simplify each term: \( \sqrt{18} = \sqrt{9 \times 2} = 3\sqrt{2} \), \( \sqrt{8} = \sqrt{4 \times 2} = 2\sqrt{2} \). Now, the expression becomes \( 3\sqrt{2} + 2\sqrt{2} - \sqrt{2} = (3+2-1)\sqrt{2} = 4\sqrt{2} \).

Explanation:
Combine like terms by adding and subtracting the coefficients before the radical term.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is not simplifying the radicals before adding or subtracting, leading to wrong coefficients.

Problem 2 (q2):
What is \( 5\sqrt{3} - 2\sqrt{12} + \sqrt{48} \) simplified?
A) 11\sqrt{3}
B) 6\sqrt{3}
C) 3\sqrt{3}
D) 5\sqrt{3}
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
Simplify \( \sqrt{12} = \sqrt{4 \times 3} = 2\sqrt{3} \) and \( \sqrt{48} = \sqrt{16 \times 3} = 4\sqrt{3} \). The expression becomes \( 5\sqrt{3} - 2(2\sqrt{3}) + 4\sqrt{3} = 5\sqrt{3} - 4\sqrt{3} + 4\sqrt{3} = 5\sqrt{3} \).

Explanation:
Simplify each radical separately and then combine like terms.

Common mistake note:
A mistake occurs if only some radicals are simplified, leading to incorrect addition of coefficients.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 3 (q3):
Calculate \( \sqrt{50} + \sqrt{50} \).
A) 10\sqrt{2}
B) 5\sqrt{2}
C) 2\sqrt{50}
D) 15\sqrt{2}
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Simplify \( \sqrt{50} = \sqrt{25 \times 2} = 5\sqrt{2} \). Adding two of these gives \( 5\sqrt{2} + 5\sqrt{2} = 10\sqrt{2} \).

Explanation:
You must first simplify the radical before performing addition.

Common mistake note:
Students might forget to simplify \( \sqrt{50} \), leading to incorrect summation of terms.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 4 (q4):
Subtract: \( \sqrt{98} - \sqrt{72} \).
A) \sqrt{2}
B) 2\sqrt{2}
C) -2\sqrt{2}
D) \sqrt{7}
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Simplify \( \sqrt{98} = \sqrt{49 \times 2} = 7\sqrt{2} \) and \( \sqrt{72} = \sqrt{36 \times 2} = 6\sqrt{2} \). Then \( 7\sqrt{2} - 6\sqrt{2} = \sqrt{2} \).

Explanation:
Radicals must be simplified before subtraction; combine coefficients of like radicals.

Common mistake note:
Omitting simplification can lead to incorrect elimination or combination of non-like radicals.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 5 (q5):
If \( 3\sqrt{5} + x\sqrt{5} = 9\sqrt{5} \), find \( x \).
A) 4
B) 6
C) 3
D) 5
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Simplify the expression by combining like terms: \( 3\sqrt{5} + x\sqrt{5} = 9\sqrt{5} \implies (3 + x) \sqrt{5} = 9\sqrt{5} \). Therefore, \( 3 + x = 9 \), so \( x = 6 \).

Explanation:
Equate coefficients of \( \sqrt{5} \), as only the coefficients determine the equality in such equations.

Common mistake note:
One might ignore like terms, leading to incorrect manipulation of the equation.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 6 (q6):
Simplify and evaluate: \( 4\sqrt{27} + \sqrt{75} \).
A) 23\sqrt{3}
B) 17\sqrt{3}
C) 7\sqrt{3}
D) 22\sqrt{3}
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Simplify \( \sqrt{27} = \sqrt{9 \times 3} = 3\sqrt{3} \) and \( \sqrt{75} = \sqrt{25 \times 3} = 5\sqrt{3} \). Then \( 4(3\sqrt{3}) + 5\sqrt{3} = 12\sqrt{3} + 5\sqrt{3} = 17\sqrt{3} \).

Explanation:
Radicals must be fully simplified first, followed by combining like terms.

Common mistake note:
Omitting the simplification of one or both radicals can result in incorrect coefficients.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 7 (q7):
Add \( 2\sqrt{6} + 3\sqrt{24} \).
A) 8\sqrt{6}
B) 10\sqrt{6}
C) 11\sqrt{6}
D) 9\sqrt{6}
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Simplify \( \sqrt{24} = \sqrt{4 \times 6} = 2\sqrt{6} \). The expression becomes \( 2\sqrt{6} + 3(2\sqrt{6}) = 2\sqrt{6} + 6\sqrt{6} = 8\sqrt{6} \).

Explanation:
After simplifying, combine like radical terms for the solution.

Common mistake note:
A common error is in simplifying the radicals incorrectly, leading to wrong addition.
