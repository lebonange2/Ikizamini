Problem 1 (Q1):
Simplify the expression \( \sqrt{50} \).
A) 5\sqrt{2}
B) 10\sqrt{2}
C) \sqrt{10}
D) 25\sqrt{2}
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
\( \sqrt{50} = \sqrt{25 \times 2} = \sqrt{25} \times \sqrt{2} = 5\sqrt{2} \).

Explanation:
The expression \( \sqrt{50} \) can be simplified by breaking it down into \( \sqrt{25 \times 2} \), where \( \sqrt{25} = 5 \). Therefore, \( \sqrt{50} = 5\sqrt{2} \).

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is using \( \sqrt{10} \) by considering \( 50 = 5 \times 10 \), but this overlooks the full simplification.

Problem 2 (Q2):
Simplify the expression \( \sqrt{18} \).
A) \sqrt{3}
B) 9\sqrt{2}
C) 3\sqrt{2}
D) 2\sqrt{3}
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
\( \sqrt{18} = \sqrt{9 \times 2} = \sqrt{9} \times \sqrt{2} = 3\sqrt{2} \).

Explanation:
Factor 18 into 9 and 2, and simplify \( \sqrt{9} = 3 \). Therefore, the expression becomes \( 3\sqrt{2} \).

Common mistake note:
Mistaking the correct factorization as \( 18 = 1 \times 18 \) can result in erroneously choosing incorrect simplifications.

Problem 3 (Q3):
Simplify \( \sqrt{72} \).
A) 12\sqrt{2}
B) 6\sqrt{2}
C) 6\sqrt{3}
D) 9\sqrt{2}
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
\( \sqrt{72} = \sqrt{36 \times 2} = \sqrt{36} \times \sqrt{2} = 6\sqrt{2} \).

Explanation:
Using \( 72 = 36 \times 2 \), simplify \( \sqrt{36} = 6 \), leading to \( 6\sqrt{2} \).

Common mistake note:
Mistaking the factorization, such as choosing \( 72 = 12 \times 6 \), leads to incorrect solutions.

Problem 5 (Q5):
What is \( 2\sqrt{44} \) simplified?
A) 4\sqrt{11}
B) 4\sqrt{22}
C) 8\sqrt{2}
D) 2\sqrt{2}
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
\( 2\sqrt{44} = 2\times\sqrt{4\times11} = 2\times2\times\sqrt{11} = 4\sqrt{11} \).

Explanation:
Recognize \( 44 = 4\times11 \). Simplify \( \sqrt{4} = 2 \), making \( 2\times2\sqrt{11} = 4\sqrt{11} \).

Common mistake note:
Failing to consider both coefficients and the radical part can lead to partial simplifications.

Problem 6 (Q6):
Simplify \( \sqrt{32} \).
A) 8\sqrt{2}
B) 2\sqrt{8}
C) 4\sqrt{2}
D) 16\sqrt{2}
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
\( \sqrt{32} = \sqrt{16\times2} = \sqrt{16}\times\sqrt{2} = 4\sqrt{2} \).

Explanation:
The number 32 is broken down as 16 times 2, simplifying \( \sqrt{16} = 4 \). Thus, the expression is \( 4\sqrt{2} \).

Common mistake note:
Confusing \( \sqrt{32} \) as \( \sqrt{8\times4} \) may lead to incorrect simplifications.

Problem 7 (Q7):
Simplify \( 5\sqrt{75} \).
A) 5\times5\sqrt{3}
B) 5\times15
C) 25\sqrt{3}
D) 15\sqrt{5}
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
\( 5\sqrt{75} = 5\times\sqrt{25\times3} = 5\times5\sqrt{3} = 25\sqrt{3} \).

Explanation:
Recognize \( \sqrt{75} \) as \( \sqrt{25\times3} \), simplify \( \sqrt{25} = 5 \), resulting in \( 25\sqrt{3} \).

Common mistake note:
Confusing the simplification step where the coefficient and radical need separation causes mistakes.
