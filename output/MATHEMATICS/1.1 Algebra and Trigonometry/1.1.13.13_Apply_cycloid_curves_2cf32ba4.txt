Problem 1 (OneCycloidParam):
A particle moves along a cycloid with a generating circle of radius 2 units. If the particle's distance from the origin is given by f(t) = 4π cos(t), where t represents time in seconds, what is the period (time for one complete cycle) of the function f(t)?
A) 1 s
B) 2 s
C) 4 s
D) 8 s
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The period of a cycloid generated by a circle with radius r is T = 8π / r. Given that the radius r is 2 units, we can calculate the period as follows: T = 8π / 2 = 4π s. The correct answer is C: 4 s.

Explanation:
The cycloid's period is directly related to the generating circle's radius and the fundamental period of the cosine function, which is 2π for one complete cycle.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to assume that the period is simply 2π s because the cosine function has a period of 2π. However, this does not account for the radius of the generating circle, which affects the overall period.

Problem 2 (TwoCycloidsRadius):
A particle moves along two different cycloids, A and B. Cycloid A has a generating circle with radius 3 units, while Cycloid B has a generating circle with radius 5 units. Which cycloid will have a longer period (time for one complete cycle)?
A) Cycloid A
B) Cycloid B
C) Both have the same period
D) Cannot be determined without more information
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The period T of a cycloid generated by a circle with radius r is given by T = 8π / r. Since Cycloid B has a larger radius (5 units) compared to Cycloid A (3 units), its period will be longer. The correct answer is B: Cycloid B.

Explanation:
The period of a cycloid is inversely proportional to the radius of the generating circle. Therefore, the larger the radius, the longer the period.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to think that Cycloid A has a longer period because it has a smaller radius. However, this misunderstanding arises from not considering the inverse relationship between the radius and the period.

Problem 3 (CycloidVelocity):
A particle moves along a cycloid with a generating circle of radius 4 units. At time t = π/2 s, what is the instantaneous velocity of the particle in terms of its position function x(t) and y(t)?
A) (4, -8)
B) (-4, 8)
C) (0, 0)
D) (8, 0)
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The velocity v at any point on a cycloid can be found by differentiating the position functions x(t) and y(t) with respect to time t. For a cycloid generated by a circle of radius r, the position functions are x(t) = 2r(π - t) cos(t) and y(t) = 2r(π - t) sin(t). Differentiating these, we get v_x = dx/dt = -4π sin(t) and v_y = dy/dt = -4π cos(t). At t = π/2 s, the velocity is (v_x, v_y) = (-4π sin(π/2), -4π cos(π/2)) = (-4, 8). The correct answer is B: (-4, 8).

Explanation:
The velocity at any point on a cycloid is found by differentiating the position functions with respect to time. At t = π/2 s, sine and cosine values can be evaluated using trigonometric identities.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget to differentiate the position functions or to use the wrong signs when differentiating. Another potential error is not evaluating the trigonometric functions at t = π/2 s correctly.

Problem 4 (CycloidArcLength):
Find the arc length of a cycloid generated by a circle with radius 5 units, from the starting point where the particle first touches the x-axis to the point where it completes its first quarter cycle.
A) 10π
B) 25π
C) 5π
D) π
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The arc length L of a cycloid from t = 0 to t = π/4 (first quarter cycle) can be found using the formula L = r(π - t) + rsin(t), where r is the radius of the generating circle. At t = π/4, we have L = 5(π - π/4) + 5sin(π/4) = 5π. The correct answer is C: 5π.

Explanation:
The arc length of a cycloid can be calculated using the formula derived from the parametric equations of the cycloid. At t = π/4, we evaluate the expression for L with the given radius.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to use the entire period (2π) instead of just one quarter cycle (π/4). Another potential error is not applying the correct formula or using the wrong values for t.

Problem 7 (CycloidIntersection):
Two cycloids are generated by circles with radii 8 units and 10 units, respectively. Both start at the origin and move counterclockwise. At what time t (in seconds) do they first intersect?
A) π/2
B) π
C) 2π
D) 4π
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The position functions for the two cycloids are x(t) = 2r(π - t) cos(t) and y(t) = 2r(π - t) sin(t), where r is the radius of the generating circle. To find the intersection, we set the equations equal to each other: x_8(t) = x_{10}(t) and y_8(t) = y_{10}(t). Solving for t, we get 2(8π - t) cos(t) = 2(10π - t) cos(t), which simplifies to 8π = 10π - t. Thus, t = 2π s. The correct answer is C: 2π.

Explanation:
To find the intersection of two cycloids, we equate their x and y positions and solve for the time t. In this case, the radii are different but the starting points and directions are the same.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to assume that the first intersection occurs when the particles have traveled the same distance along the cycloids, which is not necessarily true due to their different radii. Another potential error is not setting up the equations correctly or solving them incorrectly.
