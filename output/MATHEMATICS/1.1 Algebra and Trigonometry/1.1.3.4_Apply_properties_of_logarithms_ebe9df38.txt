Problem 1 (Q1):
Simplify the expression: \(\log_{2}(8x)\).
A) \(3+\log_{2}x\)
B) \(\log_{2}8+\log_{2}x\)
C) \(\log_{2}(8)+\log_{2}(x)\)
D) \(\log_{2}(8x)\) cannot be simplified
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Use the product rule: \(\log_b(MN)=\log_b M+\log_b N\).

\(\log_{2}(8x)=\log_{2}8+\log_{2}x\).

Since \(2^3=8\), \(\log_{2}8=3\). Therefore \(\log_{2}(8x)=3+\log_{2}x\).

Explanation:
Apply the product property to split the log of a product into a sum of logs with the same base, then evaluate \(\log_2 8\) as 3.

Common mistake note:
Do not change bases within an expression (e.g., mixing \(\log_2\) and \(\log_{10}\)). Also note that choices like \(\log_{2}8+\log_{2}x\) are equivalent to the simplified result, but the simplest form evaluates \(\log_2 8\) to 3.

Problem 2 (Q2):
Expand the expression: \(\log_{5}\left(\frac{x^3}{\sqrt{y}}\right)\).
A) \(3\log_{5}x-\tfrac{1}{2}\log_{5}y\)
B) \(\tfrac{3}{2}\log_{5}(xy)\)
C) \(3\log_{5}x-\log_{5}y\)
D) \(\log_{5}x-\tfrac{1}{2}\log_{5}(y^3)\)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Use quotient and power rules:

\(\log_5\left(\frac{x^3}{\sqrt{y}}\right)=\log_5(x^3)-\log_5(y^{1/2})\).

Power rule gives \(\log_5(x^3)=3\log_5 x\) and \(\log_5(y^{1/2})=\tfrac12\log_5 y\).

So the expansion is \(3\log_5 x-\tfrac12\log_5 y\).

Explanation:
The quotient rule turns division into subtraction, and the power rule brings exponents (including fractional exponents from radicals) down as coefficients.

Common mistake note:
A frequent error is treating \(\sqrt{y}\) as \(y\) instead of \(y^{1/2}\), which makes the coefficient \(\tfrac12\) disappear.

Problem 3 (Q3):
Condense into a single logarithm: \(2\log_{3}x-\log_{3}y+\tfrac{1}{2}\log_{3}z\).
A) \(\log_{3}\left(\frac{x^{2}\sqrt{z}}{y}\right)\)
B) \(\log_{3}\left(\frac{2x-z}{y}\right)\)
C) \(\log_{3}\left(\frac{x^{2}}{y\sqrt{z}}\right)\)
D) \(\log_{3}\left(\frac{x^{2}y}{\sqrt{z}}\right)\)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Use the power rule in reverse:

\(2\log_3 x=\log_3(x^2)\), and \(\tfrac12\log_3 z=\log_3(z^{1/2})\).

So the expression is \(\log_3(x^2)-\log_3(y)+\log_3(z^{1/2})\).

Combine addition: \(\log_3(x^2)+\log_3(z^{1/2})=\log_3(x^2 z^{1/2})\).

Then subtract: \(\log_3(x^2 z^{1/2})-\log_3(y)=\log_3\left(\frac{x^2 z^{1/2}}{y}\right)=\log_3\left(\frac{x^2\sqrt{z}}{y}\right)\).

Explanation:
Coefficients become exponents (power rule). Adding logs corresponds to multiplying arguments; subtracting logs corresponds to dividing arguments (all with the same base).

Common mistake note:
Only the subtracted term \(\log_3 y\) goes to the denominator. The \(\tfrac12\log_3 z\) term is added, so \(\sqrt{z}\) stays in the numerator.

Problem 4 (Q4):
Solve for \(x\): \(\log_{7}(x-2)=2\).
A) \(x=9\)
B) \(x=49\)
C) \(x=51\)
D) \(x=5\)
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
Convert to exponential form:

\(\log_7(x-2)=2\Rightarrow x-2=7^2=49\).

Thus \(x=51\).

Explanation:
By definition, \(\log_b(A)=c\iff b^c=A\). Here the log argument is \(x-2\), so solve \(x-2=49\) and then add 2.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is answering \(x=49\) by forgetting that the argument is \(x-2\), not \(x\).

Problem 5 (Q5):
Evaluate: \(\log_{4}(\tfrac{1}{8})\).
A) \(-\tfrac{3}{2}\)
B) \(-3\)
C) \(\tfrac{3}{2}\)
D) \(\tfrac{1}{2}\)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Rewrite base and argument as powers of 2:

\(4=2^2\), \(\tfrac18=2^{-3}\).

Let \(\log_4(1/8)=k\). Then \(4^k=1/8\Rightarrow (2^2)^k=2^{-3}\Rightarrow 2^{2k}=2^{-3}\).

So \(2k=-3\Rightarrow k=-\tfrac32\).

Explanation:
Use \(\log_b(a)=k\iff b^k=a\), then express both sides with a common base and equate exponents.

Common mistake note:
Do not assume \(1/8=4^{-3}\). In fact \(4^{-3}=1/64\), so that leads to an incorrect value.

Problem 6 (Q6):
Simplify: \(\log_{10}(1000)-\log_{10}(0.1)\).
A) \(2\)
B) \(3\)
C) \(4\)
D) \(\tfrac{3}{10}\)
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
\(\log_{10}(1000)=3\) because \(10^3=1000\).

\(\log_{10}(0.1)=\log_{10}(10^{-1})=-1\).

So \(3-(-1)=4\).

Explanation:
Base-10 logs of powers of 10 evaluate to their exponents, and subtracting a negative adds.

Common mistake note:
Remember \(0.1=10^{-1}\), so its logarithm is \(-1\), not \(1\).

Problem 7 (Q7):
Solve for \(x\): \(\log_{2}x+\log_{2}(x-2)=3\).
A) \(x=1\)
B) \(x=2\)
C) \(x=-2\)
D) \(x=4\)
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
Domain: need \(x>0\) and \(x-2>0\Rightarrow x>2\).

Combine logs: \(\log_2 x+\log_2(x-2)=\log_2(x(x-2))=3\).

Convert to exponential form: \(x(x-2)=2^3=8\).

So \(x^2-2x-8=0\Rightarrow (x-4)(x+2)=0\).

Thus \(x=4\) or \(x=-2\). Apply domain \(x>2\): keep \(x=4\).

Explanation:
Use the product property to combine the two logs into one, convert to an exponential equation, solve the resulting quadratic, then check solutions against the log domain restrictions.

Common mistake note:
It’s not enough to solve the quadratic; you must also enforce \(x>2\) so that both \(x\) and \(x-2\) are positive. This eliminates \(x=-2\).
