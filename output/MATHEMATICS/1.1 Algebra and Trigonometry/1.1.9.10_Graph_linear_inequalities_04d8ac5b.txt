Problem 1 (One-Variable-Inequality-Line):
Graph the inequality: y > 2x + 1
A) [3, 7)
B) (-∞, -1)
C) [1, 5]
D) (-∞, ∞)
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
The inequality y > 2x + 1 represents all points above the line y = 2x + 1. This means that there is no upper limit on the values of y, so the solution set is all real numbers for y (negative infinity to positive infinity). The correct graph would be a dashed line representing the boundary y = 2x + 1 and the area above it shaded.

Explanation:
A dashed line indicates an inequality that does not include equality (greater than or less than), while a solid line would indicate inclusion of equality (greater than or equal to or less than or equal to). Shading above the line represents y values greater than the line's equation.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing A, B, or C because students might forget that the inequality is 'greater than', not 'greater than or equal to'.

Problem 2 (Two-Variable-Inequality-Shaded-Area):
Graph the inequality: 2x - y ≤ 4
A) [0, 4)
B) (-∞, ∞)
C) [-2, 6]
D) (-∞, 4]
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
The inequality 2x - y ≤ 4 represents all points below or on the line 2x - y = 4. This means that for any point (x, y) on or below the line, the value of 2x - y will be less than or equal to 4. The correct graph would be a solid line representing the boundary 2x - y = 4 and the area below it shaded.

Explanation:
A solid line indicates an inequality that includes equality (less than or greater than or equal to), while a dashed line would indicate no inclusion of equality (strictly less than or strictly greater than). Shading below the line represents y values less than or equal to the line's equation.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing A, B, or C because students might forget that the inequality includes 'or equal to', not just 'less than'.

Problem 3 (System-Of-Inequalities-Shaded-Region):
Graph the system of inequalities:
1) y > 2x - 3
2) y ≤ -x + 5
A) A region in the first and third quadrants
B) A region in the second and fourth quadrants
C) The entire coordinate plane
D) A region in the first quadrant only
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
To graph the system, we graph each inequality separately. The first inequality y > 2x - 3 has a dashed line representing points above the boundary y = 2x - 3. The second inequality y ≤ -x + 5 has a solid line representing points below or on the boundary y = -x + 5. The shaded region will be where both inequalities are satisfied, which is in the second and fourth quadrants.

Explanation:
When graphing systems of inequalities, we combine the regions that satisfy all inequalities in the system.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing A because students might forget to consider the intersection of the two shaded areas.

Problem 4 (Inequality-With-Intercepts):
Graph the inequality:
1) y < -2x + 4
with x-intercept at (2, 0)
A) A line passing through (2, 0) with shading to the right
B) A line passing through (2, 0) with shading to the left
C) A line passing through (-2, 4) with shading above
D) A line passing through (0, 4) with shading below
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The x-intercept at (2, 0) means that when y = 0, x = 2. The inequality y < -2x + 4 indicates that the line should be dashed since it does not include equality. Shading to the left of the line represents values of y less than the line's equation.

Explanation:
The x-intercept helps determine the slope and one point on the line, while the inequality sign determines the shading direction.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing A because students might misinterpret the inequality as greater than.

Problem 5 (Inequality-With-Intersection):
Graph the inequality:
1) y ≥ x + 1
2) y ≤ -x + 3
A) A single line with no shading
B) An unbounded region in the first and third quadrants
C) A bounded region in the second and fourth quadrants
D) Two parallel lines with no shaded area
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The system of inequalities has two lines that intersect at (1, 2). Since both inequalities include equality, we use solid lines. The shading will be between the two lines, which forms a bounded region in the second and fourth quadrants.

Explanation:
When two lines representing inequalities intersect, the shaded area is typically between them if one line has 'or equal to' and the other does not.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing A because students might forget to shade the area between the lines.

Problem 6 (Inequality-With-Vertical-Line):
Graph the inequality:
1) x > 3
2) y < -2
A) A vertical line at x = 3 with shading above
B) A horizontal line at y = -2 with shading to the right
C) A region in the first and fourth quadrants
D) A region in the second and third quadrants
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The inequality x > 3 is a vertical line at x = 3, which means that all points with an x-coordinate greater than 3 will satisfy the inequality. The shading for y < -2 should be to the right of this line since it represents values of y less than -2.

Explanation:
Vertical lines are graphed by specifying the x-value and shading in the direction indicated by the inequality involving the other variable.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing A because students might misinterpret the inequality involving y.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 7 (Inequality-With-Horizontal-Line):
Graph the inequality:
1) y ≥ -4
2) x ≤ 5
A) A horizontal line at y = -4 with shading to the right
B) A vertical line at x = 5 with shading below
C) A region in the first and second quadrants
D) A region in the third and fourth quadrants
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The inequality x ≤ 5 is a horizontal line at x = 5, which means that all points with an x-coordinate less than or equal to 5 will satisfy the inequality. The shading for y ≥ -4 should be below this line since it represents values of y greater than or equal to -4.

Explanation:
Horizontal lines are graphed by specifying the y-value and shading in the direction indicated by the inequality involving the other variable.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing A because students might misinterpret the inequality involving x.
