Problem 1 (Q1):
Convert the expression \( \sqrt{8} \) into exponential form.
A) 8^{\frac{1}{2}}
B) 8^{2}
C) 8^{\frac{1}{3}}
D) 8^{\frac{3}{2}}
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The radical form \( \sqrt{8} \) can be rewritten in exponential form as \( 8^{\frac{1}{2}} \).

Explanation:
The square root of a number \( a \) is equivalent to raising \( a \) to the power of \( \frac{1}{2} \).

Common mistake note:
Some students mistakenly use \( \frac{1}{3} \) thinking it's a cube root, but the square root corresponds to \( \frac{1}{2} \).
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 2 (Q2):
Write \( 16^{\frac{3}{4}} \) in radical form.
A) \sqrt[4]{16^{3}}
B) \sqrt[3]{16^{4}}
C) \sqrt{16^{2}}
D) \sqrt{16^{3}}
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The expression \( 16^{\frac{3}{4}} \) can be rewritten in radical form as \( \sqrt[4]{16^{3}} \).

Explanation:
The denominator (4) of the fraction indicates the index of the root, and the numerator (3) is the power of the radicand.

Common mistake note:
Choosing \( \sqrt{16^{3}} \) would mix up square roots with fourth roots.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 3 (Q3):
Convert \( \sqrt[5]{x} \) to exponential form.
A) x^{5}
B) x^{\frac{1}{5}}
C) x^{\frac{5}{1}}
D) x^{-5}
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The radical form \( \sqrt[5]{x} \) is equivalent to \( x^{\frac{1}{5}} \) in exponential form.

Explanation:
The index of the root (5) becomes the denominator of the exponent.

Common mistake note:
Mistakenly using \( x^{5} \) ignores the concept of root, incorrectly interpreting the operation.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 4 (Q4):
What is the exponential form of \( \sqrt[3]{27y^{2}} \)?
A) 27y^{2}^{2/3}
B) (27y^{2})^{\frac{1}{3}}
C) (27y^{2})^{\frac{3}{1}}
D) (27^{\frac{1}{3}})(y^{2})^{\frac{1}{3}}
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The expression \( \sqrt[3]{27y^{2}} \) translates to exponential form as \( (27y^{2})^{\frac{1}{3}} \).

Explanation:
A cube root is equivalent to raising the expression inside the root to the \( \frac{1}{3} \) power.

Common mistake note:
Forgetting to apply the \( \frac{1}{3} \) power to both parts can lead to missing portions of the conversion.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 5 (Q5):
Express \( y^{\frac{4}{5}} \) in radical form.
A) \sqrt[5]{y^{4}}
B) \sqrt{y^{5}}
C) \sqrt[4]{y^{5}}
D) y^{\frac{5}{4}}
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
\( y^{\frac{4}{5}} \) in radical form is \( \sqrt[5]{y^{4}} \).

Explanation:
Convert the fractional exponent by using the denominator as the root index and the numerator as the power.

Common mistake note:
Choosing \( \sqrt[4]{y^{5}} \) confuses the base and exponent roles in the fractional exponent.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 6 (Q6):
Convert the radical expression \( \sqrt[6]{49} \) into exponential form.
A) 49^{6}
B) 49^{\frac{1}{6}}
C) 49^{7}
D) 49^{\frac{1}{7}}
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The exponential form of \( \sqrt[6]{49} \) is \( 49^{\frac{1}{6}} \).

Explanation:
The radical index becomes the denominator of the exponent when converting from radical to exponential form.

Common mistake note:
Selecting \( 49^{\frac{1}{7}} \) mixes up the root index incorrectly.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 7 (Q7):
Rewrite \( (9x)^{\frac{2}{3}} \) in radical form.
A) \sqrt{9x^{2}}
B) \sqrt[3]{(9x^{2})^{2}}
C) \sqrt[3]{(9x)^{2}}
D) \sqrt[2]{(9x)^{3}}
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The expression \( (9x)^{\frac{2}{3}} \) can be expressed as \( \sqrt[3]{(9x)^{2}} \).

Explanation:
The denominator of the fractional exponent becomes the root index, while the numerator becomes the power inside the radical.

Common mistake note:
Misinterpreting the expression as \( \sqrt[2]{(9x)^{3}} \) incorrectly identifies the root and power roles.
