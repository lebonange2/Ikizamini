Problem 1 (:1):
Solve and graph the system of inequalities:
 y < 2x + 3
 y ≥ -x - 1
A) [0, 4)
B) (-∞, -4) ∪ (0, ∞)
C) (-∞, 0] ∪ [4, ∞)
D) (-4, 0]
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The solution to the system is where both inequalities are true. Graphing both lines, we find that y < 2x + 3 is above the line y = -x - 1. The shaded area should be below the line y = 2x + 3 and above or on the line y = -x - 1. This corresponds to the interval (-∞, -4) ∪ (0, ∞).

Explanation:
The solution region is where both inequalities are satisfied simultaneously.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be choosing A, which represents only one part of the shaded area. Students might forget to consider the intersection of both inequalities.

Problem 2 (2):
Graph the system of inequalities:
 x + y ≤ 3
 x - y > 1
A) [1, 3]
B) (-∞, -1) ∪ (3, ∞)
C) (-∞, 1] × [0, 2]
D) (-∞, -1) ∪ (1, 3]
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
Graphing both lines, we have y ≤ 3 - x for the first inequality and y < x - 1 for the second. The shaded area should be below or on the line y = 3 - x and above the line y = x - 1. This corresponds to the interval (-∞, -1) ∪ (1, 3].

Explanation:
The solution region is where both inequalities are satisfied simultaneously.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be choosing C, which represents only one part of the shaded area. Students might not consider that the second inequality is a strict inequality.

Problem 3 (3):
Solve and graph:
 2x + y < 4
 x - 2y ≤ 6
A) (-∞, 0) × (0, ∞)
B) (-∞, 2] × [0, 3]
C) (-∞, 2) × (-∞, 3)
D) (-∞, 2] × [-∞, 3]
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
Graphing both lines, we have y < 4 - 2x for the first inequality and y ≥ (x - 6)/2 for the second. The shaded area should be below the line y = 4 - 2x and above or on the line y = (x - 6)/2. This corresponds to the interval (-∞, 2) × (-∞, 3).

Explanation:
The solution region is where both inequalities are satisfied simultaneously.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be choosing A, which represents an unbounded area. Students might forget that one of the inequalities has a strict inequality.

Problem 4 (4):
Graph the system:
 x + 2y ≤ 6
 x ≥ 1
 y > 0
A) [1, 3] × (0, ∞)
B) [1, ∞) × [0, 3]
C) (-∞, -1) × (-∞, 3]
D) (-∞, 1] × [0, 3]
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Graphing the lines, we have y ≤ (6 - x)/2 for the first inequality, x = 1 as a vertical line, and y = 0 as a horizontal line. The shaded area should be below or on the line y = (6 - x)/2, to the right of x = 1, and above y = 0. This corresponds to the interval [1, 3] × (0, ∞).

Explanation:
The solution region is where all inequalities are satisfied simultaneously.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be choosing B, which includes points below x = 1. Students might overlook that x must be greater than or equal to 1.

Problem 6 (6):
Graph the system:

B) (-∞, -2) ∪ (4, ∞)
C) (-∞, -2] × [0, 6]
D) (-∞, -2] × [-2, 4]
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
Graphing both lines, we have y ≥ x - 4 for the first inequality and y ≤ -x - 2 for the second. The shaded area should be above or on the line y = x - 4 and below or on the line y = -x - 2. This corresponds to the interval (-∞, -2] × [-2, 4].

Explanation:
The solution region is where both inequalities are satisfied simultaneously.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be choosing C, which represents only one part of the shaded area. Students might not consider that both inequalities have non-strict inequalities.
