Problem 1 (:1):
The parametric equations for a curve are x(t) = t^2 and y(t) = t^3. Find the surface area when the curve is revolved around the x-axis from t=0 to t=2.
A) 64π
B) 128π/5
C) 256π/7
D) 512π/9
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The surface area S of revolution around the x-axis for a parametric curve given by x(t) and y(t) from t=a to t=b is calculated using the formula: S = 2π∫[a, b] y(t) * sqrt(1 + (dx/dt)^2) dt. For the given curve x(t) = t^2 and y(t) = t^3, we have dx/dt = 2t and y = t^3. Substituting these into the formula gives us S = 2π∫[0, 2] t^3 * sqrt(1 + (2t)^2) dt. Solving this integral results in S = 128π/5.

Explanation:
The surface area is found by integrating the product of y(t) and the square root of one plus the square of the derivative of x with respect to t. The correct answer is obtained after evaluating the definite integral.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be forgetting to include the factor 2π in the surface area formula or incorrectly integrating the function.

Explanation:

Problem 6 (:6):
The parametric equations for a curve are x(z) = cos(z) and y(z) = sin(z). Find the surface area when the curve is revolved around the z-axis from z=0 to z=π/2.
A) π
B) 2π
C) 3π/2
D) 5π/4
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The surface area S of revolution around the z-axis for a parametric curve given by x(z) and y(z) from z=a to z=b is calculated using the formula: S = 2π∫[a, b] (x(z)^2 + y(z)^2) dz. For the given curve x(z) = cos(z) and y(z) = sin(z), we have x^2 + y^2 = cos^2(z) + sin^2(z) = 1. Substituting this into the formula gives us S = 2π∫[0, π/2] dz. Solving this integral results in S = 2π.

Explanation:
The surface area is found by integrating the sum of x(z)^2 and y(z)^2 with respect to z. Since x^2 + y^2 = 1 for a unit circle, the integral simplifies to just 2π times the length of the interval. The correct answer is obtained after evaluating the definite integral.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be forgetting that for a unit circle x^2 + y^2 = 1 or integrating with respect to the wrong variable.
