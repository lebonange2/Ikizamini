Problem 1 (Q1):
What are the coordinates of the point where the line y = 0 intersects the x-axis at x = 4?
A) (0, 0)
B) (3, 0)
C) (0, 3)
D) (4, 0)
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
On the x-axis, the y-coordinate is 0. Thus, the line y = 0 intersects the x-axis at (4, 0).

Explanation:
The x-axis is where y = 0. Therefore, y = 0 intersects the x-axis at (x, 0); specifically at x = 4, it's (4, 0).

Common mistake note:
Confusing the concept of y = 0 with x = 0 could lead to identifying the wrong intersection point.

Problem 2 (Q2):
Which quadrant is the point (-2, 3) located in?
A) Quadrant I
B) Quadrant II
C) Quadrant III
D) Quadrant IV
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Points in Quadrant II have a negative x-value and a positive y-value, matching the coordinates (-2, 3).

Explanation:
Quadrant II contains points where x < 0 and y > 0. Since -2 is negative and 3 is positive, the point lies in Quadrant II.

Common mistake note:
Misinterpreting the signs of the coordinates can lead to choosing the incorrect quadrant, like Quadrant III.

Problem 3 (Q3):
If point A (-4, -2) is reflected over the y-axis, what are the coordinates of the new point?
A) (-4, -2)
B) (4, -2)
C) (4, 2)
D) (-4, 2)
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Reflecting over the y-axis changes the sign of the x-coordinate. So, (-4, -2) becomes (4, -2).

Explanation:
Reflection over the y-axis involves changing the sign of the x-coordinate only, leading to (4, -2).

Common mistake note:
Students might mistakenly change the y-coordinate instead, choosing (4, 2), which would result from a different reflection.

Problem 4 (Q4):
Which of the following points lies on the line y = 2x + 1?
A) (1, 3)
B) (2, 5)
C) (0, 1)
D) (3, 8)
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
Substituting x = 0 into the equation gives y = 2(0) + 1 = 1, verifying that (0, 1) lies on the line.

Explanation:
A point is on a line if its coordinates satisfy the line's equation. Here, (0, 1) satisfies y = 2x + 1.

Common mistake note:
Incorrect substitutions or arithmetic errors can incorrectly confirm points not on the line, such as (1, 3).

Problem 5 (Q5):
A line passes through (1, 2) and (3, 6). What is the slope of the line?
A) 1
B) 2
C) 3
D) 4
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Slope = (6 - 2) / (3 - 1) = 4 / 2 = 2.

Explanation:
The slope of a line passing through two points (x1, y1) and (x2, y2) is calculated as (y2 - y1) / (x2 - x1).

Common mistake note:
Misplacing or reversing the points can lead to incorrect slope computation.

Problem 6 (Q6):
Which point has the same distance from both the x-axis and the y-axis for positive coordinates?
A) (2, 2)
B) (3, 3)
C) (1, 1)
D) (4, 4)
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
A point is equidistant from both axes if the x-coordinate equals the y-coordinate when positive.

Explanation:
For equidistance from both axes in positive coordinates, x = y. Thus, the point (1, 1) meets this requirement.

Common mistake note:
Selecting non-equidistant points like (2, 2) due to misunderstanding the condition of equality, not ratio.

Problem 7 (Q7):
Which graph correctly shows the solution set for the inequality y > x + 3?
A) A shaded region above the line y = x + 3
B) A shaded region below the line y = x + 3
C) A line y = x + 3 without shading
D) A shaded region above the line y = x - 3
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The inequality y > x + 3 indicates the region above the line, where y is greater for the same x.

Explanation:
Graphically, inequalities indicate a region. Here, it shows the area above the line y = x + 3.

Common mistake note:
Mistaking '>' for '<' can cause one to incorrectly shade below the line.
