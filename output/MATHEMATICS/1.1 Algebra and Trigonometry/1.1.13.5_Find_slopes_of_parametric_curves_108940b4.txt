Problem 1 (Asdf):
Given the parametric equations x(t) = 2t and y(t) = t^2, what is the slope of the curve at t=1?
A) 1
B) 2
C) 3
D) 4
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The slope of a parametric curve at a given point can be found using the formula dy/dx = (dy/dt) / (dx/dt). For the given equations x(t) = 2t and y(t) = t^2, we first find dx/dt and dy/dt: dx/dt = 2, dy/dt = 2t. At t=1, we have dx/dt = 2 and dy/dt = 2(1) = 2. Thus, the slope is dy/dx = 2/2 = 1.

Explanation:
The correct answer is found by applying the formula for the slope of a parametric curve at a specific point. Choice A (1) is incorrect because it does not account for the fact that we are finding the slope at t=1, where dy/dt is 2. Choices C and D (3 and 4 respectively) are higher values that do not result from the calculation with the given function derivatives.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget to evaluate the derivatives at t=1 or to confuse dx/dt and dy/dt with the actual x and y values.

A) -√2
B) 0
C) 1
D) √2
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
The slope of a parametric curve is given by dy/dx = (dy/dt) / (dx/dt). For x(t) = cos(t) and y(t) = sin(t), we have dx/dt = -sin(t) and dy/dt = cos(t). At t = π/4, we have dx/dt = -sin(π/4) = -√2/2 and dy/dt = cos(π/4) = √2/2. The slope is then dy/dx = (√2/2) / (-√2/2) = -1. However, since the question asks for the absolute value of the slope at t=π/4, the correct answer is 1.

Explanation:
The correct answer is found by calculating the derivatives and evaluating them at t=π/4. Choice A (−√2) is incorrect because it does not consider the absolute value. Choices B (0) and C (1) are incorrect as they do not account for the slope being 1 when considering only the magnitude.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget to take the absolute value of the slope or to misapply trigonometric identities.
