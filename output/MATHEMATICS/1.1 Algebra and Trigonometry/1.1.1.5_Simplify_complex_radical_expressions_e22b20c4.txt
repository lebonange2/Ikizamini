Problem 1 (Q1):
Simplify the expression: \( \sqrt{50} \)
A) \( 5\sqrt{2} \)
B) \( 10\sqrt{5} \)
C) \( 25 \)
D) \( \sqrt{5} \)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The expression \( \sqrt{50} \) can be rewritten as \( \sqrt{25 \times 2} \). Thus, it simplifies to \( \sqrt{25} \cdot \sqrt{2} = 5\sqrt{2} \).

Explanation:
We break down the number 50 into the product of 25 and 2. Since 25 is a perfect square, the square root of 25 is 5, leaving us with \( 5\sqrt{2} \).

Common mistake note:
Mistaking \( \sqrt{50} \) for \( 10\sqrt{5} \). The incorrect decomposition of 50 ignores the perfect square factor.

Problem 2 (Q2):
Simplify the expression: \( \sqrt{72} + 2\sqrt{18} \)
A) \( 8\sqrt{2} \)
B) \( 10\sqrt{2} \)
C) \( 12\sqrt{2} \)
D) \( 14\sqrt{2} \)
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
\( \sqrt{72} = \sqrt{36 \times 2} = 6\sqrt{2} \) and \( 2\sqrt{18} = 2\times 3\sqrt{2} = 6\sqrt{2} \). Adding gives \( 6\sqrt{2} + 6\sqrt{2} = 12\sqrt{2} \).

Explanation:
Identify and extract common radical parts with \( \sqrt{2} \) resulting in \( 12\sqrt{2} \).

Common mistake note:
Failure to factor 72 and 18 properly might lead to incorrect simplification.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 3 (Q3):
Simplify the expression: \( \sqrt{128} - \sqrt{32} \)
A) \( 2\sqrt{2} \)
B) \( 4\sqrt{2} \)
C) \( 6\sqrt{2} \)
D) \( 8\sqrt{2} \)
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
\( \sqrt{128} = \sqrt{64 \times 2} = 8\sqrt{2} \) and \( \sqrt{32} = \sqrt{16\times 2} = 4\sqrt{2} \), giving \( 8\sqrt{2} - 4\sqrt{2} = 4\sqrt{2} \).

Explanation:
Break down each radicand into the product of squares and simplify first, then subtract the terms.

Common mistake note:
Incorrect calculation of square roots might lead to errors in simplification.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 4 (Q4):
Simplify the expression: \( \sqrt{45xy^2} \) assuming \( x \geq 0 \) and \( y \geq 0 \)
A) \( 3y\sqrt{5x} \)
B) \( 9y\sqrt{5x} \)
C) \( 3\sqrt{5x} \)
D) \( 5y\sqrt{3x} \)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
\( \sqrt{45xy^2} = \sqrt{9\times 5x \times y^2} =3y\sqrt{5x} \).

Explanation:
Recognize the perfect square factors and extract them from the root, considering the given conditions.

Common mistake note:
Forgetting to simplify the \( y^2 \) correctly might lead to wrong coefficients.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 5 (Q5):
Simplify the expression: \( 3\sqrt{98} + \sqrt{72} \)
A) \( 17\sqrt{2} \)
B) \( 27\sqrt{2} \)
C) \( 21\sqrt{2} \)
D) \( 23\sqrt{2} \)
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
\( 3\sqrt{98} = 3\times 7\sqrt{2} = 21\sqrt{2} \), \( \sqrt{72} = 6\sqrt{2} \), thus it becomes \( 21\sqrt{2} + 6\sqrt{2} = 27\sqrt{2} \).

Explanation:
Identify and factor out the perfect square from each term separately and add the results.

Common mistake note:
Miscalculations in root extraction can lead to incorrect results.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 6 (Q6):
Simplify \( \sqrt{18x^2} + \sqrt{50x^2} \) where \( x \geq 0 \)
A) \( 8x\sqrt{2} \)
B) \( 12x \)
C) \( 16x \)
D) \( 5x \)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
\( \sqrt{18x^2} = 3x\sqrt{2} \), \( \sqrt{50x^2} = 5x\sqrt{2} \); \( 3x\sqrt{2} + 5x\sqrt{2} = 8x\sqrt{2} \).

Explanation:
Extract squares from the radicands and treat coefficients with the common factor.

Common mistake note:
Not simplifying \( x^2 \) correctly can lead to a wrong algebraic form.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 7 (Q7):
Simplify the expression: \( \sqrt{75xy^4} \) assuming \( x \geq 0 \) and \( y \geq 0 \)
A) \( 7.5y^2\sqrt{x} \)
B) \( 5y^2\sqrt{3x} \)
C) \( 10y^2\sqrt{3x} \)
D) \( 15y^2\sqrt{x} \)
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
\( \sqrt{75xy^4} = \sqrt{25\times 3x\times y^4} = 5y^2 \sqrt{3x} \).

Explanation:
Extract perfect squares from the expression and simplify, applying conditions appropriately.

Common mistake note:
Mistakes often arise from misidentifying perfect square factors, especially involving variables.
