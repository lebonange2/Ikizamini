Problem 1 (Q1):
Rationalize the denominator of \( \frac{5}{\sqrt{3}} \).
A) \( \frac{5\sqrt{3}}{3} \)
B) \( \frac{5\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{3}} \)
C) \( 5\sqrt{3} \)
D) \( \frac{15}{\sqrt{3}} \)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Multiply numerator and denominator by \( \sqrt{3} \): \( \frac{5}{\sqrt{3}} \times \frac{\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{5\sqrt{3}}{3} \).

Explanation:
By multiplying the numerator and the denominator by \( \sqrt{3} \), the denominator becomes a rational number.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is not multiplying both numerator and denominator or incorrectly simplifying, leading to an unchanged denominator.

Problem 2 (Q2):
Rationalize the denominator of \( \frac{2}{1 + \sqrt{5}} \).
A) \( \frac{2(1 - \sqrt{5})}{-4} \)
B) \( \frac{2 - 2\sqrt{5}}{6} \)
C) \( \frac{2 - 2\sqrt{5}}{-3} \)
D) \( \frac{2(1 - \sqrt{5})}{6} \)
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Multiply numerator and denominator by the conjugate \( 1 - \sqrt{5} \): \( \frac{2}{1 + \sqrt{5}} \times \frac{1 - \sqrt{5}}{1 - \sqrt{5}} = \frac{2 - 2\sqrt{5}}{6} \).

Explanation:
Multiplying by the conjugate \( 1 - \sqrt{5} \) removes the square root from the denominator by creating a difference of squares.

Common mistake note:
A mistake could be incorrectly expanding the denominator resulting in wrong values.

Problem 3 (Q3):
Rationalize the denominator of \( \frac{4}{2 - \sqrt{2}} \).
A) \( \frac{8 + 4\sqrt{2}}{2} \)
B) \( \frac{8}{6} \)
C) \( \frac{8 + 4\sqrt{2}}{6} \)
D) \( \frac{12 - 4\sqrt{2}}{4} \)
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
Multiply by the conjugate \( 2 + \sqrt{2} \): \( \frac{4}{2 - \sqrt{2}} \times \frac{2 + \sqrt{2}}{2 + \sqrt{2}} = \frac{8 + 4\sqrt{2}}{6} \).

Explanation:
Using the conjugate \( 2 + \sqrt{2} \) eliminates the irrational part from the denominator.

Common mistake note:
Mistakes often occur in expanding or combining terms, especially if \( \sqrt{2} \) is mishandled.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 4 (Q4):
Rationalize the denominator of \( \frac{7}{\sqrt{6} - \sqrt{2}} \).
A) \( \frac{7(\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2})}{4} \)
B) \( \frac{14 + 7\sqrt{12}}{4} \)
C) \( \frac{7+\sqrt{12}}{2} \)
D) \( \frac{7(\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2})}{3} \)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Multiply by the conjugate \( \sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2} \): \( \frac{7}{\sqrt{6} - \sqrt{2}} \times \frac{\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2}} = \frac{7(\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2})}{4} \).

Explanation:
The product \( (\sqrt{6} - \sqrt{2})(\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2}) = 4 \) removes the square roots from the denominator.

Common mistake note:
Errors often arise from incorrect multiplication when dealing with conjugates.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 5 (Q5):
Rationalize the denominator of \( \frac{3}{4 + \sqrt{3}} \).
A) \( \frac{3(4 - \sqrt{3})}{13} \)
B) \( \frac{12 - 3\sqrt{3}}{13} \)
C) \( \frac{3(4 - \sqrt{3})}{16} \)
D) \( \frac{12 + 3\sqrt{3}}{13} \)
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Multiply by the conjugate \( 4 - \sqrt{3} \): \( \frac{3}{4 + \sqrt{3}} \times \frac{4 - \sqrt{3}}{4 - \sqrt{3}} = \frac{12 - 3\sqrt{3}}{13} \).

Explanation:
Using the conjugate eliminates the square root by making the denominator \( 4^2 - (\sqrt{3})^2 = 13 \).

Common mistake note:
A mistake occurs if the denominator is not calculated as a difference of squares.

Problem 6 (Q6):
Rationalize the denominator of \( \frac{1}{\sqrt{7} + 2} \).
A) \( \frac{\sqrt{7} - 2}{3} \)
B) \( \frac{\sqrt{7} - 2}{7} \)
C) \( \frac{\sqrt{7} - 2}{13} \)
D) \( \frac{\sqrt{7} + 2}{3} \)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Multiply by the conjugate \( \sqrt{7} - 2 \): \( \frac{1}{\sqrt{7} + 2} \times \frac{\sqrt{7} - 2}{\sqrt{7} - 2} = \frac{\sqrt{7} - 2}{3} \).

Explanation:
The conjugate removes the square root by forming \( (\sqrt{7})^2 - 2^2 = 3 \) in the denominator.

Common mistake note:
Errors arise from the incorrect application of the conjugate, often leading to incorrect simplification.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 7 (Q7):
Rationalize the denominator of \( \frac{9}{\sqrt{8} - \sqrt{2}} \).
A) \( \frac{9(\sqrt{8} + \sqrt{2})}{6} \)
B) \( \frac{9(\sqrt{8} - \sqrt{2})}{2} \)
C) \( \frac{9\sqrt{8} + 9\sqrt{2}}{6} \)
D) \( \frac{9(\sqrt{8} + \sqrt{2})}{8} \)
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
Multiply by the conjugate \( \sqrt{8} + \sqrt{2} \): \( \frac{9}{\sqrt{8} - \sqrt{2}} \times \frac{\sqrt{8} + \sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{8} + \sqrt{2}} = \frac{9(\sqrt{8} + \sqrt{2})}{8} \).

Explanation:
The conjugate removes the radicals by forming \( (\sqrt{8})^2 - (\sqrt{2})^2 = 8 \) in the denominator.

Common mistake note:
Errors could occur if the square roots in the conjugate are not handled correctly, resulting in the wrong denominator.
