Problem 1 (Q1):
Rationalize the denominator: \( \frac{5}{\sqrt{2}} \)
A) \( \frac{5\sqrt{2}}{2} \)
B) \( \frac{5}{2\sqrt{2}} \)
C) \( \frac{5\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{2}} \)
D) \( \sqrt{10} \)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Multiply the numerator and the denominator by \( \sqrt{2} \) to get \( \frac{5\sqrt{2}}{2} \).

Explanation:
Multiplying both the numerator and denominator by \( \sqrt{2} \) removes the square root from the denominator.

Common mistake note:
Choosing B is a common mistake due to multiplying the numerator only, which fails to rationalize the denominator.

Problem 2 (Q2):
Rationalize the denominator: \( \frac{3}{2\sqrt{5}} \)
A) \( \frac{6\sqrt{5}}{5} \)
B) \( \frac{3\sqrt{5}}{10} \)
C) \( \frac{3}{\sqrt{10}} \)
D) \( \frac{3\sqrt{5}}{5} \)
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
Multiply both the numerator and the denominator by \( \sqrt{5} \) to get \( \frac{3\sqrt{5}}{5} \).

Explanation:
Multiplying both the numerator and denominator by \( \sqrt{5} \) removes the square root and results in a rational denominator.

Common mistake note:
Choosing A is incorrect as it involves an incorrect multiplication by \( \sqrt{10} \), which complicates rather than rationalizes.

Problem 3 (Q3):
Simplify by rationalizing the denominator: \( \frac{7}{\sqrt{3}} \)
A) 7\sqrt{3}
B) \( \frac{7\sqrt{3}}{3} \)
C) \( \frac{7}{3\sqrt{3}} \)
D) \( \frac{3\sqrt{7}}{3} \)
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Multiply both the numerator and the denominator by \( \sqrt{3} \): \( \frac{7\sqrt{3}}{3} \).

Explanation:
The denominator is made rational by eliminating the square root.

Common mistake note:
Choosing C suggests multiplying only the denominator, ignoring the need to multiply the numerator.

Problem 4 (Q4):
What is the rationalized form of \( \frac{10}{\sqrt{6}} \)?
A) \( \frac{10\sqrt{6}}{36} \)
B) \( \frac{10}{6} \)
C) \( \frac{5\sqrt{6}}{3} \)
D) \( \frac{10\sqrt{6}}{6} \)
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
Multiply both the numerator and the denominator by \( \sqrt{6} \) to get \( \frac{10\sqrt{6}}{6} \), then simplify to \( \frac{5\sqrt{6}}{3} \).

Explanation:
By multiplying by \( \sqrt{6} \) and simplifying, the expression becomes rational and streamlined.

Common mistake note:
Option D misses the final simplification step.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 5 (Q5):
Rationalize the denominator in \( \frac{4}{\sqrt{8}} \).
A) \( \frac{4\sqrt{8}}{8} \)
B) \( 2 \sqrt{2} \)
C) \( \frac{\sqrt{8}}{2} \)
D) \( \frac{\sqrt{2}}{1} \)
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Multiply both numerator and denominator by \( \sqrt{8} \) to get \( \frac{4\sqrt{8}}{8} = 2 \sqrt{2} \).

Explanation:
Rationalizing \( \frac{4}{\sqrt{8}} \) gives \( \frac{4\sqrt{8}}{8} \), which simplifies to \( 2 \sqrt{2} \).

Common mistake note:
Option A shows the right initial step but lacks simplification to \( 2 \sqrt{2} \).
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 6 (Q6):
Simplify and rationalize \( \frac{11}{\sqrt{11}} \).
A) 1
B) \( \sqrt{11} \)
C) 11
D) \( \frac{\sqrt{11}}{11} \)
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
Simplify the expression \( \frac{11}{\sqrt{11}} \) directly to 11.

Explanation:
The \(\sqrt{11}\) in the denominator cancels with the numerator, simplifying directly to 11.

Common mistake note:
Choosing B may indicate confusion about simplifying \( \sqrt{11}\) directly.

Problem 7 (Q7):
Find the rationalized form of \( \frac{2}{\sqrt{7}} \).
A) 2 \sqrt{7}
B) \( \frac{2\sqrt{7}}{7} \)
C) \( \frac{2\sqrt{7}}{14} \)
D) \( \frac{\sqrt{14}}{7} \)
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Multiply by \( \sqrt{7} \) to obtain \( \frac{2\sqrt{7}}{7} \).

Explanation:
The denominator is multiplied by its corresponding root, resulting in a rationalized version \( \frac{2\sqrt{7}}{7} \).

Common mistake note:
Errors may arise by choosing C, which over-complicates without real simplification.
