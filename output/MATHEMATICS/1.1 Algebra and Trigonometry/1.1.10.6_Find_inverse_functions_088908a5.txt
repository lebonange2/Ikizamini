Problem 1 (Q1):
Find the inverse of the function \( f(x) = 2x + 3 \).
A) \( f^{-1}(x) = \frac{x-3}{2} \)
B) \( f^{-1}(x) = 2x - 3 \)
C) \( f^{-1}(x) = \frac{x+3}{2} \)
D) \( f^{-1}(x) = -2x + 3 \)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
To find the inverse, replace \( f(x) \) with \( y \): \( y = 2x + 3 \). Then, swap \( x \) and \( y \), giving \( x = 2y + 3 \). Solve for \( y \): \( y = \frac{x-3}{2} \). Thus, \( f^{-1}(x) = \frac{x-3}{2} \).

Explanation:
Inverses swap inputs and outputs. Solve for \( y \) after swapping \( x \) and \( y \).

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is choosing option B due to incorrect subtraction sequence.

Problem 2 (Q2):
What is the inverse of \( g(x) = \sqrt{x - 4} \)?
A) \( g^{-1}(x) = x^2 + 4 \)
B) \( g^{-1}(x) = \sqrt{x + 4} \)
C) \( g^{-1}(x) = (x + 4)^2 \)
D) \( g^{-1}(x) = x^2 - 4 \)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Start with \( y = \sqrt{x - 4} \). Swap \( x \) and \( y \): \( x = \sqrt{y - 4} \). Square both sides: \( x^2 = y - 4 \). Solve for \( y \): \( y = x^2 + 4 \), noting the restriction \( x \geq 0 \) to maintain real values. Thus, \( g^{-1}(x) = x^2 + 4 \).

Explanation:
To find an inverse, swap \( x \) and \( y \) and solve for \( y \), ensuring domain restrictions are considered.

Common mistake note:
Option D may be chosen due to confusion with negative signs.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 3 (Q3):
Determine the inverse of \( h(x) = \frac{x+1}{x-1} \).
A) \( h^{-1}(x) = \frac{x+1}{x-1} \)
B) \( h^{-1}(x) = \frac{1+x}{1-x} \)
C) \( h^{-1}(x) = \frac{x-1}{x+1} \)
D) \( h^{-1}(x) = \frac{x-1}{2} \)
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
Begin with \( y = \frac{x+1}{x-1} \). Swap \( x \) and \( y \): \( x = \frac{y+1}{y-1} \). Cross-multiply: \( x(y-1) = y+1 \). Solve for \( y \): \( y = \frac{x-1}{x+1} \). Thus, \( h^{-1}(x) = \frac{x-1}{x+1} \).

Explanation:
Cross-multiplication is key to isolating \( y \) when dealing with rational functions.

Common mistake note:
Students may take option B by mistakenly flipping signs when solving.

Problem 4 (Q4):
Find the inverse of \( p(x) = 3x^3 \).
A) \( p^{-1}(x) = \sqrt[3]{\frac{x}{3}} \)
B) \( p^{-1}(x) = \sqrt[3]{3x} \)
C) \( p^{-1}(x) = \frac{1}{3} \sqrt[3]{x} \)
D) \( p^{-1}(x) = \frac{x^3}{3} \)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Start with \( y = 3x^3 \). Swap \( x \) and \( y \): \( x = 3y^3 \). Solve for \( y \): \( y = \sqrt[3]{\frac{x}{3}} \). Thus, \( p^{-1}(x) = \sqrt[3]{\frac{x}{3}} \).

Explanation:
The cubic root helps solve for \( y \) after isolating it using division.

Common mistake note:
Students may choose C due to misplacement of the division factor.

Problem 5 (Q5):
What is the inverse of \( q(x) = \frac{1}{x + 2} \)?
A) \( q^{-1}(x) = \frac{1}{x} - 2 \)
B) \( q^{-1}(x) = \frac{1}{x - 2} \)
C) \( q^{-1}(x) = \frac{1}{x} + 2 \)
D) \( q^{-1}(x) = \frac{1}{x+2} \)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
First, let \( y = \frac{1}{x+2} \). Swap \( x \) and \( y \): \( x = \frac{1}{y+2} \). Solve for \( y \): \( y+2 = \frac{1}{x} \), which simplifies to \( y = \frac{1}{x} - 2 \). Therefore, \( q^{-1}(x) = \frac{1}{x} - 2 \).

Explanation:
Isolating \( y \) requires rearranging and performing inverse operations correctly, especially with division.

Common mistake note:
Mistakes arise by incorrectly adding a negative sign, leading to option C.

Problem 6 (Q6):
Determine the inverse of \( r(x) = x^2 - 1 \) for \( x \geq 0 \).
A) \( r^{-1}(x) = \sqrt{x+1} \)
B) \( r^{-1}(x) = \sqrt{x} + 1 \)
C) \( r^{-1}(x) = x + 1 \)
D) \( r^{-1}(x) = \sqrt{x-1} \)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Start with \( y = x^2 - 1 \). Swap \( x \) and \( y \): \( x = y^2 - 1 \). Solve for \( y \): \( y = \sqrt{x+1} \). Thus, \( r^{-1}(x) = \sqrt{x+1} \).

Explanation:
Recognize the need for the sqrt function to 'reverse' squaring for nonnegative \( x \).

Common mistake note:
Overlooking the condition \( x \geq 0 \) can lead to choosing the wrong branch.

Problem 7 (Q7):
What is the inverse of \( t(x) = \ln(x - 2) \)?
A) \( t^{-1}(x) = e^{x} + 2 \)
B) \( t^{-1}(x) = e^{x} - 2 \)
C) \( t^{-1}(x) = e^{x+2} \)
D) \( t^{-1}(x) = \ln(x+2) \)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Begin with \( y = \ln(x - 2) \). Swap \( x \) and \( y \): \( x = \ln(y - 2) \). Exponentiate both sides to isolate \( y - 2 \): \( e^{x} = y - 2 \). Solve for \( y \): \( y = e^{x} + 2 \). Thus, \( t^{-1}(x) = e^{x} + 2 \).

Explanation:
Exponentiation undoes the natural log. Add 2 to adjust for the horizontal shift of the input.

Common mistake note:
Confusing the order of operations or the direction of shifts can lead to incorrect choices.
