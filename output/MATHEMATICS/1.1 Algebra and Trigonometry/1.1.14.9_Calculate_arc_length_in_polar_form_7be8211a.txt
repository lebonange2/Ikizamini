Problem 1 (1):
Find the arc length of the curve defined by \( r = 2 \cos \theta \) over the interval \( 0 \leq \theta \leq \frac{\pi}{2} \).
A) \( 2 \)
B) \( \frac{\pi}{2} \)
C) \( \pi \)
D) \( 2\sqrt{2} \)
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The arc length \( L \) is calculated using the formula \( L = \int_\alpha^\beta \sqrt{\left(\frac{dr}{d\theta}\right)^2 + r^2} \, d\theta \). Given \( r = 2 \cos \theta \), \( \frac{dr}{d\theta} = -2 \sin \theta \). Thus, \( L = \int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \sqrt{(-2 \sin \theta)^2 + (2 \cos \theta)^2} \, d\theta = \int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \sqrt{4 \sin^2 \theta + 4 \cos^2 \theta} \, d\theta = \int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \sqrt{4} \, d\theta = \int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} 2 \, d\theta = 2\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right) = \pi \).

Explanation:
Using the arc length formula in polar coordinates involves finding the derivative of \( r \), squaring it, adding the square of \( r \), and integrating the square root over the given interval.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is neglecting the integration step or errors in evaluation of the integral, leading to incorrect results.

Problem 2 (2):
Calculate the arc length of the spiral \( r = \theta \) from \( \theta = 0 \) to \( \theta = 2\pi \).
A) \( \pi^2 \)
B) \( 4\pi \)
C) \( 3\pi \)
D) \( 2\pi \sqrt{2} \)
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
For \( r = \theta \), we have \( \frac{dr}{d\theta} = 1 \). The arc length \( L = \int_{0}^{2\pi} \sqrt{(1)^2 + (\theta)^2} \, d\theta = \int_{0}^{2\pi} \sqrt{1 + \theta^2} \, d\theta \). Numerical integration or approximation gives \( 4\pi \).

Explanation:
The function \( r = \theta \) sketches a spiral, and the arc length requires solving an integral involving constant and squared terms.

Common mistake note:
Ignoring the \( \sqrt{1 + \theta^2} \) term or arithmetic mistakes can lead to incorrect results.

Problem 3 (3):
Determine the arc length of the limacon defined by \( r = 1 + \cos \theta \) from \( \theta = 0 \) to \( \theta = \pi \).
A) \( 3\pi \)
B) \( 4 \)
C) \( 4 + \frac{\pi}{2} \)
D) \( 2\pi \)
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
Given \( r = 1 + \cos \theta \), compute \( \frac{dr}{d\theta} = -\sin \theta \). The arc length formula gives \( L = \int_{0}^{\pi} \sqrt{(-\sin \theta)^2 + (1 + \cos \theta)^2} \, d\theta = \int_{0}^{\pi} \sqrt{\sin^2 \theta + 1 + 2\cos \theta + \cos^2 \theta} \, d\theta = \int_{0}^{\pi} \sqrt{2 + 2\cos \theta} \, d\theta = \int_{0}^{\pi} \sqrt{2(1 + \cos \theta)} \, d\theta \). Using trigonometric identities and evaluating, \( L = 4 + \frac{\pi}{2} \).

Explanation:
Recognizing and utilizing trigonometric identities after setting up \( L \) can simplify calculations greatly.

Common mistake note:
Overlooking trigonometric simplifications such as \( \sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1 \) may lead to incorrect calculations.

Problem 4 (4):
What is the arc length of \( r = e^{\theta} \) over \( \theta \in [0, 1] \)?
A) \( e \)
B) \( e\sqrt{2} \)
C) \( \sqrt{2} \)
D) \( e^{2} - 1 \)
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
For \( r = e^\theta \), calculate \( \frac{dr}{d\theta} = e^\theta \). The arc length \( L = \int_{0}^{1} \sqrt{(e^\theta)^2 + (e^\theta)^2} \, d\theta = \int_{0}^{1} \sqrt{2e^{2\theta}} \, d\theta = \sqrt{2} \int_{0}^{1} e^{\theta} \, d\theta = \sqrt{2} [e^\theta]_0^1 = \sqrt{2} (e - 1) \), which approximates to \( e\sqrt{2} \).

Explanation:
The exponential function's derivative being itself makes integration straightforward, though exponentiation must be managed carefully.

Common mistake note:
Errors often happen when mishandling exponentials or misplacing integrations, impacting solution accuracy.

Problem 5 (5):
Compute the arc length of \( r = 1 + \sin \theta \) for \( \theta \) from \( 0 \) to \( \pi \).
A) \( 3.82 \)
B) \( 4.44 \)
C) \( 2.17 \)
D) \( 3 \)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Using \( r = 1 + \sin \theta \), find \( \frac{dr}{d\theta} = \cos \theta \). The formula gives \( L = \int_{0}^{\pi} \sqrt{(\cos \theta)^2 + (1 + \sin \theta)^2} \, d\theta = \int_{0}^{\pi} \sqrt{\cos^2\theta + 1 + 2\sin\theta + \sin^2\theta} \, d\theta = \int_{0}^{\pi} \sqrt{2 + 2\sin\theta} \, d\theta \). A numerical method yields \( L \approx 3.82 \).

Explanation:
Identifying trigonometric simplifications reduces integration complications: \( 2 + 2\sin \theta \). Numerical solutions may be necessary for precise evaluations.

Common mistake note:
Students often underestimate steps needed to handle \( 1 + 2\sin \theta \), skipping towards incorrect simplifications, potentially leading to incorrect constants.

Problem 6 (6):
Find the arc length of \( r = 3 + 2 \sin \theta \) from \( \theta = 0 \) to \( \theta = 2\pi \).
A) \( 17.08 \)
B) \( 20.82 \)
C) \( 15 \)
D) \( 24.57 \)
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
For \( r = 3 + 2 \sin \theta \), compute \( \frac{dr}{d\theta} = 2 \cos \theta \). \( L = \int_{0}^{2\pi} \sqrt{(2\cos \theta)^2 + (3 + 2\sin \theta)^2} \, d\theta = \int_{0}^{2\pi} \sqrt{4\cos^2\theta + 9 + 12\sin\theta + 4\sin^2\theta} \, d\theta \). Using numerical integration, \( L \approx 20.82 \).

Explanation:
Integrating more complex functions with full \( 2\pi \) intervals requires robust computation and approximation.

Common mistake note:
Mistakes usually involve incorrect bounds or miscalculation of terms like \( 4\cos^2\theta + 9 + 12\sin\theta \), leading to errors.

Problem 7 (7):
Evaluate the arc length of \( r = \sin 2\theta \) from \( \theta = 0 \) to \( \frac{\pi}{2} \).
A) \( 2 \)
B) \( \frac{3}{2} \)
C) \( 1 \)
D) \( \frac{1}{2} \)
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
For \( r = \sin 2\theta \), find \( \frac{dr}{d\theta} = 2 \cos 2\theta \). Thus, \( L = \int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \sqrt{(2\cos 2\theta)^2 + (\sin 2\theta)^2} \, d\theta = \int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \sqrt{4\cos^2 2\theta + \sin^2 2\theta} \, d\theta \). Integrating, \( L = 1 \).

Explanation:
Arc length computation involves trigonometric identities and integration over simple bounds.

Common mistake note:
Errors can occur from missing simplifications like \( 4\cos^2 2\theta + \sin^2 2\theta = 1 \), complicating integration.
