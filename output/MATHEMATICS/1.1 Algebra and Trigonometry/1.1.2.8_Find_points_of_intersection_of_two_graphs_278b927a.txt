Problem 1 (Q1):
What are the points of intersection of the graphs of the equations \(y = 2x + 3\) and \(y = -x + 1\)?
A) (1, 5)
B) (1, 3)
C) (-1, 1)
D) (-\frac{2}{3}, \frac{5}{3})
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
Set the equations equal: \(2x + 3 = -x + 1\). Solve for \(x\): \(3x = -2\) so \(x = -\frac{2}{3}\). Substitute \(x = -\frac{2}{3}\) back into \(y = 2x + 3\) to find \(y = \frac{5}{3}\). Thus, the intersection point is \((-\frac{2}{3}, \frac{5}{3})\).

Explanation:
Set the equations equal because the intersection points have the same \(y\) value for both equations. Substitute the \(x\) value back into either equation to find \(y\).

Common mistake note:
Be careful with algebraic manipulation and don't forget to substitute back to find \(y\).

Problem 2 (Q2):
Determine the intersection points of \(y = x^2\) and \(y = 2x + 3\).
A) (3, 12)
B) (3, 9)
C) (-1, 2)
D) (-1, 2) and (3, 9)
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
Set \(x^2 = 2x + 3\) leading to \(x^2 - 2x - 3 = 0\). Factor to \((x-3)(x+1) = 0\) giving roots \(x = 3, -1\). For \(x = 3\), \(y = 9\); for \(x = -1\), \(y = 2\). Intersections: \((-1, 2)\), \((3, 9)\).

Explanation:
Form a quadratic by equalizing, solve for \(x\), and substitute each solution back to get \(y\)-values.

Common mistake note:
Check all roots and substitute them to find corresponding \(y\)-values.

Problem 3 (Q3):
What is the point of intersection for \(y = \sin(x)\) and \(y = \cos(x)\) over \([0, \pi/2]\)?
A) (0, 0)
B) (\pi/4, \sqrt{2}/2)
C) (\pi/2, 0)
D) (1, 1)
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Solve \(\sin(x) = \cos(x)\). This implies \(\tan(x) = 1\), occurring at \(x = \pi/4\). Verify using the unit circle: \(\sin(\pi/4) = \cos(\pi/4) = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\).

Explanation:
\(\sin\) and \(\cos\) are equal when \(\tan(x) = 1\). Using the unit circle, this occurs at \(x = \pi/4\), giving \(y = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\).

Common mistake note:
Remember the values of \(\sin\) and \(\cos\) at common angles on the unit circle.

Problem 4 (Q4):
Approximate the points where \(y = e^x\) and \(y = 3 - x\) intersect.
A) (0, 2.7)
B) (1, 2)
C) (1, 1)
D) (0, 3)
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Equating \(e^x = 3-x\), use numerical methods like graphing. The intersection occurs near \(x=1\), where \(e^1 \approx 2.7\) approaches \(y = 2\).

Explanation:
Use numerical methods or graphing to find approximate intersection at \(x \approx 1\), yielding approximately \((1, 2)\).

Common mistake note:
Graphing helps visualize intersections. Avoid round-off errors in approximations.

Problem 5 (Q5):
Where do the circles defined by \((x-1)^2 + y^2 = 4\) and \(x^2 + (y-1)^2 = 4\) intersect?
A) (1, 3) and (3, 1)
B) (0, 1) and (1, 0)
C) (2, 2) and (4, 0)
D) (2, 2) and (0, 0)
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
Expand both circles: \((x-1)^2 + y^2 = x^2 - 2x + 1 + y^2 = 4\) and \(x^2 + y^2 - 2y + 1 = 4 \). Solving leads to \((2, 2)\) and \((0, 0)\).

Explanation:
Use algebraic methods to solve the system of equations derived from the expanded circle equations.

Common mistake note:
Verify each intersection point satisfies both original circle equations.

Problem 6 (Q6):
For the curves \(y = x^3\) and \(y = 6x - 9\), find all intersection points.
A) (0, 0)
B) (1, -3)
C) (-3, 0)
D) (3, 9) and (-1.85, -11.52)
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
Set \(x^3 = 6x - 9\) leading to \(x^3 - 6x + 9 = 0\). Use numerical methods like Newton-Raphson to find roots \(x = 3\) and \(x \approx -1.85\). Calculate intersections \((3, 9)\) and \((-1.85, -11.52)\).

Explanation:
Use iterative numerical solutions to find intersection points accurately due to non-linear nature.

Common mistake note:
Numerical solutions require careful handling; verify results by substitution.

Problem 7 (Q7):
Identify where the parabola \(y = x^2 - 4x + 4\) and line \(y = x - 2\) intersect.
A) (2, 0)
B) (0, 4)
C) (3, 1)
D) (2, 0) and (3, 1)
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
Set \(x^2 - 4x + 4 = x - 2\), simplifying to \(x^2 - 5x + 6 = 0\), factored as \((x-2)(x-3) = 0\). Thus, the intersections are \((2, 0)\) and \((3, 1)\).

Explanation:
Solve for \(x\) after equalizing the equations, check roots by substituting back into the linear formula.

Common mistake note:
Make sure to substitute back and verify each point is an intersection.
