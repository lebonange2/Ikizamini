Problem 1 (Q1):
Factor the expression: \( \sqrt{18}x + 3\sqrt{2}x \)
A) \( \sqrt{2}x(\sqrt{9} + 3) \)
B) \( 3\sqrt{2}x(\sqrt{3} + 1) \)
C) \( \sqrt{6}x(\sqrt{3} + \sqrt{1}) \)
D) \( \sqrt{2}x(3\sqrt{3} + 3) \)
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Factor out the greatest common factor, \( 3\sqrt{2}x \). The expression becomes \( 3\sqrt{2}x(\sqrt{3} + 1) \).

Explanation:
Both terms contain \( 3\sqrt{2}x \) as a common factor.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is choosing A, not simplifying \( \sqrt{9} \) to 3.

Problem 2 (Q2):
Factor \( 2\sqrt{5}y - 4\sqrt{20}y \).
A) \( y\sqrt{5}(2 - 4 \sqrt{5}) \)
B) \( 2y(\sqrt{5} - 2 \sqrt{5}) \)
C) \( 2\sqrt{5}y(1 - 2 \sqrt{5}) \)
D) \( 4\sqrt{5}y(\frac{1}{2} - \sqrt{5}) \)
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The greatest common factor is \( 2\sqrt{5}y \). Factoring gives \( 2\sqrt{5}y(1 - 2\sqrt{5}) \).

Explanation:
Simplify \( \sqrt{20} \) to \( 2\sqrt{5} \), then factor out \( 2\sqrt{5}y \).

Common mistake note:
Students may choose mistaken answers due to incorrect radical simplifications.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 3 (Q3):
Factor the expression: \( \sqrt{12}z + 2\sqrt{3}z \)
A) \( z(\sqrt{12} + 2\sqrt{3}) \)
B) \( z(3\sqrt{4} + 2) \)
C) \( \sqrt{3}z(\sqrt{4} + 2) \)
D) \( \sqrt{3}z(2\sqrt{3} + 2) \)
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
Factor out \( \sqrt{3}z \). \( \sqrt{12} = 2\sqrt{3} \, \Rightarrow \, \sqrt{3}z\cdot(\sqrt{4} + 2) \).

Explanation:
Convert \( \sqrt{12} \) to \( 2\sqrt{3} \) to factor \( \sqrt{3}z \).

Common mistake note:
Choosing D indicates a misunderstanding of initial simplification steps.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 4 (Q4):
Factor \( x\sqrt{27} - x\sqrt{3} \).
A) \( x\sqrt{3}(\sqrt{9} - 1) \)
B) \( x(3\sqrt{3} - \sqrt{3}) \)
C) \( x\sqrt{3}(3 - 1) \)
D) \( x(2\sqrt{3} - \sqrt{3}) \)
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
Convert \( \sqrt{27} \) to \( 3\sqrt{3} \). Factor out \( x\sqrt{3} \), remains \( x\sqrt{3}(3 - 1) \).

Explanation:
Recognize \( \sqrt{27} = 3\sqrt{3} \) before factoring \( x\sqrt{3} \).

Common mistake note:
Students often overlook proper simplification, leading to incorrect factoring.

Problem 5 (Q5):
Factor \( 4a\sqrt{10} + 8a\sqrt{2} \).
A) \( a\sqrt{2}(4\sqrt{5} + 8) \)
B) \( a\sqrt{2}(4\sqrt{5} + 4) \)
C) \( 4a\sqrt{2}(\sqrt{5} + 2) \)
D) \( 4a\sqrt{2}(\sqrt{5} + 1) \)
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
Simplify using the greatest common factor, \( 4a\sqrt{2} \): \( 4a\sqrt{2}(\sqrt{5} + 2) \).

Explanation:
Convert \( \sqrt{10} \to \sqrt{5}\cdot\sqrt{2}\), factor \( 4a\sqrt{2} \).

Common mistake note:
Choosing D suggests confusion in distributing and simplifying correctly.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 6 (Q6):
Factor the expression: \( 6m\sqrt{50} - 3m\sqrt{2} \)
A) \( 3m\sqrt{2}(2\sqrt{5} - 1) \)
B) \( 3m(2\sqrt{25} - \sqrt{2}) \)
C) \( 3m\sqrt{2}(\sqrt{25} - \sqrt{1}) \)
D) \( 3m\sqrt{2}(5 - 1) \)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Convert \( \sqrt{50} = 5\sqrt{2} \). Factor \( 3m\sqrt{2} \): \( 3m\sqrt{2}(2\sqrt{5} - 1) \).

Explanation:
Recognize \( \sqrt{50} = 5\sqrt{2} \) then factor \( 3m\sqrt{2} \).

Common mistake note:
Choosing C indicates failure to simplify \( \sqrt{50} \) properly.

Problem 7 (Q7):
Factor \( 5p\sqrt{45} + 10p\sqrt{5} \).
A) \( 5p\sqrt{5}(3 + 2) \)
B) \( 5p(3\sqrt{5} + 2) \)
C) \( 5p\sqrt{5}(3 + 2\sqrt{5}) \)
D) \( 5p(3\sqrt{5} + 2\sqrt{5}) \)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Recognize \( \sqrt{45} = 3\sqrt{5} \). Factor \( 5p\sqrt{5} \), results in \( 5p\sqrt{5}(3 + 2) \).

Explanation:
Convert \( \sqrt{45} \to 3\sqrt{5} \) before factoring. The terms simplify to a basic form after factoring.

Common mistake note:
A common error is misidentifying \( \sqrt{45} \), leading to incorrect factoring.
