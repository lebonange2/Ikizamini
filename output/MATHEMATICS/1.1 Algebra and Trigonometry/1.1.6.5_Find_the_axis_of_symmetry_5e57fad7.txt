Problem 1 (AsympQ1):
What is the equation of the axis of symmetry for the parabola defined by the quadratic function f(x) = x^2 + 4x - 5?
A) -2
B) 2
C) x = -2
D) x = 2
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The axis of symmetry for a parabola given by the quadratic function f(x) = ax^2 + bx + c is x = -b / (2a). For f(x) = x^2 + 4x - 5, a = 1 and b = 4. Substituting these values gives x = -4 / (2 	imes 1) = -2. Therefore, the equation of the axis of symmetry is x = -2.

Explanation:
The axis of symmetry divides the parabola into two mirror images. The formula helps to find its exact location.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to confuse the axis of symmetry with a vertex point. Some students may choose 'A' or 'B', which are the x-coordinate of the vertex, not the equation of the line.

Problem 2 (AsympQ2):
Find the axis of symmetry for the parabola y = 3(x - 1)^2 + 5.
A) x = -1
B) x = 0
C) x = 1
D) x = 5
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The axis of symmetry for a parabola in the form y = a(x - h)^2 + k is x = h. For the given function, h = 1. So, the equation of the axis of symmetry is x = 1.

Explanation:
This question uses vertex form, where (h, k) is the vertex and the axis of symmetry passes through it.

Common mistake note:
Students might mistakenly think that 'A' or 'B' are correct since they are related to the vertex. However, the axis of symmetry always goes through the vertex.

Problem 3 (AsympQ3):
What is the equation of the axis of symmetry for the parabola y = -2(x + 3)^2?
A) x = -6
B) x = -3
C) x = 0
D) x = 3
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The axis of symmetry for a parabola in the form y = a(x - h)^2 + k is x = h. For this function, h = -3. So, the equation of the axis of symmetry is x = -3.

Explanation:
This question uses vertex form again, where (h, k) is the vertex and the axis of symmetry passes through it.

Common mistake note:
Students might think 'A' or 'C' are correct because they involve multiplication by 2, but that's not relevant to finding the axis of symmetry.

Problem 4 (AsympQ4):
Given the quadratic equation x^2 + 6x + 9 = 0, what is the equation of its axis of symmetry?
A) x = -3
B) x = 0
C) x = 3
D) x = 6
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The quadratic formula for finding roots is x = (-b ± sqrt(b^2 - 4ac)) / (2a). For this equation, a = 1, b = 6, and c = 9. The axis of symmetry is at x = -b / (2a) = -6 / (2 	imes 1) = -3.

Explanation:
The axis of symmetry can be found from the roots without converting to vertex form.

Common mistake note:
Students might try to solve for the roots and mistake one of them as the axis of symmetry. However, only the average of the roots is correct.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 5 (AsympQ5):
Find the equation of the axis of symmetry for the parabola defined by the vertex (2, 4) and passing through the point (1, 3).
A) x = -1
B) x = 0
C) x = 1
D) x = 2
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
The vertex form of a parabola is y = a(x - h)^2 + k, where (h, k) is the vertex. Given h = 2 and k = 4, we can write y = a(x - 2)^2 + 4. Substituting the point (1, 3), we get 3 = a(1 - 2)^2 + 4, which gives a = -1. The equation is y = -(x - 2)^2 + 4. The axis of symmetry remains x = 2.

Explanation:
This question involves finding the parabola's equation from its vertex and another point.

Common mistake note:
Students might think that 'A', 'B', or 'C' are correct because they involve the point (1, 3), but the axis of symmetry is always at the x-coordinate of the vertex.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 6 (AsympQ6):
What is the equation of the axis of symmetry for the parabola y = 4(x - 5)^2 + 7 that opens downward?
A) x = -5
B) x = 0
C) x = 5
D) x = 12
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The axis of symmetry for a parabola in vertex form y = a(x - h)^2 + k is x = h. For this function, h = 5. Since the parabola opens downward (negative leading coefficient), it still has the same axis of symmetry at x = 5.

Explanation:
The direction of opening does not affect the location of the axis of symmetry.

Common mistake note:
Students might think that the negative coefficient changes the axis, but it only affects the parabola's shape, not its position.

Problem 7 (AsympQ7):
Find the equation of the axis of symmetry for the quadratic function f(x) = 2(x + 4)(x - 1).
A) x = -4
B) x = -1
C) x = 0
D) x = 3
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
To find the axis of symmetry, we need to express the quadratic function in vertex form. First, expand f(x) to get f(x) = 2x^2 + 6x - 8. Now, complete the square: f(x) = 2(x^2 + 3x) - 8 = 2((x + 1.5)^2 - 2.25) - 8 = 2(x + 1.5)^2 - 14.75. The vertex form is y = a(x - h)^2 + k, where h = -1.5 and k = -14.75. The axis of symmetry is x = -1.5, which simplifies to x = 3.

Explanation:
This question involves expanding the quadratic function and converting it to vertex form.

Common mistake note:
Students might think that 'A' or 'B' are correct because they involve the factors (x + 4) and (x - 1), but the axis of symmetry is found from the completed square form.
