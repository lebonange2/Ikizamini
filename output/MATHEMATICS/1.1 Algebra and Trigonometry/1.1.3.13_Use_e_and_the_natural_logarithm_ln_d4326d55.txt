Problem 1 (Q1):
Solve for \(x\): \(e^{x}=12\). Give the exact answer in terms of \(\ln\).
A) \(x=\ln(12)\)
B) \(x=\dfrac{1}{\ln(12)}\)
C) \(x=\ln\!\left(\tfrac{1}{12}\right)\)
D) \(x=12\ln(e)\)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
\(\ln(e^x)=\ln(12)\Rightarrow x=\ln(12).\)

Explanation:
Apply the natural log to both sides. Since \(\ln\) is the inverse of \(e^x\), \(\ln(e^x)=x\).

Common mistake note:
Choosing C: \(\ln(1/12)=-\ln(12)\), which would make \(x<0\), contradicting \(e^x=12>1\) (so \(x>0\)).

Problem 2 (Q2):
Simplify the expression: \(\ln(e^{5})\).
A) \(5\)
B) \(e^{5}\)
C) \(\ln(5e)\)
D) \(\dfrac{1}{5}\)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
\(\ln(e^5)=5.\)

Explanation:
Use the inverse property \(\ln(e^{u})=u\) for any real \(u\).

Common mistake note:
Answering B by dropping \(\ln\) without evaluating it. The logarithm returns the exponent, not the exponential expression.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 3 (Q3):
Solve for \(x\): \(\ln(x)=3\).
A) \(x=e^{3}\)
B) \(x=3e\)
C) \(x=\ln(3)\)
D) \(x=\dfrac{1}{3}\)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Exponentiate both sides with base \(e\): \(e^{\ln(x)}=e^3\Rightarrow x=e^3.\)

Explanation:
Since \(e^x\) is the inverse of \(\ln(x)\), applying \(e^{\cdot}\) to both sides isolates \(x\).

Common mistake note:
Treating \(\ln(x)=3\) as if it were linear and trying to “divide by \(\ln\)”. You must exponentiate to undo the log.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 4 (Q4):
Rewrite as a single logarithm: \(\ln(8)+\ln(5)\).
A) \(\ln(40)\)
B) \(\ln(13)\)
C) \(\ln\!\left(\dfrac{8}{5}\right)\)
D) \(\ln(8^5)\)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
\(\ln(8)+\ln(5)=\ln(8\cdot 5)=\ln(40).\)

Explanation:
Use the product rule: \(\ln a+\ln b=\ln(ab)\).

Common mistake note:
Choosing B by adding inside the log: \(\ln(8)+\ln(5)\neq\ln(8+5)\).
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 5 (Q5):
Simplify: \(\ln(e^{x})+\ln(e^{2})\).
A) \(x+2\)
B) \(e^{x+2}\)
C) \(\ln(e^{x+2})\)
D) \(\ln(x+2)\)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
\(\ln(e^{x})=x\) and \(\ln(e^{2})=2\), so \(\ln(e^{x})+\ln(e^{2})=x+2.\)

Explanation:
Use \(\ln(e^u)=u\) on each term (equivalently, combine first: \(\ln(e^x)+\ln(e^2)=\ln(e^{x}e^{2})=\ln(e^{x+2})=x+2\)).

Common mistake note:
Choosing B by mistakenly thinking logs “disappear” leaving exponentials. Also, D is wrong because logs do not distribute over addition: \(\ln(a)+\ln(b)\neq\ln(a+b)\).
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 6 (Q6):
Solve for \(x\): \(e^{3x}=7\).
A) \(x=\dfrac{\ln 7}{3}\)
B) \(x=\ln(21)\)
C) \(x=3\ln 7\)
D) \(x=\dfrac{3}{\ln 7}\)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Take \(\ln\) of both sides: \(\ln(e^{3x})=\ln 7\Rightarrow 3x=\ln 7\Rightarrow x=\dfrac{\ln 7}{3}.\)

Explanation:
Taking \(\ln\) brings the exponent down: \(\ln(e^{3x})=3x\). Then solve the resulting linear equation.

Common mistake note:
Forgetting to divide by 3 after taking logs, leading to \(x=\ln 7\) or \(x=3\ln 7\).
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 7 (Q7):
Solve for \(x\) (give an exact value): \(\ln(x-2)=\ln(5)\).
A) \(x=7\)
B) \(x=3\)
C) \(x=\ln(7)\)
D) \(x=\dfrac{5}{2}\)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Since \(\ln\) is one-to-one (for positive inputs), \(\ln(x-2)=\ln(5)\Rightarrow x-2=5\Rightarrow x=7\). Check domain: \(x-2>0\Rightarrow x>2\), and \(7>2\).

Explanation:
Equal natural logs imply their arguments are equal, provided the log inputs are valid (positive).

Common mistake note:
Using an incorrect rule like \(\ln(x-2)=\ln x-\ln 2\). Log rules apply to products/quotients/powers, not differences.
