Problem 1 (Q1):
Solve the following system of equations for \(x\) and \(y\): \(x^2 + y^2 = 25\) and \(x + y = 7\).
A) (3, 4) and (-3, -4)
B) (3, 4) and (4, 3)
C) (5, 2) and (-5, -2)
D) (6, 1) and (1, 6)
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
First, express \(y\) in terms of \(x\) from the second equation: \(y = 7 - x\). Substitute this into the first equation: \(x^2 + (7-x)^2 = 25\). Simplifying gives \(2x^2 - 14x + 24 = 0\), which factors to \((x - 3)(x - 4) = 0\). Thus, \(x = 3\) or \(x = 4\). For \(x = 3\), \(y = 4\); for \(x = 4\), \(y = 3\). Solutions are \((3, 4)\) and \((4, 3)\).

Explanation:
The system involves a nonlinear equation (a circle) and a linear equation (a line). By substituting \(y = 7-x\) into the circle equation, we can simplify it to a quadratic equation which shows the possible intersection points.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to miscalculate the factors of the quadratic equation or incorrectly simplify expressions when substituting \(y = 7-x\).

Problem 2 (Q2):
Solve the nonlinear system: \(y = x^2 - 4\) and \(y = 2x + 6\).
A) (2, 10) and (-2, -2)
B) (1, 8) and (-1, 4)
C) (3, 12) and (2, 10)
D) (4, 14) and (0, 6)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Substitute \(y = 2x + 6\) into \(y = x^2 - 4\) to get \(x^2 - 4 = 2x + 6\). Simplify to \(x^2 - 2x - 10 = 0\). Solve this quadratic using the quadratic formula to get \(x = 2\) and \(x = -2\). For \(x = 2\), \(y = 10\); for \(x = -2\), \(y = -2\). The solutions are \((2, 10)\) and \((-2, -2)\).

Explanation:
The solutions are found by setting the equations equal, leading to a quadratic equation. Solving it yields potential x-values which are then used to find corresponding y-values.

Common mistake note:
A mistake could be incorrect application of the quadratic formula, leading to wrong x-values.

Problem 3 (Q3):
Find the solution(s) for: \(x^2 + y = 1\) and \(y^2 + x = 1\).
A) (0, 1) and (1, 0)
B) (1, 1) and (0, 0)
C) (-1, -1) and (1, 1)
D) (0, 0) and (1, -1)
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Rearrange each equation as \(y = 1 - x^2\) and \(x = 1 - y^2\). Substitute \(y = 1 - x^2\) into \(x = 1 - y^2\) to get \(x = 1 - (1 - x^2)^2\). Simplify to solve for \(x\): \(x = 1\) and \(x = 0\), leading to \(y = 0\) and \(y = 1\) respectively. Solutions are \((0, 0)\) and \((1, 1)\).

Explanation:
By expressing \(y\) and \(x\) each in terms of the other, substitute and solve for consistent solutions.

Common mistake note:
Errors often occur during substitution and simplification of powers, leading to incorrect pairings of solutions.

Problem 4 (Q4):
Determine the intersection points of \(x^2 - y = 4\) and \(x = 2y\).
A) (4, 2) and (-4, -2)
B) (8, 4) and (-8, -4)
C) (8, 4) and (4, 2)
D) (0, 0) and (4, -2)
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Substitute \(x = 2y\) into \(x^2 - y = 4\) to get \((2y)^2 - y = 4\), simplifying to \(4y^2 - y - 4 = 0\). Solving for \(y\) using the quadratic formula gives \(y = 4\) and \(y = -4\). Corresponding \(x\) values are \(8\) and \(-8\). Hence solutions are \((8, 4)\) and \((-8, -4)\).

Explanation:
Substitution reduces the nonlinear system into a quadratic form, solved via standard methods to find intersections correctly.

Common mistake note:
Sign errors in factors or ignoring correct roots lead to solution errors.

Problem 5 (Q5):
Given the system \(2x + y^2 = 7\) and \(x = y + 1\), find all solutions.
A) (3, 2) and (1, 0)
B) (4, 3) and (0, -1)
C) (1, 0) and (-2, -3)
D) (5, 4) and (-3, -4)
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
Substitute \(x = y + 1\) into \(2x + y^2 = 7\) to obtain \(2(y + 1) + y^2 = 7\). Simplification yields \(y^2 + 2y - 5 = 0\); solving gives \(y = 1\) and \(y = -2\). Back-substituting for \(x\) gives solutions \((1, 0)\) and \((-2, -3)\).

Explanation:
Substituting linear into a nonlinear equation simplifies the structure, revealing a solvable quadratic.

Common mistake note:
Common errors arise from improperly simplifying the quadratic equation during substitution.

Problem 6 (Q6):
Solve: \(x^2 - y^2 = 1\) and \(x + y = 3\).
A) (2, 1)
B) (3, 0)
C) (5, -2) and (-5, 2)
D) (2, 1) and (1, 2)
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
Express \(y = 3 - x\) from the second equation, substitute into \(x^2 - y^2 = 1\): \(x^2 - (3-x)^2 = 1\). Simplifying yields solutions \(x = 2\), \(y = 1\) and \(x = 1\), \(y = 2\).

Explanation:
Transforming a difference of squares with subsequent substitution clarifies consistent solutions.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is mishandling the difference of squares during simplification.

Problem 7 (Q7):
Find the solution(s) to \(x^2 + 2 = y\) and \(x - y = -2\).
A) (2, 6)
B) (-2, 2)
C) (0, 2) and (1, 3)
D) (2, 6) and (-2, 2)
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
Substitute \(y = x^2 + 2\) into \(x - y = -2\) to derive \(x - (x^2 + 2) = -2\), simplifying to \(x^2 - x - 4 = 0\). Applying the quadratic formula, \(x = 2\) or \(x = -2\). For \(x = 2\), \(y = 6\); for \(x = -2\), \(y = 2\).

Explanation:
Substitution reveals a path through quadratic simplification, matching results by reintegration.

Common mistake note:
Missteps often involve mishandling like terms or incorrect quadratic formula application.
