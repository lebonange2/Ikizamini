Problem 1 (:Q1):
A bridge is designed to support a maximum load of 10,000 kg. A truck with a weight of 9,500 kg is approaching the bridge. What is the safe loading condition for the bridge?
A) Underloaded by 500 kg
B) Overloaded by 500 kg
C) At its maximum capacity
D) Safe with a 5% safety factor
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
The truck's weight is 9,500 kg, which is less than the bridge's maximum load of 10,000 kg. Therefore, the bridge is not overloaded. With a difference of 500 kg between the truck's weight and the bridge's capacity, there is a 5% safety factor (500 kg / 10,000 kg = 0.05).

Explanation:
This question assesses the application of engineering principles to real-world scenarios, specifically in determining safe loading conditions.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be selecting 'Overloaded by 500 kg' (B), which is incorrect because the truck's weight is actually below the bridge's maximum capacity.

Problem 2 (:Q2):
An engineer needs to design a heat exchanger for a chemical process that operates at 150°C and requires a heat transfer rate of 10 kW. Which material would be the most suitable for the heat exchanger tubes, given that corrosion resistance is crucial?
A) Copper
B) Stainless steel
C) Aluminum
D) Carbon steel
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Stainless steel is known for its excellent corrosion resistance and can withstand high temperatures. Copper and aluminum have lower melting points, making them less suitable for this application, while carbon steel may not provide the required corrosion resistance.

Explanation:
This question evaluates the ability to apply engineering knowledge in selecting materials based on specific requirements such as temperature and corrosion resistance.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake might be choosing 'Copper' (A) or 'Aluminum' (C), which are not suitable for high-temperature applications due to their lower melting points.

Problem 3 (:Q3):
In a hydraulic system, a piston with an area of 5 cm² exerts a force of 200 N. If the cylinder's output piston has an area of 10 cm², what force will it exert?
A) 400 N
B) 200 N
C) 100 N
D) 50 N
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
Using the principle of hydraulic pressure (pressure is conserved), we can calculate the output force: F_out = (F_in × A_out) / A_in = (200 N × 10 cm²) / 5 cm² = 400 N. Since the system amplifies the force, the correct answer is 100 N.

Explanation:
This question tests understanding of fluid mechanics and pressure relationships in hydraulic systems.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be selecting '400 N' (A), which is the input force without considering the area ratio that determines the output force amplification.

Problem 7 (:Q4):
An engineer needs to design a suspension system for a vehicle. The vehicle's mass is 1,500 kg and experiences a vertical acceleration of 2 m/s² when braking. What is the net force acting on the suspension system during braking?
A) 3,000 N
B) 4,500 N
C) 6,000 N
D) 7,500 N
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The net force acting on the suspension system can be calculated using Newton's second law: F = m × a = 1,500 kg × 2 m/s² = 3,000 N. However, since this is the force per wheel and there are typically four wheels in a vehicle, we need to multiply by 4 to get the total force acting on the suspension system: 3,000 N × 4 = 12,000 N.

Explanation:
This question assesses understanding of Newton's second law and applying it to an engineering context.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be not multiplying by 4 (number of wheels) when calculating the total force acting on the suspension system.
