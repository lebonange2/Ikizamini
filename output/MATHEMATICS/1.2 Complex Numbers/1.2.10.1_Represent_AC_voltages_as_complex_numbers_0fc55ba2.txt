Problem 1 (:1):
An AC voltage with a peak value of 12 V and a frequency of 60 Hz is given by the equation: v(t) = 12 sin(2πft). Represent this voltage as a complex number.
A) 12e^{j2πft}
B) 12e^{-j2πft}
C) 6e^{jπft}
D) 6e^{-jπft}
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The complex representation of an AC voltage is given by V = Vm e^{j(ωt + φ)}, where Vm is the peak voltage, ω = 2πf is the angular frequency, and t is time. In this case, Vm = 12 V and f = 60 Hz. The equation provided in the stem can be converted to complex form by multiplying by e^{jωt}. So, V = 12e^{j2πft}.

Explanation:
The correct representation includes the peak voltage (12 V), the angular frequency (2πf), and the imaginary unit (j). The phase angle is not given, so it's assumed to be zero.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to use θ instead of 2πf, which would result in an incorrect frequency representation.

Problem 2 (:2):
An AC voltage with a peak-peak value of 16 V and a phase angle of π/4 radians is given by the equation: v(t) = 8e^{j(ωt + π/4)}. Represent this voltage as a phasor diagram.
A) A phasor with a magnitude of 8 and an angle of π/2
B) A phasor with a magnitude of 16 and an angle of π/4
C) A phasor with a magnitude of 8 and an angle of π/4
D) A phasor with a magnitude of 16 and an angle of π/2
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The phasor representation of the AC voltage is given by a vector with its length representing the peak voltage (Vm) and its angle representing the phase shift (φ). In this case, Vm = 8 V (half of the peak-peak value), and the phase angle φ is π/4 radians. Therefore, the phasor diagram shows a vector with a magnitude of 8 and an angle of π/4.

Explanation:
The phasor diagram should accurately represent the peak voltage and phase shift given in the equation.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to use the peak-peak value (16 V) instead of the peak voltage (8 V), which would result in an incorrect magnitude on the phasor.

Problem 3 (:3):
An AC voltage with a frequency of 50 Hz and a phase shift of π/2 radians is given by the equation: v(t) = 10e^{j(ωt + π/2)}. What is the complex conjugate of this voltage?
A) 10e^{-j(ωt + π/2)}
B) 10e^{j(ωt - π/2)}
C) 10e^{-jt}
D) 10e^{jt}
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The complex conjugate of a voltage V = Vm e^{j(ωt + φ)} is given by V* = Vm e^{-j(ωt + φ)}. In this case, the voltage is v(t) = 10e^{j(ωt + π/2)}, so its complex conjugate is 10e^{-j(ωt + π/2)}.

Explanation:
The complex conjugate of a voltage involves changing the sign of the phase angle while keeping the magnitude and frequency unchanged.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget to change the sign of the phase angle, which would result in an incorrect complex conjugate.

Problem 4 (:4):
An AC voltage with a peak value of 10 V and a frequency of 80 Hz is given by v(t) = 5sin(2πft). Calculate the RMS (root mean square) value of this voltage.
A) 5
B) 10
C) 5√(2)
D) 10√(2)
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The RMS value of an AC voltage V(t) = Vm sin(ωt) is given by Vrms = Vm / √(2). In this case, Vm = 10 V. So, Vrms = 10 / √(2) = 5√(2).

Explanation:
The RMS value of an AC voltage is used to compare it with a DC voltage that would produce the same heating effect. The formula involves dividing the peak voltage by √(2).

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget to divide by √(2), which would result in an incorrect RMS value.

Problem 5 (:5):
An AC voltage with a peak-peak value of 18 V and a frequency of 40 Hz is given by v(t) = 9sin(ωt + π/3). What is the period of this voltage?
A) 0.25 s
B) 0.5 s
C) 0.75 s
D) 1 s
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The period (T) of an AC voltage with frequency f is given by T = 1/f. In this case, the frequency is 40 Hz, so the period is T = 1/40 = 0.025 s or 25 ms.

Explanation:
The period of a waveform is the time it takes to complete one full cycle. It's inversely proportional to the frequency.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to use the peak-peak value instead of the frequency when calculating the period, which would result in an incorrect period.

Problem 6 (:6):
An AC voltage with a peak value of 15 V and a phase shift of π/3 radians is given by v(t) = 7.5sin(ωt + π/3). What is the average value of this voltage over one cycle?
A) 0
B) 7.5
C) 15
D) 30
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The average value of an AC voltage V(t) = Vm sin(ωt + φ) over one cycle is given by Vavg = 0. In this case, since the waveform is sinusoidal, the average value over a complete cycle is zero.

Explanation:
The average value of a pure sine wave AC voltage over one cycle is always zero because the positive and negative values cancel each other out.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to assume that the average value is equal to the peak or RMS value, which is not true for sinusoidal waveforms.

A) 12
B) 12e^{jπ/6}
C) 12e^{-jπ/6}
D) 0
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
The instantaneous voltage at a specific time t is given by plugging that value into the equation. For t = 0.01 s, v(0.01) = 12sin(ω 	imes 0.01 + π/6) = 12sin(π/100 + π/6). Since sin(π/6) is a non-zero real number, the instantaneous voltage at t = 0.01 s will be 12sin(π/100 + π/6) which is not zero.

Explanation:
The problem asks for the voltage value at a specific time, not an average or RMS value.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to assume that the instantaneous voltage is always zero or equal to the peak voltage, which is not true for AC waveforms.
