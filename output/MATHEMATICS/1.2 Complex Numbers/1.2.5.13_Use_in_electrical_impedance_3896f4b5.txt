Problem 1 (:1):
What is the unit of electrical impedance?
A) Ohm (Ω)
B) Volt (V)
C) Ampere (A)
D) Hertz (Hz)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Electrical impedance, denoted by Z, measures the opposition to current flow in an electrical circuit. Its unit is Ohm (Ω), which represents resistance.

Explanation:
Impedance combines resistance and reactance; however, the question asks for the unit of impedance itself, not its components. Therefore, choices B (Volt), C (Ampere), and D (Hertz) are incorrect as they represent other electrical quantities.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing B (Volt) or C (Ampere) because they are related to voltage and current.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 2 (:2):
In a series RLC circuit, which component(s) contribute to the impedance?
A) Resistor only
B) Inductor only
C) Capacitor only
D) Resistor, inductor, and capacitor
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
In a series RLC circuit, all three components - resistor (R), inductor (L), and capacitor (C) contribute to the overall impedance Z.

Explanation:
The impedance of a series RLC circuit is given by Z = √(R^2 + (XL - XC)^2), where XL is the inductive reactance and XC is the capacitive reactance. Each component affects the total impedance differently, making choices A, B, and C incomplete.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing A, B, or C if the learner focuses on only one aspect of the circuit.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 3 (:3):
What is the relationship between electrical impedance and frequency in a capacitor?
A) Impedance increases with frequency
B) Impedance decreases with frequency
C) Impedance remains constant regardless of frequency
D) Impedance varies randomly with frequency
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The impedance of a capacitor, XC, is inversely proportional to the frequency (f): XC = 1/(2πfC). Thus, as frequency increases, impedance decreases.

Explanation:
Capacitors allow current to flow more easily at higher frequencies, which reduces their effective opposition to current. Choices A, C, and D are incorrect because they describe different behaviors or lack a consistent relationship with frequency.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing A if the learner混淆s capacitive reactance with inductive reactance.

Problem 4 (:4):
In an AC circuit, what role does electrical impedance play?
A) It determines the voltage across each component
B) It controls the current through each component
C) It affects both the voltage and current in the circuit
D) It has no effect on the behavior of the circuit
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
Electrical impedance influences both the voltage drop across and the current flowing through each element in an AC circuit.

Explanation:
Impedance determines how much a component resists or modifies the sinusoidal waveform of an AC signal. Choices A and B are incomplete as they only address one aspect, while D is incorrect because it implies that impedance has no impact.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing A or B if the learner focuses on voltage or current independently.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 5 (:5):
What happens to the total impedance of a parallel RLC circuit when the frequency is increased?
A) Impedance increases
B) Impedance decreases
C) Impedance remains constant
D) Impedance may increase or decrease depending on the values of R, L, and C
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
The total impedance of a parallel RLC circuit is more complex than in a series circuit. At certain frequencies (resonant frequency), it can be lower than individual impedances; at other frequencies, it may increase or decrease depending on the values of R, L, and C.

Explanation:
In a parallel RLC circuit, the relationship between impedance, frequency, and component values is more intricate. Choices A, B, and C oversimplify this behavior.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Assuming that the same rules apply as in a series RLC circuit.

Problem 6 (:6):
What is the relationship between electrical impedance and reactance?
A) Reactance is a part of impedance
B) Reactance is always equal to impedance
C) Reactance is independent of impedance
D) Reactance can be greater or lesser than impedance
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Reactance (XL for inductors and XC for capacitors) is a component of electrical impedance, which also includes resistance (R).

Explanation:
Impedance is the total opposition to current flow, including both resistance and reactance. Choices B, C, and D are incorrect because they misrepresent this relationship.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing B if the learner混淆s reactance with impedance.

Problem 7 (:7):
How does electrical impedance help in designing a filter circuit?
A) By increasing resistance to certain frequencies
B) By reducing current flow at specific frequencies
C) By allowing only desired frequencies to pass through
D) By creating voltage drops that disrupt signal integrity
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
Electrical impedance is used in filter circuits to selectively allow or block specific frequency ranges, thereby shaping the signal.

Explanation:
Filter circuits, such as low-pass, high-pass, band-pass, and band-stop filters, rely on impedance variations with frequency to achieve their purpose. Choices A, B, and D describe unintended consequences or incorrect applications of impedance in filtering.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing A if the learner focuses on resistance without considering reactance.
