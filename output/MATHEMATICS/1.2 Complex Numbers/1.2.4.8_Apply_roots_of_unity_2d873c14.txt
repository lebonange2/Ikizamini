Problem 1 (:Q1):
Find the cube root of unity (a complex number that, when cubed, equals 1). Write your answer in polar form.
A) 1
B) cos(2π/3) + i sin(2π/3)
C) cos(4π/3) + i sin(4π/3)
D) cos(0) + i sin(0)
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The cube roots of unity are the solutions to the equation x^3 = 1. One way to find them is by using De Moivre's Theorem, which states that (cosθ + i sinθ)^n = cos(nθ) + i sin(nθ). For n = 3, we have three solutions: when θ = 0, 2π/3, and 4π/3. Thus, one cube root of unity is at θ = 2π/3, which gives us the polar form cos(2π/3) + i sin(2π/3).

Explanation:
De Moivre's Theorem helps find roots of complex numbers raised to a power. Answer A is incorrect because it's just 1, not a root of unity; C represents another cube root but not the one asked for in polar form; D is incorrect because it's just 1 (the trivial solution) which isn't in polar form.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing A or D as they represent the trivial solutions, not the cube roots of unity.

Problem 2 (:Q2):
Evaluate the product of the two cube roots of unity that are NOT 1. Express your answer in rectangular form.
A) -1
B) cos(π) + i sin(π)
C) cos(4π/3) - i sin(4π/3)
D) cos(2π/3) - i sin(2π/3)
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
The two non-trivial cube roots of unity are cos(2π/3) + i sin(2π/3) and cos(4π/3) + i sin(4π/3). Their product is (cos(2π/3) + i sin(2π/3))(cos(4π/3) + i sin(4π/3)). Using the trigonometric identities for products of cosine and sine, we get cos(6π/3) - sin^2(2π/3) + i(sin(2π/3)cos(4π/3) + cos(2π/3)sin(4π/3)). This simplifies to -1/2 - √3/2i, which is the rectangular form of the product.

Explanation:
The key here is to multiply the two non-trivial cube roots and simplify using trigonometric identities. Answer A is incorrect because it's not the product of the given roots; B represents the polar form of -1 but not in rectangular form; C is another possible result but not the correct one for this problem.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Mixing up the angles or simplifying incorrectly, leading to an incorrect rectangular form.

Problem 3 (:Q3):
Find the fourth root of unity (a complex number that, when raised to the power of 4, equals 1) in exponential form with a base of e. Express your answer as e^(ix), where x is a real number.
A) e^(0i)
B) e^(πi/2)
C) e^(πi)
D) e^(3πi/2)
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The fourth roots of unity are the solutions to the equation x^4 = 1. Using De Moivre's Theorem, we can find them at θ = 0, π/2, π, and 3π/2. In exponential form with base e, this corresponds to e^(0i), e^(πi/2), e^(πi), and e^(3πi/2). The one asked for is the one that's not a trivial solution (not 1), which is e^(πi/2).

Explanation:
De Moivre's Theorem helps find roots of complex numbers raised to a power. Answer A is incorrect because it's just 1, not a root of unity; C and D represent other fourth roots but not the one asked for in exponential form with base e.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing A as it represents the trivial solution, not the fourth root of unity.

Problem 4 (:Q4):
Given the complex number z = 1 + i, find its fifth power using roots of unity. Express your answer in rectangular form.
A) 2
B) 3 + 4i
C) -4 - 3i
D) 8 - 6i
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
To find z^5, we can express z in polar form and use De Moivre's Theorem. z = √2 * (cos(π/4) + i sin(π/4)). So, z^5 = (√2)^5 * (cos(5π/4) + i sin(5π/4)) = 32 * (-√2/2 - √2/2i) = -16 - 16i. Converting to rectangular form gives us -4 - 3i.

Explanation:
The key is to convert z into polar form, apply De Moivre's Theorem, and then convert the result back to rectangular form. Answer A, B, and D are incorrect because they do not match the correct calculation using roots of unity.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Applying powers without converting to polar form or making errors in trigonometric calculations.

Explanation:
