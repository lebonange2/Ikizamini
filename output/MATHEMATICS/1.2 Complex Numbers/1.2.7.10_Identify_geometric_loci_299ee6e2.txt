Problem 1 (One-Point Circle):
Given a single point P, what shape forms the set of all points equidistant from P?
A) A line passing through P
B) A ray starting at P
C) A circle centered at P
D) A segment with endpoint P
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The set of all points equidistant from a single point forms a circle, where that point is the center. Therefore, the correct answer is C: A circle centered at P.

Explanation:
Loci problems deal with sets of points satisfying certain geometric conditions. In this case, the condition is 'equidistant from P', which defines a circle.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake is to choose A (a line), assuming that equidistance would result in a straight line, but that's not true for all points; or D (a segment), which only considers two distances instead of all possible distances.

Problem 2 (Two-Point Line):
Given two distinct points A and B, what shape forms the set of all points that lie on the same line as A and B?
A) A ray starting at A and passing through B
B) A segment with endpoints A and B
C) The union of a ray starting at A and a ray starting at B
D) An infinite straight line passing through both A and B
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
The set of all points that lie on the same line as two distinct points forms an infinite straight line passing through both points. Therefore, the correct answer is D: An infinite straight line passing through both A and B.

Explanation:
Loci problems can also involve lines defined by two or more points. In this case, since we're looking for a line, the set includes all points on that line, not just those between the two given points.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake is to choose B (a segment), which only considers the portion of the line between the two points; or C (union of two rays), which doesn't account for points beyond A and B on the same line.

Problem 3 (Perpendicular Bisector):
Given a line segment AB, what shape forms the set of all points that are equidistant from both endpoints A and B?
A) A ray starting at midpoint M of AB
B) The perpendicular bisector of AB
C) The altitude from point A to line AB
D) The median from point A to line AB
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The set of all points equidistant from the endpoints of a line segment forms its perpendicular bisector, which is a line perpendicular to the segment and passing through its midpoint. Therefore, the correct answer is B: The perpendicular bisector of AB.

Explanation:
This problem involves finding the geometric locus for points that are equidistant from both endpoints of a line segment, which results in a specific type of line called the perpendicular bisector.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake is to choose A (a ray), which only considers one side of the midpoint; or C and D (altitudes and medians), which are related concepts but not the set of points equidistant from both endpoints.

Problem 4 (Inscribed Angle):
Given a circle with center O, what shape forms the set of all points that form an inscribed angle with vertex at point P?
A) A sector with central angle at P
B) The arc of the circle subtended by the inscribed angle at P
C) All points on the circle except for P itself
D) The line segment OP
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The set of all points that form an inscribed angle with vertex at a point on a circle is the arc of the circle subtended by that angle. Therefore, the correct answer is B: The arc of the circle subtended by the inscribed angle at P.

Explanation:
In this problem, we're looking for the set of points that form an inscribed angle with vertex at point P on a circle, which corresponds to the portion of the circle's circumference defined by that angle.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake is to choose A (a sector), which includes the area inside the angle and not just the arc; or C (all points except P), which doesn't account for the arc formed by the inscribed angle; or D (the line segment OP), which is a chord of the circle, not the set of points forming an inscribed angle.

Problem 5 (Circumscribed Circle):
Given a triangle ABC, what shape forms the set of all points that lie on the circumference of the circle passing through A, B, and C?
A) The intersection of the medians of triangle ABC
B) The intersection of the altitudes of triangle ABC
C) The intersection of the perpendicular bisectors of sides AB, BC, and CA
D) The intersection of the angle bisectors of angles A, B, and C
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The set of all points that lie on the circumference of a circle passing through the vertices of a triangle forms the circumcircle, which is defined by the intersection of the perpendicular bisectors of the triangle's sides. Therefore, the correct answer is C: The intersection of the perpendicular bisectors of sides AB, BC, and CA.

Explanation:
This problem involves finding the geometric locus for points that lie on a circle passing through all vertices of a triangle, which is called the circumcircle and can be constructed using perpendicular bisectors.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake is to choose A (the intersection of medians), B (the intersection of altitudes), or D (the intersection of angle bisectors), which are related concepts but not the set of points lying on the circumcircle.

Problem 6 (Median Bisector):
Given a triangle ABC, what shape forms the set of all points that divide each median into two segments with lengths in the ratio 2:1?
A) The altitudes of triangle ABC
B) The angle bisectors of angles A, B, and C
C) The perpendicular bisectors of sides AB, BC, and CA
D) The centroid of triangle ABC
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
The set of all points that divide each median of a triangle into two segments with lengths in the ratio 2:1 is the centroid of the triangle. Therefore, the correct answer is D: The centroid of triangle ABC.

Explanation:
This problem involves finding the geometric locus for points that bisect medians in a specific ratio, which turns out to be the centroid, a well-known point in any triangle.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake is to choose A (altitudes), B (angle bisectors), or C (perpendicular bisectors), which are related concepts but not the set of points dividing medians in a 2:1 ratio.

Problem 7 (Parabola Focus-Directrix):
Given a parabola with focus F and directrix d, what shape forms the set of all points that are equidistant from both F and d?
A) A line parallel to d passing through F
B) The perpendicular bisector of the line segment connecting F and any point on d
C) An infinite straight line passing through F and parallel to d
D) A curve that is symmetrical about a vertical line passing through F
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
The set of all points in a plane that are equidistant from a fixed point (focus) and a fixed line (directrix) forms a parabola. Therefore, the correct answer is D: A curve that is symmetrical about a vertical line passing through F.

Explanation:
This problem involves understanding the definition of a parabola as the set of points equidistant from a focus and a directrix, which results in a specific type of curve.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake is to choose A (a line parallel to d), which describes a different geometric figure; B (perpendicular bisector) and C (infinite straight line) are related concepts but not the set of points defining a parabola.
