Problem 1 (:1):
Find the modulus of the complex number 3 + 4i in polar form.
A) (5, 0.64)
B) (7, 0.92)
C) (5, 0.92)
D) (7, 0.64)
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The modulus of a complex number in polar form is given by the length of the vector from the origin to the point represented by the complex number. To find the modulus of 3 + 4i, we use the Pythagorean theorem: r = √(a^2 + b^2), where a and b are the real and imaginary parts of the complex number, respectively. For 3 + 4i, a = 3 and b = 4, so r = √(9 + 16) = √25 = 5. The angle in polar form (θ) can be found using the arctangent function: θ = arctan(b/a). For 3 + 4i, θ = arctan(4/3) ≈ 0.927 radians or approximately 53.1°. Therefore, the modulus in polar form is (5, 0.927), which corresponds to option C.

Explanation:
The correct answer uses the Pythagorean theorem and arctangent function to find the modulus and angle of the complex number in polar form.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to use the wrong formula or to mix up the real and imaginary parts when calculating the modulus. Another mistake might be to forget to convert the angle from radians to degrees if the question asks for it.

Problem 3 (:3):
Find the modulus of the complex number 2√3 - 2i in polar form.
A) (4, 1.25)
B) (6, 0.78)
C) (4, 0.78)
D) (6, 1.25)
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The modulus of a complex number in polar form is given by the length of the vector from the origin to the point represented by the complex number. To find the modulus of 2√3 - 2i, we use the Pythagorean theorem: r = √(a^2 + b^2), where a and b are the real and imaginary parts of the complex number, respectively. For 2√3 - 2i, a = 2√3 and b = -2, so r = √((2√3)^2 + (-2)^2) = √(12 + 4) = √16 = 4. The angle in polar form (θ) can be found using the arctangent function: θ = arctan(b/a). For 2√3 - 2i, θ = arctan(-2/(2√3)) ≈ -0.785 radians or approximately -45°. Since we're in the fourth quadrant, we add 180° or π radians to get a positive angle: θ = π + (-0.785) ≈ 2.36 radians. Therefore, the modulus in polar form is (4, 2.36), which corresponds to option C.

Explanation:
The correct answer uses the Pythagorean theorem and arctangent function to find the modulus and angle of the complex number in polar form, considering the quadrant where the point lies.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget that when the real part is positive and the imaginary part is negative, the angle will be in the fourth quadrant, which means you need to add 180° or π radians to the result of the arctangent function.
