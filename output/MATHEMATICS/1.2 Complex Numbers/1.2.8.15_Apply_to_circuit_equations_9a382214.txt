Problem 1 (:1):
A simple circuit has a voltage source of 9V and two resistors in parallel, one with 3Ω resistance and the other with 6Ω. What is the total current flowing through the circuit?
A) 1.5 A
B) 2.0 A
C) 4.5 A
D) 6.0 A
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The voltage across both resistors is 9V since they are in parallel. The total resistance (R_total) can be calculated using the formula for parallel resistors: 1/R_total = 1/3Ω + 1/6Ω = 1/2Ω. So, R_total = 2Ω. The total current (I) can be calculated using Ohm's Law: I = V/R. Thus, I = 9V / 2Ω = 4.5A. However, this is the combined current through both resistors. Since they are in parallel, the current divides between them; therefore, the total circuit current is half of this value, which is 2.0 A.

Explanation:
Ohm's Law (I = V/R) and the formula for parallel resistances were used to solve the problem.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to assume that the total current is equal to the current through one of the individual resistors, which is incorrect because the current divides between them in a parallel circuit.

Problem 2 (:2):
In a series circuit with a 12V battery and three resistors (5Ω, 7Ω, and 9Ω), what is the voltage drop across the 7Ω resistor?
A) 3.6 V
B) 4.8 V
C) 5.6 V
D) 7.2 V
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
In a series circuit, the total resistance (R_total) is the sum of all individual resistances: R_total = 5Ω + 7Ω + 9Ω = 21Ω. The voltage across each resistor is equal to the battery voltage divided by the number of ohms in that particular resistor. So, for the 7Ω resistor, the voltage drop (V_7) can be calculated as V_7 = V_total * (R_7 / R_total). Plugging in the values, we get V_7 = 12V * (7Ω / 21Ω) = 4.8V.

Explanation:
Ohm's Law and the understanding of voltage division in a series circuit were used to solve the problem.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to assume that all resistors have the same voltage drop, which is not true in a series circuit where voltage drops are distributed according to each resistor's resistance.

Problem 3 (:3):
A parallel circuit has two branches with 6Ω and 12Ω resistors. If the current through the 6Ω branch is 2A, what is the total current (I_total) in the circuit?
A) 1 A
B) 2 A
C) 3 A
D) 4 A
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The voltage across both branches is equal since they are in parallel. The current through each branch can be calculated using Ohm's Law: I = V/R. For the 6Ω branch, we have 2A = V/6Ω, so V = 12V. Now, to find the total current, we need to calculate the current through the 12Ω branch: I_12Ω = V / 12Ω = 12V / 12Ω = 1A. The total current is the sum of the currents in both branches: I_total = I_6Ω + I_12Ω = 2A + 1A = 3A.

Explanation:
Ohm's Law and understanding of current division in a parallel circuit were used to solve the problem.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to assume that the total current is equal to the current through one branch, which is incorrect because the current divides between branches in a parallel circuit.

Problem 4 (:4):
In a simple RC (resistor-capacitor) circuit with a 10V battery and a 5μF capacitor, the time constant τ is calculated to be 5ms. What is the value of the resistor (R) in ohms?
A) 25 Ω
B) 50 Ω
C) 75 Ω
D) 100 Ω
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The time constant τ is defined as the product of the resistance (R) and capacitance (C): τ = RC. Given τ = 5ms and C = 5μF, we can solve for R: R = τ / C = 5ms * (1s / 1000ms) / 5μF = 1s / 25μF = 4000 Ω/5μF = 800 Ω. However, since the answer choices are in whole numbers and we can round 800 Ω to the nearest option, the correct value is 500 Ω.

Explanation:
The definition of the time constant τ was used to solve the problem.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget to convert milliseconds (ms) to seconds (s) or microfarads (μF) to farads (F), leading to an incorrect calculation.

Problem 5 (:5):
A circuit has a 12V battery, a 4Ω resistor, and an inductor with a self-inductance of 0.5H. If the switch is closed, what is the initial current (I_initial) through the resistor?
A) 0 A
B) 1 A
C) 2 A
D) 3 A
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
When the switch is first closed in an RL circuit with a battery, the initial current (I_initial) is zero because the magnetic field around the inductor is initially zero. As time passes, the current starts to increase according to L * di/dt = V, where L is the self-inductance and V is the voltage of the battery. However, for the initial moment, I_initial = 0.

Explanation:
The behavior of an RL circuit when a switch is first closed was used to solve the problem.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to assume that the current instantly reaches its steady-state value, which is not true; it starts at zero and increases gradually.

Problem 6 (:6):
In a parallel RLC circuit with a 10V battery, a 5Ω resistor, a 2μF capacitor, and a 0.3H inductor, what is the resonant frequency (f) if the capacitive reactance (X_C) equals the inductive reactance (X_L)?
A) 10 Hz
B) 50 Hz
C) 100 Hz
D) 200 Hz
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
At resonance, X_C = X_L. The capacitive reactance is given by X_C = 1 / (2 * π * f * C), and the inductive reactance is X_L = 2 * π * f * L. Setting them equal to each other: 1 / (2 * π * f * 2μF) = 2 * π * f * 0.3H. Solving for f, we get f = 1 / (4 * π^2 * 0.3H * 2μF) ≈ 100 Hz.

Explanation:
The condition for resonance and the formulas for capacitive and inductive reactances were used to solve the problem.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget to convert capacitance from microfarads (μF) to farads (F) or inductance from henries (H) to webers (Wb), leading to an incorrect calculation.

Problem 7 (:7):
An AC circuit has a 50Ω resistor, a 10μF capacitor with a reactance of -30Ω, and a 0.2H inductor with a reactance of 60Ω. What is the impedance (Z) of the circuit at the frequency where X_C = X_L?
A) 50 Ω
B) 80 Ω
C) 110 Ω
D) 140 Ω
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
At the frequency where X_C = X_L, we can set up the equation: 1 / (2 * π * f * C) = 2 * π * f * L. Solving for f, we get f = 1 / (4 * π^2 * 0.2H * 10μF) ≈ 318 Hz. Now, the impedance (Z) of an AC circuit with a resistor and reactive components is given by Z = √(R^2 + (X_L - X_C)^2). Plugging in the values, we get Z = √(50Ω^2 + (60Ω - (-30Ω))^2) ≈ 50Ω.

Explanation:
The condition for X_C = X_L and the formula for impedance in an AC circuit were used to solve the problem.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget that the capacitive reactance is negative when calculating the impedance, leading to an incorrect result.
