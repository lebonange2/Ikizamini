Problem 1 (:1):
Evaluate the contour integral of f(z) = z^2 dz along the unit circle |z| = 1, counterclockwise.
A) π
B) 0
C) -π
D) 2π
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The contour integral of a function f(z) around a closed curve C is given by Φ_C f(z) dz. For the unit circle |z| = 1, we parametrize z as z(t) = e^(it), where t ranges from 0 to 2π. The integral becomes Φ_0^(2π) (e^(it))^2 * i e^(it) dt = Φ_0^(2π) i e^(3it) dt. Integrating this, we get (-1/3)e^(3it)|_0^(2π) = 0 because the exponential function evaluates to 1 and -1 at the limits.

Explanation:
The integral of an odd power of cosine or sine over a full period is zero.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to integrate z^2 without considering the differential dz. Another mistake might be forgetting to convert from rectangular to polar coordinates.

Problem 2 (2):
Calculate the contour integral of f(z) = 1/z dz along the semicircle C with center at origin, radius R (R > 0), and lying in the upper half-plane, counterclockwise.
A) πi
B) 0
C) -πi
D) (π/2)i
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The contour integral of f(z) = 1/z dz along a semicircle in the upper half-plane can be split into two parts: one along the real axis from -R to R and another along the semicircle. The integral along the real axis is zero because of Cauchy's theorem (or by direct integration). For the semicircle, we parametrize z as z(t) = Re^(it), where t ranges from 0 to π. The integral becomes Φ_0^π (Ri e^(it)) / (Re^(it)) dt = Φ_0^π i dt = πi.

Explanation:
The residue theorem or Cauchy's integral formula can be used to evaluate this contour integral.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget that the semicircle contributes only to the integral along the imaginary axis, and not along the real axis.

Problem 3 (3):
Evaluate Φ_C (z^2 + 1) dz where C is the circle |z - 2| = 3, counterclockwise.
A) 4πi
B) 0
C) 8πi
D) -4πi
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
Parametrize z as z(t) = 2 + 3e^(it), where t ranges from 0 to 2π. The integral becomes Φ_0^(2π) ((2+3e^(it))^2 + 1) (3i e^(it)) dt = Φ_0^(2π) (9e^(2it) + 12ie^(it) + 5) (3i e^(it)) dt. Integrating, we get -27/2 e^(3it)|_0^(2π) + 36/3 e^(2it)|_0^(2π) - 15/2 e^(it)|_0^(2π) = 8πi.

Explanation:
The integral is evaluated by substituting the parametrization of z into the function and integrating over t.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget to substitute the correct expression for z in terms of t, or to integrate the polynomial incorrectly.

Problem 4 (4):
Calculate Φ_C (1/(z^2 + 1)) dz where C is a circle centered at origin with radius 2, counterclockwise.
A) 0
B) πi
C) -πi
D) 2πi
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The function f(z) = 1/(z^2 + 1) has a simple pole at z = i. By the residue theorem, the integral is 2πi * Res(f, i) = 2πi * (1/(-2i)) = πi.

Explanation:
The residue theorem can be used to evaluate this contour integral by finding the residue of f(z) at the pole inside the contour.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to try to integrate directly without considering the residue theorem or the presence of a pole.

Problem 5 (5):
Evaluate Φ_C (z^3 + 2z) dz where C is the straight line segment from -1 to 1, counterclockwise.
A) 0
B) 1/4
C) 1/2
D) 3/4
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The integral along a straight line can be evaluated by converting to rectangular coordinates. Let z = x + 0i, where x ranges from -1 to 1. The integral becomes Φ_-1^1 (x^3 + 2x) dx = [x^4/4 + x^2]|_-1^1 = 0.

Explanation:
The integral of an odd function over a symmetric interval is zero.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget that the integral of an odd function over a symmetric interval is zero, or to integrate incorrectly.

Problem 6 (6):
Calculate Φ_C (e^z) dz where C is the rectangle with vertices at -1 + i, 1 + i, 1 - i, and -1 - i, counterclockwise.
A) 0
B) πi
C) 4πi
D) 6πi
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The function f(z) = e^z is entire (analytic everywhere), so by Cauchy's integral theorem, the contour integral around a closed curve is zero.

Explanation:
Cauchy's integral theorem states that the integral of an analytic function over a simple closed curve is zero.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to try to integrate directly without considering the properties of the function or the theorem.

Problem 7 (7):
Evaluate Φ_C (z^2 - 3z + 2)/(z - 1) dz where C is a circle centered at origin with radius 4, counterclockwise.
A) 0
B) πi
C) 4πi
D) 6πi
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The function f(z) = (z^2 - 3z + 2)/(z - 1) has a simple pole at z = 1. By the residue theorem, the integral is 2πi * Res(f, 1) = 2πi * (1 - 3 + 2) = 2πi.

Explanation:
The residue theorem can be used to evaluate this contour integral by finding the residue of f(z) at the pole inside the contour.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to try to integrate directly without considering the residue theorem or the presence of a pole.
