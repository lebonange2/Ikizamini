Problem 1 (:1):
A low-pass filter is designed to pass signals with a cutoff frequency of 1 kHz and attenuate signals above it. What type of filter would be suitable for this purpose?
A) Butterworth filter
B) Chebyshev Type I filter
C) Elliptic filter
D) Bessel filter
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
A Butterworth filter provides a flat frequency response in the passband and a gradual roll-off in the stopband, making it suitable for low-pass filtering with a smooth cutoff.

Explanation:
Butterworth filters have a maximally flat response in the passband, which minimizes distortion. Chebyshev Type I and Elliptic filters have steeper rolloffs but may introduce ripples in the passband or stopband. Bessel filters prioritize phase linearity over steepness of roll-off.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing C (Elliptic filter) because it has a steep roll-off. However, Elliptic filters have ripple in both the passband and stopband, which may not be desirable for a low-pass filter.

Problem 2 (2):
A high-pass filter is required to attenuate signals below 10 kHz and allow higher frequencies to pass through. Which type of filter would provide the best performance?
A) Chebyshev Type II filter
B) Bessel filter
C) Elliptic filter
D) RC passive filter
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Chebyshev Type II filters have a steeper roll-off in the stopband compared to other types, making them suitable for high-pass filtering with minimal ripple.

Explanation:
Chebyshev Type II filters are designed to minimize ripples in the stopband while providing a steep roll-off. Bessel filters prioritize phase linearity over steepness of roll-off, and Elliptic filters have ripple in both passband and stopband. RC passive filters don't offer as much control over frequency response.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing D (RC passive filter) because it's a simple circuit. However, RC passive filters do not provide the steep roll-off needed for high-pass filtering.

Problem 3 (3):
A designer needs to create a bandpass filter with a passband from 2 kHz to 5 kHz. Which type of filter would be best suited for this application?
A) Bessel filter
B) Chebyshev Type I filter
C) Butterworth filter
D) Bandpass filter (specifically designed)
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
A bandpass filter specifically designed for the desired frequency range will provide the most accurate and efficient performance.

Explanation:
While Butterworth, Chebyshev, and Bessel filters can be configured to approximate a bandpass response, a custom-designed bandpass filter will have the exact passband and transition regions needed. Other types may not achieve the required selectivity or roll-off.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing C (Butterworth filter) because it has a flat passband. However, Butterworth filters are typically used for low- or high-pass filtering, not bandpass.

Problem 4 (4):
A designer wants to design a notch filter to remove interference at 10 kHz in an audio signal. Which type of filter would be most appropriate?
A) RC passive filter
B) Chebyshev Type II filter
C) Band-stop filter (specifically designed)
D) Butterworth filter
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
A band-stop filter specifically designed for the interference frequency will provide the best attenuation at that specific point.

Explanation:
Band-stop filters, also known as notch filters, are designed to remove a narrow range of frequencies. RC passive filters and Butterworth filters do not offer the selectivity needed for a notch filter. Chebyshev Type II filters may not provide sufficient attenuation at a single frequency.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing A (RC passive filter) because it's simple. However, RC passive filters are not suitable for creating a narrow band-stop response.

Problem 5 (5):
In an audio system, a designer wants to reduce the bass frequencies below 100 Hz while preserving higher frequencies. Which type of filter would be best for this task?
A) High-pass filter (specifically designed)
B) Butterworth filter
C) Chebyshev Type I filter
D) Band-stop filter
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
A high-pass filter specifically designed to remove bass frequencies will provide the desired effect without affecting higher frequencies.

Explanation:
High-pass filters are used to attenuate low frequencies and allow higher ones to pass. Butterworth, Chebyshev Type I, and band-stop filters do not target a specific frequency range for attenuation while preserving others.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing D (Band-stop filter) because it removes frequencies. However, a band-stop filter would remove a range of frequencies, not just the low bass.
