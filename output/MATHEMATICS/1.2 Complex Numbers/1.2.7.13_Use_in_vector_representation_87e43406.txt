Problem 1 (?):
What is the dot product of two vectors A and B, where A = (3, 4) and B = (-2, 5)?
A) -7
B) 17
C) -17
D) 7
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
(3 * -2) + (4 * 5) = -6 + 20 = 14

Explanation:
The dot product of two vectors is calculated by multiplying corresponding components and summing the results.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to multiply all components together, rather than pairing them up. This would result in an incorrect answer.

Problem 2 (?):
Given vector C = (1, 2, 3) and vector D = (4, -5, 6), what is the cross product of C and D?
A) (7, -9, 1)
B) (7, -1, 9)
C) (-7, 9, -1)
D) (-7, -1, 9)
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
C x D = (2 * 6 - 3 * -5)i - (1 * 6 - 3 * 4)j + (1 * -5 - 2 * 4)k = (-7, 9, -1)

Explanation:
The cross product of two vectors is a new vector perpendicular to both original vectors. It's calculated using the determinant of a matrix with i, j, and k as row headers.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to mix up the signs when calculating the components of the resulting vector.

Problem 3 (?):
What is the unit vector in the direction of vector E = (6, 8)?
A) (3/5, 4/5)
B) (2/5, 3/5)
C) (1/5, 2/5)
D) (4/5, 3/5)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
||E|| = sqrt(6^2 + 8^2) = sqrt(36 + 64) = 10, unit vector = E / ||E|| = (6/10, 8/10) = (3/5, 4/5)

Explanation:
A unit vector is a vector with magnitude 1 in the same direction as the original vector. To find it, divide the original vector by its magnitude.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to forget to normalize the vector by dividing by its magnitude.

Problem 4 (?):
What is the projection of vector F = (2, 3) onto vector G = (-1, 4)?
A) -2
B) 2
C) 10
D) 6
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
proj_G(F) = (F . G) / ||G||^2 * G = ((2 * -1 + 3 * 4) / sqrt(-1^2 + 4^2)^2) * (-1, 4) = -2

Explanation:
The projection of one vector onto another is calculated by finding the scalar projection and multiplying it by the direction vector of the second vector.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to forget to divide by the square of the norm of the second vector.

Problem 5 (?):
Given a 2D vector H = (5, -3), what is its reflection across the x-axis?
A) (5, 3)
B) (-5, -3)
C) (-5, 3)
D) (5, -3)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Reflection across the x-axis changes only the sign of the y-component: (x, -y) = (5, -(-3)) = (5, 3)

Explanation:
Reflecting a vector across the x-axis means flipping its position in the y-direction while keeping the x-coordinate unchanged.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to forget to change only the sign of the y-component.

Problem 6 (?):
What is the angle between vectors I = (1, 2) and J = (-3, 4), in radians?
A) π/4
B) π/2
C) 3π/4
D) π
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
θ = arccos((I . J) / (||I|| ||J||)) = arccos((-3 + 8) / sqrt(1^2 + 2^2) * sqrt(-3^2 + 4^2)) = arccos(5 / sqrt(5) * sqrt(25)) = arccos(1/5) ≈ 1.37 radians

Explanation:
The angle between two vectors can be found using the dot product and the magnitudes of the vectors.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to use the wrong formula or forget to convert the result from degrees to radians.

Problem 7 (?):
What is the magnitude of vector K = (2√2, 0)?
A) 2
B) 2√2
C) 4
D) 4√2
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
||K|| = sqrt((2√2)^2 + 0^2) = sqrt(8 + 0) = 2√2

Explanation:
The magnitude of a vector is the square root of the sum of the squares of its components.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to forget to take the square root or misapply the Pythagorean theorem.
