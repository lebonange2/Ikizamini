Problem 1 (One):
Find the imaginary part of e^(iπ/4).
A) -1/√2
B) 1/√2
C) -√2
D) √2
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Using Euler's formula, we have e^(iθ) = cos θ + i sin θ. For θ = π/4, we get e^(iπ/4) = cos(π/4) + i sin(π/4). The imaginary part is sin(π/4), which equals 1/√2.

Explanation:
The imaginary part of the complex exponential comes from the sine term in Euler's formula.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to select A (-1/√2) as it might be confused with the cosine value at π/4, but we are looking for the imaginary part which is given by the sine.

Problem 2 (Two):
Evaluate e^(iπ) - cos(π).
A) -1
B) 0
C) 1
D) 2
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Using Euler's formula, we have e^(iπ) = cos π + i sin π. Thus, e^(iπ) - cos(π) = (cos π + i sin π) - cos π = i sin π. At π, sin π is 0, so the result is 0.

Explanation:
The difference between the complex exponential and its cosine part at π simplifies to zero due to sin π being zero.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to select A (-1) as it might be the value of cos(π), but Euler's formula also includes an imaginary term which cancels out with the cosine.

Problem 4 (Four):
Express cos(4θ) in terms of e^(iθ).
A) (e^(iθ) + e^(-iθ))^4
B) (e^(iθ) - e^(-iθ))^2
C) (e^(iθ) + e^(-iθ))^2
D) 1/2(e^(iθ) + e^(-iθ))^2
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
Using the cosine double-angle identity, cos(4θ) = 2cos²(2θ) - 1. Now, we can use Euler's formula to express cos(2θ): cos(2θ) = (e^(i2θ) + e^(-i2θ))/2. Squaring this gives us cos²(2θ) = [(e^(i2θ) + e^(-i2θ))^2]/4. Substituting back into the original expression, we get cos(4θ) = 2[(e^(i2θ) + e^(-i2θ))^2]/4 - 1/2 = (e^(i2θ) + e^(-i2θ))^2/2 - 1/2. Simplifying further, we have cos(4θ) = (e^(i4θ) + 2 + e^(-i4θ))/4 - 1/2 = (e^(iθ) + e^(-iθ))^2.

Explanation:
We used the cosine double-angle identity and Euler's formula to express cos(4θ) in terms of e^(iθ).

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to select A or B, as they involve powers of 4, but that's not correct for the cosine of four times the angle.

Problem 5 (Five):
Find the value of sin(2θ) if e^(iθ) = 1+i.
A) 2/√5
B) 1/√5
C) -1/√5
D) -2/√5
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Given e^(iθ) = 1+i, we can use Euler's formula to find sin(θ): e^(iθ) = cos θ + i sin θ. Comparing real and imaginary parts, we have cos θ = 1 and sin θ = 1. Now, using the sine double-angle identity, sin(2θ) = 2sin θ cos θ = 2 * (1) * (1) = 2. To express this in terms of √5, we get sin(2θ) = 2/√5.

Explanation:
We first found the sine and cosine values from the given complex exponential, then used the sine double-angle identity to find sin(2θ).

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to select B or C as they might come from a misapplication of the Pythagorean theorem, but we are looking for sin(2θ), not sin θ.

Problem 6 (Six):
Simplify (e^(iπ/3))^4.
A) -1/8
B) 1/8
C) -1
D) 1
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
Using Euler's formula, we have e^(iθ) = cos θ + i sin θ. For θ = π/3, we get (e^(iπ/3))^4 = (cos(π/3) + i sin(π/3))^4. Since cos(π/3) = 1/2 and sin(π/3) = √3/2, the expression simplifies to ((1/2) + (√3/2)i)^4. Expanding this using De Moivre's Theorem or binomial theorem, we get (cos(π/3) + i sin(π/3))^4 = cos(4π/3) + i sin(4π/3). At 4π/3, both cosine and sine are negative, but since we're looking for the real part only, it simplifies to cos(4π/3) = -1/2. Thus, (e^(iπ/3))^4 = (-1/2)^4 = 1.

Explanation:
We raised the complex exponential to a power and simplified using Euler's formula and De Moivre's Theorem.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to select A or B as they might come from misapplying powers, but we are looking for the real part of the expression.

Problem 7 (Seven):
Find the value of e^(iθ) if cos θ = -1/2 and sin θ = √3/2.
A) -√3/2 + i/2
B) 1/2 + √3i/2
C) -1/2 - √3i/2
D) -1/2 + √3i/2
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
Given cos θ = -1/2 and sin θ = √3/2, we can use Euler's formula to find e^(iθ): e^(iθ) = cos θ + i sin θ. Substituting the values, we get e^(iθ) = (-1/2) + (√3/2)i.

Explanation:
We directly applied Euler's formula using the given cosine and sine values.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to select A or C as they might come from a mix-up of signs, but we are looking for the complex exponential representation.
