Problem 1 (:question_1):
Given the complex number z = 3 + 4i, what is the value of (z̄)?
A) 3 - 4i
B) -3 + 4i
C) -3 - 4i
D) 3 + 4i
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The conjugate of a complex number z = a + bi is given by (z̄) = a - bi. For the given complex number z = 3 + 4i, its conjugate is (z̄) = 3 - 4i.

Explanation:
Applying the conjugate property to z = 3 + 4i, we change the sign of the imaginary part: (3 + 4i)̄ = 3 - 4i. Therefore, option A is correct.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to incorrectly switch both parts of the complex number (e.g., choosing B, which switches the real and imaginary parts). Remember that only the sign of the imaginary part changes.

Problem 2 (:question_2):
If z = 2i, what is the value of (z̄)^2?
A) -4
B) 4
C) 0
D) 8
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
First, find the conjugate of z: (2i)̄ = -2i. Then square the conjugate: (-2i)^2 = 4i^2 = -4.

Explanation:
The conjugate of 2i is -2i. Squaring this gives us (-2i)(-2i) = 4(-1) = -4, as i^2 = -1. So, option A is correct.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget that i^2 equals -1 when squaring the conjugate. Make sure to apply both operations correctly: conjugation and squaring.

Problem 3 (:question_3):
Given z = 5 - 6i, what is the value of (z + (z̄))^2?
A) 100
B) -100
C) 49
D) 7
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
First, find the sum of z and its conjugate: z + (z̄) = 5 - 6i + (5 + 6i) = 10. Squaring this sum gives us (10)^2 = 100.

Explanation:
The sum of a complex number and its conjugate is twice the real part: z + (z̄) = 2a, where a is the real part. For z = 5 - 6i, a = 5, so z + (z̄) = 10. Squaring this sum gives us 100. Therefore, option C is correct.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to incorrectly add the real and imaginary parts of z and its conjugate before squaring. Remember to first find the sum and then square it.

Problem 4 (:question_4):
If z = 1 + i, what is the value of (z^2)̄?
A) 2 - 2i
B) 2 + 2i
C) -2 + 2i
D) -2 - 2i
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
First, square z: z^2 = (1 + i)^2 = 1 + 2i + i^2 = 1 + 2i - 1 = 2i. Now find the conjugate of the result: (2i)̄ = -2i.

Explanation:
Squaring z gives us z^2 = (1 + i)(1 + i) = 1 + 2i + i^2 = 2i, as i^2 = -1. The conjugate of 2i is -2i. So, option A is correct.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget that i^2 equals -1 when squaring z. Make sure to apply both operations correctly: squaring and taking the conjugate.
