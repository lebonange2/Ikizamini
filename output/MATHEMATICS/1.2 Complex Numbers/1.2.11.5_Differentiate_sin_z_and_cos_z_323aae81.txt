Problem 1 (AsinBcosC):
Find the derivative of f(z) = sin(z) with respect to z.
A) cos(z)
B) sin(z)
C) 2sin(z)
D) cos^2(z)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The derivative of sin(z) with respect to z is cos(z). This is a basic property of trigonometric functions.

Explanation:
Recall that the derivative of the sine function is the cosine function and vice versa. The power rule was not applied here since there's no exponent on sin(z).

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to choose B, which is the original function itself instead of its derivative.

Problem 3 (CsinZcos2Z):
Find the derivative of h(z) = sin(2z) with respect to z.
A) 2cos(2z)
B) cos(2z)
C) 4sin(2z)
D) sin^2(2z)
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The derivative of sin(2z) with respect to z is 2cos(2z). This involves the chain rule.

Explanation:
To differentiate a composite function like this, we use the chain rule: [d/dz(sin(u)) * du/dz]. Here, u = 2z, so du/dz = 2. Thus, the derivative is 2cos(2z).

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget the chain rule and multiply by 2 (choice C), which would result in differentiating sin(z) instead of sin(2z).

Problem 5 (EsinZcosZ2):
Find the derivative of k(z) = sin(z)cos^2(z) with respect to z.
A) cos^2(z) - 2sin(z)cos(z)
B) cos(z) + 2sin(z)cos^2(z)
C) cos(z)cos^2(z) - 2sin(z)cos(z)
D) cos^3(z) - sin^2(z)cos(z)
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
The derivative of sin(z)cos^2(z) with respect to z is cos^3(z) - sin^2(z)cos(z). This involves the product rule and chain rule.

Explanation:
Using the product rule [u'v + uv'], where u = sin(z), v = cos^2(z), u' = cos(z), and v' = 2sin(z)cos(z). The derivative is thus cos(z)cos^2(z) - 2sin(z)cos^3(z). Simplifying, we get cos^3(z) - sin^2(z)cos(z).

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget the negative sign or apply the chain rule incorrectly on cos^2(z), leading to an incorrect result (choice C).
