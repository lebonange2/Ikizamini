Problem 1 (:1):
Find the cube of the complex number 3∠45° using De Moivre's theorem.
A) 3^3∠45°
B) 3∠135°
C) 3^3∠135°
D) 9∠45°
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
Using De Moivre's theorem, (r∠θ)^n = r^n∠(nθ), we have (3∠45°)^3 = 3^3∠(3*45°) = 27∠135°. Therefore, the correct answer is C: 3^3∠135°.

Explanation:
De Moivre's theorem allows us to raise a complex number in polar form to a power by raising the modulus to the power and multiplying the angle by the power.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to cube only the modulus (3^3∠45°, choice A) or to multiply the angle without raising the modulus to the power (9∠135°, choice D). Both of these mistakes fail because they do not apply De Moivre's theorem correctly.

Problem 3 (3):
Find the square root of the complex number 16∠-90° using De Moivre's theorem.
A) 4∠-45°
B) 2∠-45°
C) 4∠45°
D) 2∠-90°
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
To find the square root, we use De Moivre's theorem with n=1/2: (r∠θ)^(1/2) = r^(1/2)∠(θ/2). So, (16∠-90°)^(1/2) = 4∠(-90°/2) = 4∠-45°. Therefore, the correct answer is B: 2∠-45°.

Explanation:
When finding a root of a complex number, we use De Moivre's theorem with n equal to the reciprocal of the desired root.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to divide both the modulus and angle by 2 (4∠-90°/2, choice D), which is incorrect because only the angle should be divided when finding a square root.

Problem 5 (5):
Find the fourth power of the complex number -2∠-120° using De Moivre's theorem.
A) -16∠-480°
B) -16∠-120°
C) -16∠480°
D) 16∠-480°
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
Using De Moivre's theorem, (r∠θ)^n = r^n∠(nθ), we have (-2∠-120°)^4 = (-2)^4∠(-120°*4) = 16∠-480°. Since the angle is negative and we're multiplying by an even number, it will wrap around to a positive angle: 16∠(360° + (-480°)) = 16∠480°. Therefore, the correct answer is C: -16∠480°.

Explanation:
De Moivre's theorem allows us to raise a complex number in polar form to a power by raising the modulus to the power and multiplying the angle by the power. When dealing with negative angles, remember to adjust them to be within the standard range of 0 to 360 degrees or -180 to 180 degrees.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget that a negative angle can wrap around (e.g., choice B: -16∠-120°), which is incorrect because the angle should be adjusted to fall within the standard range.

Problem 6 (6):

A) 1/9∠180°
B) 1/9∠-180°
C) 9∠180°
D) 9∠-180°
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
When raising a complex number to a negative power, we invert the modulus and change the sign of the angle. So, (3∠-90°)^(-2) = 1/(3^2)∠(90°*2) = 1/9∠180°. Therefore, the correct answer is A: 1/9∠180°.

Explanation:
When raising a complex number to a negative power, we invert the modulus and change the sign of the angle.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to think that only the angle changes sign (e.g., choice B: 1/9∠-180°), but both the modulus and the angle must be adjusted when raising to a negative power.

Problem 7 (7):
Apply De Moivre's theorem to find the fifth root of -32∠60°.
A) -2∠12°
B) 2∠-12°
C) -2∠-12°
D) 2∠12°
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
To find the fifth root, we use De Moivre's theorem with n=1/5: (r∠θ)^(1/5) = r^(1/5)∠(θ/5). So, (-32∠60°)^(1/5) = -2^(1/5)∠(60°/5) = 2∠12°. Since the modulus is negative, we need to find a principal root (the one with the smallest positive angle), which in this case is 2∠12°. Therefore, the correct answer is D: 2∠12°.

Explanation:
When finding a root of a complex number, we use De Moivre's theorem with n equal to the reciprocal of the desired root. When dealing with negative moduli, we must choose the principal root with the smallest positive angle.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake would be to forget that the modulus is negative and choose a root with a negative angle (e.g., choice B: 2∠-12°), which is incorrect because we need to select the principal root with the smallest positive angle.
