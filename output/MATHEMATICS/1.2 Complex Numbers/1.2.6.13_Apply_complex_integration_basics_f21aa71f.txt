Problem 1 (Q1):
Evaluate the integral of \( f(z) = z^2 \) over the path \( C \), where \( C \) is the line segment from \( z = 0 \) to \( z = 1 + i \).
A) 1 + i
B) 1 + 2i
C) -i
D) 0
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The integral is \( \int_C z^2 \, dz = \int_0^{1+i} (x+iy)^2 \, dz \). Parametrize \( z = t + it \) for \( t \) from 0 to 1. Then \( dz = (1+i) dt \). The integral becomes \( \int_0^1 (t+it)^2 (1+i) \ dt = \int_0^1 t^2 (1+2i) dt = \frac{(1+2i)}{3} \), which evaluates to \( 1+i \).

Explanation:
Parametrization and substitution allow evaluating the line integral directly.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to confuse the parametrization or incorrectly compute \( dz \), resulting in wrong integrals.

Problem 2 (Q2):
Evaluate the contour integral \( \int_C \frac{1}{z} \, dz \) where \( C \) is the unit circle \( |z| = 1 \).
A) 0
B) 2\pi i
C) i
D) \pi i
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
By the Cauchy Integral Theorem, \( \int_C \frac{1}{z} \, dz = 2\pi i \). This is because \( \frac{1}{z} \) has a simple pole at the origin, which is inside the contour \( C \).

Explanation:
Use the residue theorem or Cauchy's integral formula for single poles to evaluate integrals easily.

Common mistake note:
Assuming the integral is zero due to misapplying the theorem ignoring the pole at the origin.

Problem 3 (Q3):
Compute \( \int_C e^z \, dz \) where \( C \) is the semicircle from \( -i \) to \( i \).
A) e^{i} - e^{-i}
B) 2i
C) 0
D) i(e^{-1} + e)
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
\( \int_C e^z \, dz = 0 \) because any entire function (like \( e^z \)) over a closed curve has an integral of zero due to the Cauchy Integral Theorem.

Explanation:
Entire functions over closed paths yield zero integrals by Cauchy's Theorem.

Common mistake note:
Forgetting the function \( e^z \) is entire could lead to incorrect non-zero calculations.

Problem 4 (Q4):
Find the value of \( \int_{|z|=2} \frac{dz}{z^2 - 1} \).
A) 0
B) \pi i
C) 2\pi i
D) \frac{\pi i}{2}
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The function has simple poles at \( z = 1 \) and \( z = -1 \). Both are inside \( |z|=2 \). The residue at \( z=1 \) is \( \frac{1}{2} \), and at \( z=-1 \) is also \( \frac{1}{2} \). Thus, the integral is \( 2\pi i (\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2}) = 2\pi i \).

Explanation:
Residues at singularities inside the contour sum to give the integral via the residue theorem.

Common mistake note:
Neglecting a pole or computing residues incorrectly can lead to zero or wrong integrals.

Problem 5 (Q5):
Evaluate the integral \( \int_C (z - 2) e^z \, dz \) where \( C \) is a circle of radius 3 centered at the origin.
A) 0
B) 6\pi i
C) 9\pi i
D) 2\pi ie^2
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Since \( (z - 2) e^z \) is entire (analytic everywhere), the contour integral around any closed path is zero by Cauchy's theorem.

Explanation:
Integral of an entire function over a closed path is zero.

Common mistake note:
A misconception is to consider the function non-entire due to a perceived pole.

Problem 6 (Q6):
Evaluate \( \int_C \overline{z} \, dz \), where \( C \) is the unit circle \( |z| = 1 \).
A) 0
B) \pi i
C) 2\pi i
D) -2\pi i
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
Use \( \overline{z} = \frac{1}{z} \) on the unit circle, so \( \int_C \overline{z} \, dz = \int_C \frac{1}{z} \, dz = -2\pi i \) by reversing orientation (negative of the standard result).

Explanation:
\( \overline{z} \) on \( |z|=1 \) is \( \frac{1}{z} \), reversing orientation gives \( -2\pi i \).

Common mistake note:
Forgetting the reversal due to \( \overline{z} \) could lead to a positive result.

Problem 7 (Q7):
Determine \( \int_C \frac{1}{(z-1)^2} \, dz \) where \( C \) is the circle \( |z-1| = 2 \).
A) 0
B) 2\pi i
C) -2\pi i
D) 4\pi i
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
The point \( z = 1 \) is outside the path \( |z-1|=2 \), hence by Cauchy's theorem, the integral over this path is 0.

Explanation:
If the singularity is outside the contour, the integral is 0 by Cauchy's theorem.

Common mistake note:
Failing to verify whether a singularity is inside or outside the path can lead to incorrect non-zero integrals.
