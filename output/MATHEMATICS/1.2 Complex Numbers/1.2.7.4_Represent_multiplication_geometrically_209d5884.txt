Problem 1 (InequalityGraphs):
The graph below represents the inequality y > 3x. Which of the following graphs represents the solution to the equation 2y = 6x?
A) A line with a positive slope passing through the origin.
B) A line with a negative slope not passing through the origin.
C) A line with a slope of 2 passing through the point (0, 0).
D) A line with a slope of 3 passing through the point (0, 6).
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The equation 2y = 6x simplifies to y = 3x. This is equivalent to multiplying both sides by 2, which does not change the inequality relationship. The correct graph should represent a line with a slope of 3 and passing through the origin. Only choice C satisfies these conditions.

Explanation:
Multiplication by a positive constant does not change the direction of inequality; it only scales the graph. Since we multiplied both sides of y > 3x by 2, the new equation should have the same slope as the original but be scaled up by a factor of 2.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing A or B because they do not consider the effect of multiplying both sides by a positive number. This does not change the direction of the inequality.

Problem 2 (RectanglesArea):
A rectangle has a length of 5 units and a width of 4 units. What is the area of a similar rectangle with twice the length and half the width?
A) 10 square units
B) 20 square units
C) 40 square units
D) 80 square units
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The area of a rectangle is length times width. Doubling the length and halving the width results in an area of (2 * 5) * (1/2 * 4) = 10 * 2 = 20 square units.

Explanation:
When multiplying two factors being multiplied together, you can multiply each factor separately. In this case, doubling one factor and halving the other does not change the area.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Assuming that multiplying both length and width by different factors would result in a more complex calculation. However, since they are being multiplied together, the multiplication can be done separately.

Problem 3 (TriangleArea):
A triangle has base 6 units and height 3 units. If we double both the base and the height, what is the area of the new triangle?
A) 9 square units
B) 18 square units
C) 27 square units
D) 36 square units
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The area of a triangle is 1/2 * base * height. Doubling both the base and the height results in an area of (1/2 * 2 * 6) * (2 * 3) = 6 * 6 = 36 square units. However, since we doubled both dimensions, the new area is four times the original.

Explanation:
When multiplying a factor by a constant in an area formula, multiply that factor directly. In this case, doubling both base and height quadruples the area because each of the two factors is doubled.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Doubling the area by multiplying only one of the dimensions (base or height) instead of considering the effect on both dimensions.

Problem 4 (ScalingCircleArea):
A circle has a radius of 3 units. If we double the radius, what is the area of the new circle?
A) 9π square units
B) 18π square units
C) 27π square units
D) 36π square units
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The area of a circle is πr^2. Doubling the radius results in an area of π(2r)^2 = 4πr^2. With r = 3, the new area is 4π(3^2) = 36π square units.

Explanation:
When scaling a circle's radius by a factor, the area scales by the square of that factor. In this case, doubling the radius results in an area four times the original.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Assuming that multiplying the radius by 2 would double the area, not realizing that the area is proportional to the square of the radius.

Problem 5 (ScalingVolumeCuboid):
A cuboid has dimensions 3 units by 4 units by 5 units. If we triple the length and halve the width, what is the new volume?
A) 180 cubic units
B) 270 cubic units
C) 405 cubic units
D) 540 cubic units
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The volume of a cuboid is length * width * height. Tripling the length and halving the width results in a new volume of (3 * 3) * (1/2 * 4) * 5 = 9 * 2 * 5 = 90 cubic units. However, since the height remains unchanged, the total volume is 90 * 5 = 450 cubic units.

Explanation:
When scaling different dimensions of a cuboid by different factors, multiply each dimension separately and then multiply them together. In this case, length was tripled, width was halved, and height remained the same, resulting in a total volume increase.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Incorrectly multiplying all three dimensions together without considering the effect of keeping one dimension unchanged.

Problem 6 (ScalingVolumeCylinder):
A cylinder has a radius of 3 units and a height of 5 units. If we double both the radius and the height, what is the new volume?
A) 90π cubic units
B) 180π cubic units
C) 270π cubic units
D) 360π cubic units
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The volume of a cylinder is πr^2h. Doubling both the radius and the height results in a new volume of π(2r)^2 * 2h = 4πr^2 * 2h = 8πr^2h. With r = 3 and h = 5, the new volume is 8π(3^2) * 5 = 720π cubic units.

Explanation:
When scaling a cylinder's radius and height by a factor, the volume scales by the product of the squares of those factors. In this case, doubling both radius and height results in an 8 times increase in volume.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Assuming that multiplying the radius and height by 2 would double the volume, not realizing that the volume is proportional to the square of the radius and linearly proportional to the height.

Problem 7 (ScalingVolumeSphere):
A sphere has a radius of 3 units. If we quadruple the radius, what is the new volume?
A) 108π cubic units
B) 216π cubic units
C) 432π cubic units
D) 729π cubic units
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The volume of a sphere is (4/3)πr^3. Quadrupling the radius results in a new volume of (4/3)π(4r)^3 = 64πr^3. With r = 3, the new volume is (4/3)π(4*3)^3 = 1024π cubic units.

Explanation:
When scaling a sphere's radius by a factor, the volume scales by the cube of that factor. In this case, quadrupling the radius results in a 64 times increase in volume.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Assuming that multiplying the radius by 4 would simply multiply the volume by 4, not realizing that the volume is proportional to the cube of the radius.
