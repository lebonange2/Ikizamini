Problem 1 (:1):
Which of the following equations represents a circle with radius 3 centered at the origin in polar coordinates?
A) r = 9θ
B) r = 3
C) r^2 = 9
D) \theta = 3
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The equation of a circle with radius $r$ centered at the origin in polar coordinates is given by $r = r$. So, for a circle with radius 3, the equation is $r = 3$.

Explanation:
Option A represents an angle-dependent curve, option C represents a concentric circle pattern, and option D represents a straight line along the angular direction.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing option C, which describes a set of circles with different radii centered at the origin, rather than a single circle with radius 3.

Problem 2 (2):
The polar equation $r = 4	ext{cos}(2θ)$ represents what type of curve?
A) Circle
B) Line
C) Ellipse
D) Cardioid
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The given polar equation is of the form $r = a	ext{cos}(nθ)$, which represents an ellipse with the origin as one of its foci when $n ≠ 1$. Since $n = 2$ in this case, it's an ellipse.

Explanation:
Option A (circle) requires $n = 1$, option B (line) is not represented by a polar equation, and option D (cardioid) would have $r = a	ext{cos}(θ)$.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing option A because of the presence of $	ext{cos}(θ)$, but forgetting that the coefficient of $	ext{cos}(θ)$ and the value of $n$ determine the shape.

Problem 3 (3):
What is the polar representation of the rectangular equation $x^2 + y^2 = 9$?
A) $r = 3	ext{sin}(θ)$
B) $r = 3	ext{cos}(θ)$
C) $r = 9$
D) $r^2 = 9$
Correct Answer: D

Solution:
The rectangular equation $x^2 + y^2 = r^2$ represents a circle in polar coordinates. Given $x^2 + y^2 = 9$, we can directly write the polar representation as $r^2 = 9$.

Explanation:
Option A and B represent ellipses, option C represents a single point at a distance of 9 from the origin.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing options A or B by equating $x^2 + y^2$ to $r	ext{sin}(θ)$ or $r	ext{cos}(θ)$, which is incorrect for converting a circle equation.

Problem 4 (4):
The polar equation $r = 5	ext{sin}(θ) + 2	ext{cos}(θ)$ represents what type of curve?
A) Line
B) Parabola
C) Hyperbola
D) Lemniscate
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
The given polar equation is a sum of $r	ext{sin}(θ)$ and $r	ext{cos}(θ)$, which represents a parabola in polar coordinates.

Explanation:
Option A (line) would have only one trigonometric function, option C (hyperbola) requires different coefficients for sine and cosine, and option D (lemniscate) has a form $r^n = a^n	ext{cos}^n(θ) + b^n	ext{sin}^n(θ)$.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing option C because of the presence of both sine and cosine, but not recognizing that their coefficients are equal.

Problem 6 (6):
The polar equation for a cardioid with its cusp at the origin is?
A) $r = 2 + 2	ext{cos}(θ)$
B) $r = 2 - 2	ext{sin}(θ)$
C) $r = 1 + 	ext{cos}(θ)$
D) $r = 1 + 	ext{sin}(θ)$
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
The polar equation for a cardioid with its cusp at the origin is given by $r = a + a	ext{cos}(θ)$ or $r = a + a	ext{sin}(θ)$. The correct choice here is $r = 1 + 	ext{cos}(θ)$.

Explanation:
Option A and B represent limaçons, option D represents an epicycloid.

Common mistake note:
Common mistake: Choosing options A or B because they have a similar form to the cardioid equation but with sine instead of cosine.
