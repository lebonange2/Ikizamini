Problem 1 (One):
Given a complex number z = a + bi, where a is the real part and b is the imaginary part, prove that z - z̄ = 2i·Im(z)
A) (a + b) + (a - b)i
B) (a + b) - (a - b)i
C) 2bi
D) 2ai
Correct Answer: C

Solution:
z = a + bi and z̄ = a - bi. So, z - z̄ = (a + bi) - (a - bi) = 2bi.

Explanation:
The correct solution subtracts the complex conjugate of z from z to obtain twice the imaginary part.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to add the real parts instead of subtracting them, resulting in answer A. Another mistake might be multiplying b by i twice, leading to D.

Problem 2 (Two):
Given z = 3 + 4i, prove that z - z̄ = 2i·Im(z)
A) -8i
B) 8i
C) 6
D) 10
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Given z = 3 + 4i, z̄ = 3 - 4i. So, z - z̄ = (3 + 4i) - (3 - 4i) = 8i.

Explanation:
The correct solution substitutes the given values for z and its conjugate into the formula and calculates the difference.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to subtract the real parts, leading to C. Another might be not recognizing that only the imaginary part contributes to the result.

Problem 3 (Three):
Prove that for any complex number z = x + yi, z - z̄ = 2i·Im(z)
A) True
B) False
C) Cannot be determined without specific values for x and y
D) Depends on the value of x
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Let z = x + yi. Then, z̄ = x - yi. So, z - z̄ = (x + yi) - (x - yi) = 2yi. Since this is twice the imaginary part (y), the statement is true.

Explanation:
The correct solution demonstrates that the difference between a complex number and its conjugate always equals twice the imaginary part, regardless of x and y.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake might be thinking that the result depends on x or specific values for x and y.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Problem 4 (Four):
Given z = 2i, prove that z - z̄ = 2i·Im(z)
A) 0
B) 4i
C) -4i
D) 2
Correct Answer: A

Solution:
Given z = 2i, z̄ = -2i. So, z - z̄ = 2i - (-2i) = 0.

Explanation:
The correct solution substitutes the given value for z and its conjugate into the formula and calculates the difference.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to think that the real part of z is zero, leading to D. Another might be not recognizing that the imaginary parts cancel each other out.

Problem 5 (Five):
Given z = 1 + 3i and w = 4 - 2i, prove that (z - z̄) + (w - w̄) = 2i·Im(z) + 2i·Im(w)
A) -6i
B) 6i
C) 0
D) 12
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Given z = 1 + 3i, z̄ = 1 - 3i and w = 4 - 2i, w̄ = 4 + 2i. So, (z - z̄) + (w - w̄) = (1 + 3i - (1 - 3i)) + (4 - 2i - (4 + 2i)) = 6i.

Explanation:
The correct solution substitutes the given values for z, w, and their conjugates into the formula and calculates the sum.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to add or subtract the real parts incorrectly, leading to C. Another might be not recognizing that only the imaginary parts contribute to the result.

Problem 7 (Seven):
Given z = -2 + 5i, prove that (z - z̄)^2 = (2i·Im(z))^2
A) -144
B) 144
C) 0
D) 9
Correct Answer: B

Solution:
Given z = -2 + 5i, z̄ = -2 - 5i. So, z - z̄ = (-2 + 5i) - (-2 - 5i) = 10i. Thus, (z - z̄)^2 = (10i)^2 = -100, which simplifies to 144.

Explanation:
The correct solution substitutes the given value for z and its conjugate into the formula, calculates the difference, and squares it.

Common mistake note:
A common mistake is to square the imaginary part without accounting for the negative sign, leading to D. Another might be not recognizing that squaring an imaginary number results in a negative real number.
